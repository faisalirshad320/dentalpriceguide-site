Retainers After Braces: Types, How Long to Wear Them and Whether It Is Forever

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated November 1, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

After braces, most orthodontists ask you to wear a retainer full time for the first few months, then at night, often indefinitely. Teeth tend to drift back toward their old positions throughout life, so long-term nighttime wear is the most reliable way to protect a result that can cost thousands of dollars, with metal braces averaging $6,343 according to CareCredit.

Key takeaways
  • Retainers hold teeth in place while bone and gums settle after braces.
  • Typical advice: full-time wear at first, then nightly, often for life.
  • Types include clear removable, wire (Hawley) and bonded fixed retainers.
  • Stopping retainer wear is a common reason teeth shift and braces are redone.
  • Clean retainers daily and replace lost or broken ones promptly.

Why do you need a retainer after braces?

You need a retainer after braces because teeth do not become fixed in their new positions the moment the brackets come off. The ligaments and bone around the roots need months to reorganise, and the stretched gum fibres tend to pull teeth back toward where they started.

Teeth also continue to shift gradually throughout life, regardless of braces, due to ageing, chewing forces and changes in the jaw. A retainer holds the teeth steady while the tissues settle and then guards against this lifelong drift. Without one, crowding often returns, especially in the lower front teeth.

Do you have to wear retainers forever after braces?

Many orthodontists now recommend wearing retainers at night indefinitely after braces, because teeth can shift at any age. That does not mean wearing them all day forever; after the initial period, nightly wear is usually enough.

Some people find that wearing a retainer a few nights a week holds their teeth well once things are stable, but stopping completely carries a real risk of relapse. A practical test is whether your retainer still slips in easily. If it feels tight after a break, your teeth have started to move, and you should wear it more often and check with your orthodontist.

How long do you wear a retainer each day?

A typical retainer schedule starts with full-time wear, often about 22 hours a day, for the first several months after braces, removing it only to eat and clean. Your orthodontist then usually moves you to nighttime-only wear.

Schedules vary between orthodontists and patients. Someone who had large gaps closed or significant rotations corrected may be asked to wear retainers more. Follow your own orthodontist's instructions, and bring your retainers to follow-up visits so the fit can be checked.

What are the different types of retainers?

The main types of retainers are clear removable retainers, Hawley retainers and bonded retainers. Each holds teeth in place in a slightly different way.

  • Clear removable retainers are thin plastic trays made from a scan or impression. They are discreet and comfortable but can crack and wear over time.
  • Hawley retainers have an acrylic base and a wire across the front teeth. They are durable, can be adjusted and allow the upper and lower teeth to settle together.
  • Bonded retainers are thin wires glued to the back of the front teeth. They work around the clock without relying on you, but need careful cleaning and can break or come unstuck.

Many patients get a combination, such as a bonded lower retainer plus a removable upper retainer.

What happens if you stop wearing your retainer?

If you stop wearing your retainer, your teeth may gradually shift back toward their original positions. The change can be slow, so you might not notice it for months, until the retainer no longer fits or crowding becomes visible.

Minor movement can sometimes be reversed by wearing the retainer more, if it still fits. Larger movement may need a new retainer, clear aligners or another round of braces, which means paying again. Given that CareCredit reports averages of $6,343 for metal braces and $5,108 for clear aligners, consistent retainer wear is a very cost-effective habit.

How do you clean and care for a retainer?

Cleaning a retainer daily keeps it hygienic and extends its life. Rinse it after removing it, brush it gently with a soft toothbrush and cool or lukewarm water, and use mild soap or a retainer cleaner. Avoid hot water, which can warp plastic retainers, and avoid toothpaste on clear retainers, which can scratch them.

  • Store the retainer in its case when it is not in your mouth.
  • Keep it away from pets, which often chew retainers.
  • Do not wrap it in a napkin at meals; this is how many retainers are thrown away.
  • Brush your teeth before putting it back in.
  • For bonded retainers, use floss threaders or interdental brushes under the wire.

How often do retainers need replacing?

Retainers need replacing when they crack, warp, wear thin or stop fitting properly. Clear removable retainers tend to need replacement more often than Hawley retainers. Bonded retainers can last many years but may need repair if the wire breaks or detaches.

If a retainer breaks or is lost, contact your orthodontist promptly rather than going weeks without one. Ask whether replacement retainers are included in your treatment fee or charged separately, and consider keeping a spare set made from your final scan.

Are retainers included in the cost of braces?

Retainers are often included in the overall cost of braces, at least the first set, but practices differ. Some charge separately for retainers or for replacements. Ask at the start of treatment so the full cost is clear.

CareCredit reports average braces costs of $6,343 for metal, $5,834 for ceramic, $9,221 for lingual and $5,108 for clear aligners. Delta Dental cites $3,000 to $7,000 for traditional metal braces, and Pearl lists comprehensive treatment at $3,680 to $9,650. Orthodontic insurance may cover retainers as part of treatment; Delta Dental's coverage guide explains benefits. See how much braces cost and how much braces cost without insurance.

What should parents know about retainers for kids?

Parents should know that retainers are the stage of orthodontic treatment most often neglected by children and teenagers. Once braces come off, the motivation to keep up with appliances can fade, yet this is when teeth are most likely to move.

Build retainer wear into the bedtime routine, keep the case in the same place every night, and check fit periodically. Losing retainers at school or during sleepovers is common, so a spare set can be worth having. The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first orthodontic check by age 7, as Delta Dental notes, so many families start the orthodontic journey early and need to plan for retainers well into the teenage years. Healthline also discusses retainers in its braces cost guide. If your child chose braces colors or clear braces, the retainer phase is the same.

Frequently asked questions

Do you have to wear retainers forever after braces?

Many orthodontists recommend wearing retainers at night indefinitely after braces, because teeth can shift throughout life. After an initial full-time period, nightly wear is usually enough. Stopping completely carries a real risk that teeth will move back.

How long should I wear my retainer each day after braces?

Most people wear a retainer about 22 hours a day for the first several months after braces, then switch to nighttime wear. Follow your orthodontist's schedule, which depends on how much your teeth were moved.

What happens if I stop wearing my retainer?

If you stop wearing your retainer, your teeth may gradually shift back toward their original positions, often starting with the lower front teeth. Small changes may be reversed by wearing the retainer again if it fits; larger changes may need new treatment.

Which type of retainer is best?

No retainer is best for everyone. Clear removable retainers are discreet, Hawley retainers are durable and adjustable, and bonded retainers work constantly without relying on you. Many orthodontists use a combination, such as a bonded lower wire and a removable upper retainer.

How do I clean my retainer?

Clean your retainer daily by rinsing it and brushing it gently with a soft toothbrush, cool or lukewarm water and mild soap or a retainer cleaner. Avoid hot water and toothpaste on clear retainers, and store it in its case.
