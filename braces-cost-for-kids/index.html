When Should Kids Get Braces, and How Much Do Braces Cost for Kids?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 28, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

Most kids get braces between about ages 9 and 14, once most adult teeth have come in, and the American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first orthodontic check no later than age 7 (Delta Dental). Braces for kids cost about the same as for adults: CareCredit reports averages of $6,343 for metal braces and $5,834 for ceramic.

Key takeaways
  • A first orthodontic evaluation is recommended no later than age 7.
  • Most children start full braces between about 9 and 14.
  • Some kids benefit from early (Phase 1) treatment to guide jaw growth.
  • Metal braces average $6,343 (CareCredit); Delta Dental cites $3,000 to $7,000.

What age do kids get braces?

Most kids get braces between about ages 9 and 14. By then, most or all of the permanent teeth have come in, the jaws are still growing, and children are usually old enough to care for braces responsibly.

The exact timing depends on the child's teeth rather than age alone. Some children are ready earlier because their adult teeth come in early; others need to wait until their last baby teeth fall out. Bite problems that involve jaw growth are often easier to correct while a child is still growing, which is one reason orthodontists like to see children early.

When should a child first see an orthodontist?

A child should see an orthodontist for a first check no later than age 7, according to the American Association of Orthodontists, as cited by Delta Dental. By age 7, the first adult molars and front teeth have usually come in, and an orthodontist can spot developing bite and spacing problems.

A first visit does not usually mean braces right away. Most children are simply monitored every six to twelve months until the right time. The visit gives parents a plan and helps catch problems that are easier to treat early. Your child's dentist can also refer you if they see a concern.

What are signs a child may need braces?

Signs a child may need braces include crowded or overlapping teeth, large gaps, teeth that stick out, and a bite where the upper and lower teeth do not meet properly. Delta Dental's parent guide lists common clues.

  • Early or late loss of baby teeth.
  • Difficulty chewing or biting.
  • Mouth breathing or snoring.
  • Thumb or finger sucking after about age 5.
  • Crowded, misplaced or blocked-out teeth.
  • Jaws that shift, click or make sounds.
  • Biting the cheek or the roof of the mouth.
  • Teeth that meet abnormally or not at all.
  • A face that looks out of proportion.

What is early (Phase 1) orthodontic treatment?

Early or Phase 1 orthodontic treatment is a short course of treatment for younger children, often around ages 7 to 10, while some baby teeth remain. Its goal is to correct problems with jaw growth or bite early, rather than to straighten every tooth.

Examples include expanding a narrow upper jaw, correcting a crossbite, creating space for crowded adult teeth or addressing habits such as thumb sucking. Many children who have Phase 1 treatment still need a second phase of full braces later, but the second phase may be shorter or simpler. Not every child needs early treatment, so ask your orthodontist to explain the specific benefit before agreeing.

How much do braces cost for kids?

Braces for kids cost about the same as braces for adults, because the fee depends on the treatment rather than the patient's age. CareCredit's procedure cost list reports averages of $6,343 for metal braces, $5,834 for ceramic braces, $5,108 for clear aligners and $9,221 for lingual braces.

Delta Dental cites $3,000 to $7,000 for traditional metal braces, and Pearl lists comprehensive orthodontic treatment at $3,680 to $9,650 nationally, with an average of $5,645. Phase 1 treatment is usually billed separately from later full braces, so ask for the cost of both phases when early treatment is suggested. Our how much braces cost guide compares all types.

What type of braces is best for kids?

Metal braces are the most common choice for kids because they are durable, effective for almost every problem and usually the most affordable. Many children enjoy choosing coloured elastic ties at each visit; see our guide to braces colors.

Ceramic braces are less visible but more fragile, which can be a concern for active kids. Clear aligners can work for some older children and teens, but they only work if worn most of the day, which requires good responsibility. Lingual braces are rarely used for young children. See clear braces for more on less visible options.

How long do kids wear braces?

Kids usually wear braces for about one to three years, depending on how much correction their teeth and bite need. Mild crowding may finish toward the shorter end, while bite problems and impacted teeth take longer.

Children who had early Phase 1 treatment may have a shorter second phase, because some of the work, such as widening the upper jaw, has already been done. Growth spurts during the early teen years can help orthodontists correct certain bite problems more efficiently, which is one reason timing matters.

After braces come off, children wear retainers to keep teeth in place. This stage is easy to neglect, especially for teenagers, but skipping retainers is a common reason teeth shift and families end up paying for treatment again. Many orthodontists recommend nighttime retainer wear for years.

Does insurance cover braces for kids?

Insurance covers braces for kids more often than for adults. Many dental plans with orthodontic benefits cover children up to a certain age, typically paying a percentage of the cost up to a separate lifetime orthodontic maximum.

Delta Dental's coverage guide explains how benefits work. In some cases, braces for children with severe bite problems may be covered by Medicaid or the Children's Health Insurance Program when deemed medically necessary; rules vary by state. How can parents help kids succeed with braces?

Parents can help kids succeed with braces by supporting good habits at home. Treatment goes faster and costs less in repairs when children follow the rules.

  1. Check brushing and flossing, especially around brackets.
  2. Pack braces-friendly lunches and snacks.
  3. Keep orthodontic wax and spare elastics handy.
  4. Remind kids to wear rubber bands as prescribed.
  5. Use a mouthguard for sports.
  6. Keep every adjustment appointment.

Frequently asked questions

What age should kids get braces?

Most kids get braces between about ages 9 and 14, when most adult teeth have come in and the jaws are still growing. The exact time depends on the child's teeth. A first orthodontic check is recommended no later than age 7 to plan timing.

How much do braces cost for kids?

Braces for kids cost about the same as for adults. CareCredit reports averages of $6,343 for metal braces, $5,834 for ceramic braces and $5,108 for clear aligners. Delta Dental cites $3,000 to $7,000 for traditional metal braces. Early Phase 1 treatment is usually billed separately.

How old do you have to be to get braces?

There is no minimum age to get braces, but full braces are usually placed between about 9 and 14. Some children start early Phase 1 treatment around ages 7 to 10 for jaw or bite problems. Adults can get braces at any age.

Does insurance cover braces for children?

Many dental plans with orthodontic benefits cover children, often up to a certain age and a lifetime orthodontic maximum. Medicaid and CHIP may cover braces for children with severe bite problems when medically necessary. Check your plan details and ask for a pre-treatment estimate.

Is early orthodontic treatment worth it?

Early orthodontic treatment is worth it for some children with jaw growth problems, crossbites or severe crowding, because these are easier to correct while growing. Many children do not need it. Ask your orthodontist what specific problem early treatment would fix and what it costs.
