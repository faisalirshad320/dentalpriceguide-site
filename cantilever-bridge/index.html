Cantilever Bridge Dental: What It Is, When It Is Used and What It Costs

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 6, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

A cantilever bridge is a dental bridge in which the false tooth is supported by a crown on only one neighbouring tooth, instead of on both sides. It is used when there is a healthy tooth on only one side of the gap, often for front teeth. Bridge costs vary by type: a three-unit bridge averages about $3,600 out-of-network, according to Delta Dental.

Key takeaways
  • A cantilever bridge is anchored on one side only.
  • It is used when only one neighbouring tooth can support a bridge.
  • It works best for front teeth and small, low-stress gaps.
  • Leverage on the single supporting tooth is the main risk.
  • Insurance typically covers 50% to 80% of bridge costs (Delta Dental).

What is a cantilever bridge?

A cantilever bridge is a fixed bridge that replaces a missing tooth with a false tooth, called a pontic, attached to a crown on just one adjacent tooth. The pontic extends out over the gap like a cantilever, supported from one side.

A traditional bridge, by contrast, uses crowns on both teeth either side of the gap. Cantilever bridges are less common than traditional bridges but are useful in specific situations. Our guide to dental bridges explains all the main types.

When is a cantilever bridge used?

A cantilever bridge is used when there is a suitable supporting tooth on only one side of the gap. Common situations include a missing tooth at the end of a row where there is no tooth behind it, or a missing front tooth where one neighbour is healthy and strong.

Cantilever bridges are most often used to replace small teeth, such as upper lateral incisors, where biting forces are relatively low. For back teeth that take heavy chewing forces, a traditional bridge or an implant is usually preferred.

What are the advantages of a cantilever bridge?

The main advantages of a cantilever bridge are that it needs only one supporting tooth and can be completed without surgery. Only one tooth has to be prepared for a crown, which preserves the other neighbour.

  • Fewer teeth prepared than a traditional bridge.
  • No surgery, unlike an implant.
  • Relatively quick, usually two visits.
  • Useful where one neighbour is missing or unsuitable.
  • Good appearance for front teeth with modern ceramics.

What are the disadvantages of a cantilever bridge?

The main disadvantage of a cantilever bridge is leverage. Because the false tooth is supported on one side only, biting forces act like a lever on the supporting tooth, which can loosen the crown, fracture the tooth or stress its root over time.

  • Higher risk of complications on the supporting tooth.
  • Not suitable for heavy biting areas such as molars.
  • Supporting tooth must be prepared for a crown, removing enamel.
  • Bone loss under the pontic continues, as with other bridges.

Careful case selection by the dentist is important to reduce these risks.

How does a cantilever compare with other options?

A cantilever bridge compares with other options mainly on how many teeth it uses and how much force it can handle. A traditional bridge is stronger but needs two supporting teeth. A Maryland bridge uses bonded wings with little tooth preparation but suits low-force front areas. An implant stands alone without involving neighbouring teeth.

Healthline's implant versus bridge guide and the Cleveland Clinic's prosthodontics overview explain the trade-offs between bridges and implants.

What happens when you get a cantilever bridge?

Getting a cantilever bridge usually takes two visits. At the first, the dentist numbs the area, shapes the supporting tooth for a crown, takes a scan or impression and fits a temporary bridge.

A dental lab makes the bridge as a single unit: the crown on the supporting tooth joined to the pontic. At the second visit, the dentist checks the fit, colour and bite carefully, making sure the pontic does not take heavy biting force, then cements the bridge.

How much does a cantilever bridge cost?

A cantilever bridge cost depends on the materials, the tooth being replaced and your dentist. Published national averages usually group bridges by type rather than listing cantilever bridges separately. Delta Dental reports about $3,600 out-of-network for a three-unit bridge, and CareCredit reports averages of $5,197 for a traditional single-tooth bridge and $1,569 for a Maryland bridge.

A cantilever bridge involves fewer units than a three-unit bridge, but price is set by each practice. Ask for a written quote. See dental bridge cost for all types.

Does insurance cover cantilever bridges?

Insurance often covers cantilever bridges as major restorative services. Delta Dental notes coverage for bridges is typically 50% to 80% of the cost, depending on the plan, subject to the deductible and annual maximum.

Some plans have a missing tooth clause for teeth lost before coverage began or waiting periods for major services. Request a pre-treatment estimate before treatment.

How do you care for a cantilever bridge?

Caring for a cantilever bridge means protecting the single supporting tooth and keeping the area clean. Clean under the pontic daily with a floss threader, super floss or water flosser, and brush around the crown margin carefully.

Avoid biting hard foods directly with the bridged tooth, do not use teeth to open packages, and wear a night guard if you grind. Regular check-ups let your dentist watch the supporting tooth for decay or looseness. See before and after dental bridge examples for typical results.

Who is a good candidate for a cantilever bridge?

A good candidate for a cantilever bridge has a single missing tooth in a low-stress area, usually at the front of the mouth, and a strong, healthy neighbouring tooth with a good root and solid bone support. The bite should not place heavy force on the false tooth, and the patient should not grind heavily.

The supporting tooth ideally already needs a crown or has a large filling, so crowning it brings an extra benefit. If the neighbouring tooth is untouched and healthy, a Maryland bridge or an implant may preserve more natural tooth structure. Your dentist will assess the bite, the supporting tooth and the gap with an exam and X-rays before recommending a cantilever design.

How long does a cantilever bridge last?

A cantilever bridge can last many years when it is placed in the right situation and well cared for. Its lifespan depends mostly on the health of the single supporting tooth and how much force the false tooth takes. Regular check-ups let your dentist catch looseness or decay on the supporting tooth early.

Frequently asked questions

What is a cantilever bridge?

A cantilever bridge is a dental bridge in which the false tooth is supported by a crown on only one neighbouring tooth. It is used when there is a suitable tooth on just one side of the gap, most often for front teeth with low biting forces.

Are cantilever bridges strong?

Cantilever bridges are strong enough for low-force areas such as front teeth, but less suitable for back teeth. Because the pontic is supported on one side, biting forces create leverage on the supporting tooth, which can cause problems over time.

How much does a cantilever bridge cost?

Cantilever bridge costs vary by practice and are rarely listed separately in national data. Delta Dental reports about $3,600 out-of-network for a three-unit bridge, and CareCredit reports $5,197 for a traditional single-tooth bridge and $1,569 for a Maryland bridge.

How long does a cantilever bridge last?

A cantilever bridge can last many years with careful case selection and good care, but it carries more risk of complications than a traditional bridge because of leverage on the single supporting tooth. Regular check-ups help catch problems early.

Is a cantilever bridge better than an implant?

A cantilever bridge avoids surgery and is usually quicker, but an implant does not rely on neighbouring teeth and helps preserve bone. Implants are often preferred when bone and health allow; cantilever bridges suit specific situations with one healthy neighbour.
