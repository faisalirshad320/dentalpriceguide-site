How Much Are Braces Without Insurance, and How Much With It?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 21, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

How much are braces without insurance?

Braces without insurance cost $3,680 to $9,650 for comprehensive orthodontic treatment nationally, with an average of $5,645, according to Pearl dental cost data based on ADA fee surveys. That figure covers full treatment under dental code D8080, the code orthodontists use for comprehensive braces for adolescents.

Other national sources land in the same area. CareCredit reports an average of $6,343 for metal braces in its 2023–2024 cost study, and Delta Dental gives a range of $3,000 to $7,000 for traditional metal braces. The sources measure slightly different things, a fee-survey range, a cost-study average and a consumer guide estimate, so treat them as a reference band rather than one exact price.

Braces quotes are usually a single fee for the whole treatment. Ask whether that fee includes records and X-rays, all adjustment visits, emergency repairs and retainers after the braces come off, because those items are sometimes billed separately. Our main guide to how much braces cost covers each of these line items.

How much do different types of braces cost without insurance?

Without insurance, lingual braces cost the most and clear aligners the least on average, with metal and ceramic braces in between, based on CareCredit's 2023–2024 cost study. The averages are:

  • Metal braces: $6,343 average (CareCredit). Delta Dental's guide gives $3,000 to $7,000.
  • Ceramic braces: $5,834 average (CareCredit). Tooth-colored brackets are less visible; see our guide to clear braces.
  • Clear aligners: $5,108 average (CareCredit).
  • Lingual braces: $9,221 average (CareCredit). Delta Dental's guide gives $8,000 to $10,000. Lingual brackets sit behind the teeth and need custom fabrication and more chair time.

Braces type is only part of the price. Two people choosing metal braces can receive different quotes because one needs a longer treatment, extra appliances or tooth extractions first. Your orthodontist will recommend the type that suits your bite, not only your budget.

Does dental insurance cover braces?

Dental insurance covers braces only when the plan includes orthodontic benefits, and many plans do not. Orthodontic coverage is a separate benefit from the cleanings, fillings and crowns described in a standard plan, as Delta Dental explains in its overview of what dental insurance covers.

Plans that do include braces usually set these limits:

  • Lifetime orthodontic maximum. The plan pays up to a fixed dollar amount per person for orthodontics, once, rather than resetting every year.
  • Age limits. Many plans cover orthodontics for children and teens only. Adult orthodontic coverage exists but is less common.
  • Waiting periods. Some plans require months of membership before orthodontic benefits begin.
  • Medical necessity. A few plans pay only when the bite problem meets a clinical threshold rather than for cosmetic alignment.

Medical insurance rarely covers braces. Exceptions usually involve conditions such as cleft lip and palate or jaw surgery. Medicaid and CHIP may cover braces for children with severe, medically necessary bite problems, with rules that vary by state.

How much are braces with insurance?

Braces with insurance cost the orthodontist's total fee minus your plan's orthodontic benefit, after any deductible. Because most plans cap orthodontic coverage with a lifetime maximum, families often still pay a large share of the fee out of pocket.

Working out your cost with insurance takes three numbers: the orthodontist's total quoted fee, the percentage your plan pays toward orthodontics, and your lifetime orthodontic maximum. The plan pays its percentage until it reaches the maximum, and you pay the rest. Ask the orthodontist's office to request a pre-treatment estimate from your insurer so the figure is confirmed in writing.

Braces with insurance are usually paid monthly. Many orthodontists ask for a down payment, then split the rest into monthly installments across the treatment period. Insurers also often pay their share in installments rather than all at once. If you change jobs or plans partway through, coverage for the remaining months may stop, so check how a new plan handles treatment already in progress.

What makes braces cost more or less?

Braces cost more when treatment is complex or long, when the orthodontist is in a high-cost area, or when extra appliances are needed. The main price factors are:

  • Severity of the bite problem. Crowding, overbite, underbite and crossbite cases vary widely in difficulty.
  • Treatment length. More months mean more adjustment visits.
  • Type of braces. Lingual braces cost the most on average.
  • Extra appliances. Expanders, elastics and power chain braces may be part of the plan, usually included in the total fee.
  • Location and provider. Fees differ by region and between orthodontists and general dentists who offer orthodontics.
  • Retainers and repairs. Replacement retainers and broken brackets may cost extra.

Cosmetic choices such as braces colors for the elastic ties are typically included and changed at each adjustment.

What is included in the price of braces?

The price of braces usually includes the brackets and wires, regular adjustment visits and removal at the end of treatment, but records, retainers and repairs vary from office to office. Knowing what is in the fee makes quotes comparable.

A typical braces quote may or may not cover these items, so ask about each one:

  • Consultation and records. Initial exam, X-rays, photos and scans or impressions.
  • Appliances. Brackets, wires, elastics and any expander or other appliance in the treatment plan.
  • Adjustment visits. Whether every visit is included, or whether extra visits are charged if treatment runs longer than planned.
  • Emergency repairs. Loose brackets and broken wires, and whether repeated breakages carry a fee.
  • Retainers. The first set of retainers after braces come off, follow-up retention visits and the cost of replacements.

A braces quote that leaves out retainers can look cheaper than one that includes them. Retainers matter, because teeth tend to drift back without them; follow your orthodontist's instructions on how long to wear them.

How can I pay less for braces without insurance?

The best ways to pay less for braces without insurance are using pre-tax HSA or FSA funds, taking an in-house payment plan, comparing quotes, and checking university orthodontic clinics. These steps can make the same treatment more affordable.

  1. Use HSA or FSA money. Orthodontic treatment for health reasons is generally an eligible expense, so you pay with pre-tax dollars.
  2. Ask for in-house payment plans. Many orthodontists spread the fee over the treatment, sometimes without interest. Compare carefully with third-party financing.
  3. Ask about a pay-in-full discount. Some offices reduce the fee for paying up front.
  4. Try a university clinic. Orthodontic residency programs often charge less, with residents supervised by faculty.
  5. Get two or three quotes. Compare what each fee includes, not only the total.
  6. Check early. The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first orthodontic evaluation no later than age 7, according to Delta Dental. Early checks can catch problems that are simpler to treat while a child is growing.

Be cautious with mail-order or unsupervised aligner treatment chosen only on price. Ask your dentist or orthodontist whether in-person supervision is needed for your bite. Healthline also reviews average braces costs and payment options.

Frequently asked questions

How much do braces cost without insurance?

Braces cost $3,680 to $9,650 without insurance for comprehensive treatment, with an average of $5,645, according to Pearl's ADA-based data. CareCredit reports an average of $6,343 for metal braces and $9,221 for lingual braces. These are national figures, and prices in your area may be higher or lower.

Does dental insurance cover braces?

Dental insurance covers braces only if the plan includes orthodontic benefits. Plans that do usually pay a share of the fee up to a lifetime orthodontic maximum, often only for children and teens, sometimes after a waiting period. Check your plan documents or request a pre-treatment estimate.

Does medical insurance cover braces?

Medical insurance rarely covers braces. Exceptions usually involve medical conditions such as cleft lip and palate or orthodontics linked to jaw surgery. Medicaid and CHIP may cover braces for children with severe, medically necessary bite problems, but rules and approval criteria vary by state.

How much do braces cost monthly with insurance?

Monthly braces payments with insurance depend on the total fee, your down payment, your plan's orthodontic benefit and how many months the orthodontist spreads the balance over. Subtract the insurance benefit from the total fee, then divide the remainder by the payment term your orthodontist offers.

Are clear aligners cheaper than braces without insurance?

Clear aligners are slightly cheaper than metal braces on average. CareCredit reports an average of $5,108 for clear aligners and $6,343 for metal braces. Complex cases may cost more with either option, and not every bite problem is suited to aligners, so ask your orthodontist.

At what age should a child see an orthodontist?

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a child's first orthodontic evaluation no later than age 7, according to Delta Dental. Most children do not start braces that young, but an early check helps the orthodontist spot problems and plan the best timing for treatment.
