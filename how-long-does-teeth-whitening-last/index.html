How Long Does Teeth Whitening Last, and How Can You Make It Last Longer?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 5, 20262 price sources
Quick answer

Teeth whitening usually lasts from a few months to about three years, depending on the method and your habits. Professional in-office and custom-tray whitening tend to last longest, while whitening strips and toothpaste results fade sooner. Coffee, tea, red wine and tobacco shorten results; touch-ups help maintain them (Cleveland Clinic).

Key takeaways
  • Whitening results commonly last from a few months to about three years.
  • Professional whitening tends to last longer than over-the-counter products.
  • Staining foods, drinks and tobacco are the biggest factors in how quickly results fade.
  • Periodic touch-ups with trays or strips can maintain your shade.
  • Crowns, veneers and fillings do not whiten and may need matching later.

How long does teeth whitening last?

Teeth whitening generally lasts from a few months to about three years. The wide range reflects differences in the whitening method, the strength of the product, your natural tooth colour and, most of all, your daily habits.

Whitening works by using peroxide to break down stain molecules inside the enamel and dentin. Once teeth are lightened, they gradually pick up new stains from food, drinks and tobacco. Teeth also naturally darken a little with age. The goal is not permanent whiteness but a lighter shade that you maintain over time.

How long does each whitening method last?

How long whitening lasts depends heavily on the method used.

  • In-office professional whitening: often lasts about one to three years with good habits and occasional touch-ups.
  • Dentist-supervised custom trays: similar longevity, and the trays can be reused for touch-ups.
  • Whitening strips: results commonly last several months.
  • Whitening toothpaste: removes surface stains and helps maintain results, but the effect is modest and ongoing.

What makes whitening fade faster?

Whitening fades faster when teeth are frequently exposed to staining substances. The most common culprits are coffee, tea, red wine, cola and dark juices, along with curry, soy sauce, tomato sauce and berries.

Tobacco in any form causes stubborn yellow and brown stains. Acidic foods and drinks can soften enamel and make it more prone to staining. Poor oral hygiene allows plaque and tartar to build up, which also dulls the shade. The first 48 hours after whitening are when teeth pick up stains most easily.

How can you make teeth whitening last longer?

You can make teeth whitening last longer with a few practical habits.

  1. Avoid or limit staining foods and drinks, especially in the first 48 hours.
  2. Rinse with water after coffee, tea or wine.
  3. Drink cold dark drinks through a straw to reduce contact with front teeth.
  4. Stop smoking or using tobacco.
  5. Brush twice daily and clean between teeth daily.
  6. Use a whitening or stain-removing toothpaste.
  7. Keep regular professional cleanings.
  8. Do periodic touch-ups as your dentist recommends.

How often should you touch up whitening?

Many people touch up whitening every six months to a year, depending on how quickly their teeth restain. Custom trays from a dentist make touch-ups easy, because you can apply gel for a few days whenever your shade starts to dull.

Avoid overdoing touch-ups. Whitening too often can increase sensitivity and may make teeth look unnaturally bright. Ask your dentist how often is safe for you.

Does whitening last on crowns, veneers and fillings?

Whitening does not change the colour of crowns, veneers, bonding or fillings, because these materials do not respond to peroxide. If you whiten natural teeth next to restorations, the restorations may look darker by comparison.

Plan ahead: many people whiten first, then have restorations matched to their new shade. If you already have visible restorations on front teeth, ask your dentist how whitening will affect your smile.

Does longer-lasting whitening mean more sensitivity?

Stronger or more frequent whitening can increase tooth sensitivity, so there is a balance between longevity and comfort. Sensitivity is usually temporary and fades after treatment.

People with sensitive teeth can still whiten successfully with lower-strength products, shorter sessions and desensitising toothpaste. Ask your dentist about gentler, lower-strength options if your teeth are sensitive, and see whether to brush your teeth after whitening strips for strip routines.

Is longer-lasting whitening worth the cost?

Longer-lasting professional whitening costs more upfront but may be worth it for people who want a noticeable, durable result. Delta Dental lists in-office bleaching at $40 to $700 per visit out-of-network, according to its whitening cost guide. CareCredit reports averages of $792 for laser whitening and $583 for UV whitening.

Delta Dental notes whitening is usually not covered by insurance because it is cosmetic. See our teeth whitening cost guide for a full comparison of options.

What if whitening does not last for you?

If whitening does not last for you, or does not change your teeth enough, other cosmetic options may work better. Deep intrinsic stains, such as those from certain medications or tooth injury, often respond poorly to whitening.

Bonding and veneers can cover discoloured teeth with a stable colour that does not fade the same way. The Cleveland Clinic's cosmetic dentistry overview describes these alternatives.

Why do teeth become stained again after whitening?

Teeth become stained again after whitening because enamel is slightly porous and keeps absorbing coloured compounds from food, drinks and tobacco. Whitening breaks down existing stain molecules, but it does not seal the enamel against new ones.

Ageing also plays a part. Over the years, enamel gradually thins and the yellower dentin underneath shows through more, so teeth naturally darken a little even with perfect habits. Some medicines and medical conditions cause intrinsic discoloration from inside the tooth, which whitening may only partly improve.

That is why maintenance matters as much as the initial whitening. Small habits repeated daily, such as rinsing after coffee and brushing with a stain-removing toothpaste, often do more to keep teeth bright than repeating strong whitening treatments.

Should you see a dentist before whitening again?

Seeing a dentist before whitening again is a good idea if your teeth have become sensitive, you have new fillings or crowns, or your results faded unusually quickly. A professional cleaning alone often removes much of the surface staining and may brighten teeth enough without more bleaching.

Your dentist can also check for cavities, cracks or gum recession that could make whitening uncomfortable, and suggest a safe touch-up schedule.

Frequently asked questions

How long does teeth whitening last?

Teeth whitening usually lasts from a few months to about three years. Professional in-office and custom-tray whitening tend to last longest, while strips and toothpaste results fade sooner. Staining foods, drinks and tobacco shorten results.

How long does professional teeth whitening last?

Professional teeth whitening often lasts about one to three years with good habits and occasional touch-ups. Limiting coffee, tea, red wine and tobacco and keeping up with cleanings helps results last.

How can I make my teeth whitening last longer?

Make whitening last longer by limiting staining foods and drinks, rinsing with water after coffee or wine, avoiding tobacco, brushing twice daily, using a stain-removing toothpaste, keeping regular cleanings and doing occasional touch-ups.

What should I avoid after teeth whitening?

After teeth whitening, avoid coffee, tea, red wine, cola, dark sauces, berries and tobacco, especially in the first 48 hours when teeth stain most easily. Very hot, cold and acidic foods may also cause sensitivity.

Does whitening work on crowns and veneers?

Whitening does not change the colour of crowns, veneers, bonding or fillings. If you whiten natural teeth next to restorations, the restorations may look darker. Many people whiten first, then match new restorations to the new shade.
