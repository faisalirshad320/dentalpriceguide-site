How Much Do Veneers Cost With Insurance, and Does Insurance Cover Them?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 29, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

Most dental insurance plans do not cover veneers, because they are usually treated as cosmetic, according to Delta Dental. That means veneers usually cost the same with or without insurance: $1,180 to $2,185 per porcelain veneer. Partial coverage is sometimes possible when a veneer restores a damaged tooth.

Key takeaways
  • Veneers are usually considered cosmetic and are often not covered by insurance.
  • A porcelain laminate veneer costs $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth (Delta Dental).
  • CareCredit reports a $1,765 average per veneer and $15,486 for a set of six to eight.
  • Coverage may apply when a veneer repairs a chipped, broken or damaged tooth.
  • Bonding, at a $431 average per tooth (CareCredit), is a lower-cost alternative.

Does dental insurance cover veneers?

Dental insurance usually does not cover veneers. Delta Dental notes that veneers are generally treated as a cosmetic procedure, and most dental plans exclude treatment whose main purpose is improving appearance.

Insurance plans focus on procedures that prevent disease or restore function: cleanings, fillings, root canals, crowns and replacements for missing teeth. Whitening, veneers and other purely cosmetic work fall outside that scope on most policies. Read the exclusions section of your benefits summary to confirm how your plan treats veneers.

When might insurance cover veneers?

Insurance might cover part of a veneer when it is used to restore a tooth that is damaged rather than to change its appearance. Examples include a front tooth broken in an accident, a tooth with a large chip, or enamel lost to a medical condition.

In these cases, some plans pay what they would have paid for the least costly adequate treatment, such as a filling or bonding, and you pay the difference for the veneer. This is called an alternate benefit. A pre-treatment estimate with X-rays and photos showing the damage gives the best chance of a clear answer. Accident-related damage may sometimes be covered by medical insurance instead.

How much do veneers cost per tooth?

Porcelain veneers cost $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth without insurance, according to Delta Dental. CareCredit reports a national average of $1,765 for a single porcelain veneer.

The price depends on the material, the lab, the dentist's experience and your city. Composite veneers made directly in the mouth usually cost less than porcelain but do not last as long. See our veneers cost guide for a full breakdown.

How much do veneers cost for a full mouth without insurance?

A full set of veneers costs much more than a single tooth. CareCredit reports an average of $15,486 for a full set of six to eight porcelain veneers, which is the most common "smile makeover" covering the front teeth visible when you smile.

Some people need more veneers to cover a wider smile, which raises the total. Very few people need veneers on every tooth, because back teeth are rarely visible. Ask your dentist which teeth show in your smile and how many veneers are truly needed for the result you want.

Why do insurers treat veneers as cosmetic?

Insurers treat veneers as cosmetic because the most common reasons for getting them are colour, shape and minor alignment, which are appearance concerns rather than health problems. A tooth can be fully healthy and functional without a veneer.

That classification can feel unfair when a stained or chipped tooth affects confidence, but it is consistent across most plans. When a tooth is weakened or broken, a crown is often the covered treatment, while a veneer is considered an elective upgrade. Your dentist can explain whether a crown or a veneer suits a particular tooth, and which of the two your plan would help pay for.

What does a typical veneer smile makeover involve?

A typical veneer smile makeover involves several visits over a few weeks. It starts with a consultation where the dentist examines your teeth and gums, takes X-rays, photos and scans, and discusses the shape and shade you want.

Many dentists create a digital preview or a wax model, and some place a temporary "trial smile" so you can see the proposed shape before committing. At the preparation visit, a thin layer of enamel is removed from each tooth and impressions or scans go to the dental lab. Temporary veneers protect the teeth for a couple of weeks while the porcelain veneers are made.

At the final visit, each veneer is checked, adjusted and bonded in place. A follow-up appointment confirms your bite and gums are healthy. Because each visit and step is part of the overall fee, ask for a single written price covering the whole process, including the trial smile and temporaries, so there are no surprises later.

How can you pay less for veneers without coverage?

You can pay less for veneers without insurance coverage by comparing providers and considering alternatives. Several routes are commonly available.

  • Fewer veneers: treat only the teeth that show in your smile.
  • Composite instead of porcelain: lower cost, shorter lifespan.
  • Dental bonding: CareCredit reports a $431 average per tooth.
  • Whitening first: sometimes whitening alone achieves the goal.
  • Orthodontics: for crooked teeth, straightening may be better than covering.
  • Payment plans, financing and HSA or FSA funds: HSA and FSA rules usually exclude purely cosmetic treatment, so check eligibility.
  • Dental schools: supervised clinics may offer reduced fees.

Can insurance help with treatment before veneers?

Insurance can often help with the treatment needed before veneers, even when the veneers themselves are not covered. Exams, X-rays, cleanings, fillings for decay and gum treatment are usually covered, and these must be done before veneers are placed.

Healthy teeth and gums are essential for veneers to last. Using your insurance for this groundwork can reduce your overall cost. Delta Dental's general coverage guidance explains common percentages for preventive, basic and major care.

Should you budget for replacing veneers later?

You should budget for replacing veneers later, because even well-made porcelain veneers eventually need replacement after many years. Replacement costs are typically similar to the original cost per tooth and are also usually not covered by insurance.

Protecting veneers with a night guard, good hygiene and regular check-ups extends their life. Read whether veneers are permanent for lifespan details and what veneers are for types and materials. Healthline's veneers guide and the Cleveland Clinic's cosmetic dentistry overview offer more background.

What should you ask before paying for veneers?

Before paying for veneers, ask questions that make the total clear and protect your investment. Useful questions include how many veneers you need, what material will be used, whether temporaries are included, what happens if a veneer chips or comes off in the first year, and whether follow-up adjustments cost extra.

Ask to see photos of similar cases the dentist has treated, and browse veneers before and after examples to set realistic expectations. A written, itemised quote lets you compare offers fairly.

Frequently asked questions

Does insurance cover veneers?

Insurance usually does not cover veneers because they are treated as a cosmetic procedure, according to Delta Dental. Some plans may pay a partial alternate benefit when a veneer restores a broken or damaged tooth. Check your plan's exclusions and request a pre-treatment estimate.

How much do veneers cost with insurance?

Veneers usually cost the same with or without insurance because most plans exclude them. Delta Dental lists $1,180 to $2,185 per porcelain veneer. If your plan pays an alternate benefit for a damaged tooth, your share may be lower.

How much is a full set of veneers without insurance?

A full set of six to eight porcelain veneers averages $15,486 according to CareCredit. The total depends on how many teeth show in your smile, the material and your dentist. Composite veneers usually cost less but do not last as long.

Are there dental insurance plans that cover veneers?

Very few standard dental insurance plans cover veneers for cosmetic reasons. Some may contribute when a veneer is needed to repair damage. Discount dental plans are not insurance but may offer reduced veneer fees at participating dentists.

What is a cheaper alternative to veneers?

Dental bonding is a common cheaper alternative to veneers, averaging $431 per tooth according to CareCredit. Whitening can fix discoloration, and orthodontics can correct crooked teeth. Your dentist can suggest the option that fits your goals and budget.
