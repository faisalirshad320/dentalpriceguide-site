Ceramic Braces: How They Work, Pros and Cons, and What They Cost

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 21, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

Ceramic braces are braces with clear or tooth-colored ceramic brackets that work the same way as metal braces but are much less noticeable. They are bonded to the teeth and connected by a wire. CareCredit reports an average cost of $5,834 for ceramic braces; local prices vary.

Key takeaways
  • Ceramic braces move teeth the same way metal braces do, using brackets, wires and elastics, but the brackets blend in with the teeth.
  • Ceramic brackets are more brittle and somewhat bulkier than metal, and the elastic ties can stain.
  • CareCredit reports average costs of $5,834 for ceramic braces and $6,343 for metal braces.
  • Ceramic braces suit many teens and adults who want less visible treatment; your orthodontist decides whether they fit your bite.
  • Follow your orthodontist's instructions on food, cleaning and wearing elastics to keep treatment on schedule.

What are ceramic braces?

Ceramic braces are fixed orthodontic braces that use brackets made of clear or tooth-colored ceramic instead of stainless steel. The ceramic brackets are glued to the front of each tooth and joined by an archwire, exactly like traditional braces, so the way teeth move is the same.

Ceramic braces are popular with adults and older teens because they are much harder to spot from a normal speaking distance. Many orthodontists pair the ceramic brackets with clear or white elastic ties, and some offer tooth-colored or frosted archwires to make the whole appliance blend in further. People sometimes call them clear braces; our guide to clear braces covers the different tooth-colored options.

Ceramic braces can treat most of the same problems as metal braces, including crowding, gaps, overbite, underbite and crossbite. The orthodontist adjusts the wire at regular visits, and elastics or other appliances may be added as treatment progresses.

Are ceramic braces better than metal braces?

Ceramic braces are better than metal braces for appearance, while metal braces are stronger, smaller and less prone to staining. Neither is better for every patient; the right choice depends on your bite, your habits and how much you care about how the braces look.

Advantages of ceramic braces:

  • Less visible. Tooth-colored brackets blend in, especially on lighter teeth.
  • Same tooth movement. Ceramic braces use the same brackets-and-wire system as metal braces, so they can handle complex cases that aligners may not.
  • Fixed in place. Like metal braces, ceramic brackets cannot be left out, so treatment does not depend on remembering to wear an aligner.

Drawbacks of ceramic braces:

  • More brittle. Ceramic brackets can chip or crack under heavy force, which may mean extra repair visits.
  • Slightly larger. Ceramic brackets are often a little bulkier than metal ones, which some people notice against their lips at first.
  • Staining of ties. The brackets themselves resist stains, but the elastic ties can discolor between visits.
  • Wear on opposing teeth. Ceramic is harder than enamel, so orthodontists sometimes avoid ceramic brackets on lower teeth where the upper teeth bite against them.

Many orthodontists use a mix, placing ceramic brackets on the visible upper teeth and metal brackets on the lower teeth. Ask whether that option makes sense for you.

Who are ceramic braces good for?

Ceramic braces are a good fit for teens and adults who want fixed braces that are less noticeable and who can be careful with what they bite. They are often chosen by people in client-facing jobs, students who feel self-conscious about metal, and adults who need more tooth movement than aligners can provide.

Ceramic braces may be less suitable for people who grind or clench heavily, people with a deep bite where the lower teeth would hit the brackets, and very young children who may find it harder to avoid hard foods. Contact sports call for a mouthguard with any type of braces.

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a child's first orthodontic evaluation no later than age 7, according to Delta Dental. That visit is a good time to ask which bracket materials suit your child's bite as treatment approaches.

How do you keep ceramic braces from staining?

Keeping ceramic braces from staining mostly means protecting the elastic ties and keeping the brackets clean. The ceramic brackets themselves resist staining well, but the elastic ties that hold the wire can pick up color from food and drinks until they are replaced at your next adjustment.

  • Limit strong colors. Coffee, tea, red wine, cola, curry, tomato sauce, berries and mustard are common culprits. Rinse with water after them if you cannot brush.
  • Avoid tobacco. Smoking stains ties and teeth and harms gum health.
  • Brush after meals. Food trapped around brackets can stain ties and cause white spots on enamel.
  • Clean between teeth daily. Floss threaders, water flossers or small interdental brushes reach under the wire.
  • Choose your tie color. Clear ties stain more easily; pearl, white or tooth-colored ties often hide discoloration better. See our guide to braces colors.

Ceramic braces need the same food rules as metal braces: avoid hard foods such as ice, nuts and hard candy, and sticky foods such as caramel. Follow your orthodontist's instructions, which take priority over general advice.

Call your orthodontist if a bracket breaks or loosens or a wire pokes your cheek. Seek prompt care for a mouth injury, swelling, or signs of infection such as fever or pus.

What happens when ceramic braces are put on and taken off?

Ceramic braces are put on in a single appointment by bonding each bracket to a cleaned, prepared tooth, and they are taken off at the end of treatment with special care because ceramic is more brittle than metal. Neither step normally needs anesthetic.

Ceramic braces placement starts with a cleaning and drying of the teeth. The orthodontist applies a mild etching solution and bonding material, positions each bracket, hardens the adhesive with a curing light and then threads the archwire through the brackets. Mild soreness for several days is common as the teeth begin to move; soft foods and over-the-counter pain relievers usually help, if your orthodontist or doctor agrees they are safe for you.

Ceramic braces removal uses instruments designed to release the bracket from the adhesive without stressing the enamel, and the remaining glue is polished away. Orthodontists take extra care with ceramic brackets because they bond firmly and can fracture during removal.

After ceramic braces come off, you will wear a retainer to hold the teeth in their new positions. Follow your orthodontist's instructions on how many hours a day and for how long, because teeth tend to drift back without a retainer.

How much do ceramic braces cost?

Ceramic braces cost an average of $5,834, according to CareCredit's 2023–2024 cost study, compared with an average of $6,343 for metal braces and $5,108 for clear aligners in the same study. These are national averages, and local prices vary.

A broader reference comes from Pearl dental cost data, which puts comprehensive braces at $3,680 to $9,650 nationally, with an average of $5,645, based on ADA fee surveys. Many orthodontists charge somewhat more for ceramic brackets than for metal at the same office, so compare quotes within one practice as well as across practices. Healthline also reviews average costs for each type.

Dental insurance pays toward ceramic braces only if your plan includes orthodontic benefits, which usually come with a lifetime maximum; Delta Dental explains what standard dental plans cover. For a full breakdown, read how much are braces and our guide to how much braces are without insurance.

Frequently asked questions

Do ceramic braces work as fast as metal braces?

Ceramic braces move teeth with the same brackets-and-wire system as metal braces, so treatment time is often similar. Broken brackets or the need for gentler adjustments with brittle ceramic can sometimes add time. Your orthodontist can estimate how long your own treatment should take based on your bite.

Do ceramic braces stain?

Ceramic brackets resist staining, but the elastic ties around them can discolor from coffee, tea, red wine, curry and tobacco. The ties are replaced at each adjustment visit. Brushing after meals, rinsing after strongly colored foods and choosing pearl or white ties all help keep ceramic braces looking clear.

How much do ceramic braces cost?

Ceramic braces cost an average of $5,834, according to CareCredit's 2023–2024 cost study, compared with $6,343 for metal braces in the same study. Pearl's ADA-based data puts comprehensive braces at $3,680 to $9,650 nationally. These are national figures, and your orthodontist's fee may differ.

Are ceramic braces more painful than metal braces?

Ceramic braces are not usually more painful than metal braces, because both apply similar forces through the archwire. Soreness for a few days after placement and adjustments is normal with either type. Ceramic brackets can feel slightly bulkier at first, and orthodontic wax helps protect the lips and cheeks.

Can adults get ceramic braces?

Adults can get ceramic braces, and they are a popular choice for adults who need fixed braces but want a less noticeable look. Healthy gums and teeth matter more than age. Your orthodontist will check your gum health, bite and any crowns or missing teeth before recommending ceramic brackets.
