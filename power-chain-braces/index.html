Power Chain Braces: What Power Chains Do, How They Feel and What They Cost

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 21, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

Power chain braces use a stretchy chain of connected elastic rings, linked across several brackets, to pull teeth together and close gaps. Orthodontists usually add power chains partway through treatment and replace them at each adjustment. Power chains are normally part of the overall braces fee, which CareCredit puts at $6,343 on average for metal braces.

Key takeaways
  • A power chain is a row of linked elastic O-rings that connects several brackets at once.
  • Power chains close gaps, consolidate spaces after extractions and help rotate teeth.
  • Power chains are usually changed at each adjustment visit and can stay on for months.
  • Soreness for a few days after a new chain is common and usually eases on its own.
  • Power chains are typically included in the comprehensive braces fee rather than billed separately.

What are power chains on braces?

Power chains on braces are continuous strips of linked elastic rings that stretch across several brackets to apply steady pulling force between teeth. Each ring in a power chain fits around a bracket in the same way a single colored elastic tie does, but the rings are joined, so the whole chain acts like one long rubber band.

Regular braces use one small elastic tie (a ligature) on each bracket to hold the archwire in place. A power chain does that job and adds a second one: because the rings are connected and slightly stretched when placed, the chain constantly tries to shrink back, drawing the teeth it connects toward each other.

Power chains are a routine part of orthodontic treatment with traditional metal or ceramic braces. Many patients get them at some stage, and they are not a sign that treatment is going badly. Your orthodontist decides when to add them, which teeth to include and how long to keep them on.

What do power chains do?

Power chains close spaces between teeth and help move groups of teeth together along the archwire. Orthodontists use power chain braces for several specific jobs.

  • Closing gaps: Natural spaces between teeth, such as a gap between the front teeth, can be pulled closed.
  • Closing extraction spaces: When teeth are removed to make room, power chains help pull neighboring teeth into the space.
  • Consolidating spaces: Small gaps that open up during treatment as teeth straighten can be tightened up.
  • Helping with rotations: A chain attached in a particular pattern can help turn a rotated tooth into line.
  • Holding spaces closed: Toward the end of treatment, a chain can keep closed spaces from reopening while teeth settle.

Power chains are usually added after the first stage of treatment, once the teeth are roughly aligned and a sturdier archwire is in place. The exact timing depends on your bite and treatment plan.

What are the different types of power chains?

The main types of power chains are closed (continuous), short and long chains, which differ in how far apart the rings are spaced. Orthodontists pick the type that matches the distance between your brackets and how much force is needed.

  • Closed or continuous chain: No space between the rings, so a ring fits on every bracket. Closed chains are common for closing small gaps across several teeth.
  • Short chain: A small gap between each ring, so the chain may skip a bracket.
  • Long chain: Longer gaps between rings, used when brackets are farther apart or when the orthodontist wants to connect specific teeth.

Power chains come in many colors as well as clear, gray and tooth-colored shades. Clear chains can stain from coffee, tea, curry and similar foods over the weeks between visits, which is one reason some people prefer a darker or colored option. Do power chains hurt, and how long do you wear them?

Power chains often cause soreness or pressure for a few days after they are placed or replaced, and many people wear them for several months during the gap-closing stage of treatment. The discomfort is usually similar to the tightness after a regular braces adjustment.

The elastic material loses some of its pull over time, so orthodontists typically replace power chains at each adjustment visit, which is often every four to eight weeks. How long you wear power chains overall depends on how much space needs to close and how your teeth respond.

  • Soft foods such as pasta, soup, yogurt and scrambled eggs are easier for the first few days.
  • An over-the-counter pain reliever may help if it is safe for you; follow the label or your orthodontist's advice.
  • Orthodontic wax can cover any spot that rubs the inside of your lip or cheek.

Follow your orthodontist's instructions, which take priority over general advice. Call the office if a chain breaks, comes off a bracket or a wire pokes your cheek. Seek urgent care for facial swelling, fever, bleeding that does not stop with pressure or severe pain.

How do you take care of power chains?

Power chains need careful daily cleaning because food and plaque collect easily between the linked rings and around the brackets. Good hygiene protects the teeth from white spots and cavities while the chain does its work.

  • Brush after meals, angling the bristles above and below the brackets.
  • Use an interdental brush or floss threader to clean under the chain and wire.
  • A water flosser can help flush out trapped food, though it does not replace brushing.
  • Avoid hard, sticky and chewy foods such as hard candy, nuts, caramel and gum, which can snap a chain or loosen a bracket.
  • Do not pick at or pull on the chain, even if it feels loose.

A broken power chain is not usually an emergency, but the space it was closing can start to reopen. Call your orthodontist's office for advice on whether to come in early or wait for the next scheduled visit.

Do power chains cost extra on braces?

Power chains usually do not cost extra, because most orthodontists include them in the comprehensive braces fee along with wires, ties and adjustment visits. Ask your orthodontist whether any items, such as replacing lost appliances or extra visits, are billed separately.

The braces fee itself is the number to budget for. CareCredit reports national averages of $6,343 for metal braces, $5,834 for ceramic braces, $9,221 for lingual braces and $5,108 for clear aligners. Pearl dental cost data, based on ADA fee survey data, gives a range of $3,680 to $9,650 (average $5,645) for comprehensive braces, and Delta Dental puts traditional metal braces at $3,000 to $7,000 and lingual braces at $8,000 to $10,000. These sources measure different things, cost surveys versus a consumer guide, and local prices vary.

Frequently asked questions

What is the purpose of power chains on braces?

Power chains on braces pull teeth together to close gaps, including natural spaces and spaces left after extractions. Power chains can also help rotate teeth and keep closed spaces from reopening. Your orthodontist decides when to add them and which teeth to connect based on your treatment plan.

How long do you wear power chains?

Many people wear power chains for several months during the space-closing stage of treatment. The chain itself is usually replaced at each adjustment visit, often every four to eight weeks, because the elastic loses some pull. Total time depends on how much space needs closing and how your teeth respond.

Do power chains mean braces are almost done?

Power chains do not always mean braces are almost done. They are often added in the middle stage of treatment to close spaces, after the teeth have been roughly aligned. Some patients also wear them near the end to hold spaces closed. Ask your orthodontist where you are in your plan.

What happens if a power chain breaks?

If a power chain breaks, the space it was closing may start to open again, so call your orthodontist's office for advice. A broken chain is rarely an emergency. If a loose wire or chain is poking your cheek, cover it with orthodontic wax until you can be seen.

Can you choose the color of power chains?

Yes, power chains usually come in many colors, plus clear, gray and tooth-colored shades, and most orthodontists let you choose. Clear and very light chains can stain from coffee, tea and strongly colored foods between visits, so some people pick a darker or bold color instead.
