Gum Graft Before and After: What Changes, How It Heals and What It Costs

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 2, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

Gum graft before and after results usually show exposed tooth roots covered by new gum tissue, a more even gumline and less sensitivity, once healing is complete over several weeks to a few months. Gum graft surgery costs about $2,742 on average, with a range of $2,120 to $4,982, according to CareCredit.

  • Gum grafts cover exposed roots caused by gum recession.

What does gum recession look like before a graft?

Before a gum graft, gum recession usually looks like teeth that appear longer than normal, with the gumline pulled back to expose part of the root. The exposed root is often slightly darker or more yellow than the enamel above it, and there may be a visible notch near the gumline.

People with recession often notice sensitivity to cold or brushing, food catching near the gum, or a gumline that looks uneven. The Cleveland Clinic's gum recession guide explains common causes, including aggressive brushing, gum disease, thin gum tissue, grinding and orthodontic tooth movement.

What is a gum graft?

A gum graft is a periodontal surgery that adds gum tissue where it has receded. The most common type, a connective tissue graft, takes tissue from under a small flap on the roof of the mouth and stitches it over the exposed root.

Other types include free gingival grafts, which take tissue directly from the palate to thicken thin gums, and pedicle grafts, which slide nearby gum tissue over the root. Some periodontists use donor tissue or collagen materials to avoid a palate donor site. Healthline's gum graft guide describes each type.

What does a gum graft look like after healing?

After healing, a gum graft usually covers part or all of the previously exposed root, so the tooth looks shorter and the gumline more even with its neighbours. The new tissue blends in with the surrounding gum over time.

In the first weeks, the graft can look white, puffy or slightly raised, which is normal as it establishes a blood supply. It gradually turns pink and settles. Full colour and contour can take a few months. Not every graft achieves complete root coverage; the result depends on bone levels between the teeth, tissue thickness and healing.

What is the gum graft healing timeline?

The gum graft healing timeline usually follows a predictable pattern, though everyone heals at a slightly different pace.

  • Days 1 to 3: swelling, tenderness and some bleeding from the palate if tissue was taken there.
  • Week 1: the graft may look white or grey; this is normal. Soft, cool foods only.
  • Weeks 1 to 2: stitches dissolve or are removed; discomfort eases.
  • Weeks 2 to 4: the graft becomes pinker and firmer.
  • Months 2 to 3 and beyond: tissue matures and the final result becomes clear.

The palate donor site often feels more sore than the graft itself for the first week.

What are the benefits of a gum graft?

The benefits of a gum graft go beyond appearance. Covering exposed roots reduces sensitivity, protects roots from decay and abrasion, and can stop recession from progressing further.

Thicker gum tissue is also more resistant to future recession, which is why grafts are sometimes recommended before orthodontic treatment or around teeth with thin gums. For people with recession on front teeth, the cosmetic improvement in smile line can be significant.

How much does a gum graft cost?

A gum graft costs about $2,742 on average, with a range of $2,120 to $4,982, according to CareCredit. Prices vary by state; CareCredit reports connective tissue graft averages from $2,341 in Oklahoma to $4,013 in Hawaii, including $2,536 in Texas, $2,726 in Florida, $3,021 in New York and $3,478 in California.

The cost depends on the number of teeth treated, the graft type, whether donor material is used and the periodontist's fee. Treating several adjacent teeth in one surgery can be more efficient than separate procedures. See our main gum graft cost guide and CareCredit's procedure cost list.

Does insurance cover gum grafts?

Dental insurance often covers gum grafts when they are needed to treat recession for health reasons, such as protecting exposed roots or stopping progressing recession. Coverage is usually under periodontal or major services, and cosmetic-only grafts may be excluded.

Ask your periodontist to submit a pre-treatment estimate with measurements and photos documenting the recession. Your out-of-pocket share depends on your deductible, coverage percentage and annual maximum.

How do you protect a gum graft after surgery?

Protecting a gum graft after surgery is essential for a good result. Follow your periodontist's instructions closely; they take priority over general advice.

  1. Do not brush or floss the graft area until told to.
  2. Avoid pulling your lip to look at the graft.
  3. Eat soft, cool foods and avoid chewing on the treated side.
  4. Avoid smoking, which can cause graft failure.
  5. Use any prescribed rinse as directed.
  6. Wear a palatal stent if one was provided.

Contact your periodontist for bleeding that will not stop with pressure, increasing pain or swelling after a few days, fever, or if the graft appears to be coming loose.

What if the after result is not what you expected?

If the after result of a gum graft is not what you expected, talk to your periodontist. Some partial coverage is common, and a second procedure can sometimes improve the result. True graft failure, where the tissue dies or detaches, is uncommon but can happen, especially in smokers.

See our guide to failed gum grafts for signs and options. Addressing the cause of recession, such as switching to a soft toothbrush and gentle technique or wearing a night guard for grinding, helps protect both the graft and your other teeth.

How can you prevent further gum recession after a graft?

Preventing further gum recession after a graft protects your investment and your other teeth. The most important change for many people is brushing technique: use a soft-bristled brush, light pressure and small circular motions rather than hard scrubbing. An electric toothbrush with a pressure sensor can help.

Wear a night guard if you grind or clench, keep up regular cleanings to control gum disease, and avoid tobacco. Lip or tongue piercings that rub the gums can also cause recession and may need removing. Regular check-ups with gum measurements let your dentist spot new recession early, when it is easier to manage.

Frequently asked questions

What does a gum graft look like after healing?

After healing, a gum graft usually covers part or all of the exposed root, making the tooth look shorter and the gumline more even. In the first weeks the graft may look white or puffy, which is normal, before turning pink and blending in over a few months.

How long does a gum graft take to heal?

A gum graft usually takes about two weeks for initial healing, when stitches dissolve and discomfort eases. The tissue continues to mature and blend in over a few months. The palate donor site often feels sore for the first week.

How much does a gum graft cost?

A gum graft costs about $2,742 on average, with a range of $2,120 to $4,982, according to CareCredit. State averages for connective tissue grafts range from $2,341 in Oklahoma to $4,013 in Hawaii.

Is a gum graft worth it?

A gum graft is often worth it to cover exposed roots, reduce sensitivity, protect roots from decay and stop recession progressing. Results vary, and not every graft gives complete coverage. Your periodontist can estimate the likely result for your teeth.

Does a gum graft hurt?

A gum graft is done under local anesthetic, so the procedure itself should not hurt. Afterward, soreness and swelling are common for several days, especially at the palate donor site. Pain relievers, soft foods and following aftercare instructions help.
