How Much Does a Deep Cleaning of Teeth Cost?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 27, 20264 price sources
Quick answer

A deep cleaning (scaling and root planing) costs about $180 to $295 per quadrant of the mouth without dental benefits, according to Delta Dental, and CareCredit reports an average of $242 per quadrant. Because the mouth has four quadrants, a full-mouth deep cleaning costs several times as much as a routine cleaning.

Key takeaways
  • Deep cleaning is priced per quadrant: $180 to $295 each (Delta Dental), $242 average (CareCredit).
  • You may need one, two, three or all four quadrants treated.
  • Exams and X-rays are often billed separately before a deep cleaning.
  • Many insurance plans cover deep cleaning as a basic periodontal service.
  • Ongoing periodontal maintenance cleanings ($140 to $220) usually follow.

What is a deep cleaning of teeth?

A deep cleaning, known as scaling and root planing, is a treatment for gum disease that removes plaque and hardened tartar from below the gumline, along the tooth roots. A routine cleaning focuses on the visible tooth surfaces and just under the gum edge; a deep cleaning goes further down into the pockets that form when gum disease pulls the gums away from the teeth.

Scaling removes the deposits, and root planing smooths the root surfaces so gums can reattach and bacteria have fewer rough places to cling. The area is usually numbed with local anesthetic, and the work is often split across two visits, one side of the mouth at a time.

Dentists recommend deep cleaning when X-rays and gum measurements show pockets deeper than healthy gums allow, often with bleeding and bone loss. Common warning signs at home include gums that bleed when you brush, persistent bad breath, gums that look red or puffy, and teeth that feel slightly loose.

How much does a deep cleaning cost per quadrant?

A deep cleaning costs $180 to $295 per quadrant without dental benefits, according to Delta Dental's cleaning cost guide. CareCredit's procedure cost list reports a national average of $242 per quadrant.

Dentists divide the mouth into four quadrants: upper right, upper left, lower right and lower left. Each quadrant treated is billed separately. Some codes also distinguish between quadrants with four or more affected teeth and those with only one to three, with the smaller version usually costing less.

The total depends on how many quadrants need treatment. Someone with gum disease in one area might need only one quadrant, while widespread gum disease requires all four. Ask for the number of quadrants and the fee for each in writing.

What is the full cost of a deep cleaning visit?

The full cost of deep cleaning usually includes more than the scaling and root planing itself. Before treatment, most offices perform a comprehensive exam and full-mouth X-rays to diagnose gum disease and measure bone levels.

CareCredit reports a national average of $203 for a routine exam and $226 for full-mouth X-rays. If heavy tartar prevents the dentist from examining the gums, a full mouth debridement ($150 to $260, per Delta Dental) may come first. Some offices also place antibiotic gel in deep pockets for an extra fee.

When comparing quotes, add up every line so you compare the same total. Our page on how much a dental cleaning is without insurance explains the routine cleaning fees.

Does insurance cover deep cleaning?

Many dental insurance plans cover deep cleaning, usually as a basic or periodontal service rather than as preventive care. On a typical 100-80-50 plan described by Delta Dental, basic services are often covered at 80% after the deductible, but your plan may differ.

Insurers often require evidence that deep cleaning is needed, such as gum pocket measurements and X-rays showing bone loss. Some plans limit how often each quadrant can be treated. Ask the office to send a pre-treatment estimate if you are unsure of your coverage.

What are the disadvantages of deep cleaning teeth?

Deep cleaning is generally safe and effective, but it has some downsides worth knowing before treatment.

  • Sensitivity: teeth can be sensitive to hot and cold for days to weeks as exposed root surfaces settle.
  • Soreness: gums may be tender and bleed a little for a few days.
  • Gum shrinkage: as inflammation goes down, gums can tighten and appear to recede, making teeth look longer.
  • Cost: treating several quadrants adds up quickly.
  • Infection risk: uncommon, but bacteria can enter the bloodstream; people with certain heart or joint conditions should tell their dentist.
  • Not a one-time fix: gum disease needs ongoing maintenance to stay controlled.

The disadvantages of skipping a needed deep cleaning are usually greater: untreated gum disease can lead to bone loss, loose teeth and tooth loss.

Is a deep cleaning really necessary?

A deep cleaning is necessary when you have gum disease with deep pockets that a routine cleaning cannot reach. Tartar below the gumline keeps feeding bacteria and inflammation, and only professional scaling can remove it.

If you are unsure, ask to see your pocket measurements and X-rays. Healthy gum pockets are shallow, and readings that are consistently deeper, especially with bleeding, support the need for treatment. A second opinion is reasonable if you are told you need a deep cleaning on all four quadrants without clear evidence.

What happens after a deep cleaning?

After a deep cleaning, gums usually heal over a few weeks and pocket depths often shrink. Your dentist will re-measure the gums at a follow-up visit to see how well they responded.

Most people then move to periodontal maintenance cleanings, which Delta Dental prices at $140 to $220 without benefits. These are typically scheduled more often than routine cleanings, often every three to four months, to keep gum disease from returning. Good home care, including cleaning between the teeth daily, makes a big difference.

How can you save on deep cleaning costs?

You can save on deep cleaning costs by asking about cash-pay discounts, in-house membership plans and dental school clinics, where supervised students provide care at lower fees. Spreading treatment across visits can also spread the cost.

Prevention saves the most. Regular routine cleanings ($85 to $160 at Delta Dental's figures) and daily brushing and flossing help prevent the gum disease that makes deep cleanings necessary. Frequently asked questions

How much is a deep cleaning without insurance?

A deep cleaning without insurance costs about $180 to $295 per quadrant according to Delta Dental, and CareCredit reports a $242 average per quadrant. The total depends on how many of the four quadrants need treatment, plus exam and X-ray fees.

Why is deep cleaning so expensive?

Deep cleaning costs more than a routine cleaning because it treats gum disease below the gumline, usually requires numbing, takes longer, and is billed per quadrant. Treating all four quadrants multiplies the fee. Exams and X-rays to diagnose gum disease add to the total.

Is deep cleaning painful?

Deep cleaning is usually not painful during treatment because the area is numbed with local anesthetic. Afterward, gums may feel sore and teeth may be sensitive for a few days to weeks. Over-the-counter pain relievers and sensitivity toothpaste usually help.

How often do you need a deep cleaning?

Most people need a deep cleaning only when gum disease is diagnosed, not on a regular schedule. Afterward, periodontal maintenance cleanings every few months help keep it under control. A repeat deep cleaning may be needed if gum disease returns in some areas.

Can I get just a regular cleaning instead of a deep cleaning?

A regular cleaning cannot remove tartar deep below the gumline in diseased pockets, so it will not treat gum disease. You can decline a deep cleaning, but untreated gum disease can progress to bone loss and loose teeth. Ask to see your gum measurements to understand the need.
