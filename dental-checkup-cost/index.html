How Much Is a Dental Check-Up? Exam, X-Ray and Cleaning Costs

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 6, 20264 price sources
Quick answer

A dental check-up usually includes an exam, often X-rays, and a cleaning, each billed separately. CareCredit reports a national average of $203 for a routine exam (range $50 to $350), $65 for bitewing X-rays and $226 for full-mouth X-rays (CareCredit). A standard cleaning without dental benefits adds $85 to $160, according to Delta Dental.

What is included in a dental check-up?

A dental check-up usually includes three main parts: an exam by the dentist, X-rays when due, and a professional cleaning by the hygienist. Each is billed under its own code, so the total depends on which parts you receive at that visit.

The exam checks your teeth for decay, your gums for disease, your bite, existing fillings and crowns, and the soft tissues of the mouth for signs of oral cancer. X-rays show decay between teeth and below the gumline that cannot be seen by eye. The cleaning removes plaque and tartar and polishes the teeth. Delta Dental's cleaning guide describes what happens during each step.

How much does a dental exam cost?

A routine dental exam costs about $203 on average, with a range of $50 to $350, according to CareCredit. The type of exam affects the fee. A comprehensive exam for a new patient is more detailed and usually costs more than a periodic exam for an existing patient.

A limited or emergency exam focused on one problem, such as a toothache, is another separate code. Many offices offer new-patient specials that bundle the exam, X-rays and cleaning at a reduced price.

How much do dental X-rays cost?

Dental X-rays cost about $65 on average for bitewing X-rays and $226 for a full-mouth series, according to CareCredit. Bitewings show the back teeth and are typically taken about once a year to check for decay between teeth.

A full-mouth series includes several images of every tooth and root and is usually taken every few years or at a new-patient visit. Panoramic X-rays and 3D scans are used for specific purposes such as wisdom teeth or implant planning and cost more. Your dentist decides how often you need X-rays based on your risk of decay and gum disease.

How much does the cleaning part cost?

The cleaning part of a check-up costs $85 to $160 for a standard cleaning without dental benefits, according to Delta Dental. People with gum disease may need periodontal maintenance cleanings instead, which Delta Dental lists at $140 to $220.

If heavy tartar prevents a proper exam, a full mouth debridement ($150 to $260, per Delta Dental) may be needed first. See dental cleaning cost, how much a dental cleaning is without insurance and deep cleaning teeth cost.

What is the total cost of a check-up without insurance?

The total cost of a check-up without insurance is the sum of the exam, any X-rays taken that day and the cleaning. A visit with an exam and cleaning only costs less than a new-patient visit that also includes a full set of X-rays.

Because each component varies by office and city, ask the front desk for the fee for each code before your appointment. Many practices quote a combined self-pay price, which makes budgeting easier.

Does insurance cover dental check-ups?

Insurance usually covers dental check-ups well. Most plans treat exams, routine X-rays and cleanings as preventive care, often covered at or near 100%, usually twice a year, as described in Delta Dental's coverage guide.

Plans often limit how often X-rays are covered, such as bitewings once a year and full-mouth series every several years. If you need more frequent X-rays or cleanings, you may pay part of the cost.

How often should you have a dental check-up?

Many adults have a dental check-up every six months, but the right interval depends on your oral health. People with healthy teeth and gums and low cavity risk may be fine with longer intervals, while people with gum disease, frequent cavities or certain health conditions may need more frequent visits.

Your dentist will recommend a schedule after assessing your risk. Regular check-ups catch problems early, when treatment is simpler and less expensive.

How can you save on dental check-ups without insurance?

You can save on dental check-ups without insurance through several options.

  • New-patient specials that bundle exam, X-rays and cleaning.
  • In-house membership plans covering two check-ups a year.
  • Dental hygiene and dental schools with lower fees.
  • Community health centers with sliding-scale fees.
  • Discount dental plans with reduced fees at participating dentists.

Skipping check-ups to save money can cost more later. Small cavities and early gum disease are cheaper to treat than root canals, extractions or deep cleanings.

What should you ask before your check-up?

Before your check-up, ask whether the visit will include X-rays and which type, whether the cleaning is routine or periodontal, and what the total self-pay price will be. If you have insurance, ask the office to confirm your preventive benefits and frequency limits.

Bring a list of medicines and mention any symptoms, such as sensitivity, bleeding gums or jaw pain, so the dentist can check them during the exam.

Why does a first visit cost more than a regular check-up?

A first visit to a new dentist usually costs more than a regular check-up because it includes a comprehensive exam and often a full set of X-rays. The dentist needs a complete baseline of your teeth, gums, bite and existing dental work before planning future care.

The comprehensive exam takes longer than a periodic exam, and a full-mouth X-ray series, which CareCredit averages at $226, is more extensive than the yearly bitewings at $65. If you have recent X-rays from a previous dentist, ask for them to be sent over; this can avoid repeating images and save money.

Subsequent routine check-ups usually involve a shorter periodic exam, bitewing X-rays when due, and a cleaning, so they generally cost less.

How much does a child's dental check-up cost?

A child's dental check-up includes similar parts to an adult visit, often with fluoride treatment and sealants added when appropriate. Many dental plans cover children's preventive visits fully, and Medicaid and the Children's Health Insurance Program cover dental check-ups for eligible children.
