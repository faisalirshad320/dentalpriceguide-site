How Much Is a Gold Tooth? Gold Crown Costs, Pros and Cons

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 7, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

A gold tooth is usually a gold or gold-alloy crown, and its cost falls within metal crown pricing: CareCredit reports a $1,211 average for a metallic crown (CareCredit), and permanent crowns run $1,100 to $2,000 out-of-network according to Delta Dental. The price of gold itself, the alloy and the dentist's fees all affect the final cost.

Key takeaways
  • A gold tooth is typically a full gold or gold-alloy crown.
  • Metallic crowns average $1,211 (CareCredit); permanent crowns run $1,100 to $2,000 (Delta Dental).
  • Gold crowns are very durable and gentle on opposing teeth.
  • The visible colour makes gold less popular for front teeth today.
  • Insurance often covers gold crowns at 50% when the tooth needs a crown.

How much does a gold tooth cost?

A gold tooth usually costs about the same as other metal crowns, with CareCredit reporting a $1,211 average for metallic crowns. Delta Dental lists permanent crowns at $1,100 to $2,000 out-of-network. A gold crown's price can move with the cost of gold and the alloy used.

Dental gold is rarely pure. It is usually an alloy mixed with metals such as palladium, silver or copper to make it stronger. Alloys with a higher gold content tend to cost more. Ask your dentist which alloy they use and how its price affects your quote. See types of dental crowns and cost for all materials.

Why do some people choose gold crowns?

People choose gold crowns mainly for durability and gentleness. Gold alloy crowns rarely chip or break, can be made thinner than ceramic crowns so less tooth is removed, and wear at a rate similar to natural enamel, so they are kind to the opposing teeth.

Gold crowns are often recommended for back molars in people who grind their teeth, or when there is little space between teeth. They also have a long track record of lasting many years. The Cleveland Clinic's crowns guide describes the different crown materials.

What about gold teeth for style?

Some people want a gold tooth for appearance or cultural reasons rather than a dental need. A dentist will generally only place a crown on a tooth when there is a clinical reason, because preparing a healthy tooth for a crown removes tooth structure permanently.

Removable gold covers, sometimes called grills, sit over the teeth without altering them. They are not a dental treatment. If you wear one, keep it clean, remove it to eat and sleep, and ask a dentist to check that it fits properly and is not irritating your gums or trapping plaque.

What are the pros and cons of gold crowns?

Gold crowns have clear advantages and some drawbacks.

  • Pros: extremely durable, rarely fracture, need less tooth removal, gentle on opposing teeth, long track record.
  • Cons: visible metal colour, cost can rise with gold prices, rare metal sensitivities, not ideal for front teeth for most people.

For back teeth that are not visible when smiling, many dentists consider gold one of the most reliable crown materials.

Is a gold crown better than porcelain?

A gold crown is not better than porcelain for every tooth, but it is often more durable. Porcelain and ceramic crowns look like natural teeth and are preferred for visible areas; CareCredit reports a $1,399 average for porcelain crowns.

Gold crowns are stronger and gentler on opposing teeth but stand out visually. For back molars, especially in people who grind, gold can be an excellent choice. For front teeth, most people choose porcelain. See front tooth crown cost.

Does insurance cover a gold crown?

Insurance usually covers a gold crown when the tooth needs a crown for clinical reasons, typically at about 50% as a major procedure on a 100-80-50 plan, according to Delta Dental's coverage guide.

Some plans pay the same benefit regardless of material, so gold may cost you a similar amount to other options. A gold tooth placed purely for appearance is unlikely to be covered. See tooth crown cost with insurance.

What happens when you get a gold crown?

Getting a gold crown usually takes two visits. At the first, the dentist numbs and shapes the tooth, takes an impression or scan and fits a temporary crown. A dental lab casts the gold crown to fit precisely.

At the second visit, the gold crown is checked for fit and bite and cemented in place. Because gold can be made thin and fits very accurately, gold crowns often seal well against the tooth.

How long does a gold tooth last?

A gold crown can last many years, often longer than other crown materials, with good oral hygiene and regular check-ups. Decay at the edge of the crown and gum disease are the main reasons a gold crown may need replacing, rather than the gold wearing out.

Brush twice daily, clean between teeth and keep regular dental visits to protect the tooth under the crown. Learn more about what a dental crown is.

Can you be allergic to a gold crown?

Allergies to gold crowns are rare, but some people react to metals in the alloy, such as nickel or palladium, rather than the gold itself. Signs can include irritation or redness of the gum next to the crown, a burning sensation, or a rash in the mouth.

Tell your dentist about any known metal allergies before treatment. High-gold-content alloys and alloys without nickel are generally well tolerated. If you develop symptoms after getting a crown, your dentist can assess whether the material is the cause and discuss replacing it with a ceramic crown if needed.

Which teeth are gold crowns best for?

Gold crowns are best suited to back teeth, especially second molars, where they are not visible when you smile and where their strength matters most. They are a common choice for people who grind or clench their teeth heavily, because gold resists fracture and is gentle on the opposing teeth.

Gold is also useful when there is little vertical space between the teeth, because a gold crown can be made thinner than a ceramic one while staying strong. For premolars and front teeth that show when smiling, most people choose porcelain or zirconia. Your dentist can explain which material is best for each tooth based on your bite and appearance goals.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a gold tooth?

A gold tooth is usually a gold or gold-alloy crown, priced within metal crown ranges. CareCredit reports a $1,211 average for metallic crowns, and Delta Dental lists permanent crowns at $1,100 to $2,000 out-of-network. Gold prices and alloy content affect the cost.

Are gold crowns better than porcelain?

Gold crowns are more durable and gentler on opposing teeth than porcelain, and need less tooth removal. Porcelain looks more natural. Gold is often preferred for back molars, while porcelain is preferred for visible front teeth.

Does insurance cover gold crowns?

Insurance usually covers gold crowns when the tooth clinically needs a crown, typically at about 50% as a major procedure. Gold teeth placed only for appearance are unlikely to be covered.

How long does a gold crown last?

Gold crowns often last many years, frequently longer than other crown materials. Decay or gum disease around the crown, rather than wear of the gold, is the usual reason for replacement.

Can I get a gold tooth just for looks?

Dentists usually only place crowns when there is a clinical need, because preparing a healthy tooth removes enamel permanently. Removable gold covers do not alter teeth but should fit well and be removed to eat and sleep.
