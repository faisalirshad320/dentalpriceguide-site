What Is a Root Canal Procedure? Steps, Recovery and Cost

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 26, 20264 price sources
Quick answer

A root canal is a treatment that removes infected or inflamed pulp (the nerve and blood vessels) from inside a tooth, cleans and disinfects the canals, then fills and seals them so the tooth can be kept. Delta Dental's out-of-network data puts it at about $1,200 for a front tooth and about $1,500 for a molar, before the crown (Delta Dental).

Key takeaways
  • A root canal saves a tooth whose inner pulp is infected or badly inflamed.
  • The procedure is done under local anesthetic and feels much like a large filling.
  • Back teeth usually need a crown afterward to protect them from cracking.
  • Delta Dental's out-of-network costs run from about $1,200 (front tooth) to about $1,500 (molar).
  • Those fees do not include the final crown or filling.

What is a root canal, in plain terms?

A root canal is a dental procedure that treats the inside of a tooth. Every tooth has a soft core called the pulp, which contains nerves and blood vessels and runs down through narrow channels in the roots. When deep decay, a crack or repeated dental work lets bacteria reach the pulp, it can become infected or inflamed.

A root canal removes that damaged pulp, cleans and shapes the empty canals, and seals them with a rubber-like filling material called gutta-percha. The tooth stays in your mouth and keeps working; it simply no longer has a living nerve inside.

A root canal is also called endodontic treatment. General dentists perform many root canals, and endodontists are specialists who handle complex cases such as curved roots or retreatment. Delta Dental's root canal guide describes the treatment in more detail.

Why would you need a root canal?

A root canal is needed when the pulp inside a tooth is infected or irreversibly inflamed and the tooth is still worth saving. Without treatment, the infection can spread to the bone around the root and form an abscess.

Common reasons include deep cavities, a cracked or chipped tooth, a tooth that has had several large fillings, and injury to a tooth even without a visible crack. Warning signs can include lingering pain to hot or cold, pain when biting, a darkening tooth, or a pimple-like bump on the gum. Some infected teeth cause no pain at all and are found on X-rays.

Only a dentist can decide whether you need a root canal, after an exam and X-rays. Our guide to root canal symptoms and how to avoid a root canal covers the warning signs.

Seek urgent dental or medical care for facial swelling, fever, swelling that spreads toward the eye or neck, or trouble swallowing or breathing. These can signal a spreading infection.

What happens during a root canal procedure?

A root canal procedure follows a predictable set of steps and is usually completed in one or two visits.

  1. Numbing: the dentist numbs the tooth and surrounding area with local anesthetic.
  2. Isolation: a thin rubber sheet (dental dam) keeps the tooth dry and clean.
  3. Access: a small opening is made in the top of the tooth to reach the pulp chamber.
  4. Cleaning and shaping: tiny files remove the pulp and shape each canal, while disinfecting rinses flush out bacteria.
  5. Filling: the canals are filled and sealed with gutta-percha.
  6. Temporary seal: a temporary filling closes the opening until the final restoration.
  7. Final restoration: a permanent filling or crown is placed, often at a later visit.

The treatment itself is usually no more uncomfortable than a large filling because the tooth is fully numb. See how long a root canal takes and whether a root canal hurts for more detail.

Is a root canal on a front tooth different from a molar?

A root canal on a front tooth is usually simpler than one on a molar. Front teeth typically have a single root and canal, while molars often have three or four canals that can be narrow or curved.

That difference shows up in price. Delta Dental's out-of-network data lists about $1,200 for a front tooth, about $1,300 for a premolar and about $1,500 for a molar. CareCredit reports averages of $984 for a front tooth and $1,337 for a molar. Front teeth also often need only a filling afterward, while back teeth usually need a crown to protect them from biting forces.

How much does a root canal cost?

A root canal usually costs somewhere around a thousand dollars or a little more for people paying full fees, depending on the tooth and the data source. Pearl's dental cost data gives a national range of $685 to $1,800 for root canal codes D3310 to D3330, with an average of $1,050. CareCredit's averages are $984 for a front tooth and $1,337 for a molar.

Delta Dental notes that these fees do not include the final crown or filling, which is a separate charge. Insurance commonly covers 50%–80% of a root canal after the deductible, according to Delta Dental. Our root canal cost guide breaks down prices with and without insurance.

What are the alternatives to a root canal?

The main alternative to a root canal is extracting the tooth. Extraction is quicker and usually costs less on the day, but it leaves a gap. Neighbouring teeth can drift into that space, the opposing tooth can over-erupt, and chewing on that side can become harder over time.

Replacing an extracted tooth with an implant, a bridge or a partial denture adds cost and more treatment time. That is why dentists generally recommend saving a natural tooth with a root canal when the tooth has enough healthy structure left to restore.

In a few situations a smaller procedure may work instead. When decay comes close to the pulp but the nerve is still healthy, a dentist may place a protective lining (pulp capping) and a filling. A tooth with a crack running below the gumline, very little remaining structure or advanced gum disease around it may not be a good candidate for a root canal at all. Your dentist will explain the options for your specific tooth, including the likely long-term outcome of each.

Antibiotics alone do not cure an infected tooth pulp. They can calm a spreading infection, but the source inside the tooth still needs a root canal or extraction.

What is recovery like after a root canal?

Recovery after a root canal is usually quick. Most people return to normal activities the same day, although the tooth and gum may feel tender for a few days.

Over-the-counter pain relievers taken as directed on the label usually manage the soreness. Avoid chewing hard food on the treated tooth until the permanent filling or crown is placed, because a tooth with only a temporary filling can crack. Your dentist may prescribe antibiotics only if infection has spread beyond the tooth.

Call your dentist if pain gets worse after several days, swelling appears, the temporary filling falls out, or your bite feels high. A well-treated and properly restored tooth can last many years.

Frequently asked questions

What is a root canal in simple words?

A root canal is a treatment that cleans out the infected or inflamed soft tissue inside a tooth, disinfects the inner channels, and seals them so you can keep the tooth instead of having it pulled. It is done under local anesthetic and usually followed by a filling or crown.

Is a root canal better than pulling the tooth?

A root canal is usually preferred when the tooth can be saved, because keeping a natural tooth protects your bite and avoids the cost of an implant or bridge to replace it. Extraction may be advised if the tooth is cracked below the gum or too damaged to restore.

How much does a root canal on a front tooth cost?

A root canal on a front tooth costs about $1,200 out-of-network according to Delta Dental, and CareCredit reports a $984 average. Front teeth are usually simpler to treat than molars because they typically have a single canal. Local prices vary.

Do you always need a crown after a root canal?

You do not always need a crown after a root canal. Back teeth such as molars and premolars usually need one because they take heavy chewing forces and can crack. Front teeth with enough remaining structure can often be restored with a filling instead.

How long does a root canal last?

A root canal treated tooth can last many years, often for life, when it is properly sealed and restored and you keep up good oral hygiene. A missing or leaking crown, new decay or a crack can cause it to fail and need retreatment.

Can you drive after a root canal?

Most people can drive after a root canal because it is usually done with local anesthetic only. If you received sedation, such as nitrous oxide combined with other medicines or IV sedation, you will need someone else to drive you home.
