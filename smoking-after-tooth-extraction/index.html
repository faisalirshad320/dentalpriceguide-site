Smoking After Tooth Extraction: How Long to Wait, Plus Straws, Coffee and Alcohol

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 26, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

Dentists usually advise not smoking for at least 48 to 72 hours after a tooth extraction, and longer if you can, because suction and chemicals in smoke can dislodge the blood clot and cause dry socket. The same suction risk applies to straws for roughly the first week. Follow your own dentist's instructions, which take priority (Cleveland Clinic).

Key takeaways
  • Avoid smoking for at least 48 to 72 hours after an extraction; longer is better.
  • Smoking raises the risk of dry socket and slows healing.
  • Skip straws for about a week, since suction can pull the clot loose.
  • Lukewarm coffee is usually fine after the first day; avoid very hot drinks early.
  • Avoid alcohol for at least 24 hours, and longer if you take prescription pain medicine.

Why is smoking bad after a tooth extraction?

Smoking is bad after a tooth extraction because it threatens the blood clot that forms in the empty socket. That clot is the foundation of healing: it protects the bone and nerve endings and becomes the scaffold for new tissue.

Smoking affects the clot in two ways. First, the act of drawing on a cigarette creates suction in the mouth that can pull the clot out of the socket. Second, chemicals in tobacco smoke narrow blood vessels and reduce blood flow to the gums, which slows healing and makes infection more likely. Heat from the smoke can also irritate the wound.

The result is a much higher risk of dry socket, a painful condition where the clot is lost and bone is exposed. The Cleveland Clinic's dry socket guide lists smoking as a leading risk factor.

When can you smoke after a tooth extraction?

Most dentists advise waiting at least 48 to 72 hours before smoking after a tooth extraction. Waiting a full week, or using the extraction as a reason to quit, gives the socket the best chance to heal without complications.

Surgical extractions and wisdom tooth removals usually call for a longer break because the wound is larger. Your dentist may give you a specific time frame; follow it over any general advice, including this page.

If you do smoke before the socket has healed, reduce the risk as much as possible. Keep gauze over the socket while smoking, take shallow puffs, avoid creating strong suction, and rinse gently with warm salt water afterward if your dentist has cleared you to rinse.

Is vaping or using nicotine products safer after an extraction?

Vaping is not considered safe right after a tooth extraction, because the inhaling motion creates the same suction as smoking a cigarette. Vape aerosol can also dry the mouth and irritate healing tissue.

Nicotine patches avoid suction entirely, which is why some dentists suggest them for smokers who are struggling to stop for a few days. Nicotine itself still narrows blood vessels, so it is not ideal for healing, but a patch is generally less risky than smoking or vaping. Nicotine gum involves chewing, which is best avoided near the socket at first. Ask your dentist or pharmacist which option suits you.

Is a tooth extraction a good time to quit smoking?

A tooth extraction can be a useful moment to quit smoking, because you already need a break of several days to protect the socket. Many people find the first few days the hardest, and getting through them for healing reasons gives a head start.

Quitting also helps your mouth in the long term. Smoking is linked to gum disease, tooth loss, slower healing after dental surgery, staining and a higher risk of oral cancer. If you are planning an implant to replace the extracted tooth, quitting improves the odds that the implant will bond to the bone and last.

Ask your doctor, pharmacist or dentist about nicotine replacement and other support. Free quit lines and apps are also widely available. Even if you do not quit for good, staying smoke-free until the socket has healed is one of the best things you can do for a smooth recovery.

How long after a tooth extraction can you use a straw?

You should usually avoid straws for about a week after a tooth extraction. Sucking through a straw creates negative pressure in the mouth that can dislodge the clot, just like smoking.

By around seven days the socket has typically formed enough new tissue that the clot is no longer as fragile, and many dentists consider straws acceptable from then on. For surgical or wisdom tooth extractions, your dentist may recommend waiting a little longer. Drink straight from a glass or cup in the meantime.

Can you drink coffee after a tooth extraction?

You can usually drink coffee after a tooth extraction once the first day has passed, as long as it is lukewarm rather than hot. Very hot drinks in the first 24 hours can dissolve the clot or restart bleeding.

Sip coffee from a cup without a straw, avoid swishing it around the socket, and skip very sugary or creamy additions that can leave residue in the wound. Caffeine does not directly harm the socket, but it can raise blood pressure slightly, so moderation is sensible in the first couple of days.

Can you drink alcohol 24 hours after a tooth extraction?

Dentists commonly advise avoiding alcohol for at least 24 hours after a tooth extraction, and longer after surgical extractions. Alcohol can slow healing, increase bleeding and irritate the wound.

Alcohol is especially risky if you have been prescribed pain medication or antibiotics. Mixing alcohol with opioid pain relievers can cause dangerous drowsiness and slowed breathing, and some antibiotics interact badly with alcohol. Check the medicine label and ask your pharmacist. Alcohol-based mouthwashes are also best avoided until your dentist says otherwise.

What are the warning signs after smoking too soon?

The main warning sign after smoking too soon is pain that gets worse two to four days after the extraction instead of better. Other signs include an empty-looking socket, visible whitish bone, a bad taste or bad breath, and pain spreading toward the ear.

Contact your dentist for worsening pain, fever, pus, swelling that keeps increasing after day three, or bleeding that will not stop with firm gauze pressure. Seek emergency care for trouble breathing or swallowing.

Can smoking increase the cost of a tooth extraction?

Smoking can increase the cost of a tooth extraction indirectly, because dry socket and infections often need extra visits. Whether those are billed separately depends on your dentist.

For context, Delta Dental lists $150 to $310 per tooth for a simple extraction and $260 to $460 for a surgical extraction out-of-network, according to its extraction cost guide. CareCredit reports averages of $177 and $363, and $811 for an allograft bone graft if one is placed to preserve the socket. Smoking can also lower the success of bone grafts and future implants. Frequently asked questions

How long after a tooth extraction can I smoke?

Most dentists recommend waiting at least 48 to 72 hours before smoking after a tooth extraction, and longer after surgical or wisdom tooth extractions. Waiting a full week, or quitting, lowers the risk of dry socket the most. Follow your own dentist's specific instructions.

Can I use a straw 7 days after a tooth extraction?

Many dentists consider a straw acceptable about seven days after a simple tooth extraction, because the socket has started to fill with new tissue. After surgical or wisdom tooth extractions, you may be told to wait longer. Check with your dentist if unsure.

Can I drink coffee after a tooth extraction?

You can usually drink lukewarm coffee after the first 24 hours following a tooth extraction. Avoid very hot coffee on the first day, do not use a straw, and do not swish it around the socket. Hot drinks too early can disturb the clot and restart bleeding.

Can I drink alcohol 24 hours after a tooth extraction?

Many dentists advise avoiding alcohol for at least 24 hours after a tooth extraction, and longer after surgery. Alcohol can slow healing and should not be mixed with prescription pain relievers or some antibiotics. Read your medicine labels and ask your pharmacist.

What happens if I smoke after a tooth extraction?

Smoking after a tooth extraction raises the risk of dry socket, where the blood clot is lost and bone is exposed, causing severe pain a few days later. It also slows healing and increases infection risk. Contact your dentist if pain worsens after the second day.
