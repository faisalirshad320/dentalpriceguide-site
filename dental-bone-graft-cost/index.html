How Much Does a Dental Bone Graft Cost?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 30, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

A dental bone graft costs about $811 on average for an allograft (donor bone) and $2,779 for an autograft (your own bone), according to CareCredit. Grafts are often placed after an extraction or before an implant when the jawbone is too thin, as the Cleveland Clinic explains.

Key takeaways
  • Allograft bone grafts average $811; autografts average $2,779 (CareCredit).
  • Grafts rebuild jawbone so an implant has enough support.
  • A socket graft after extraction can preserve bone for a future implant.
  • Larger grafts, sinus lifts and autografts cost more and take longer to heal.
  • Some insurance plans cover grafts, often as part of oral surgery or major benefits.

What is a dental bone graft?

A dental bone graft is a procedure that adds bone or bone-like material to the jaw where bone has been lost. The graft acts as a scaffold that your body gradually replaces with its own new bone over several months.

Jawbone shrinks after a tooth is lost because it no longer receives stimulation from the tooth root. Gum disease, infection and injury can also destroy bone. When too little bone remains, an implant cannot be placed securely, so a graft rebuilds the area first. The Cleveland Clinic's bone graft guide describes the procedure.

How much does a dental bone graft cost?

A dental bone graft costs about $811 on average for an allograft and $2,779 for an autograft, according to CareCredit's procedure cost list. The type of graft material and the size of the area being rebuilt are the biggest factors.

A small socket graft placed right after an extraction is usually at the lower end. Larger grafts that rebuild the width or height of the ridge, or sinus lifts that add bone under the sinus for upper back implants, are more complex and cost more. Imaging adds to the bill; CareCredit reports a $466 average for a cone-beam CT scan.

What are the types of bone graft material?

Bone graft material comes from several sources, and the choice affects cost and healing.

  • Autograft: your own bone, usually taken from another part of the jaw. It heals well but needs a second surgical site and costs more.
  • Allograft: processed human donor bone from a tissue bank. It is the most common choice and avoids a second site.
  • Xenograft: processed animal bone, usually bovine, that provides a long-lasting scaffold.
  • Alloplast: synthetic bone substitutes made from materials such as calcium phosphate.

Grafts are often covered with a membrane that keeps gum tissue out while bone grows. Your surgeon chooses the material based on the defect size, location and your health.

Do you need a bone graft after tooth extraction?

You may need a bone graft after tooth extraction if you plan to replace the tooth with an implant later. A socket preservation graft fills the empty socket at the time of extraction and helps prevent the ridge from collapsing as it heals.

Not every extraction needs a graft. If you do not plan an implant, or the socket is surrounded by thick bone, your dentist may let it heal naturally. Ask before the extraction whether a graft would make a future implant easier and how much it would add.

When is a bone graft needed for implants?

A bone graft is needed for implants when the jawbone is too thin, too short or too soft to hold an implant securely. This is common when a tooth has been missing for a long time, after severe gum disease, or in the upper back jaw where the sinus sits close to the ridge.

A cone-beam CT scan shows bone height and width in three dimensions so the surgeon can decide. Sometimes a small graft is placed at the same time as the implant; larger grafts may need several months of healing before the implant goes in. How long does a bone graft take to heal?

A bone graft usually takes several months to heal enough for an implant, often around three to six months for socket and smaller grafts, and longer for large or sinus grafts. The gum heals within a few weeks, while bone regenerates more slowly.

Swelling and discomfort are usually manageable and peak in the first few days. Follow your surgeon's instructions on eating soft foods, avoiding smoking, taking prescribed medicines and protecting the site. Smoking significantly reduces graft success.

Contact your surgeon for increasing pain or swelling after the first few days, fever, pus, or graft particles continuing to come out of the site. Seek emergency care for trouble breathing or swallowing.

Does insurance cover dental bone grafts?

Insurance sometimes covers dental bone grafts, depending on the plan and the reason for the graft. Grafts may fall under oral surgery, periodontal or major benefits. Plans that exclude implants may also exclude grafts placed only to prepare for an implant.

Delta Dental describes a typical 100-80-50 plan structure with major work at 50% up to the annual maximum, using $1,500 as an example, in its coverage guide. A graft needed after an accident or for certain medical reasons may be considered by medical insurance. Get a pre-treatment estimate. See what insurance covers for dental implants.

How does a bone graft affect total implant cost?

A bone graft adds to the total cost of implant treatment, on top of the implant, abutment and crown. Delta Dental puts a single implant at $2,800 to $5,600 without dental benefits, according to its implant cost guide, and that figure does not usually include grafting.

For full-arch treatment such as All-on-4, which CareCredit averages at $15,176 per arch, implants are often angled to use existing bone and avoid grafting. See the cost of full mouth dental implants and types of dental implants.

Can you avoid a bone graft?

You can sometimes avoid a bone graft with shorter or narrower implants, angled implants, or by choosing a bridge or denture instead of an implant. Healthline's implant versus bridge and dentures versus implants guides outline alternatives.

The best way to avoid needing a graft later is to replace a tooth soon after losing it, or to place a socket graft at extraction if an implant is planned. The Cleveland Clinic's prosthodontics guide explains how specialists plan tooth replacement.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a dental bone graft cost?

A dental bone graft averages $811 for an allograft and $2,779 for an autograft according to CareCredit. Larger grafts and sinus lifts cost more. Imaging, such as a cone-beam CT scan averaging $466, may be billed separately.

Is a bone graft necessary after tooth extraction?

A bone graft is not always necessary after extraction. It is often recommended when an implant is planned, because a socket graft helps preserve the ridge. If you will not get an implant, the socket can usually heal on its own.

Does insurance cover bone grafts for implants?

Some insurance plans cover bone grafts, often as oral surgery or major services, while plans that exclude implants may also exclude grafts placed only for implants. A pre-treatment estimate shows your exact coverage.

How long after a bone graft can I get an implant?

You can usually get an implant several months after a bone graft, often around three to six months for socket and smaller grafts, and longer for large or sinus grafts. Small grafts are sometimes placed at the same time as the implant.

Is a dental bone graft painful?

A dental bone graft is done under local anesthetic, often with sedation, so it should not hurt during the procedure. Afterward, swelling and soreness usually peak in the first few days and are managed with pain relievers as directed.
