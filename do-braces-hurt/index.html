Do Braces Hurt? What Getting Braces Feels Like and How Long Soreness Lasts

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 27, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

Getting braces put on does not hurt, but teeth usually feel sore for about three to five days afterward as they start to move, and again for a day or two after each adjustment. Brackets can also rub the cheeks until your mouth toughens up. Soft foods, orthodontic wax and over-the-counter pain relievers usually make it manageable (Healthline).

Key takeaways
  • The fitting appointment itself involves no needles or drilling and is not painful.
  • Aching teeth for several days after fitting is normal and expected.
  • Adjustments, new wires and new rubber bands can bring back soreness for a day or two.
  • Orthodontic wax stops brackets rubbing on cheeks and lips.
  • Severe, sharp or worsening pain is not normal and needs a call to your orthodontist.

Does getting braces put on hurt?

Getting braces put on does not hurt. The appointment involves cleaning the teeth, painting on a bonding agent, placing each bracket and curing the glue with a light. There are no injections and no drilling.

The most uncomfortable part for most people is keeping the mouth open for an hour or more with cheek retractors in place. Some people find the taste of the bonding materials unpleasant, and fitting bands around back teeth can feel tight and pressured for a moment.

Once the wire is placed, many people feel a gentle pressure almost immediately. The real soreness usually begins a few hours later as the teeth start to respond.

How long do braces hurt after you first get them?

Braces usually hurt for about three to five days after you first get them. The ache tends to peak on the second or third day, when the teeth and the ligaments around them are most inflamed, and then fades steadily.

During this time, biting into food may feel tender, as if your teeth are bruised. Most people stick to soft foods for the first few days. By the end of the first week, the majority of patients are eating more normally and barely notice the braces. The discomfort is a sign that the teeth are starting to move, which is the whole point of treatment.

Why do braces hurt my cheeks and lips?

Braces hurt the cheeks and lips because the brackets are new, firm objects that rub the soft tissue inside your mouth. Small sores can form where the brackets press, especially on the inside of the lips and cheeks.

Orthodontic wax is the main fix. Pinch off a small piece, roll it into a ball and press it over the bracket that is rubbing. Rinsing with warm salt water helps sores heal. Within one to two weeks, the inside of the mouth usually toughens up and irritation becomes rare.

A wire end that sticks out and pokes the cheek is different. Cover it with wax and call your orthodontist, who can clip or tuck it quickly.

Do braces hurt after adjustments?

Braces often hurt for one to three days after adjustments, when the orthodontist changes the wire, tightens the system or adds new springs or chains. The soreness is usually milder than in the first week.

Starting rubber bands (elastics) can also bring a few days of aching teeth and a tired jaw. Wearing elastics consistently, rather than taking them out when they feel sore, gets you through the adjustment fastest.

How can you relieve braces pain?

You can relieve braces pain with simple steps at home. Most people need these only for the first days after fitting or adjustments.

  • Soft foods: yogurt, soups, pasta, mashed potatoes, eggs and smoothies.
  • Cold: ice water, cold drinks or frozen fruit can soothe aching teeth.
  • Pain relievers: over-the-counter medicines taken as directed on the label.
  • Orthodontic wax: covers brackets or wires that rub.
  • Salt-water rinses: warm salt water helps mouth sores heal.
  • Topical gels: oral numbing gels can ease sore spots for a short time; follow the label.

Avoid hard and sticky foods, which can hurt sore teeth and break brackets.

What can you eat when braces hurt?

When braces hurt, soft and cool foods are easiest to eat because they need little chewing. Good choices for the first few days include yogurt, smoothies, oatmeal, scrambled eggs, soup, mashed potatoes, pasta, rice, soft fish, bananas and cottage cheese. Ice cream and frozen yogurt can also soothe aching teeth.

Cut food into small pieces and chew with your back teeth, which are usually less tender than the front teeth in the first days. Avoid hard, crunchy, sticky and chewy foods such as nuts, popcorn, hard candy, caramel, bagels and tough meat. These hurt sore teeth and can break brackets, which adds repair visits and can lengthen treatment.

Do braces hurt the whole time?

Braces do not hurt the whole time. Most of treatment feels like nothing at all, with brief, predictable flare-ups after adjustments. Many patients say they forget they are wearing braces between appointments.

Sensitivity varies from person to person. Some people feel very little soreness, while others find each adjustment uncomfortable for a couple of days. Neither is a sign that treatment is going better or worse.

Do some types of braces hurt less?

All types of braces cause some soreness because all of them move teeth. The differences are mostly in how they feel against soft tissue.

Metal and ceramic braces can rub the cheeks and lips. Lingual braces, which sit behind the teeth, tend to irritate the tongue and can cause a temporary lisp. Clear aligners have no brackets to rub, but each new tray brings a day or two of pressure. Self-ligating brackets and modern heat-activated wires are designed to use lighter forces. See clear braces for a comparison of options.

When is braces pain not normal?

Braces pain is not normal when it is severe, sharp, worsening after the first week, or comes with swelling or fever. Normal soreness is a dull ache that improves over a few days.

Call your orthodontist for a broken bracket or wire that cuts your mouth, a tooth that becomes very loose, pain that keeps getting worse, swelling of the gums or face, or fever. Seek urgent care for swelling that spreads or trouble swallowing or breathing.

How much do braces cost?

Braces cost about $6,343 on average for metal braces, $5,834 for ceramic braces, $9,221 for lingual braces and $5,108 for clear aligners, according to CareCredit. Delta Dental cites $3,000 to $7,000 for traditional metal braces.

Pearl lists comprehensive treatment at $3,680 to $9,650 nationally. The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first orthodontic check no later than age 7, as noted by Delta Dental. See how much braces cost, how much braces cost without insurance and braces colors.

Frequently asked questions

Does getting braces hurt?

Getting braces put on does not hurt because there are no injections or drilling. Teeth usually start to ache a few hours later and stay sore for about three to five days as they begin to move. Soft foods and over-the-counter pain relievers help.

How long do braces hurt?

Braces typically hurt for about three to five days after they are first fitted, and for one to three days after each adjustment. Cheek and lip irritation from brackets usually settles within one to two weeks as the mouth toughens up.

What helps braces pain the most?

Soft foods, cold drinks, orthodontic wax over rubbing brackets, warm salt-water rinses and over-the-counter pain relievers taken as directed on the label help braces pain the most. Most soreness fades within a few days.

Do braces hurt more than aligners?

Braces and aligners both cause soreness because both move teeth. Braces may rub the cheeks and lips at first, while aligners cause pressure for a day or two with each new tray. Overall discomfort is similar for most people.

Does taking braces off hurt?

Taking braces off usually does not hurt. You will feel pressure as each bracket is popped off and some vibration as leftover glue is polished away. Teeth may feel slightly loose or sensitive for a few days afterward.
