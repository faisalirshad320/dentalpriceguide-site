What to Eat After Wisdom Teeth Removal: Soft Foods and a Day-by-Day Timeline

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 21, 20262 price sources
Quick answer

What to eat after wisdom teeth removal: cool liquids and smooth soft foods for the first day or two, such as yogurt, applesauce, smoothies eaten with a spoon and lukewarm blended soup. Move to soft, easy-chew foods over the first week and back to normal food over about one to two weeks, following your surgeon's instructions.

Key takeaways
  • Days 1 to 2: cool liquids and smooth foods you do not need to chew, eaten with a spoon, never through a straw.
  • Days 3 to 7: soft foods such as scrambled eggs, mashed potatoes, pasta and soft fish, chewed away from the sockets.
  • Rice, chips, pizza crust, burgers and spicy food usually wait until the sockets have closed over, often around one to two weeks.
  • Your oral surgeon's or dentist's instructions take priority over any general timeline.
  • Pain that worsens after day 3, fever or a bad taste are reasons to call your surgeon.

What can I eat after wisdom teeth removal?

After wisdom teeth removal you can eat cool, smooth foods that need little or no chewing, such as yogurt, applesauce, pudding and blended soup that has cooled to lukewarm. Soft foods protect the blood clots in the sockets, which shield the bone and nerves while healing begins. Your oral surgeon's or dentist's instructions take priority over the general advice on this page.

Good foods to eat after wisdom teeth removal, roughly in the order most people add them:

  • Water, milk and diluted fruit juice (sipped, not through a straw)
  • Plain or smooth yogurt, kefir and pudding
  • Applesauce and mashed banana
  • Smoothies made without seeds, eaten with a spoon
  • Protein shakes or meal-replacement drinks
  • Lukewarm blended soups such as butternut squash or tomato
  • Broth, cooled to warm
  • Jell-O and ice cream without chunks or cones
  • Mashed potatoes and mashed sweet potatoes
  • Hummus and smooth bean dips
  • Cottage cheese and ricotta
  • Avocado or guacamole without chips
  • Scrambled eggs
  • Oatmeal and cream of wheat, cooled
  • Soft-cooked pasta and macaroni and cheese
  • Soft fish such as salmon or tilapia, flaked
  • Tofu
  • Well-cooked, mashed vegetables
  • Soft bread with the crust removed
  • Pancakes soaked in syrup or milk

Eating enough after wisdom teeth surgery matters. Protein and fluids help healing, and taking pain medicine on an empty stomach can cause nausea, so plan simple meals before surgery day.

When can I eat solid food after wisdom teeth removal?

Most people can start eating soft solid food such as scrambled eggs and pasta around days 3 to 4 after wisdom teeth removal and return to most normal foods in about one to two weeks. Healing speed varies with how many teeth were removed, whether they were impacted, and your own health, so your surgeon's guidance wins.

  1. First 24 hours. Wait until the numbness wears off so you do not bite your cheek or tongue. Stick to cool liquids and smooth foods eaten with a spoon.
  2. Days 2 to 3. Add lukewarm blended soups, mashed potatoes and cottage cheese. Swelling often peaks around this time, so opening wide may still be hard.
  3. Day 4 and the rest of the first week. If you are wondering what you can eat on day 4 of wisdom teeth removal, think fork-tender foods: scrambled eggs, soft pasta, flaked fish, oatmeal and well-cooked vegetables. Chew with your front teeth or on the side that hurts less.
  4. Week 2 onward. Reintroduce firmer foods one at a time. If something hurts, back off for a few more days.

When can I eat rice, pizza, chips or a burger after wisdom teeth removal?

Rice, pizza, chips and burgers are usually safe after wisdom teeth removal once the sockets have closed over and chewing is comfortable, which for many people is around one to two weeks. These foods are popular questions because each carries a specific risk.

  • Rice. Small grains can lodge in the open sockets. Many surgeons suggest waiting about a week; if you eat rice sooner, choose very soft, sticky rice and rinse gently afterward as instructed.
  • Ramen. Soft noodles can work in the first week if the broth has cooled to lukewarm. Skip spicy seasoning packets and slurp gently rather than sucking hard.
  • Pizza. Soft, cooled pizza without crust is often manageable late in the first week. Crisp crust and stringy, hot cheese are better left for week two.
  • Chips. Sharp chip fragments can scratch or lodge in healing sockets. Most people wait until about the second week or later.
  • Burgers. A burger needs wide opening and firm chewing. Many people manage a burger around the end of the first week to the second week, cut into small pieces.
  • Spicy food. Spice can sting healing tissue. Waiting about a week, until the sockets are less tender, is a common recommendation.

A sensible rule: if a food is hard, crunchy, sharp, sticky, seeded or spicy, wait until your surgeon says the sockets have healed enough.

What foods and habits should I avoid after wisdom teeth removal?

After wisdom teeth removal, avoid straws, hot drinks, alcohol, smoking, and foods that are crunchy, seeded, sticky or spicy during the first week. These can dislodge the blood clot or irritate the sockets and raise the risk of dry socket.

  • Straws and sucking. Suction can pull the clot out. Sip from a cup and eat smoothies with a spoon.
  • Hot food and drinks. Heat can increase bleeding and swelling early on. Let soups and coffee cool to lukewarm.
  • Alcohol. Alcohol can slow healing and interacts with many pain medicines.
  • Smoking and vaping. Both raise the risk of dry socket; ask your surgeon how long to wait.
  • Small, hard pieces. Nuts, seeds, popcorn and granola can get trapped in sockets.
  • Acidic food. Citrus and tomato-heavy dishes can sting at first.

Eating carefully also means keeping food out of the sockets afterward. Follow your surgeon's instructions on gentle saltwater rinses or a syringe for flushing the lower sockets, which usually start after the first day or two. Cleveland Clinic outlines standard aftercare after wisdom teeth removal.

When should I call my surgeon about eating or pain after wisdom teeth removal?

Call your oral surgeon or dentist if pain gets worse instead of better after about day 3, if you notice a bad taste or smell, or if you cannot eat or drink enough. Worsening pain a few days after surgery is a classic sign of dry socket, which Cleveland Clinic describes in detail.

Normal soreness after wisdom teeth removal tends to peak in the first couple of days and then improve. Our page on wisdom teeth pain explains what is typical and what is not.

Seek urgent care for bleeding that does not slow with firm gauze pressure, swelling that keeps increasing after the third day or spreads toward the eye or neck, fever, trouble swallowing or breathing, or severe pain that your prescribed medicine does not control.

How much does wisdom teeth removal cost?

Wisdom teeth removal costs an average of $3,120 for all four teeth removed surgically with sedation, out-of-network, according to Delta Dental 2021 data. Delta Dental puts four fully erupted teeth removed without surgery at an average of $720, and one surgical wisdom tooth at $550 per tooth.

A second source looks specifically at impacted teeth. CareCredit reports an average of $835 for a single impacted wisdom tooth and $3,340 for four impacted wisdom teeth. The two sources measure different things (out-of-network claims versus a cost survey), and both are national figures; local prices vary.

Wisdom teeth removal is often partly covered by dental insurance, typically about 50% to 80% of the total cost according to Delta Dental. A complication such as dry socket can mean extra visits, so careful eating is one of the simplest ways to avoid added cost. Frequently asked questions

How long after wisdom teeth removal can I eat?

You can usually eat cool, smooth foods such as yogurt or applesauce as soon as the numbness wears off after wisdom teeth removal. Waiting for feeling to return prevents biting your cheek or tongue. Soft solid foods typically follow around days 3 to 4, guided by your surgeon's instructions.

When can I eat normally after wisdom teeth removal?

Most people eat normally again about one to two weeks after wisdom teeth removal, introducing firmer foods gradually. Impacted teeth, several extractions or slow healing can extend that time. If chewing a food causes pain, return to softer meals for a few more days and follow your surgeon's advice.

Can I eat rice after wisdom teeth removal?

You can usually eat rice about a week after wisdom teeth removal. Rice grains are small enough to lodge in open sockets, so many surgeons suggest waiting until the sockets begin to close. If you eat rice earlier, choose very soft rice and rinse gently afterward as your surgeon directs.

When can I eat pizza after wisdom teeth removal?

Many people can eat soft, cooled pizza without the crust late in the first week after wisdom teeth removal. Crisp crust and very hot cheese are better left until the second week, when the sockets are less tender. Cut pizza into small pieces and chew away from the extraction sites.

What can I eat on day 4 after wisdom teeth removal?

On day 4 after wisdom teeth removal, most people can manage soft, fork-tender foods such as scrambled eggs, soft pasta, mashed potatoes, oatmeal, flaked fish and well-cooked vegetables. Keep avoiding straws, crunchy, seeded and spicy foods, and chew with your front teeth or the less sore side.

When can I eat spicy food after wisdom teeth removal?

Spicy food is usually best avoided for about a week after wisdom teeth removal, because spice can sting healing tissue and increase discomfort. Reintroduce mild spice first once the sockets feel less tender. If spicy food causes burning or pain, wait a few more days and check with your surgeon.
