How Long After Wisdom Teeth Removal Can I Use a Straw, Drink Coffee, Soda or Alcohol?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 21, 20262 price sources
Quick answer

Most oral surgeons advise waiting about one week after wisdom teeth removal before using a straw, because the suction can pull the blood clot out of the socket and cause dry socket, as Cleveland Clinic explains. Drink straight from a cup until then, and follow your own surgeon's instructions if they differ.

Key takeaways
  • Avoid straws for about a week after wisdom teeth removal; suction can dislodge the blood clot.
  • Water and other cool, non-carbonated drinks sipped from a cup are fine from the first day.
  • Lukewarm coffee is usually fine after the first day or so; avoid very hot drinks while numb and in the first days.
  • Wait at least 72 hours for soda and smoking, and avoid alcohol while taking prescription pain medicine.
  • Your surgeon's written instructions take priority over any general timeline.

When can I use a straw after wisdom teeth removal?

You can usually use a straw again about one week after wisdom teeth removal, once the sockets have started to close and the blood clots are more secure. Some surgeons allow it a little earlier for simple extractions, while others ask patients with impacted teeth to wait longer.

Straws are a problem after wisdom teeth removal because sucking creates negative pressure in the mouth. That pressure can pull the blood clot out of the socket before new tissue has covered the bone. The result is dry socket, a painful condition in which bone and nerve endings are exposed. Cleveland Clinic lists sucking through a straw among the common risk factors.

Drinking after wisdom teeth removal is still important, so sip directly from a cup or glass. Take small sips, keep the liquid on the side away from the surgery when you can, and swallow gently. Spoon-feeding thin smoothies or soups is another way to stay nourished without suction. Our guide on what to eat after wisdom teeth removal covers soft foods that pair well with this stage.

What can I drink in the first 24 hours after wisdom teeth removal?

In the first 24 hours after wisdom teeth removal, the best drinks are cool water, milk, smooth non-acidic juices and cool broths, all sipped gently from a cup. Wait until the numbness has worn off before drinking anything warm, so you do not burn your lips or tongue without noticing.

Drinking on the first day helps prevent dehydration, which can make you feel worse and slow healing, especially if you had sedation or are eating very little. Aim for small, frequent sips rather than large gulps. Skip rinsing and spitting on the first day; let saliva and liquid drain from your mouth instead of forcing it out.

Avoid hot drinks, alcohol, carbonated drinks and anything acidic, such as orange juice or lemonade, on day one. If you feel nauseated after sedation, clear fluids taken slowly are usually easiest. Your surgeon's printed aftercare sheet may list approved drinks; follow it if it differs from these general tips.

Can you drink coffee after wisdom teeth removal?

You can usually drink coffee after wisdom teeth removal once the first day has passed, as long as it is lukewarm rather than hot and sipped from a cup. Many surgeons suggest skipping coffee entirely on the day of surgery.

Coffee after wisdom teeth removal causes two possible problems. Very hot drinks can dilate blood vessels and restart bleeding, and they can burn tissue you cannot feel while your mouth is still numb. Caffeine may also make it harder to rest, which matters during the first couple of days.

Iced coffee is often an easier choice early in recovery, but drink it without a straw. Avoid adding crunchy toppings or anything that leaves particles in the socket. If you rely on caffeine daily, a lukewarm cup can help prevent withdrawal headaches that make recovery feel worse.

When can I drink soda after wisdom tooth extraction?

You can usually drink soda after wisdom tooth extraction once about 72 hours have passed, and only from a cup, never through a straw. Some surgeons say flat soda is acceptable a little sooner.

Soda after wisdom tooth extraction is discouraged early on for a few reasons. Carbonation creates bubbles and pressure that may disturb the clot, and the acids and sugar can irritate an open wound. Sugary drinks also feed bacteria while you cannot clean the area normally.

When you do return to soda, rinse gently with water or warm salt water afterward if your surgeon has approved rinsing. Choosing water, milk, or non-acidic juices during the first few days is the easier path for healing.

When can you drink alcohol after wisdom teeth removal?

You can usually drink alcohol after wisdom teeth removal once at least 72 hours have passed and you are no longer taking prescription pain medicine; many surgeons prefer you wait a full week. If you are taking opioids or certain other medicines, alcohol should be avoided entirely until you finish them.

Alcohol after wisdom teeth removal slows healing in several ways. It can thin the blood and increase bleeding, dehydrate the body, and irritate the wound. Combining alcohol with opioid pain relievers or sedatives is dangerous because both depress breathing. Some antibiotics also interact with alcohol.

When you return to alcohol, start with small amounts, drink from a glass rather than a straw, and avoid carbonated mixers in the first week. If your surgeon gives you a specific timeline, follow it.

How long after wisdom teeth removal can I smoke?

Most oral surgeons ask you to wait at least 72 hours after wisdom teeth removal before smoking or vaping, and longer is safer. Smoking is one of the strongest risk factors for dry socket.

Smoking after wisdom teeth removal causes harm through suction, heat and chemicals. Drawing on a cigarette or vape pulls on the clot the same way a straw does. Nicotine narrows blood vessels and reduces blood flow to the healing socket. If you can use recovery as a chance to stop smoking altogether, your mouth will heal faster.

If you cannot stop, ask your surgeon about nicotine patches as a short-term option, since they avoid suction. Watch closely for signs of dry socket, which our wisdom teeth recovery guide describes day by day. For pain that is more than expected, see wisdom teeth pain.

Call your surgeon if pain gets sharply worse around days 3 to 5, you notice a bad taste or smell, or the socket looks empty. Seek urgent care for bleeding that will not stop with pressure, fever, spreading swelling, or trouble swallowing or breathing.

What does it cost if a dry socket or complication happens?

Wisdom teeth removal itself averages $720 for four erupted teeth and $3,120 for four surgical extractions with sedation out of network, according to Delta Dental's 2021 data, with one surgical tooth averaging $550. The CareCredit 2023–2024 cost survey reports $835 for a single impacted tooth and $3,340 for four. The sources measure different things, out-of-network claims versus a price survey, and all are national averages; local prices vary.

A dry socket or infection can add to that bill. Treatment usually means extra office visits for cleaning and medicated dressings, and sometimes antibiotics or stronger pain relief. Some surgeons include follow-up visits in the surgical fee, while others bill them separately, so ask before your procedure. Delta Dental notes insurance typically covers about 50% to 80% of the total cost of wisdom teeth removal.

Protecting the clot by avoiding straws, smoking and early alcohol is the simplest way to avoid those extra costs. For a full breakdown of prices, insurance and ways to save, see our wisdom teeth removal cost guide.

Frequently asked questions

How long after wisdom teeth removal can I drink?

You can drink water and other cool, non-carbonated drinks from a cup on the day of wisdom teeth removal, once bleeding has slowed. Avoid straws for about a week, hot drinks for the first day or so, soda for about 72 hours, and alcohol while taking prescription pain medicine.

Can I drink from a cup after wisdom teeth removal?

You can drink from a cup after wisdom teeth removal, and it is the recommended way to stay hydrated. Take small sips, avoid swishing, and swallow gently so you do not disturb the blood clot. Keep drinks cool or lukewarm during the first day or two.

What happens if I accidentally use a straw after wisdom teeth removal?

Using a straw once by accident does not always cause a problem, but it does raise the risk of dislodging the clot. Stop using it, avoid further suction, and watch for worsening pain, a bad taste or an empty-looking socket over the next few days. Call your surgeon if those signs appear.

Can I drink cold water after wisdom teeth removal?

Cold water after wisdom teeth removal is fine and can feel soothing. Sip it from a cup rather than a straw and avoid swishing it around the surgical sites. Staying well hydrated supports healing, especially if you are eating less than usual during the first few days.

When can I drink a milkshake after wisdom teeth removal?

You can have a milkshake soon after wisdom teeth removal if you eat it with a spoon or sip it from a cup rather than using a straw. Cold, smooth foods are often comfortable early in recovery. Avoid shakes with seeds, nuts or crunchy mix-ins that could lodge in the socket.

Can you drink alcohol 24 hours after wisdom teeth removal?

Drinking alcohol 24 hours after wisdom teeth removal is not recommended. Alcohol can increase bleeding, slow healing and interact dangerously with prescription pain medicine. Most surgeons advise waiting at least 72 hours and until you have stopped prescription pain relievers, with many preferring a full week.
