Clear Braces Cost in 2026: Ceramic Braces and Clear Aligner Prices Compared

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 21, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

Clear braces cost about $5,834 on average for ceramic (tooth-colored) braces and $5,108 for clear aligners, according to the CareCredit cost study. Full orthodontic treatment of any type ranges from $3,680 to $9,650 nationally in ADA-based fee data. Your price depends on case complexity, treatment length and where you live.

How much do clear braces cost?

Clear braces cost about $5,834 on average for ceramic braces and $5,108 on average for clear aligners, according to the CareCredit cost study (Synchrony Average Procedural Cost Study, 2023–2024). These are national averages for full treatment, and fees in your area can be higher or lower.

For a wider view, Pearl's fee data based on ADA surveys puts comprehensive orthodontic treatment (CDT code D8080) at $3,680 to $9,650 nationally, with an average of $5,645. That code covers full braces treatment regardless of appliance, so it is a useful yardstick for any clear braces quote you receive.

One surprise in the CareCredit data is that ceramic braces ($5,834) and clear aligners ($5,108) both averaged less than metal braces ($6,343). Survey averages mix different patients and case types, so that ordering will not hold at every office. The lesson is that clear options are not automatically the most expensive choice.

Compare total fees, not monthly payments. Ask whether the quote includes records and X-rays, all adjustment visits, emergency repairs, aligner refinements and the first set of retainers.

Clear braces vs clear aligners: what is the difference?

Clear braces usually means ceramic braces, which are fixed, tooth-colored brackets bonded to the teeth and connected by a wire, while clear aligners are a series of removable plastic trays. Both are less visible than metal braces, but they work differently and suit different cases.

  • Ceramic braces: work like metal braces, so they handle complex tooth movements well. The elastic ties can stain if you drink coffee, tea or red wine often, and ceramic brackets are slightly bulkier and more fragile than metal. Clear or white ties keep them discreet, though some patients pick colors; see our guide to braces colors.
  • Clear aligners: removable for eating and brushing, nearly invisible, and with fewer emergency visits. They only work if worn for most of the day as your orthodontist directs, and some complex bite problems are better treated with fixed braces.

Ceramic braces sometimes use an elastic power chain to close gaps between teeth, just as metal braces do. An orthodontist can tell you whether your bite can be corrected with aligners alone or needs brackets.

What affects the price of clear braces?

The price of clear braces depends mostly on how complex your case is, how long treatment lasts, the type of appliance, the provider and where you live.

  • Case complexity: crowding, bite correction and jaw relationships drive treatment time and cost.
  • Treatment length: longer cases mean more appointments and adjustments.
  • Appliance type: ceramic brackets cost more to make than metal ones, and aligner fees vary with the number of trays and refinements included. Lingual braces, hidden behind the teeth, cost the most: $9,221 on average in the CareCredit study, and $8,000 to $10,000 according to Delta Dental.
  • Provider: orthodontists and general dentists set their own fees.
  • Location: fees tend to be higher in large metro areas.
  • Retainers: some quotes include retainers; others charge separately, and replacement retainers are extra.

For comparison, Delta Dental puts traditional metal braces at $3,000 to $7,000. Our main guide to how much braces cost covers every type side by side.

Does insurance cover clear braces?

Insurance covers clear braces only if your plan includes orthodontic benefits, and those benefits usually come with a separate lifetime maximum rather than the regular annual maximum. Many plans offer orthodontic coverage for children and teens, while adult coverage is less common.

Check these details with your plan, using Delta Dental's overview of what dental insurance covers as a starting point:

  • Whether orthodontics is included at all, and for which ages.
  • The lifetime orthodontic maximum.
  • Whether clear aligners are covered the same way as ceramic or metal braces.
  • Whether the plan pays in installments over the treatment period.
  • Any waiting period before orthodontic benefits begin.

If your plan has no orthodontic benefit, see our guide to how much braces cost without insurance for self-pay strategies. For children, the American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first orthodontic evaluation no later than age 7, according to Delta Dental. An early check can catch bite and jaw problems while the jaw is still growing.

How can you lower the cost of clear braces?

The main ways to lower the cost of clear braces are using insurance or tax-advantaged accounts, comparing several orthodontists, asking about payment plans and considering a university orthodontic clinic.

  1. Get two or three consultations. Many orthodontists offer free first consultations, and fees for the same case can differ widely.
  2. Use HSA or FSA funds. Orthodontic treatment to correct a dental problem is generally an eligible expense.
  3. Ask about payment plans. Orthodontists commonly spread fees over the treatment period, often after a down payment.
  4. Ask for a pay-in-full discount. Some offices reduce the fee if you pay upfront.
  5. Check university clinics. Orthodontic residency programs often charge less, with treatment supervised by faculty.
  6. Mix materials. Some patients choose ceramic brackets on the upper teeth and metal on the less visible lower teeth.

Retainers deserve a line in your budget too. Teeth tend to drift after braces come off, so retainers are usually worn long term, and lost or broken retainers are a common extra cost after treatment ends.

Be cautious about very cheap options that skip an in-person exam and X-rays. Moving teeth without checking gum and bone health can cause problems that cost more to fix. Healthline outlines typical costs and factors worth discussing with your orthodontist.

What should you expect while wearing clear braces?

Wearing clear braces means some soreness after adjustments, extra cleaning around brackets or aligners, and regular orthodontist visits every several weeks.

Mild aching for a few days after brackets are placed or a new aligner starts is normal; follow your orthodontist's advice on pain relief and soft foods. Ceramic brackets can chip, and a loose bracket or a poking wire should be reported so it can be fixed, often at a short repair visit. Orthodontic wax over a sharp spot helps until then.

Clear aligners need to be removed for eating and anything other than water, and the trays should be brushed and rinsed daily. Losing or breaking trays can add replacement fees, so ask how your office handles them before you start.

Contact a dentist promptly if you have facial swelling, fever, a mouth sore that does not heal, or an injury to the mouth. If you play contact sports, ask about a mouthguard designed to fit over braces.

Frequently asked questions

Are clear braces more expensive than metal braces?

Not always. In CareCredit's cost study, ceramic braces averaged $5,834 and clear aligners $5,108, while metal braces averaged $6,343. Individual offices may still charge more for ceramic brackets because they cost more to make. Compare full quotes for each option from the same orthodontist to see the real difference for your case.

How much are clear ceramic braces?

Clear ceramic braces average $5,834 for full treatment, according to CareCredit's 2023–2024 cost study. Pearl's ADA-based fee data puts comprehensive orthodontic treatment at $3,680 to $9,650 nationally. Your price depends on how complex your bite is, how long treatment takes and where you live, so get a written quote that lists everything included.

Does insurance pay for clear aligners?

Insurance pays for clear aligners only if your plan has orthodontic coverage, and many plans treat aligners like braces up to a lifetime orthodontic maximum. Some plans limit orthodontic benefits to children. Call your insurer, ask whether aligners are covered and request a pre-treatment estimate before you sign a treatment contract.

Do clear braces stain?

Ceramic brackets resist staining, but the clear elastic ties that hold the wire can discolor from coffee, tea, red wine, curry and tobacco. The ties are replaced at each adjustment. Brushing after meals and rinsing after dark drinks helps. Clear aligners can also stain if you drink anything other than water while wearing them.

Can adults get clear braces?

Yes, adults can get clear braces or clear aligners, and many choose them because they are less noticeable. Adult treatment may take longer because the jaw is no longer growing, and gum health must be checked first. Adult orthodontic coverage is less common in insurance plans, so budget for self-pay or use HSA or FSA funds.

When should a child first see an orthodontist?

A child should have a first orthodontic evaluation no later than age 7, as the American Association of Orthodontists recommends, according to Delta Dental. An early visit does not always mean early braces. It lets the orthodontist spot bite or jaw problems and plan the best timing for any treatment.
