Is Teeth Whitening Covered by Insurance?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 7, 20262 price sources
Quick answer

Teeth whitening is usually not covered by dental insurance, because it is considered cosmetic, according to Delta Dental. Professional in-office bleaching costs $40 to $700 per visit out-of-network, and CareCredit reports averages of $792 for laser and $583 for UV whitening. A few exceptions apply, such as whitening a tooth darkened after a root canal.

Does dental insurance cover teeth whitening?

Dental insurance usually does not cover teeth whitening. Delta Dental notes that whitening is generally considered a cosmetic procedure, and most dental plans exclude treatment whose main purpose is improving appearance rather than health or function.

Insurance focuses on preventing and treating disease: cleanings, exams, fillings, root canals, crowns and replacements for missing teeth. Whitening, veneers for appearance and similar treatments fall outside that scope on most policies. Check the exclusions section of your plan to confirm.

Are there exceptions where whitening is covered?

There are a few situations where some plans may cover whitening or related treatment. The most common is internal whitening of a single tooth that has darkened after a root canal, which some plans consider part of restoring that tooth.

Discoloration caused by trauma or by certain medical treatments may occasionally be considered, depending on the plan. Coverage is rare and not guaranteed, so request a pre-treatment estimate with documentation if you think your case may qualify.

How much does teeth whitening cost without coverage?

Teeth whitening without coverage costs $40 to $700 per visit for in-office bleaching out-of-network, according to Delta Dental. CareCredit reports averages of $792 for laser in-office whitening and $583 for UV whitening.

Custom take-home trays from a dentist and over-the-counter products cost less but usually work more slowly. See our teeth whitening cost guide for a full comparison of methods.

Can you use an HSA or FSA for teeth whitening?

Health savings accounts and flexible spending accounts usually cannot be used for teeth whitening, because whitening is considered cosmetic rather than medical. HSA and FSA rules generally exclude purely cosmetic procedures.

Check with your plan administrator if a dentist has recommended whitening as part of treating a specific condition, but expect it to be excluded in most cases.

How can you whiten teeth for less without insurance?

You can whiten teeth for less without insurance by choosing the method that fits your budget and goals.

  • In-house membership plans sometimes include a whitening discount or a free take-home kit.
  • New-patient promotions may bundle whitening with a cleaning.
  • Custom take-home trays usually cost less than in-office whitening and can be reused for touch-ups.
  • Over-the-counter strips and gels are the cheapest option for mild staining.
  • Whitening toothpaste helps maintain results.

Will a covered cleaning make teeth look whiter?

A professional cleaning, which insurance usually covers as preventive care, can make teeth look noticeably whiter by removing surface stains and tartar. Many people are surprised how much brighter their teeth look after a cleaning and polish.

Having a cleaning before whitening also helps the whitening gel reach the enamel evenly. If your main concern is surface staining from coffee or tea, start with a cleaning and see whether that is enough before paying for whitening.

Who should see a dentist before whitening?

People with sensitive teeth, gum problems, untreated cavities, cracked teeth, or crowns and fillings on front teeth should see a dentist before whitening. Whitening can increase sensitivity and does not change the colour of restorations.

The Cleveland Clinic explains who is a good candidate for whitening and which stains respond best. A dentist can also tell you whether bonding or veneers would serve you better for certain types of discoloration, as described in the Cleveland Clinic's cosmetic dentistry overview.

Does insurance cover other cosmetic treatments?

Insurance usually does not cover other cosmetic treatments either, such as veneers placed for appearance or cosmetic bonding. When a treatment also restores damage, such as bonding a chipped tooth or crowning a broken one, partial coverage may apply.

Ask your dentist how each part of a cosmetic plan will be coded, and request a pre-treatment estimate so you know what you will pay before starting.

What is internal whitening, and is it covered?

Internal whitening is a treatment for a single tooth that has darkened from the inside, usually after a root canal or an injury that caused the nerve to die. The dentist places a whitening agent inside the tooth through a small opening at the back, seals it temporarily and repeats the process over a few visits until the colour improves.

Because internal whitening is linked to restoring a root canal treated tooth, some plans consider it part of the restorative treatment, while others still treat it as cosmetic. Coverage varies widely, so ask your dentist to request a pre-treatment estimate. Internal whitening can be a cost-effective alternative to a crown or veneer for a single dark front tooth.

How should you plan whitening around other dental work?

Plan whitening before any new crowns, veneers or front-tooth fillings, because these restorations do not change colour once placed. Whitening first lets your dentist match new restorations to your brighter shade, so your smile looks even.

If you already have visible restorations on front teeth, ask your dentist whether whitening will make them stand out and whether they would need replacing. Replacement is a separate cost and, when done only to match whitened teeth, is usually considered cosmetic by insurance too. Having a cleaning and check-up first, which insurance usually covers, also ensures there are no cavities or gum problems that whitening could aggravate.

How do whitening methods compare on value?

Whitening methods compare on value by balancing price, speed and how long results last. In-office whitening costs the most per session but gives the fastest change under supervision. Custom take-home trays usually cost less, take a week or two, and can be reused for touch-ups, which makes them good long-term value.

Over-the-counter strips cost least upfront and suit mild staining, but results are usually more modest and fade sooner. Whitening toothpaste is best for maintenance rather than changing your shade.

Frequently asked questions

Is teeth whitening covered by insurance?

Teeth whitening is usually not covered by dental insurance because it is considered cosmetic, according to Delta Dental. A rare exception is internal whitening of a tooth darkened after a root canal, which some plans may cover.

How much does teeth whitening cost without insurance?

Without insurance, in-office bleaching costs $40 to $700 per visit out-of-network, according to Delta Dental. CareCredit reports averages of $792 for laser whitening and $583 for UV whitening. At-home options cost less.

Can I use my FSA or HSA for teeth whitening?

You usually cannot use an FSA or HSA for teeth whitening because it is considered cosmetic. Check with your plan administrator, but expect whitening to be excluded in most cases.

Are there dental plans that cover whitening?

Very few dental insurance plans cover whitening. Some in-house dental membership plans and discount plans offer reduced whitening fees or free take-home kits, which are not insurance but can lower the cost.

Does a dental cleaning whiten teeth?

A dental cleaning does not bleach teeth, but it removes surface stains and tartar, which can make teeth look noticeably brighter. Cleanings are usually covered by insurance as preventive care.
