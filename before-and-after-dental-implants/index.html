Before and After Dental Implants: What Changes and What It Costs

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 3, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

Before and after dental implants, people typically go from a gap, a failing tooth or loose dentures to a fixed tooth or set of teeth that look and function like natural teeth. Treatment usually takes several months because the implant must bond to the bone. A single implant costs $2,800 to $5,600 without dental benefits, according to Delta Dental.

Key takeaways
  • Implants replace missing teeth with fixed, natural-looking crowns or full arches.
  • Results include restored chewing, confident smiling and support for facial structure.
  • Treatment usually spans several months, longer if bone grafting is needed.
  • Single implant: $2,800 to $5,600 (Delta Dental); All-on-4 averages $15,176 per arch (CareCredit).
  • Good care keeps the after result lasting for many years.

What does a single implant look like before and after?

Before a single implant, there is usually a gap from a missing tooth or a damaged tooth that needs removing. The gap may affect your smile, cause neighbouring teeth to drift, and make chewing on that side harder.

After a single implant, a custom crown fills the space and matches the colour and shape of the surrounding teeth. When done well, the implant tooth is hard to tell apart from natural teeth. It is fixed in place, so you brush and floss it like your other teeth and chew normally. Our dental implant cost guide explains the full price of one tooth.

What do full-mouth implants look like before and after?

Before full-mouth implants, people often have many failing teeth, advanced gum disease or loose dentures that make eating and speaking difficult. Facial appearance may look sunken where bone and teeth have been lost.

After full-arch implants such as All-on-4, a fixed set of teeth is attached to four or more implants in each jaw. The teeth do not come out, the palate is not covered, and chewing ability is restored dramatically compared with conventional dentures. Many people describe the change as life-changing. CareCredit averages All-on-4 at $15,176 per arch; see the cost of full mouth dental implants.

Do dental implants change your face?

Dental implants can change your face by restoring support for the lips and cheeks and by preserving jawbone. After teeth are lost, the bone gradually shrinks, which can make the lower face look shorter and the lips fold inward.

Implants transmit chewing forces into the bone, which helps maintain its volume. Full-arch implant teeth also restore the height of the bite, filling out the lower face. The change is usually subtle for single implants and more noticeable for full-mouth cases.

How long does it take to go from before to after?

Going from before to after with dental implants usually takes several months. A typical single-implant timeline includes an extraction if needed, a healing period, implant placement, a further healing period while the bone bonds to the implant, and finally the crown.

Bone grafting adds months if the bone is too thin. Some cases allow an implant and a temporary tooth on the same day, especially for full-arch treatment, with final teeth placed after healing. Your surgeon will outline your specific timeline after a 3D scan, which CareCredit averages at $466.

What if you need a bone graft first?

If you need a bone graft first, the before-to-after journey is longer but often achieves a better final result. Grafts rebuild bone height or width so the implant has secure support and the crown sits at a natural-looking level.

CareCredit reports averages of $811 for an allograft and $2,779 for an autograft. The Cleveland Clinic's bone graft guide explains when grafts are needed and how they heal.

How do implants change eating and speaking?

Implants usually restore eating and speaking to near-natural function. Because implant teeth are anchored in bone, they do not slip, so you can bite into foods that are difficult with dentures and speak without worrying about teeth moving.

Healthline's dentures versus implants guide notes the improvement in chewing ability. People replacing a single tooth often notice they can chew evenly on both sides again.

How should you judge implant before and after photos?

When judging implant before and after photos, look at how natural the gumline looks around the implant crown, whether the crown matches neighbouring teeth in colour and shape, and whether photos show the result some time after treatment rather than on the day.

Ask your implant dentist for photos of cases similar to yours and how long ago they were completed. The Cleveland Clinic's prosthodontics guide explains how specialists design implant teeth for natural results.

How much do dental implants cost before and after insurance?

Dental implants cost $2,800 to $5,600 for a single tooth without dental benefits, according to Delta Dental. CareCredit reports a $2,143 average for a single full-size implant and $4,344 for a standard endosteal implant per tooth.

With insurance, many plans pay about 50% for major work up to the annual maximum, with $1,500 as Delta Dental's example in its coverage guide. See what insurance covers for dental implants and types of dental implants.

How do you keep implant results looking good?

You keep implant results looking good by caring for them like natural teeth: brushing twice daily, cleaning around the implant daily with floss, interdental brushes or a water flosser, and keeping regular dental cleanings and check-ups.

Avoid smoking, wear a night guard if you grind, and report any bleeding, swelling or looseness early. Peri-implantitis, an infection of the gum and bone around implants, is the most common cause of long-term problems and is largely preventable with good hygiene.

What are the alternatives if implants are not right for you?

If implants are not right for you because of health, bone or budget, alternatives include bridges and dentures. Healthline's implant versus bridge guide compares the options for a single missing tooth.

Many people start with a removable option and move to implants later when health or finances allow.

Who is a good candidate for an implant transformation?

A good candidate for an implant transformation has healthy gums, enough jawbone or is willing to have grafting, and is healthy enough for minor oral surgery. Adults of most ages can have implants; older age alone is not a barrier.

Some factors make implants riskier or need extra planning. Smoking slows healing and raises failure risk. Uncontrolled diabetes, some bone medications, previous radiation to the jaw and heavy grinding also need careful discussion. Active gum disease must be treated first, because the same bacteria can damage the tissue around implants.

Your implant dentist will review your medical history and medicines, take a 3D scan and explain what results are realistic for you. Being an ideal candidate gives the best chance that your after photo lasts for many years.

Frequently asked questions

What do dental implants look like before and after?

Before dental implants, there is a gap, a failing tooth or loose dentures. After treatment, a custom crown or full set of fixed teeth fills the space, matched in colour and shape to look natural. Well-done implant teeth are difficult to tell apart from natural teeth.

How long does it take to get dental implants from start to finish?

Getting dental implants usually takes several months from start to finish, because the implant must bond with the jawbone before the final crown is placed. Bone grafting can add more months. Some full-arch cases receive temporary teeth on the day of surgery.

How much do dental implants cost?

A single dental implant costs $2,800 to $5,600 without dental benefits, according to Delta Dental. CareCredit reports a $2,143 average for a single full-size implant and $15,176 for All-on-4 on one arch. Grafts and scans may add to the total.

Do implants change your face shape?

Implants can subtly improve facial shape by supporting the lips and cheeks and helping preserve jawbone. The change is more noticeable with full-mouth implants that restore bite height than with single implants.

Are implant results permanent?

Implants can last many years and often decades with good care, but the crown on top may need replacing after some years due to wear. Good oral hygiene, regular check-ups and avoiding smoking help keep results long-lasting.
