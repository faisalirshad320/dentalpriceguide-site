Root Canal Crown: How Much Does a Root Canal and Crown Cost?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 30, 20264 price sources
Quick answer

A root canal and crown are billed as separate procedures. The root canal alone costs about $1,200 for a front tooth, $1,300 for a premolar and $1,500 for a molar out-of-network, according to Delta Dental, which notes these fees do not include the final crown or filling. The crown, and often a core build-up, are added on top.

Key takeaways
  • The root canal and the crown are separate procedures with separate fees.
  • Root canal fees: about $1,200 (front), $1,300 (premolar), $1,500 (molar) out-of-network.
  • Back teeth usually need a crown; front teeth may need only a filling.
  • A core build-up is often billed between the root canal and the crown.
  • Insurance commonly covers 50%–80% of the root canal; crowns are often covered at a lower rate.

Why do you need a crown after a root canal?

You often need a crown after a root canal because the treated tooth is weaker than before. Most teeth that need root canals already have large cavities, fractures or big old fillings, and the access opening made during treatment removes more structure.

A root canal treated tooth no longer has a living pulp, so it can become more brittle over time. Back teeth such as molars and premolars carry heavy chewing forces, and without a crown they are at risk of cracking, sometimes so badly that the tooth cannot be saved. A crown covers and holds the tooth together, protecting the investment made in the root canal. The Cleveland Clinic's dental crowns guide explains how crowns protect teeth.

Do front teeth need a crown after a root canal?

Front teeth do not always need a crown after a root canal. If enough healthy tooth structure remains and the tooth is not heavily filled, a tooth-coloured filling to seal the access opening may be enough, because front teeth take less biting force than molars.

A front tooth may still need a crown if it was badly broken, heavily filled, or has darkened noticeably after the nerve died. In some cases internal whitening or a veneer can improve colour instead. Your dentist will recommend the least invasive restoration that will hold up.

What does the total cost of a root canal and crown include?

The total cost of a root canal and crown usually includes several separately billed items. Asking for each line on your estimate makes comparisons easier.

  • Exam and X-rays to diagnose the tooth.
  • Root canal, priced by tooth type.
  • Temporary filling, usually included in the root canal fee.
  • Core build-up or post, which rebuilds the tooth so the crown has something to hold onto.
  • Crown, priced by material such as porcelain, zirconia, metal or porcelain-fused-to-metal.
  • Follow-up X-ray to check healing.

Delta Dental is explicit that its root canal figures exclude the final crown or filling, so add the crown quote to the root canal quote to see your full bill.

How much is the root canal portion?

The root canal portion costs about $1,200 for a front tooth, about $1,300 for a premolar and about $1,500 for a molar out-of-network, according to Delta Dental's 2025 data. CareCredit reports averages of $984 for a front tooth and $1,337 for a molar, and Pearl's dental cost data gives a national range of $685 to $1,800.

Molars cost more because they have more canals. A specialist endodontist may charge more than a general dentist but is often recommended for complex teeth. See our root canal cost guide for the full breakdown.

Does the crown material change the total?

The crown material does change the total cost of a root canal and crown. All-ceramic and zirconia crowns are popular for their natural look and strength, porcelain-fused-to-metal crowns are a long-standing option, and gold or other metal crowns are very durable but visible.

Some offices make same-day ceramic crowns with in-office milling machines, saving a second visit and a temporary crown. Ask which material your dentist recommends for your tooth and why, and whether your insurance pays a different amount for different materials.

How soon after a root canal should you get a crown?

You should usually get a crown within a few weeks after a root canal. Until the crown is placed, the tooth is sealed only with a temporary filling and is vulnerable to cracking and to bacteria leaking back in.

Delaying the crown for months to save money can backfire. If the tooth fractures, it may need extraction, and replacing a missing tooth costs much more than the crown would have. If cost is the issue, ask about payment plans rather than waiting. Chew on the other side until the crown is in place.

How does insurance cover a root canal and crown?

Insurance usually covers the root canal and the crown at different rates. Delta Dental reports that insurance commonly covers 50%–80% of a root canal after the deductible. Crowns are frequently classified as major services, often covered at about 50%.

Both procedures count toward your annual maximum, which can be used up quickly by a root canal plus crown on one tooth. A pre-treatment estimate shows exactly what you will owe. Ask the office whether timing the crown into a new benefit year is safe for your tooth.

Is a root canal and crown worth the cost?

A root canal and crown is usually worth the cost when the tooth can be saved, because keeping a natural tooth avoids the higher cost and longer treatment of extraction followed by an implant or bridge. A well-restored root canal treated tooth can last many years.

What happens during root canal and crown treatment?

Root canal and crown treatment usually takes two or three visits spread over a few weeks. Knowing the sequence helps you plan time off and payments.

  1. Diagnosis: an exam, X-rays and tests confirm the pulp is inflamed or infected.
  2. Root canal visit: the tooth is numbed, the pulp is removed, the canals are cleaned, shaped and sealed, and a temporary filling closes the tooth. Complex molars sometimes need a second visit.
  3. Build-up and crown preparation: the dentist rebuilds missing structure, shapes the tooth and takes a scan or impression. A temporary crown goes on.
  4. Crown fitting: the permanent crown is checked for fit, colour and bite, then cemented.

Some offices combine the build-up with the root canal visit, and some make same-day crowns. Ask your dentist how many appointments your tooth will need and whether each visit is billed separately or as a package.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a root canal and crown?

A root canal and crown are billed separately. The root canal alone costs about $1,200 for a front tooth, $1,300 for a premolar and $1,500 for a molar out-of-network, according to Delta Dental, and the crown and any core build-up are added on top.

Do you always need a crown after a root canal?

You do not always need a crown after a root canal. Back teeth usually need one to prevent cracking, while front teeth with enough healthy structure may only need a filling. Your dentist decides based on how much tooth remains.

How long can you wait to get a crown after a root canal?

Most dentists recommend getting a crown within a few weeks after a root canal. Waiting longer leaves the tooth with only a temporary filling, raising the risk of fracture and reinfection. A cracked tooth may need extraction.

Does insurance cover a root canal and crown?

Many dental plans cover both, at different rates. Delta Dental reports root canals are commonly covered at 50%–80% after the deductible, while crowns are often major services covered at about 50%. Both count toward your annual maximum.

Why is the crown more expensive than the filling after a root canal?

A crown costs more than a filling because it is a custom-made restoration covering the whole tooth, often made by a dental lab from porcelain, zirconia or metal. A filling only seals the access opening and is placed directly in one visit.
