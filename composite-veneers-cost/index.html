How Much Do Composite Veneers Cost, and Are They Worth It?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 30, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

Composite veneers usually cost noticeably less than porcelain veneers, which run $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth without insurance according to Delta Dental. Composite veneers are made from tooth-coloured resin sculpted directly onto the teeth, often in one visit, and their price is closer to dental bonding, which averages $431 per tooth (CareCredit).

Key takeaways
  • Composite veneers are resin layers sculpted onto the front of teeth, often in one visit.
  • They usually cost less than porcelain veneers ($1,180 to $2,185 per tooth, Delta Dental).
  • Pricing tends to sit between dental bonding ($431 average) and porcelain veneers.
  • Composite stains and chips more easily and usually lasts fewer years than porcelain.
  • Insurance usually treats veneers as cosmetic and often does not cover them.

What are composite veneers?

Composite veneers are thin layers of tooth-coloured composite resin applied to the front surfaces of teeth to change their colour, shape or size. The dentist builds up the resin in layers, sculpts it by hand, hardens it with a curing light and polishes it to a natural shine.

Because composite veneers are usually made directly in the mouth, many cases are finished in a single appointment, without a dental lab. Some practices also offer indirect composite veneers made in a lab, which take two visits. Composite veneers often require little or no removal of tooth enamel, which makes them a more conservative and sometimes reversible choice.

Composite veneers are closely related to dental bonding. Bonding usually refers to repairing part of a tooth, such as a chip, while composite veneers cover the whole front surface of one or more teeth.

How much are composite veneers?

Composite veneers generally cost less than porcelain veneers per tooth. National survey data for composite veneers specifically is limited, so use the nearest published figures as guide rails: CareCredit reports a $431 average for dental bonding per tooth, and Delta Dental lists porcelain laminate veneers at $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth.

Composite veneer quotes usually fall somewhere between those two points, depending on how much resin is needed, how much artistry the case requires and the dentist's experience. A highly skilled cosmetic dentist who spends a long time layering colours to mimic natural enamel will usually charge more than a simple repair.

Ask for a per-tooth price and a total for the full set you need. See our veneers cost guide for porcelain pricing in more detail.

What affects the price of composite veneers?

Several factors affect the price of composite veneers. Knowing them helps you understand why quotes differ between practices.

  • Number of teeth: most smiles show six to ten upper teeth.
  • Direct or indirect: lab-made composite veneers involve extra fees.
  • Complexity: closing gaps, lengthening worn teeth or masking dark stains takes more time.
  • Dentist skill and location: cosmetic specialists and big-city practices often charge more.
  • Preparatory treatment: cleaning, whitening or fillings beforehand add to the total.
  • Maintenance: polishing and repairs over the years.

Are composite veneers better than porcelain veneers?

Composite veneers are not better or worse than porcelain overall; they suit different priorities. Composite veneers cost less, can be done quickly, usually need little enamel removal and are easy to repair. Porcelain veneers are stronger, keep their colour and gloss much longer and generally look more lifelike for many years.

Composite resin absorbs stains from coffee, tea, wine and tobacco over time and can chip at the edges. Porcelain resists staining and wear. For someone wanting a durable, long-term transformation, porcelain is usually the stronger choice. For someone on a budget, a younger patient, or someone wanting a reversible option, composite may make more sense.

How long do composite veneers last?

Composite veneers usually last fewer years than porcelain veneers, commonly around five to seven years before significant repair or replacement, though this varies widely. Good care can extend their life, while grinding and staining habits shorten it.

One advantage is that composite veneers can be repaired. A chip can often be fixed by adding new resin, and dull or stained surfaces can be re-polished. Over time, many people choose to replace composite veneers with porcelain. Read whether veneers are permanent for details on lifespan.

What happens when you get composite veneers?

Getting composite veneers usually takes a single visit of a few hours for several teeth. The dentist starts by agreeing the shape and shade with you, sometimes using a mock-up.

  1. The teeth are cleaned, and a shade of resin is chosen.
  2. The enamel surface is lightly roughened or etched so the resin bonds.
  3. Resin is applied in layers, shaped and hardened with a curing light.
  4. The dentist refines the shape, checks your bite and polishes the surface.

Local anesthetic is often not needed because little or no tooth is removed. You can usually eat normally the same day, though staining foods are best avoided for the first day or two.

How do you look after composite veneers?

You look after composite veneers much like natural teeth, with a few extra precautions. Brush twice daily with a soft brush and non-abrasive toothpaste, clean between teeth every day, and keep regular dental cleanings, where the hygienist should use polishing pastes safe for composite.

Limit dark staining drinks or rinse with water after them, avoid smoking, and do not bite hard objects such as ice, pens or fingernails. Wear a night guard if you grind. Many dentists recommend a professional polish once or twice a year to keep composite veneers looking glossy.

Does insurance cover composite veneers?

Insurance usually does not cover composite veneers when they are placed for cosmetic reasons. Delta Dental notes that veneers are usually treated as cosmetic and often not covered.

If a tooth is chipped or broken, insurance may cover a bonding repair as a restorative procedure, but not the extra cost of covering the whole front of several teeth for appearance. Ask for a pre-treatment estimate if part of your treatment repairs damage.

Are composite veneers worth it?

Composite veneers are worth it for many people who want a noticeable improvement at a lower upfront cost and with minimal tooth removal. They are a good fit for small chips, gaps, uneven edges and mild discolouration.

They are less suitable for people who grind heavily, for very dark stains, or for people wanting results that last as long as possible with little maintenance. Frequently asked questions

How much do composite veneers cost?

Composite veneers usually cost less per tooth than porcelain veneers, which Delta Dental lists at $1,180 to $2,185. Their price tends to sit between dental bonding, which CareCredit averages at $431 per tooth, and porcelain. Quotes depend on the number of teeth and complexity.

What are composite veneers?

Composite veneers are layers of tooth-coloured resin bonded and sculpted onto the front of teeth to change their shape, colour or size. They are usually made directly in the mouth in one visit and often need little or no removal of enamel.

How long do composite veneers last?

Composite veneers commonly last around five to seven years before needing major repair or replacement, although this varies. They stain and chip more easily than porcelain but can usually be repaired by adding or polishing resin.

Are composite veneers cheaper than porcelain?

Composite veneers are usually cheaper than porcelain veneers upfront because they are made directly in the mouth without a lab. Porcelain lasts longer and resists stains better, so long-term costs can be closer than the upfront price suggests.

Can composite veneers be removed?

Composite veneers can often be removed or replaced because they usually require little or no enamel removal. A dentist can polish them away and restore the natural tooth or replace them with porcelain veneers.
