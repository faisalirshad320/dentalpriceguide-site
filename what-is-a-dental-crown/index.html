What Is a Dental Crown? Types, the Crown Procedure and What Crowns Cost

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 21, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

A dental crown is a custom, tooth-shaped cap cemented over a damaged or weakened tooth to restore its shape, strength and appearance. Crowns are made from porcelain, ceramic, metal, resin or a combination, and they cover the whole visible part of the tooth above the gum line, as Cleveland Clinic explains.

Key takeaways
  • A dental crown is a cap that covers the entire visible part of a tooth and is cemented in place.
  • Crowns protect cracked, heavily filled, root canal–treated or worn teeth, and cap dental implants.
  • Common materials are porcelain or ceramic, zirconia, porcelain fused to metal, gold or other metal alloys, and resin.
  • The usual procedure takes two visits, with a temporary crown in between; some offices make same-day crowns.
  • Delta Dental lists a permanent crown at $1,100 to $2,000 out-of-network; insurance often covers 50% on a 100-80-50 plan.

What is a dental crown?

A dental crown is a hollow, tooth-shaped cover that fits over the whole visible part of a natural tooth or a dental implant and is cemented permanently in place. People often call a dental crown a cap. Once fitted, the crown becomes the new outer surface of the tooth that you bite and chew with.

A dental crown differs from a filling in how much of the tooth it replaces. A filling repairs one area of a tooth from the inside, while a crown wraps around the entire tooth. Dentists choose a crown when too little healthy tooth structure is left for a filling to hold, or when the tooth needs to be held together so it does not crack further.

A dental crown also differs from a veneer. A veneer is a thin shell bonded only to the front surface of a tooth for cosmetic reasons, while a crown covers every side and adds strength. Crowns are also a building block of other treatments: a bridge uses crowns on neighboring teeth to hold a replacement tooth, and an implant is finished with a crown on top.

Why would I need a crown on a tooth?

Dentists recommend a crown on a tooth when the tooth is too damaged or weakened for a filling, or when a tooth needs extra protection after treatment. The most common reasons include:

  • Large cavities or old fillings. When a filling would take up most of the tooth, a crown holds what remains together.
  • Cracked or broken teeth. A crown can stop a crack from spreading and protect the tooth from splitting.
  • After a root canal. Back teeth treated with a root canal become more brittle and are usually crowned to prevent fractures.
  • Worn teeth. Grinding or acid erosion can shorten teeth; crowns rebuild their height and shape.
  • Dental implants and bridges. An implant needs a crown as the visible tooth, and a bridge is anchored by crowns on the teeth beside a gap.
  • Appearance. Badly discolored or misshapen teeth are sometimes crowned, though less invasive options such as veneers or bonding may be considered first.

A crown is a decision your dentist makes after an exam and usually X-rays. Ask what the alternatives are for your specific tooth, including whether a large filling or an onlay could work instead.

What are the types of dental crowns?

The main types of dental crowns are porcelain or all-ceramic, zirconia, porcelain fused to metal, gold or other metal alloys, and resin. Each material balances appearance, strength, how much tooth must be removed and cost differently.

Porcelain and ceramic crowns look the most like natural enamel, so they are a common choice for visible teeth; our guide to front tooth crown cost covers this situation. Zirconia is a very strong ceramic often used on back teeth and for people who grind their teeth.

Porcelain fused to metal crowns have a metal inner layer for strength with porcelain on the outside. Gold and other metal crowns are very durable and need less tooth reduction, which is why they remain popular for back molars. If you are curious about a gold crown for appearance, see how much is a gold tooth.

Resin crowns cost less but wear down and break more easily, and resin is commonly used for temporary crowns worn between appointments. Your dentist will recommend a material based on where the tooth is, your bite and whether you grind.

What happens during the dental crown procedure?

The dental crown procedure usually takes two visits: one to prepare the tooth and take impressions, and a second to cement the permanent crown. Some offices can design and mill a ceramic crown in a single visit using digital scanning.

  1. Exam and numbing. The dentist checks the tooth, often with X-rays, and numbs the area with local anesthetic.
  2. Tooth preparation. A thin layer of the tooth is reshaped on all sides so the crown can fit over it. Decay is removed, and missing structure may be built up with filling material first.
  3. Impression or scan. A mold or digital scan of the prepared tooth is sent to a dental lab, or used in the office for a same-day crown.
  4. Temporary crown. A temporary crown, usually resin, protects the tooth while the lab makes the permanent one. Avoid sticky and hard foods on that side.
  5. Fitting and cementing. At the second visit, the dentist checks the fit, color and bite, then fixes the crown with dental cement. Dental cement for crowns is a permanent bonding material placed by the dentist; it is different from temporary cement sold in pharmacies for emergencies.

Mild sensitivity or gum tenderness for a few days after the crown procedure is common. Follow your dentist's instructions on eating and cleaning while the tooth settles.

How long do crowns on teeth last?

Crowns on teeth commonly last many years, often a decade or longer, when they are cared for well. How long a crown lasts depends on the material, your bite, grinding habits and how well you clean around the edge where the crown meets the tooth.

A crowned tooth can still decay at the gum line, so brush twice a day, clean between teeth daily and keep regular dental checkups. A night guard can protect crowns if you grind your teeth. Avoid chewing ice, hard candy or using your teeth to open packages.

See your dentist promptly if a crown feels loose, falls off, cracks, or if the tooth underneath becomes painful. Seek urgent care for facial swelling, fever or severe pain, which can signal an infection under the crown. Keep a crown that has come off and bring it to the appointment.

How much are crowns for teeth?

Crowns for teeth cost $1,100 to $2,000 for a permanent crown out-of-network, according to Delta Dental 2023 internal data. Material matters: CareCredit reports averages of $1,399 for a porcelain crown, $1,211 for a metallic crown and $697 for a resin-based or temporary crown.

The two sources measure different things, out-of-network claims versus a cost survey, and both are national figures; local prices vary. Our page on types of dental crowns and cost breaks the numbers down further.

Dental insurance usually treats crowns as a major procedure. On a common 100-80-50 plan, major procedures such as crowns are covered at 50%, according to Delta Dental, subject to deductibles, waiting periods and annual maximums. See tooth crown cost with insurance to estimate your share.

Frequently asked questions

Is getting a dental crown painful?

Getting a dental crown is usually not painful because the tooth is numbed with local anesthetic during preparation. Most people feel pressure and vibration rather than pain. Mild sensitivity or gum soreness for a few days afterward is common. Persistent pain or swelling is a reason to call your dentist.

How long does the dental crown procedure take?

The dental crown procedure usually takes two appointments a couple of weeks apart: one to prepare the tooth and take impressions, and one to cement the permanent crown. Offices with in-house milling equipment can sometimes place a ceramic crown in a single visit. Your dentist will explain which approach they use.

What is the difference between a crown and a filling?

A filling repairs one damaged area inside a tooth, while a dental crown covers the entire visible tooth like a cap. Dentists choose a crown when too little healthy tooth remains to support a filling, after most root canals on back teeth, or when a cracked tooth needs to be held together.

What dental cement is used for crowns?

Dentists fix permanent crowns with professional dental cements, such as resin or glass ionomer cements, chosen to suit the crown material. Pharmacy temporary cement is meant only as a short-term fix if a crown comes off. See your dentist to have a loose crown properly recemented.

Can a tooth with a crown still get a cavity?

A tooth with a crown can still get a cavity where the crown edge meets the natural tooth near the gum line. The crown itself does not decay, but the tooth underneath can. Brushing, daily cleaning between teeth and regular checkups help protect crowned teeth.

Does insurance cover dental crowns?

Dental insurance often covers part of a dental crown when it is needed for health reasons rather than appearance. On a common 100-80-50 plan, major procedures such as crowns are covered at 50%, per Delta Dental. Deductibles, waiting periods, annual maximums and replacement limits also apply.
