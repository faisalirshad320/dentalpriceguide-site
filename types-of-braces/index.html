Types of Braces and Their Cost: Which Option Is Right for You?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 31, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

The main types of braces are metal braces, ceramic braces, lingual braces, self-ligating braces and clear aligners. CareCredit reports average costs of $6,343 for metal, $5,834 for ceramic, $9,221 for lingual and $5,108 for clear aligners (CareCredit). The best type depends on your bite, your budget and how visible you want treatment to be.

Key takeaways
  • Metal braces are the most versatile and widely used option.
  • Ceramic braces blend in with the teeth but are more fragile.
  • Lingual braces are hidden behind the teeth and cost the most.
  • Clear aligners are removable and nearly invisible but rely on wearing them most of the day.
  • Self-ligating braces use clips instead of elastic ties.

What are metal braces?

Metal braces are the traditional type of braces, made of stainless-steel brackets bonded to the front of each tooth and connected by an archwire. Small elastic ties hold the wire in place, and kids and teens often choose coloured ties.

Metal braces can treat almost any orthodontic problem, from simple crowding to complex bite corrections. They are durable, rarely break compared with ceramic, and are usually the most affordable fixed option. CareCredit reports an average of $6,343, and Delta Dental cites $3,000 to $7,000 for traditional metal braces. The main downside is visibility. What are ceramic braces?

Ceramic braces work just like metal braces but use clear or tooth-coloured brackets that blend with the teeth. From a distance, they are much less noticeable, which appeals to adults and older teens.

Ceramic brackets are more brittle than metal and can chip or break, and the elastic ties can stain from coffee, tea or curry between appointments. Treatment time is similar to metal for most cases. CareCredit reports an average of $5,834 for ceramic braces. Our clear braces guide compares ceramic braces and aligners in detail.

What are lingual braces?

Lingual braces are fixed braces bonded to the back of the teeth, facing the tongue, so they are hidden from view. They are usually custom-made for each tooth.

Lingual braces cost the most of the common types: CareCredit reports an average of $9,221 and Delta Dental cites $8,000 to $10,000. They can irritate the tongue and cause a temporary lisp, and not every orthodontist offers them. They suit people who want fixed braces that nobody will see.

What are self-ligating braces?

Self-ligating braces use brackets with a built-in clip or door that holds the archwire, instead of elastic ties. They come in metal or ceramic versions.

Without elastic ties, there are fewer places for plaque to collect and adjustment visits can be a little quicker. Some practices suggest they create less friction, though whether they shorten treatment depends on the case. Self-ligating braces may cost a little more than conventional brackets at some practices; ask your orthodontist whether they offer an advantage in your situation.

What are clear aligners?

Clear aligners are a series of custom-made, removable plastic trays that gradually move teeth. Each set is worn for one to two weeks before switching to the next.

Aligners are nearly invisible and can be removed to eat, brush and floss, which many adults prefer. They only work if worn most of the day, usually around 20 to 22 hours, so they require discipline. Some complex bite problems are harder to treat with aligners alone. CareCredit reports an average of $5,108 for clear aligners.

How do the types of braces compare?

The types of braces differ mainly in visibility, cost, comfort and the range of problems they treat well.

  • Most affordable fixed option: metal braces.
  • Least visible fixed option: lingual braces.
  • Least visible overall: clear aligners or lingual braces.
  • Best for complex cases: metal or ceramic fixed braces.
  • Easiest cleaning: clear aligners, because they come out.
  • Most durable: metal braces.

Cost averages overlap between types, so the best way to compare is to get written quotes for the same treatment goal. Our main guide to how much braces cost covers all options.

How do you choose the right type of braces?

Choosing the right type of braces starts with what your teeth need, then what you prefer. Your orthodontist will tell you which options can achieve a good result for your bite after an exam and records.

  1. Ask which types can treat your specific problem well.
  2. Decide how much visibility matters to you.
  3. Consider your lifestyle: aligners need discipline; fixed braces need food restrictions.
  4. Compare total costs, including retainers.
  5. Check what your insurance covers.

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first orthodontic check no later than age 7, as Delta Dental notes.

Does insurance pay the same for every type of braces?

Insurance usually pays the same orthodontic benefit regardless of the type of braces you choose. If you select a more expensive type, such as lingual braces, you typically pay the difference yourself.

Orthodontic benefits often have a separate lifetime maximum, and many adult plans exclude orthodontics. Delta Dental's coverage guide explains how benefits work, and Pearl lists comprehensive orthodontic treatment at $3,680 to $9,650 nationally. What other orthodontic appliances might be used with braces?

Other orthodontic appliances are sometimes used alongside braces to correct jaw or bite problems. These include palatal expanders that widen a narrow upper jaw, headgear that guides jaw growth in children, rubber bands (elastics) that adjust the bite, and temporary anchorage devices, tiny screws that help move teeth precisely.

Space maintainers keep room for adult teeth in children who lose baby teeth early. After treatment, retainers, either removable or bonded behind the teeth, hold the result. Healthline's braces cost guide notes that the need for such appliances can affect the overall fee.

Which types of braces suit kids, teens and adults?

The types of braces that suit kids, teens and adults differ mainly because of lifestyle and responsibility rather than biology. Children usually do best with metal braces, which are durable, affordable and work without relying on the child to wear anything. Many children also enjoy choosing coloured ties at each visit.

Teenagers often choose between metal, ceramic and clear aligners. Aligners can work very well for responsible teens with suitable cases, but lost trays and low wear time are common problems. Ceramic braces give a less visible fixed option for teens who want to avoid metal.

Adults more often choose ceramic braces, lingual braces or clear aligners because of work and social settings. Adults can also be more consistent with aligner wear. Whatever the age, the orthodontist's assessment of the bite comes first, and appearance preferences come second. A type that cannot correct your bite well is not a bargain, even if it is cheaper or less visible.

Frequently asked questions

What are the different types of braces?

The main types of braces are metal braces, ceramic braces, lingual braces behind the teeth, self-ligating braces with built-in clips, and clear aligners. Each moves teeth with gentle pressure but differs in visibility, cost, comfort and the problems it treats best.

Which type of braces is cheapest?

Metal braces are usually among the most affordable fixed options, averaging $6,343 according to CareCredit, while clear aligners average $5,108. Delta Dental cites $3,000 to $7,000 for traditional metal braces. Your quote depends on your case.

Which type of braces is the least noticeable?

Lingual braces, which sit behind the teeth, and clear aligners are the least noticeable types of braces. Ceramic braces blend in with tooth colour but are still visible up close. Lingual braces cost the most, averaging $9,221 per CareCredit.

Which braces work fastest?

No type of braces is reliably fastest for every case, because tooth movement is limited by biology. Fixed braces work around the clock, while aligners work only when worn. Following instructions and avoiding broken brackets matter more than the type.

Can I switch types of braces during treatment?

Switching types of braces during treatment is sometimes possible, such as moving from metal to aligners for finishing, but it may add cost and time. Discuss it with your orthodontist, who can explain whether it would affect your result.
