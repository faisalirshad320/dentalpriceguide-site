How Much Do Dentures Cost Without Insurance?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 6, 20264 price sources
Quick answer

Without insurance, a full denture costs about $1,115 to $2,425 for a lower arch and $1,220 to $2,540 for an upper arch out-of-network, according to Delta Dental. CareCredit reports averages for upper and lower sets of $452 for basic, $1,968 for traditional and $6,514 for premium dentures.

Key takeaways
  • Full dentures: $1,115 to $2,425 (lower) and $1,220 to $2,540 (upper) out-of-network.
  • Partial dentures: $1,425 to $2,490 (lower) and $1,435 to $2,785 (upper).
  • Upper and lower sets average $452 basic, $1,968 traditional, $6,514 premium (CareCredit).
  • Implant-supported full dentures cost $10,500 to $21,500 without insurance (GoodRx).
  • Relines, repairs and replacements add to lifetime costs.

Why do basic and premium dentures cost so differently?

Basic and premium dentures cost differently because of the materials, the number of appointments and the level of customisation. Basic dentures often use stock teeth and simpler fabrication, with fewer fitting steps. They can be functional but may look less natural and fit less precisely.

Premium dentures use higher-quality teeth with more lifelike shapes and shading, more detailed gum contouring and additional try-in visits to perfect fit, bite and appearance. They usually look more natural and can be more comfortable, but the price is much higher. Traditional mid-range dentures balance the two.

What do other types of dentures cost?

Other types of dentures carry different prices. GoodRx's denture cost guide lists an immediate full denture at about $1,900, placed on the day teeth are extracted, and a flexible nylon partial at about $1,700.

Implant-supported full dentures, which clip onto implants for far better stability, cost $10,500 to $21,500 without insurance according to GoodRx. See snap in dentures, flexible dentures and partial dentures for details.

What other costs come with dentures?

Other costs that often come with dentures include an exam and X-rays, extractions of any remaining teeth, bone smoothing, and follow-up adjustments. Some practices include adjustments in the denture fee; others charge per visit.

Over time, dentures need relines as the gums and bone change shape; GoodRx notes a chairside reline costs about $350 or more. Repairs for cracks or broken teeth, and eventual replacement every several years, add to the lifetime cost. Ask what is included in your quote.

How can you get cheaper dentures without insurance?

You can get cheaper dentures without insurance in several ways.

  • Dental schools offer reduced fees with supervised students.
  • Community health centers may have sliding-scale fees.
  • Discount dental plans reduce fees at participating dentists.
  • Payment plans or financing spread the cost; read interest terms carefully.
  • HSA or FSA funds let you pay with pre-tax money.
  • Compare itemised quotes, including what adjustments are covered.
  • Choose a mid-range option rather than premium if appearance is less critical.

Do Medicare or Medicaid cover dentures?

Original Medicare generally does not cover dentures. Some Medicare Advantage plans include dental benefits that may help with dentures, often with limits.

Medicaid dental coverage for adults varies by state; some states cover dentures, while others offer only emergency dental care. Check your state's Medicaid dental benefits directly. Veterans may be eligible for dental care through the VA depending on their circumstances.

Are cheap dentures worth it?

Cheap dentures can be worth it for people who need an affordable, functional solution, especially as a first or temporary set. However, very low-cost dentures may fit less precisely, look less natural and need more adjustments or earlier replacement.

Poorly fitting dentures can cause sore spots, difficulty eating and faster bone loss. When budget allows, a mid-range denture from a dentist who includes follow-up adjustments often gives better long-term value. The Cleveland Clinic's prosthodontics guide explains how dentures are made and fitted.

Are dentures cheaper than implants?

Dentures are much cheaper than implants upfront. Implant-supported dentures cost $10,500 to $21,500 according to GoodRx, compared with a conventional full denture at a fraction of that price.

Implants offer better stability, chewing and bone preservation. Healthline's dentures versus implants guide compares the options. Many people start with conventional dentures and add implants later.

What is the process of getting dentures?

Getting dentures usually takes several visits over a few weeks. The dentist takes impressions or scans, records your bite, and arranges a try-in with the teeth set in wax so you can check appearance and fit before the final denture is made.

After delivery, expect a few follow-up visits to relieve sore spots and fine-tune the fit. Adjusting to new dentures takes a few weeks for most people.

What is the lifetime cost of dentures?

The lifetime cost of dentures is higher than the first set's price, because dentures need ongoing care and eventual replacement. As the jawbone and gums shrink after teeth are lost, dentures loosen and need relining to restore their fit; GoodRx notes a chairside reline costs about $350 or more.

Cracked bases, broken or worn teeth and lost dentures need repair or replacement. Most dentures are replaced every several years as they wear and the mouth changes. When comparing a cheaper denture with a more expensive one, ask how often each is likely to need relines and replacement, and whether adjustments are included in the first year.

Implant-supported dentures cost more upfront but help preserve bone, which can reduce how often the fit changes. For some people, that stability is worth the extra cost over many years.

How do you make dentures last longer?

You can make dentures last longer with careful daily care. Remove and rinse them after meals, brush them daily with a soft denture brush and a denture cleaner rather than regular toothpaste, and soak them overnight unless your dentist advises otherwise. Handle them over a folded towel or a basin of water so they do not break if dropped.

Avoid hot water, which can warp the base, and never try to adjust or repair dentures yourself. Keep regular dental visits so the fit and the health of your gums can be checked, and book a reline as soon as dentures start to slip or cause sore spots.

Frequently asked questions

How much do dentures cost without insurance?

What is the cheapest type of dentures?

Basic dentures are usually the cheapest, averaging $452 for an upper and lower set according to CareCredit. They use simpler materials and fewer fitting steps, so they may look less natural and need more adjustments than traditional or premium dentures.

How much do partial dentures cost without insurance?

Partial dentures cost $1,425 to $2,490 for a lower and $1,435 to $2,785 for an upper out-of-network, according to Delta Dental. GoodRx lists a flexible nylon partial at about $1,700.

Does Medicare pay for dentures?

Original Medicare generally does not pay for dentures. Some Medicare Advantage plans include dental benefits that may help. Medicaid coverage for adult dentures varies by state.

How often do dentures need to be replaced?

Dentures usually need replacing every several years, with relines in between as the gums and bone change shape. Signs you need a reline or new dentures include slipping, sore spots and difficulty chewing.
