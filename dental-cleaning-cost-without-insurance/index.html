How much does a teeth cleaning cost without insurance?

A routine teeth cleaning without insurance costs about $85 to $160, according to Delta Dental's cost guide. A routine cleaning (prophylaxis) is the scaling and polishing done at a regular check-up visit for someone whose gums are generally healthy.

The cleaning fee is rarely the whole bill. Most offices also charge for an exam by the dentist and, at some visits, X-rays. CareCredit reports a national average of $203 for a routine exam, with a range of $50 to $350, plus $65 on average for bitewing X-rays and $226 for full-mouth X-rays. Practices that quote a single "cleaning visit" price often combine these.

These are national averages. Fees in large cities and high-cost areas tend to be higher, and small-town or community clinics are often lower. Our main dental cleaning cost guide covers pricing with and without coverage.

What do you get for the price of a dental cleaning?

A routine dental cleaning pays for a hygienist's time removing plaque and hardened tartar from your teeth, both above the gumline and just below it. Tartar cannot be removed with a toothbrush once it forms, so this is the part of the visit you cannot do yourself at home.

A typical routine cleaning visit includes several steps:

  1. Gum check: the hygienist looks at your gums and may measure the depth of the pockets around each tooth.
  2. Scaling: hand instruments or an ultrasonic scaler remove plaque and tartar.
  3. Polishing: a gritty paste and a rotating cup remove surface stains.
  4. Flossing: the hygienist flosses between all teeth to clear remaining debris.
  5. Fluoride: some offices apply fluoride varnish, sometimes for an extra fee.
  6. Exam: the dentist checks for cavities, gum problems and signs of oral cancer, often with X-rays.

When you compare prices between offices, check that each quote covers the same steps. A low cleaning price paired with a high exam fee can end up costing the same as a higher bundled price elsewhere.

If the hygienist finds heavy tartar or bleeding, deep gum pockets, the office may recommend a different, higher-priced cleaning. You can ask to see the pocket measurements and X-rays before agreeing to it, and you can ask whether the work can be split across visits to spread the cost.

Why do dental cleaning prices vary so much?

Dental cleaning prices vary because offices set their own fees and because different mouths need different kinds of cleaning. The same visit can cost noticeably more in one zip code than another.

  • Type of cleaning: a routine cleaning costs least; a full mouth debridement, periodontal maintenance or deep cleaning costs more.
  • New versus returning patient: a first visit usually includes a comprehensive exam and a full X-ray set.
  • Location: rent, wages and local competition shape fees.
  • Extras: fluoride treatment, oral cancer screening tools or sealants may be added.

Ask the front desk for the exact procedure codes they plan to bill and the fee for each before the appointment. That turns a vague estimate into a firm number.

What if you need a deep cleaning instead?

A deep cleaning (scaling and root planing) costs more than a routine cleaning because it treats gum disease below the gumline and is priced per quadrant. Delta Dental lists $180 to $295 per quadrant, and CareCredit reports an average of $242 per quadrant.

A full mouth has four quadrants, so a deep cleaning on all of them costs several times more than one routine cleaning. Sometimes only one or two quadrants need it. Before a deep cleaning, a dentist will usually measure gum pocket depths and take X-rays to show why it is needed.

Two other cleaning types sit in between. A full mouth debridement ($150 to $260, per Delta Dental) removes heavy tartar so the dentist can examine the gums properly. Periodontal maintenance ($140 to $220) is the ongoing cleaning for people already treated for gum disease. See our guide to deep cleaning teeth cost for details.

How can you get a cheaper dental cleaning without insurance?

The easiest way to pay less for a dental cleaning without insurance is to look for a practice or program built for self-pay patients. Several options are widely available.

  • New-patient specials: many offices advertise a bundled exam, X-rays and cleaning for first visits.
  • In-house membership plans: a yearly fee often covers two cleanings and exams plus a discount on other work.
  • Dental hygiene and dental schools: supervised students provide cleanings at lower fees, though visits take longer.
  • Community health centers: many charge on a sliding scale based on income.
  • Discount dental plans: these are not insurance, but they give reduced fees at participating offices.

Skipping cleanings to save money often costs more later. Tartar build-up and gum disease can lead to deep cleanings, fillings or tooth loss that cost far more than routine care.

How often should you get your teeth cleaned?

Most adults with healthy gums get a professional cleaning once or twice a year, and many dentists recommend every six months. People with gum disease, diabetes, heavy tartar build-up or a history of frequent cavities may be advised to come more often.

Your dentist sets the right interval for you based on your gums, your cavity risk and your general health. Delta Dental's cleaning guide describes what happens during a visit. If you are budgeting for a full check-up, see how much a dental check-up costs.

Is dental insurance worth it just for cleanings?

Dental insurance is often worth it for people who expect to need more than cleanings, because most plans cover preventive care at or near 100% after enrollment. If you only need two cleanings a year, compare the yearly premium against the self-pay cost of those visits.

Delta Dental's coverage guide explains the common 100-80-50 plan structure. Check for waiting periods and annual maximums before you buy a plan, and do the arithmetic for your own expected care.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a teeth cleaning without insurance?

A routine teeth cleaning without insurance typically costs $85 to $160, according to Delta Dental. Many offices also charge separately for an exam and X-rays, so a first visit usually costs more than the cleaning alone. Fees vary by city and practice.

Why is my cleaning bill higher than the quoted cleaning price?

A cleaning bill is often higher than the cleaning price because the exam and X-rays are separate charges. CareCredit reports a $203 national average for a routine exam and $65 for bitewing X-rays. A deep cleaning or debridement also costs more than a routine cleaning.

How much is a deep cleaning without insurance?

A deep cleaning without insurance is priced per quadrant of the mouth. Delta Dental lists $180 to $295 per quadrant, and CareCredit reports a $242 average per quadrant. The total depends on how many quadrants need treatment.

Can I get a free or cheap dental cleaning?

Low-cost dental cleanings are available at dental hygiene schools, dental schools and community health centers with sliding-scale fees. Many private offices also run new-patient specials or in-house membership plans that bundle cleanings and exams at a reduced price.

Is a dental cleaning necessary every six months?

A dental cleaning every six months is a common recommendation for adults, but the right interval depends on your gum health and cavity risk. People with gum disease may need cleanings more often. Your dentist will recommend a schedule after examining you.
