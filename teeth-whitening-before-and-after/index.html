Teeth Whitening Before and After: What Results to Expect and What It Costs

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 6, 20262 price sources
Quick answer

Teeth whitening before and after results usually show teeth several shades lighter, with the biggest change on yellowish surface and age-related stains. Grey or very dark stains and existing crowns or fillings change less or not at all. Professional in-office bleaching costs $40 to $700 per visit out-of-network, according to Delta Dental.

Key takeaways
  • Most people see teeth several shades lighter after whitening.
  • Yellow stains respond best; grey stains and restorations respond poorly.
  • Professional whitening tends to give bigger, faster results than over-the-counter products.
  • Results fade over months to years depending on habits.
  • Whitening is usually not covered by insurance.

How much whiter will teeth get after whitening?

Most people see their teeth become several shades lighter after whitening. The exact change depends on your starting shade, the type of stains, the strength of the product and how long it is used.

Before and after photos in advertising often show the most dramatic cases under ideal lighting. A realistic expectation is a noticeably brighter, fresher smile rather than a dramatic change for everyone. Your dentist can measure your shade before and after with a shade guide so you can see the actual difference.

Which stains change most in before and after photos?

Yellowish stains change most in before and after photos. Surface stains from coffee, tea, wine and tobacco, and the gradual yellowing that comes with age, usually respond well to peroxide whitening, as the Cleveland Clinic explains.

Brownish stains often improve but may take longer. Greyish stains, such as those from certain antibiotics taken in childhood or a tooth that darkened after injury, respond poorly to whitening and may need bonding, veneers or internal whitening by a dentist. White spots can sometimes look more noticeable at first after whitening, though they often blend in as the rest of the tooth lightens.

How do before and after results differ by method?

Before and after results differ by whitening method mainly in speed and strength.

  • In-office whitening: uses stronger peroxide under professional supervision; a noticeable change often appears after one visit.
  • Custom take-home trays: gradual whitening over one to two weeks, often with results similar to in-office treatment.
  • Whitening strips: visible improvement over a course of days to weeks, usually more modest.
  • Whitening toothpaste: removes surface stains; results are subtle.

Why don't crowns and fillings change after whitening?

Crowns, veneers, bonding and fillings do not change after whitening because porcelain and composite materials do not respond to peroxide. Only natural tooth enamel and dentin lighten.

In before and after photos, restorations on front teeth may suddenly look darker than the newly whitened teeth around them. If you have visible restorations, ask your dentist whether you will need to replace them after whitening to match, and budget for that.

How should you judge before and after whitening photos?

When judging before and after whitening photos, check that both images use the same lighting, angle and camera, and ideally a shade guide held next to the teeth. Bright lights, filters and editing can exaggerate results.

Ask your dentist to take standardised photos before and after your own treatment. That gives you an honest record of the change and helps decide when touch-ups are needed.

Will whitening make teeth sensitive?

Whitening often causes temporary sensitivity, especially with stronger products. Teeth may feel sensitive to cold or air for a day or two after a session.

Using a desensitising toothpaste before and during whitening, choosing lower-strength products and spacing sessions out can reduce discomfort. People with very sensitive teeth should talk to a dentist first. The best teeth whitening toothpaste guide covers gentler maintenance options.

How do you keep your after results?

You keep whitening after results by limiting staining foods and drinks, especially in the first 48 hours, rinsing with water after coffee, tea or wine, avoiding tobacco and brushing twice daily.

Results usually last from a few months to a few years. Periodic touch-ups with trays or strips can maintain your shade. Regular professional cleanings also remove new surface stains and help teeth look brighter between touch-ups.

How much does teeth whitening cost?

Professional teeth whitening costs $40 to $700 per visit for in-office bleaching out-of-network, according to Delta Dental. CareCredit reports averages of $792 for laser whitening and $583 for UV whitening.

Delta Dental notes whitening is usually not covered by insurance because it is cosmetic. Over-the-counter options cost less but generally give smaller results. See our teeth whitening cost guide.

What if whitening does not give the result you want?

If whitening does not give the result you want, other cosmetic options can help. Bonding and veneers can cover stains that do not respond to bleaching, and internal whitening can lighten a single dark tooth after a root canal.

The Cleveland Clinic's cosmetic dentistry overview describes these options. A dentist can recommend the best approach for your stain type.

Who gets the best before and after whitening results?

The best before and after whitening results usually come from people with healthy teeth and gums, natural teeth on the front of the smile rather than crowns or veneers, and yellowish staining from food, drink or age. These stains break down readily with peroxide.

People who see less change include those with grey or bluish discoloration, very translucent enamel, many front-tooth restorations, or a single dark tooth after an injury or root canal. For them, a combination of whitening and other cosmetic treatments often works better.

Before whitening, a dental check-up rules out cavities, cracks and gum recession that could make whitening uncomfortable. A cleaning beforehand also removes surface tartar and stain, so the whitening gel reaches the enamel evenly and the after result looks more uniform.

How quickly do before and after changes appear?

Before and after changes appear at different speeds depending on the method. In-office whitening often shows a visible difference after a single session. Custom trays and strips usually show gradual improvement over several days, with the full result after the recommended course.

Teeth can look slightly whiter immediately after whitening because they are temporarily dehydrated. The shade usually settles a little over the following day or two, which is the more accurate after result to compare.

Frequently asked questions

How many shades whiter will my teeth get?

Most people see teeth several shades lighter after whitening, but results vary with your starting shade, stain type, product strength and how long you whiten. Yellowish stains improve most; grey stains and restorations change little.

Does teeth whitening work on all stains?

Teeth whitening works best on yellowish surface and age-related stains. Brown stains often improve more slowly, while grey stains from certain medications or injury respond poorly and may need bonding, veneers or internal whitening.

Will my crowns and fillings whiten too?

Crowns, veneers, bonding and fillings do not whiten because the materials do not respond to peroxide. After whitening, they may look darker than your natural teeth and may need replacing to match.

How long do teeth whitening results last?

Teeth whitening results usually last from a few months to a few years, depending on the method and habits. Limiting coffee, tea, wine and tobacco and doing periodic touch-ups helps results last longer.

Is professional whitening better than at-home whitening?

Professional whitening usually gives faster and more noticeable results because it uses stronger products under supervision. Custom take-home trays from a dentist can give similar results over a couple of weeks. Over-the-counter options are cheaper but usually more modest.
