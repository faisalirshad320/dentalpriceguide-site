Veneers vs Implants: Which Do You Need, and How Do They Compare?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 5, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

Veneers and implants solve different problems. A veneer is a thin shell bonded to the front of an existing tooth to change its colour or shape, while an implant replaces a missing tooth with a post in the jawbone and a crown. You cannot put a veneer where a tooth is missing. Porcelain veneers cost $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth without insurance, according to Delta Dental.

Key takeaways
  • Veneers improve the appearance of existing, healthy teeth.
  • Implants replace teeth that are missing or need extracting.
  • A veneer cannot be placed where there is no tooth.
  • Many smile makeovers combine implants for gaps and veneers for remaining teeth.
  • Veneers cost $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth (Delta Dental) and are usually not covered by insurance.

What is the difference between veneers and implants?

The main difference between veneers and implants is what they replace. A veneer covers only the front surface of a tooth you already have, changing its colour, shape or length. The natural tooth, roots and nerve stay in place underneath.

An implant replaces an entire tooth, including the root. A titanium or zirconia post is placed in the jawbone, the bone bonds to it over several months, and then a crown is attached. Implants are used when a tooth is missing or must be removed because it cannot be saved. Our guide to what veneers are explains veneer types and materials.

When are veneers the right choice?

Veneers are the right choice when your teeth are healthy and structurally sound, but you want to improve their appearance. Typical reasons include:

  • Stains that whitening cannot remove.
  • Chipped or worn edges.
  • Small gaps between front teeth.
  • Slightly misshapen or uneven teeth.
  • Minor crookedness when orthodontics is not wanted.

Veneers are not suitable for teeth with large cavities, severe damage or gum disease, which need to be treated first. See veneers before and after for typical results.

When is an implant the right choice?

An implant is the right choice when a tooth is missing, or when a tooth is so damaged, cracked or infected that it cannot be saved. Implants restore both the look and the function of a tooth and help preserve the jawbone where the tooth was lost.

Implants require enough healthy bone, or a bone graft, and general health suitable for minor surgery. Smoking, uncontrolled diabetes and some medicines raise the risk of complications. Your dentist will assess these factors before recommending an implant.

Can you have veneers and implants together?

You can have veneers and implants together, and many smile makeovers combine them. For example, a person missing one front tooth might have an implant to fill the gap and veneers on the neighbouring teeth so the colour and shape match perfectly across the smile.

Planning is important. The implant crown and the veneers are usually made at the same time by the same lab, so the shades match. If whitening is part of the plan, it is done before the restorations are made, because neither veneers nor implant crowns respond to whitening.

What if a front tooth is damaged but still there?

If a front tooth is damaged but still there, a veneer or a crown may save it without needing an implant. Small chips and stains can be fixed with a veneer or bonding. Larger breaks often need a crown, which covers the whole tooth for strength.

An implant is only considered if the tooth cannot be restored, for example if it is fractured below the gumline or has a failed root canal that cannot be retreated. Keeping a natural tooth is generally preferred when it is possible.

Which costs more, veneers or implants?

Comparing the cost of veneers and implants is not like-for-like, because they solve different problems. A porcelain veneer costs $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth without insurance according to Delta Dental, and CareCredit reports a $1,765 average for a single porcelain veneer and $15,486 for a full set of six to eight.

Implant treatment usually costs more per tooth because it involves surgery, a post, an abutment and a crown, and sometimes a bone graft. If cost is a concern for cosmetic changes, dental bonding averages $431 per tooth according to CareCredit. See our veneers cost guide.

How does insurance treat veneers and implants?

Insurance treats veneers and implants differently. Delta Dental notes that veneers are usually considered cosmetic and often not covered. Implants are covered by some plans as a major service, usually with a percentage paid up to the annual maximum, while other plans exclude them.

Ask for a pre-treatment estimate for any combined treatment. Restoring a missing or broken tooth is more likely to be covered than purely cosmetic work.

How long do veneers and implants last?

Veneers and implants both last many years with good care, but in different ways. Porcelain veneers commonly last 10 to 15 years or more before replacement. An implant post can last decades, while the crown on top may need replacing after some years of wear.

Both require good oral hygiene, regular check-ups and protection from grinding. Read whether veneers are permanent for more on veneer lifespan.

How do you decide between them?

Deciding between veneers and implants starts with one question: is the tooth there and healthy enough to keep? If yes, cosmetic options such as whitening, bonding or veneers are considered. If the tooth is missing or cannot be saved, replacement options such as an implant, bridge or denture are considered.

A dentist experienced in cosmetic and restorative work can plan a combined approach if you need both. Healthline's veneers guide and the Cleveland Clinic's cosmetic dentistry overview offer more background.

How do the treatment processes compare?

The treatment processes for veneers and implants differ a great deal in time and complexity. Porcelain veneers usually take two or three visits over a few weeks: a consultation and planning visit, a preparation visit where a thin layer of enamel is removed and impressions or scans are taken, and a final visit to bond the veneers in place.

An implant usually takes several months. If the tooth is still present, it is extracted first, sometimes with a bone graft. The implant post is placed in a minor surgical procedure, then the bone needs time to bond to it. Finally, an abutment and crown are attached. A temporary tooth can usually be worn during healing so you are not left with a visible gap.

If you need both, your dentist will plan the sequence so the final veneers and implant crown are made together and match.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between veneers and implants?

Veneers are thin shells bonded to the front of existing teeth to improve their appearance. Implants replace missing teeth with a post in the jawbone and a crown. Veneers need a natural tooth to attach to; implants are used when a tooth is gone.

Can you get veneers instead of implants?

You cannot get veneers instead of implants for a missing tooth, because a veneer must bond to an existing tooth. If the tooth is present and healthy, a veneer may improve its look. If it is missing, an implant, bridge or denture is needed.

Are veneers cheaper than implants?

Veneers are usually cheaper per tooth than implants. Delta Dental lists porcelain veneers at $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth. Implants involve surgery, a post, abutment and crown, and sometimes a bone graft, which generally costs more.

Can you have veneers and implants at the same time?

You can have veneers and implants as part of the same treatment plan. An implant fills a gap, and veneers on neighbouring teeth match its colour and shape. They are usually made by the same lab so the shades match.

Does insurance cover veneers or implants?

Insurance usually does not cover veneers because they are cosmetic, according to Delta Dental. Some plans cover implants as a major service up to the annual maximum, while others exclude them. Request a pre-treatment estimate.
