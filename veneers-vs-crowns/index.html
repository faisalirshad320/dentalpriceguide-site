Veneers vs Crowns: Key Differences, Cost and How to Choose

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 28, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

A veneer is a thin shell bonded to the front of a tooth, mainly to improve appearance, while a crown is a cap that covers the entire tooth to protect and strengthen it. Veneers suit healthy front teeth with cosmetic issues; crowns suit teeth that are broken, heavily filled or root canal treated. A porcelain veneer costs $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth (Delta Dental).

Key takeaways
  • Veneers cover only the front surface; crowns cover the whole tooth.
  • Veneers remove less tooth structure than crowns.
  • Veneers are mainly cosmetic; crowns restore damaged or weakened teeth.
  • Veneers are usually not covered by insurance; crowns often are when medically needed.
  • A porcelain veneer costs $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth (Delta Dental), $1,765 on average (CareCredit).

What is the difference between veneers and crowns?

The main difference between veneers and crowns is how much of the tooth they cover. A veneer is a thin layer of porcelain or composite resin bonded to the front surface of a tooth. A crown is a thicker restoration that covers the entire visible part of the tooth, front, back and biting surface.

That difference drives everything else. Veneers change the colour, shape and size of front teeth and preserve more natural tooth. Crowns hold together teeth that are cracked, broken, heavily filled or weakened by a root canal, and can restore biting function on back teeth.

Which removes more tooth structure, a veneer or a crown?

A crown removes more tooth structure than a veneer. To fit a crown, the dentist shapes the tooth on all sides so the crown can slide over it, which typically removes a substantial amount of enamel and sometimes dentin.

A traditional porcelain veneer requires removing a thin layer of enamel from the front of the tooth only. Some minimal-prep veneers remove even less. Because enamel does not grow back, veneers are still a permanent decision, but they are more conservative than crowns. Dentists generally prefer the least invasive option that achieves the goal.

When is a veneer the better choice?

A veneer is usually the better choice when the tooth is healthy and strong and the problem is mainly cosmetic. Typical reasons for veneers include:

  • Discoloration that whitening cannot fix, such as tetracycline staining.
  • Chips or worn edges on front teeth.
  • Small gaps between front teeth.
  • Slightly misshapen or uneven teeth.
  • Minor crookedness, when orthodontics is not wanted.

Veneers are less suitable for people who grind heavily without a night guard, have significant decay or gum disease, or have very little enamel left. See veneers before and after for typical results.

When is a crown the better choice?

A crown is usually the better choice when the tooth needs protection or strength, not just a new look. Common reasons for crowns include:

  • A large cavity or failing large filling that leaves little healthy tooth.
  • A cracked or broken tooth at risk of splitting.
  • A root canal treated tooth, especially a back tooth.
  • Severe wear from grinding.
  • Covering a dental implant or anchoring a bridge.

Crowns can also be used for cosmetic reasons on front teeth when a tooth is too damaged or discoloured for a veneer to work well.

How does getting a veneer compare with getting a crown?

Getting a veneer and getting a crown follow similar steps, usually over two visits, but the amount of preparation differs. At the first visit for either, the dentist examines the tooth, discusses the shade and shape you want, and may take X-rays and photos.

For a veneer, the dentist removes a thin layer of enamel from the front of the tooth, takes a scan or impression, and may place a temporary veneer. At the second visit, the custom veneer is checked for fit and colour, the tooth surface is prepared, and the veneer is bonded with a special resin cement and hardened with a curing light.

For a crown, the dentist reshapes the tooth on all sides, sometimes building up missing structure first with a filling material called a core. A scan or impression goes to the lab, and a temporary crown protects the tooth. At the second visit, the permanent crown is fitted and cemented. Some offices can mill ceramic crowns the same day with in-office equipment.

Both procedures are usually done with local anesthetic, and most people return to normal activities immediately. Mild sensitivity for a few days afterward is common with either.

Do veneers or crowns look more natural?

Both veneers and crowns can look very natural when made from modern porcelain or ceramic and matched carefully to neighbouring teeth. Veneers can look particularly lifelike on front teeth because light passes through the thin porcelain and the natural tooth behind it.

All-ceramic crowns also look natural. Older crowns with a metal base sometimes show a dark line at the gum, which modern materials avoid. Whichever you choose, the skill of the dentist and dental lab matters as much as the material.

Do crowns last longer than veneers?

Crowns and porcelain veneers both commonly last many years, often 10 to 15 years or more, with good care. Lifespan depends more on habits and the quality of the work than on the type of restoration.

Grinding, biting hard objects, decay at the edges and gum recession shorten the life of both. A night guard, careful cleaning and regular check-ups extend it. See whether veneers are permanent for veneer-specific details.

Are veneers or crowns more expensive?

Veneers and crowns can cost similar amounts per tooth, but insurance often makes crowns cheaper out of pocket. Delta Dental lists a porcelain laminate veneer at $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth without insurance, and CareCredit reports an average of $1,765 for a single porcelain veneer and $15,486 for a full set of six to eight.

Delta Dental notes veneers are usually treated as cosmetic and often not covered by insurance. Crowns that restore a damaged tooth are commonly covered as a major service. If cost is a concern and the change is small, dental bonding averages $431 per tooth according to CareCredit. See our veneers cost guide.

How do you decide between a veneer and a crown?

Deciding between a veneer and a crown comes down to the health of the tooth and your goals. A good dentist will start with the question of how much healthy tooth remains and what the tooth needs to function for years.

Ask your dentist why they recommend one over the other, how much tooth will be removed, what material they will use, what it costs and what insurance will pay. Healthline's veneers guide and the Cleveland Clinic's cosmetic dentistry overview are useful background reading.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between veneers and crowns?

A veneer is a thin shell bonded to the front surface of a tooth, mainly for appearance. A crown is a cap that covers the whole tooth to protect and strengthen it. Veneers preserve more natural tooth; crowns are used when a tooth is damaged or weakened.

Which is better, veneers or crowns?

Neither is better in every case. Veneers are better for healthy front teeth with cosmetic issues because they remove less tooth. Crowns are better for teeth that are broken, heavily filled, cracked or root canal treated. Your dentist recommends based on the tooth's condition.

Are veneers cheaper than crowns?

Veneers and crowns can cost similar amounts per tooth. Delta Dental lists a porcelain veneer at $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth without insurance. Crowns are more often covered by insurance when they restore a damaged tooth, so they may cost less out of pocket.

Do crowns or veneers last longer?

Crowns and porcelain veneers both commonly last 10 to 15 years or more with good care. Grinding, decay at the edges and gum recession shorten their life. Wearing a night guard and keeping regular dental check-ups helps both last longer.

Can you put a veneer on a back tooth?

Veneers are rarely placed on back teeth because back teeth take heavy chewing forces and are less visible. Crowns or onlays are usually recommended for back teeth that need restoration. Veneers are mainly used on front teeth seen when you smile.
