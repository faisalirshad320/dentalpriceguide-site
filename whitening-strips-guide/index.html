Do You Brush Your Teeth After Whitening Strips? Before vs After, Safety and Results

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 21, 20262 price sources
Quick answer

Do you brush your teeth after whitening strips? Yes, you can brush gently after removing the strips to clear leftover gel, with a soft brush. Brushing before is also fine if you wait about 30 minutes before applying strips, since brushing right before can irritate gums. Follow the package directions and your dentist's advice. Cleveland Clinic covers whitening safety.

Key takeaways
  • You can brush gently after whitening strips to remove leftover gel; use a soft brush and avoid hard scrubbing.
  • If you brush before strips, wait about 30 minutes so freshly brushed gums are less likely to sting.
  • Whitening strips work on natural enamel stains but do not change crowns, veneers or fillings.
  • Temporary sensitivity and gum irritation are the most common side effects; overusing strips makes them worse.
  • In-office bleaching costs $40 to $700 per visit out of network (Delta Dental), far more than most strip kits.

Do you brush your teeth after whitening strips?

You can brush your teeth after whitening strips, and many people do so to remove the sticky gel left on the teeth and gums. Brush gently with a soft-bristled toothbrush, because enamel and gums can feel more sensitive right after a whitening session.

Brushing after whitening strips works best with these habits:

  • Rinse with plain water first to loosen leftover gel.
  • Use a soft brush and light pressure rather than scrubbing.
  • Choose a regular fluoride toothpaste or one made for sensitive teeth; skip gritty or charcoal pastes right after whitening.
  • Brush along the gumline carefully, where gel tends to collect and irritate.

Brushing after whitening strips is not strictly required by every product. Some instructions simply say to rinse and remove any remaining gel. Product directions differ by peroxide strength and wear time, so read the box and follow it. If your dentist gave you specific instructions, those take priority over general advice.

After whitening, try to avoid coffee, tea, red wine, colas and other dark foods or drinks for at least a short while. Teeth can pick up stains more easily right after treatment.

Should you brush your teeth before using whitening strips?

Brushing your teeth before using whitening strips is a good idea for clean contact between the gel and the enamel, but it is best to wait about 30 minutes between brushing and applying the strips. Brushing can cause tiny abrasions on the gums, and peroxide gel on freshly brushed gums is more likely to sting.

Brushing before whitening strips helps because plaque and food film act as a barrier between the gel and the tooth surface. A cleaner surface lets the whitening agent reach the enamel more evenly. Many people brush and floss, wait a bit, then dry their teeth lightly with a tissue so the strips stay in place.

Brushing before whitening strips is not the only option. If you prefer, you can floss and rinse thoroughly before the strips, then brush gently after. Either routine is reasonable as long as you avoid aggressive brushing immediately before applying peroxide.

A simple order that works for most people:

  1. Brush and floss as usual.
  2. Wait about 30 minutes.
  3. Rinse with water and gently pat teeth dry.
  4. Apply strips for the time listed on the package, no longer.
  5. Remove strips, rinse, and brush gently if gel remains.

Do teeth whitening strips work?

Teeth whitening strips do work for many people, lightening yellowish stains on natural teeth over a course of daily use. They contain hydrogen peroxide or carbamide peroxide, the same type of active ingredient dentists use, but in lower strengths.

Whitening strips work best on stains from coffee, tea, red wine, tobacco and ageing. They are less effective on grayish stains, deep internal discoloration, or a single tooth that darkened after an injury. Results build gradually across the treatment course, and how much lighter teeth get varies from person to person.

Whitening strips do not change the color of crowns, veneers, bonding or tooth-colored fillings. If you have restorations on your front teeth, they may stand out after whitening. A dentist can tell you before you start whether your restorations are likely to mismatch.

Whitening strips also cover only the teeth they touch, so very crooked teeth or back teeth may whiten unevenly. Are whitening strips bad for your teeth?

Whitening strips are not bad for healthy teeth when used as directed, but they commonly cause temporary tooth sensitivity and gum irritation. Cleveland Clinic notes that a dental checkup beforehand helps confirm you are a good candidate.

Whitening strips cause problems mostly when they are overused. Wearing strips longer than directed, using them more often than the package says or stacking several products at once raises the risk of sensitivity without adding much whitening. Strips also should not sit on gums for long periods, so trim or fold them to fit if needed.

Whitening strips may not suit everyone. They are usually not recommended:

  • During pregnancy or breastfeeding
  • For children and young teens, unless a dentist advises otherwise
  • With untreated cavities, cracked teeth, exposed roots or active gum disease
  • With known allergy to peroxide or strip ingredients

Whitening strips and sensitive teeth can work together with some adjustments: shorter wear times, every-other-day use, or a sensitivity toothpaste for a couple of weeks before starting. Our guide to teeth whitening for sensitive teeth explains these options.

Stop using strips and see a dentist if you have severe or lasting tooth pain, gum blisters or white patches, or swelling. Seek urgent care for facial swelling or fever, which are not normal whitening side effects.

How do you get the best results from whitening strips?

The best results from whitening strips come from clean, dry teeth, consistent use for the full course, and good habits that keep new stains away. Small details in how you apply the strips make a noticeable difference.

  • Get a cleaning first. A professional cleaning removes tartar and surface stain so the gel reaches enamel evenly.
  • Dry your teeth. A light pat with a tissue helps strips stick and stay in place.
  • Press strips into the gaps. Smooth them along each tooth so the gel touches the whole surface.
  • Stay on schedule. Skipping days slows results; doubling up raises sensitivity.
  • Limit staining foods and drinks during and right after the course, and rinse with water after coffee or tea.
  • Plan touch-ups. Results fade gradually, so occasional short courses keep teeth bright.

Whitening strips give their best results on teeth without restorations and with mostly yellow surface staining. If strips have not changed your shade after a full course, a dentist can check whether the discoloration is internal or caused by something strips cannot reach.

What does it cost if whitening strips are not enough?

If whitening strips are not enough, professional in-office bleaching costs $40 to $700 per visit when billed out of network, according to Delta Dental's 2024 data. Light-activated treatments cost more: CareCredit reports averages of $792 for laser in-office whitening and $583 for UV whitening.

Whitening of any kind is usually not covered by dental insurance because it is cosmetic, per Delta Dental. These are national averages, and local prices vary. Frequently asked questions

Do you brush your teeth before or after whitening strips?

You can brush either before or after whitening strips. If you brush before, wait about 30 minutes so the gel is less likely to irritate freshly brushed gums. After removing strips, rinse and brush gently with a soft brush to clear leftover gel. Follow the product directions and your dentist's advice, which take priority.

How long after whitening strips can I brush my teeth?

You can usually brush right after removing whitening strips, as long as you brush gently with a soft brush and avoid abrasive toothpaste. Rinsing with water first helps loosen leftover gel. If your teeth feel sensitive, a toothpaste for sensitive teeth is a good choice. Check the package for any product-specific timing.

Can I eat or drink after using whitening strips?

You can eat and drink after whitening strips, but it is wise to avoid coffee, tea, red wine, cola and other dark foods for a while afterwards, because teeth pick up stains more easily right after whitening. Water is fine. Some product instructions give a specific waiting time, so follow the package directions.

Are teeth whitening strips safe?

Teeth whitening strips are generally safe for healthy adult teeth when used as directed. Temporary sensitivity and gum irritation are the most common side effects. Strips are usually not recommended during pregnancy, for young teens, or with untreated cavities or gum disease. A dental checkup before starting helps confirm strips are suitable for you.

Why are my teeth sensitive after whitening strips?

Teeth feel sensitive after whitening strips because peroxide passes through enamel and temporarily affects the nerve inside the tooth. The twinges usually fade within a few days of stopping. Shorter wear times, skipping days, and a sensitivity toothpaste can help. See a dentist if pain is severe or does not settle.

Do whitening strips work on crowns or veneers?

Whitening strips do not work on crowns, veneers, bonding or tooth-colored fillings, because these materials do not respond to peroxide. Only natural enamel lightens. If you have restorations on visible teeth, they may look darker than surrounding teeth after whitening. Ask your dentist before starting whether replacement or a different approach makes more sense.
