How Much Does a 3-Tooth Bridge Cost?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 7, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

A 3-tooth bridge, also called a three-unit bridge, averages about $3,600 out-of-network, according to Delta Dental. It is made of two crowns on the teeth either side of a gap plus one false tooth in the middle, replacing a single missing tooth. Insurance typically covers 50% to 80% of bridge costs.

  • A three-unit bridge has two crowns and one false tooth (pontic).
  • It replaces one missing tooth using the neighbouring teeth for support.

What is a 3-tooth bridge?

A 3-tooth bridge is a fixed dental bridge made of three connected units: a crown on the tooth on each side of the gap and a false tooth, called a pontic, in between. Although it is called a 3-tooth bridge, it replaces only one missing tooth; the other two units cap your own teeth.

The two supporting teeth, called abutments, are shaped so the crowns fit over them, and the whole bridge is cemented as a single piece. This is the most common type of traditional bridge. Our guide to dental bridges explains all bridge types.

How much does a three-unit bridge cost?

A three-unit bridge costs about $3,600 on average out-of-network, according to Delta Dental's bridge cost guide. That figure covers the two crowns and the pontic made as one unit.

CareCredit's procedure cost list reports an average of $5,197 for a traditional single-tooth bridge and $1,569 for a single-tooth Maryland bridge. The sources measure different things, so use them as guide rails and get a written quote. See dental bridge cost for a full comparison.

What affects the cost of a 3-tooth bridge?

Several factors affect the cost of a 3-tooth bridge.

  • Material: all-ceramic, zirconia, porcelain-fused-to-metal or metal.
  • Location: front bridges need more cosmetic detail; back bridges need more strength.
  • Preparatory work: fillings, root canals, build-ups or gum treatment on the supporting teeth.
  • Extraction: if the failing tooth is still present.
  • Dentist and lab fees, which vary by region.
  • X-rays and exam fees before treatment.

When is a 3-unit bridge recommended?

A 3-unit bridge is recommended when one tooth is missing and the teeth on both sides are strong enough to support crowns. It is especially sensible when those neighbouring teeth already have large fillings or need crowns, because the bridge protects them at the same time.

If the neighbouring teeth are healthy and untouched, an implant or a Maryland bridge may preserve more natural tooth. Healthline's implant versus bridge guide compares the options.

What happens when you get a 3-tooth bridge?

Getting a 3-tooth bridge usually takes two visits over a few weeks. At the first visit, the dentist numbs the area, reshapes the two supporting teeth, takes a scan or impression and fits a temporary bridge.

A dental lab makes the permanent bridge. At the second visit, the dentist checks the fit, colour and bite and cements it. If a tooth was recently extracted, healing time may be needed before the final bridge is made.

Does insurance cover a 3-tooth bridge?

Insurance often covers a 3-tooth bridge as a major restorative service. Delta Dental notes coverage for bridges is typically 50% to 80% of the cost, depending on the plan, subject to the deductible and annual maximum.

Some plans have a missing tooth clause that excludes teeth lost before coverage began, and many have waiting periods for major services. Request a pre-treatment estimate. If you do not have insurance, ask the office about cash-pay pricing and payment plans.

How long does a three-unit bridge last?

A three-unit bridge commonly lasts 10 to 15 years or more with good care. The most common reason bridges fail is decay or gum disease on the supporting teeth, not the bridge itself breaking.

Clean under the pontic daily with a floss threader, super floss or water flosser, and brush carefully around the crown edges. Regular check-ups help catch problems early. See before and after dental bridge examples for typical results over time.

What are the alternatives to a three-unit bridge?

Alternatives to a three-unit bridge include a single implant, a Maryland bridge and a removable partial denture. An implant replaces the missing tooth without touching the neighbouring teeth and helps preserve bone, but costs more and requires surgery.

A Maryland bridge uses bonded wings on the backs of the neighbouring teeth, removing less tooth, and costs less, but suits mainly front teeth. A partial denture is the least expensive but removable. The Cleveland Clinic's prosthodontics guide explains each option.

How can you lower the cost of a 3-unit bridge?

You can lower the cost of a 3-unit bridge by comparing itemised quotes, using an in-network dentist if insured, asking about cash-pay discounts or membership plans, considering a dental school clinic, and using HSA or FSA funds.

Ask whether the quote includes the temporary bridge, X-rays and follow-up adjustments, and whether any work on the supporting teeth is needed first. Those items can make one quote look cheaper than another when the totals are similar.

What if more than one tooth is missing?

If more than one tooth is missing in a row, a bridge needs more units, such as a four-unit bridge with two pontics, which costs more. Long bridges place more stress on the supporting teeth, so dentists may recommend implants or an implant-supported bridge instead.

Ask your dentist to price each option for your specific gap, including the long-term outlook for the supporting teeth.

Which material is best for a 3-tooth bridge?

The best material for a 3-tooth bridge depends on where it sits in your mouth. For front bridges, all-ceramic or layered zirconia gives the most natural look. For back bridges, monolithic zirconia or porcelain-fused-to-metal provide strength against chewing forces. Metal bridges are the most durable but visible.

Ask your dentist why they recommend a particular material and how it affects the price and expected lifespan.

How do you manage with a temporary bridge?

While the permanent bridge is being made, you wear a temporary bridge that protects the prepared teeth and fills the gap. Temporary bridges are made of acrylic or resin and are weaker than the final bridge, so avoid sticky and hard foods on that side and clean around them gently. If the temporary comes loose, call your dentist promptly rather than leaving the prepared teeth exposed.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a 3-tooth bridge cost?

A 3-tooth (three-unit) bridge averages about $3,600 out-of-network, according to Delta Dental. CareCredit reports $5,197 for a traditional single-tooth bridge and $1,569 for a Maryland bridge. Materials, location and preparatory work affect your price.

What is a 3-unit bridge?

A 3-unit bridge is a fixed dental bridge with crowns on the two teeth beside a gap and a false tooth (pontic) in the middle. It replaces one missing tooth, using the neighbouring teeth for support.

Does insurance cover a three-unit bridge?

Many dental plans cover three-unit bridges as major services. Delta Dental notes coverage is typically 50% to 80% of the cost depending on the plan, subject to the deductible, annual maximum and any missing tooth clause.

How long does a 3-tooth bridge last?

A three-unit bridge commonly lasts 10 to 15 years or more with good care. Decay or gum disease on the supporting teeth is the most common reason bridges fail, so daily cleaning under the bridge matters.

Is a bridge or implant better for one missing tooth?

A bridge is usually quicker and cheaper upfront, while an implant does not involve the neighbouring teeth and helps preserve bone. A bridge makes most sense when the neighbouring teeth already need crowns.
