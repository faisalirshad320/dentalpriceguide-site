Wisdom Teeth Removal Cost: What You'll Pay With and Without Insurance

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 21, 20262 price sources
Quick answer

Wisdom teeth removal cost for all four teeth averages $720 for simple extractions and $3,120 for surgical removal with sedation, based on out-of-network claims reported by Delta Dental. One surgical wisdom tooth averages $550. These are national averages, and local prices vary.

Key takeaways
  • Delta Dental reports an out-of-network average of $3,120 for removing all four wisdom teeth surgically with sedation, and $720 when all four are fully erupted.
  • CareCredit's cost survey puts four impacted wisdom teeth at an average of $3,340 and a single impacted tooth at $835.
  • Dental insurance typically covers about 50% to 80% of the total cost once your deductible is met, according to Delta Dental.
  • Impaction depth, the type of anesthesia, imaging and where you live drive most of the price difference.
  • In-network surgeons, dental schools, HSA or FSA funds and payment plans can lower what you pay out of pocket.

How much does wisdom teeth removal cost?

Wisdom teeth removal cost depends mostly on whether the teeth have fully erupted or are stuck in the gum and bone. Delta Dental reports an out-of-network average of $720 to remove all four wisdom teeth when they are fully erupted and come out without surgery. Removing all four surgically with sedation or anesthesia averages $3,120, and a single surgical wisdom tooth averages $550 per tooth.

Wisdom tooth extraction prices from a second source look a little different. The CareCredit procedure cost list, based on the Synchrony Average Procedural Cost Study for 2023–2024, puts a single impacted wisdom tooth at an average of $835 and four impacted wisdom teeth at $3,340.

The two sets of numbers measure different things. Delta Dental's figures come from out-of-network claims in 2021, which reflect what dentists bill without an insurance discount. CareCredit's figures come from a newer survey of average procedure prices and focus on impacted teeth, which are the harder, more expensive cases. Read them as two snapshots of the same market rather than as competing answers.

All of these are national averages. Your own quote can land above or below them depending on your city, the surgeon, and what your X-rays show. The only way to get a reliable number for your mouth is a written treatment estimate after an exam and imaging.

Wisdom tooth removal cost by type of extraction

Wisdom tooth removal cost rises with each step of difficulty, from a simple pull to a full bony impaction. Dentists and oral surgeons usually sort wisdom teeth into four broad groups, and each group is billed under a different procedure code.

  • Erupted (simple) extraction: the tooth has fully come through the gum and can be loosened and lifted out. Delta Dental's $720 out-of-network average for all four teeth reflects this group.
  • Soft tissue impaction: the crown is covered by gum but not bone. The surgeon makes a small incision, which adds time and cost.
  • Partial bony impaction: part of the crown is trapped under jawbone. Some bone is removed and the tooth is often sectioned into pieces.
  • Full bony impaction: the tooth sits completely inside the jawbone, sometimes at an angle. This is the most involved surgery and usually the most expensive per tooth.

Impacted wisdom teeth are the main reason surgical prices sit far above simple extractions. The CareCredit survey's $835 average for one impacted tooth and $3,340 for four reflects this harder work. Our guide to impacted wisdom teeth explains how impaction is diagnosed and why angled teeth are harder to remove.

Wisdom teeth age also matters for the type of extraction you face. Teeth removed in the late teens often have roots that are not fully formed, which can make surgery simpler. If you are unsure whether your teeth have finished erupting, see when do wisdom teeth come in for the usual age range and signs.

What changes the price of wisdom tooth extraction?

The price of wisdom tooth extraction changes with the number of teeth, the depth of impaction, the anesthesia used, the imaging needed, and who does the surgery. A quote that looks high is often bundling several of these items together.

  • Number of teeth: some people need one or two removed, others all four. Removing all four in one visit usually costs less overall than four separate visits because the anesthesia and setup are shared.
  • Anesthesia and sedation: local numbing is included in most extraction fees. Nitrous oxide, IV sedation and general anesthesia are billed separately and are often billed by time.
  • Imaging: a panoramic X-ray is standard. Some surgeons order a 3D cone beam CT scan when roots sit close to the nerve in the lower jaw, which adds to the bill.
  • Consultation: many oral surgeons charge a separate consultation fee, though some waive or credit it if you book the surgery.
  • Provider type: a general dentist may remove erupted wisdom teeth. Impacted teeth are often referred to an oral and maxillofacial surgeon, whose fees are usually higher.
  • Location: fees in large cities and high-cost regions tend to run higher than in smaller towns.
  • Follow-up care: treating a dry socket, an infection or extra pain medicine can add costs after the surgery.

When comparing quotes, ask each office to list every line item: exam, X-rays, each tooth's extraction code, anesthesia and follow-up visits. Two quotes that seem far apart often differ only in what is included.

How bad is wisdom teeth removal without anesthesia?

Wisdom teeth removal is never done without any anesthesia; at minimum, the area is numbed with local anesthetic, so you should feel pressure and movement but not sharp pain. When people ask about removal without anesthesia, they usually mean without sedation, which is a real and common option for erupted teeth.

Wisdom tooth extraction with local anesthesia only is often the lowest-cost route. You stay fully awake, you can usually drive yourself home, and you avoid the separate sedation fee. The trade-off is that you hear and feel the work, which some people find stressful, especially for impacted teeth that need bone removal.

Sedation options add to the cost but change the experience. Nitrous oxide takes the edge off anxiety. IV sedation leaves most people with little memory of the procedure. General anesthesia is used for complex cases or very anxious patients. Delta Dental's $3,120 out-of-network average for four surgical extractions includes sedation or anesthesia, which is one reason it sits so far above the $720 figure for erupted teeth.

Talk with your surgeon about which level of anesthesia fits your case. Your medical history, the difficulty of the extraction and your anxiety level all matter, and the safest choice is the one your surgeon recommends after reviewing your health.

Does dental insurance cover wisdom teeth removal?

Dental insurance usually covers wisdom teeth removal when the extraction is considered necessary, typically paying about 50% to 80% of the total cost according to Delta Dental. The share you pay depends on your plan design, whether the surgeon is in your network, and how much of your deductible and annual maximum is left.

Wisdom tooth coverage has a few common catches:

  • Annual maximum: many dental plans cap what they pay each year. A full surgical extraction with sedation can use up much or all of that cap.
  • Deductible: you usually pay the deductible before coverage starts.
  • Waiting periods: new plans sometimes delay coverage for oral surgery.
  • Sedation rules: some plans cover IV sedation or general anesthesia only for surgical extractions, not simple ones.
  • Medical insurance: deeply impacted teeth, cysts or infections may be billed partly to medical insurance, depending on the plan.

Ask the surgeon's office to send a pre-treatment estimate to your insurer before you book. The estimate shows what the plan expects to pay and what you will owe. How to pay less for wisdom teeth removal

The most effective ways to pay less for wisdom teeth removal are using an in-network surgeon, checking dental school clinics, and paying with pre-tax HSA or FSA money. Several smaller steps can also trim the bill.

  1. Stay in network. In-network surgeons have agreed to discounted fees with your insurer, so both the total and your share are usually lower than out-of-network prices like Delta Dental's averages.
  2. Try a dental school. Dental schools and residency programs in oral surgery often charge less. Treatment is supervised by licensed faculty, but appointments can take longer.
  3. Use HSA or FSA funds. Wisdom tooth extraction is a qualified medical expense, so paying with a health savings account or flexible spending account means using pre-tax dollars.
  4. Ask about payment plans. Many oral surgery offices offer in-house plans or accept medical credit cards. Read the terms closely, because deferred-interest cards can charge back interest if the balance is not paid in full on time.
  5. Ask for the self-pay price. Some offices give a lower fee to patients paying in full without insurance.
  6. Time it around your benefits. If your annual maximum is low, your surgeon may be able to split treatment across two benefit years when it is safe to wait.
  7. Look at community clinics. Federally qualified health centers and some public health clinics offer sliding-scale dental fees based on income.

Avoid choosing a surgeon on price alone. Experience with impacted teeth and a clear plan for aftercare matter more than a modest saving.

Can you keep your wisdom teeth?

Many people can keep their wisdom teeth if the teeth are fully erupted, healthy, positioned correctly and easy to clean. Removal is usually advised when wisdom teeth are impacted, decayed, causing gum infections, crowding nearby teeth, or forming cysts.

Wisdom teeth that stay put still need monitoring. Your dentist will check them with exams and periodic X-rays, because problems such as decay on the back of the second molar can develop without pain. Our guide on the benefits of keeping wisdom teeth covers when watching and waiting is reasonable.

Wisdom teeth are not present in everyone. Some people are born without one or more of them, which is part of the evolutionary story explained in does everyone have wisdom teeth. A panoramic X-ray shows whether you have them and how they are angled.

Why do wisdom teeth hurt? Common causes include a tooth pushing through the gum, inflamed gum tissue over a partly erupted tooth (pericoronitis), trapped food, decay and pressure on neighboring teeth. An infection around a wisdom tooth can cause swelling, a bad taste, jaw stiffness and pain when opening the mouth. See wisdom teeth pain for relief options and warning signs.

See a dentist urgently if you have facial swelling that is spreading, fever, trouble swallowing or breathing, or you cannot open your mouth. An infected wisdom tooth can spread to nearby tissues and needs prompt care.

How long does it take to get wisdom teeth out?

Getting wisdom teeth out usually takes under an hour of surgery time for all four teeth, though simple single extractions can be much quicker and deep impactions can take longer. Plan for extra time at the office for check-in, sedation and recovery before you go home.

Wisdom teeth removal typically follows these steps, as described by Cleveland Clinic:

  1. Exam and X-rays to map the teeth, roots and nearby nerves.
  2. Anesthesia: local numbing, with or without sedation.
  3. An incision in the gum if the tooth is covered, and removal of bone if needed.
  4. Sectioning the tooth into smaller pieces when that makes removal easier.
  5. Removing the tooth, cleaning the socket and placing stitches if needed.
  6. Placing gauze to control bleeding and help a blood clot form.

If you were sedated, you will need a responsible adult to drive you home. Our page on how long does wisdom teeth removal take breaks down timing by case type, including the appointment as a whole.

Wisdom teeth removal recovery: swelling, gauze, smoking and vaping

Wisdom teeth removal recovery usually means a few days of swelling and soreness, with most people back to normal routines within about a week and full healing taking longer. Following your surgeon's written instructions is the best way to avoid extra costs from complications.

When to stop using gauze after wisdom tooth extraction: keep firm pressure on the gauze for the time your surgeon specifies, commonly 30 to 60 minutes, and replace it if bleeding continues. Most people can stop once bleeding has slowed to light pink oozing, often within the first day.

How to reduce swelling after wisdom teeth removal: use cold packs on the cheeks in on-and-off intervals during the first day or two, keep your head raised when resting, and take pain relievers exactly as directed. Swelling usually peaks around day 2 or day 3, then fades over the following days. By day 5, many people notice clear improvement.

Day 3 and day 5 milestones: day 3 is often the most swollen and stiff day. If pain eases and then sharply worsens around days 3 to 5, that can point to a dry socket, where the blood clot is lost and bone is exposed. Cleveland Clinic describes the symptoms, and our guide to dry socket wisdom teeth covers prevention and treatment.

When can you smoke or vape after wisdom teeth removal? Suction and chemicals from smoking and vaping raise the risk of dry socket. Surgeons commonly ask patients to avoid smoking and vaping for at least 72 hours, and longer is better. The same suction concern applies to straws; see how long after wisdom teeth removal can i use a straw for drinking guidance.

Wisdom teeth recovery also depends on diet. Stick to cool, soft foods at first and move toward normal textures as comfort allows. Our guides to what to eat after wisdom teeth removal and the day-by-day wisdom teeth recovery timeline give practical detail.

Call your surgeon or seek urgent care for bleeding that soaks through gauze and does not slow with pressure, fever, swelling that worsens after day 3, pus, numbness that does not wear off, or severe pain that pain medicine does not control.

Frequently asked questions

How much is wisdom teeth removal without insurance?

Wisdom teeth removal without insurance averages $720 for four erupted teeth and $3,120 for four surgical extractions with sedation, based on Delta Dental out-of-network data from 2021. CareCredit's survey puts four impacted teeth at $3,340. These are national averages; ask local offices for self-pay prices and payment plans.

Does insurance cover wisdom teeth removal?

Insurance usually covers wisdom teeth removal when the extraction is needed for health reasons, typically paying about 50% to 80% of the total cost, according to Delta Dental. Your deductible, annual maximum, waiting periods and network status all affect what you owe, so request a pre-treatment estimate first.

How much is a single wisdom tooth extraction?

A single surgical wisdom tooth extraction averages $550 per tooth out of network, according to Delta Dental's 2021 data. CareCredit's 2023–2024 survey lists a single impacted wisdom tooth at an average of $835. A simple extraction of an erupted tooth usually costs less than either figure.

When can I smoke after wisdom tooth extraction?

Most oral surgeons ask patients to avoid smoking for at least 72 hours after wisdom tooth extraction, and longer if possible. Smoking creates suction and slows healing, which raises the risk of dry socket. Follow your surgeon's specific instructions, since they may advise a longer wait for difficult extractions.

How long does swelling last after wisdom teeth removal?

Swelling after wisdom teeth removal usually peaks around day 2 or day 3, then gradually goes down over the rest of the first week. Cold packs during the first day or two and resting with your head raised help. Swelling that grows after day 3, or comes with fever, needs a call to your surgeon.

Can you vape after wisdom teeth removal?

Vaping after wisdom teeth removal should be avoided for at least 72 hours, and ideally longer. Inhaling creates suction that can dislodge the blood clot, and the chemicals can irritate the wound. Your surgeon's instructions take priority, especially if you had impacted teeth or stitches.

What age should you get wisdom teeth removed?

Many dentists recommend evaluating wisdom teeth in the late teens or early twenties, when roots are not fully formed and recovery tends to be easier. Removal is not required for everyone. Healthy, well-positioned wisdom teeth that are easy to clean can often stay, with regular monitoring by your dentist.
