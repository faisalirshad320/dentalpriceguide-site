How Much Is Wisdom Teeth Removal Without Insurance?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 5, 20262 price sources
Quick answer

Without insurance, wisdom teeth removal costs about $720 for all four fully erupted teeth removed non-surgically and about $3,120 for all four removed surgically with sedation, based on Delta Dental's out-of-network averages. One surgically removed tooth averages $550. CareCredit reports $835 for one impacted tooth and $3,340 for four.

Key takeaways
  • All four erupted wisdom teeth: about $720 out-of-network (Delta Dental).
  • All four surgical with sedation: about $3,120 (Delta Dental); four impacted: $3,340 (CareCredit).
  • One surgical wisdom tooth: about $550 (Delta Dental); one impacted: $835 (CareCredit).
  • Impaction, sedation type and imaging are the biggest cost drivers.
  • Dental schools, payment plans and HSA or FSA funds can make removal more affordable.

How much is wisdom teeth removal without insurance?

Wisdom teeth removal without insurance usually costs about $720 for all four fully erupted teeth removed non-surgically, about $550 per tooth for one surgical extraction, and about $3,120 for all four removed surgically with sedation or anesthesia, according to Delta Dental's out-of-network averages.

CareCredit's procedure cost list reports $835 for a single impacted wisdom tooth and $3,340 for four impacted wisdom teeth. The figures differ because the sources measure different things, but together they show that impacted teeth and sedation multiply the cost. See our main wisdom teeth removal cost guide.

What makes wisdom teeth removal cost more?

Several factors make wisdom teeth removal cost more without insurance.

  • Impaction: teeth trapped in gum or bone need surgical removal; full bony impactions cost the most.
  • Number of teeth: removing all four costs more than one, though doing them together avoids repeat sedation fees.
  • Sedation type: local anesthetic costs least; IV sedation or general anesthesia adds substantially.
  • Imaging: a panoramic X-ray or 3D scan is needed to plan surgery.
  • Provider: oral surgeons often charge more than general dentists but handle complex cases.
  • Location: fees vary by city and region.

Why are impacted wisdom teeth so much more expensive?

Impacted wisdom teeth cost much more because they cannot simply be lifted out. The surgeon must open the gum, often remove bone, and section the tooth into pieces. The Cleveland Clinic's impacted wisdom teeth guide describes the types of impaction.

Lower impacted teeth near the nerve need extra care and sometimes a 3D scan. Because of this added time, skill and equipment, Delta Dental's four-tooth surgical average with sedation ($3,120) is several times its four-tooth non-surgical average ($720).

How does sedation affect the cost?

Sedation affects the cost significantly. Local anesthetic is usually included in the extraction fee, while nitrous oxide, oral sedation, IV sedation and general anesthesia are billed separately, often by time.

IV sedation is common for removing impacted wisdom teeth because the surgery can be lengthy and many people prefer not to remember it. If cost is a major concern, ask whether local anesthetic with nitrous oxide would be suitable for your case.

How can you save on wisdom teeth removal without insurance?

You can save on wisdom teeth removal without insurance by comparing options and providers.

  • Dental schools and residency programs often charge lower fees, with supervision by experienced surgeons.
  • Community health centers may offer sliding-scale fees.
  • Ask for a cash-pay or package price for all four teeth together.
  • Payment plans and medical financing; read interest terms carefully.
  • HSA or FSA funds to pay with pre-tax money.
  • Compare quotes from a general dentist and an oral surgeon for erupted teeth.

Do you need to remove all four wisdom teeth?

You do not always need to remove all four wisdom teeth. Healthy, fully erupted wisdom teeth that you can keep clean may stay, which avoids surgery and cost entirely. Removal is usually recommended for teeth that are impacted, repeatedly infected, decayed or damaging neighbouring teeth.

The Cleveland Clinic's wisdom teeth removal guide explains common reasons for removal. If only one or two teeth are a problem, ask whether the others can be monitored.

How does the cost compare with insurance?

With insurance, Delta Dental notes coverage for wisdom teeth removal is typically about 50% to 80% of the total cost, depending on the plan, subject to the deductible and annual maximum. Sedation may be covered differently or limited.

If you plan to buy a dental plan before removal, check the waiting period for oral surgery. Many plans require several months of membership first, which may not suit an urgent extraction.

Are there costs during recovery?

Recovery costs are usually small but worth budgeting: prescription or over-the-counter pain relief, ice packs, soft foods and time off work or school. Follow-up visits are often included in the surgical fee.

Dry socket, the most common complication, may need extra visits for cleaning and dressing; the Cleveland Clinic explains how to prevent it. Plan with our wisdom teeth recovery timeline, what to eat after wisdom teeth removal and wisdom teeth pain guides.

Is it cheaper to remove wisdom teeth earlier?

Removing wisdom teeth in the late teens or early twenties can mean simpler surgery, because the roots are not fully formed and the bone is less dense. That can make removal quicker and recovery smoother, though fees still depend on the case.

Waiting until problems develop, such as infection or damage to the second molar, can lead to more complex treatment and extra costs. If your dentist predicts problems, discussing timing early can save money.

What should a wisdom teeth removal quote include?

A wisdom teeth removal quote should list every item you will be billed for, so you can compare surgeons fairly. Ask for the consultation fee, the panoramic X-ray or 3D scan, the extraction fee for each tooth by type (erupted, soft tissue impaction, partial bony or full bony impaction), the sedation or anesthesia fee and how it is calculated, and any bone graft or medication charges.

Also ask whether post-operative visits are included and what happens if you develop dry socket or need extra care. Some practices quote a single package price for all four teeth with sedation, which can be simpler to budget. Others bill each item separately. Neither approach is better in itself, but having everything in writing prevents surprises after surgery.

If one quote is much lower than others, check whether it assumes simpler extractions than your X-ray shows, or leaves out sedation.

Is it worth getting a second opinion on cost?

Getting a second opinion on wisdom teeth removal is worthwhile when the quote is high or you are told to remove teeth that cause no symptoms. A second surgeon may read the X-rays differently, suggest monitoring healthy teeth, or offer a lower package price for the same work.

Frequently asked questions

How much is wisdom teeth removal without insurance?

Without insurance, removing all four erupted wisdom teeth costs about $720 and all four surgically with sedation about $3,120, based on Delta Dental's out-of-network averages. CareCredit reports $835 for one impacted tooth and $3,340 for four.

How much does it cost to remove one wisdom tooth without insurance?

Removing one wisdom tooth surgically costs about $550 out-of-network according to Delta Dental, and CareCredit reports $835 for a single impacted wisdom tooth. A fully erupted tooth removed simply usually costs less.

Why is wisdom teeth removal so expensive?

Wisdom teeth removal is expensive when teeth are impacted, because surgery requires opening the gum, removing bone and sectioning teeth. Sedation, 3D imaging and oral surgeon fees add to the cost.

Where can I get cheap wisdom teeth removal?

Lower-cost wisdom teeth removal may be available at dental school clinics, residency programs and community health centers. Asking for a package price for all four teeth, using payment plans or HSA or FSA funds can also help.

Can I get wisdom teeth removed without sedation to save money?

Many wisdom teeth, especially erupted ones, can be removed with local anesthetic alone, which avoids sedation fees. Complex impacted teeth are often removed with IV sedation for comfort. Ask your surgeon what is suitable for your case.
