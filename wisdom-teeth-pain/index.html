Wisdom Teeth Pain: What It Feels Like, What Helps and When to See a Dentist

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 21, 20262 price sources
Quick answer

Wisdom teeth pain is usually a dull, throbbing ache at the very back of the jaw, often with swollen, tender gums, and it can spread to the jaw joint, ear or head. Saltwater rinses and over-the-counter pain relievers help short term, but pain that keeps returning, swelling or fever needs a dental exam. Cleveland Clinic explains the causes.

Key takeaways
  • Wisdom teeth pain usually comes from a tooth pushing through the gum, an impacted tooth, or infection of the gum flap over a partly erupted tooth.
  • The pain can spread to the jaw, ear, temple and throat, so it is sometimes mistaken for an earache or headache.
  • Saltwater rinses, cold compresses and over-the-counter pain relievers can ease mild pain, but they do not fix the cause.
  • Facial swelling, fever, trouble swallowing or opening your mouth, or pus need prompt dental or medical care.
  • Removal of all four impacted wisdom teeth averages $3,340 nationally (CareCredit); local prices vary.

What does wisdom teeth pain feel like?

Wisdom teeth pain usually feels like a dull, throbbing ache behind the last molar, sometimes with sharp twinges when you bite or chew. Many people notice it comes and goes over weeks or months, flaring for a few days and then settling.

Wisdom teeth pain often arrives with other signs at the back of the mouth:

  • Red, swollen or tender gum behind the last molar
  • A small flap of gum partly covering a tooth
  • A bad taste or bad breath that does not clear with brushing
  • Stiffness when opening the mouth wide
  • Pain when biting down on that side
  • Food getting trapped behind the back teeth

Wisdom teeth, the third molars, usually come in during the late teens or early twenties. Our page on when wisdom teeth come in covers the typical age range. Not every ache in this area is a wisdom tooth, though. A cavity in the second molar, gum disease, a cracked tooth or jaw-joint problems can cause similar pain, which is why an exam and X-ray are the reliable way to find the source.

Wisdom teeth pain with no visible tooth is also common. A swollen gum behind the back tooth when no wisdom tooth shows can mean an impacted tooth is still under the gum, or it can point to something else entirely. A dentist can tell the difference.

What causes wisdom teeth pain?

Wisdom teeth pain is most often caused by a tooth pushing through the gum, a tooth that is impacted (blocked from coming in fully), or an infection around a partly erupted tooth. Most jaws do not have enough room for third molars, so they often come in at an angle or only part of the way.

  • Eruption pressure: A tooth moving through bone and gum causes temporary soreness, similar to teething.
  • Impaction: A tooth stuck against the neighboring molar or under bone can press on nearby teeth and nerves. Cleveland Clinic describes the different impaction angles.
  • Pericoronitis: Food and bacteria trapped under the gum flap over a partly erupted tooth cause painful, swollen, infected gum tissue. This is one of the most common reasons for sudden wisdom tooth pain.
  • Decay: Back teeth are hard to clean, so wisdom teeth and the molars beside them develop cavities easily.
  • Abscess or cyst: Untreated infection can form a pocket of pus (a wisdom tooth abscess), and impacted teeth occasionally develop cysts that damage bone.

Wisdom teeth pain caused by infection tends to build quickly, with swelling and a bad taste, while eruption pain is milder and fades over a few days.

Can wisdom teeth cause headaches, jaw pain or ear pain?

Wisdom teeth can cause headaches, jaw pain and ear pain, because the nerves that serve the back teeth share pathways with the jaw joint, ear and side of the head. Pain from one area is often felt in another, which doctors call referred pain.

Wisdom teeth jaw pain usually sits at the angle of the jaw and may come with stiffness when you open your mouth. Swelling from an infected gum flap can make the jaw muscles tighten, and an impacted tooth can add pressure along the jawbone.

Wisdom teeth ear pain often feels like a deep ache in front of or inside the ear on the same side as the tooth. If your ear hurts but an ear exam is normal, a back tooth is one possible source worth checking.

Wisdom teeth headaches tend to be one-sided, felt at the temple or behind the eye, and they often follow jaw clenching caused by tooth discomfort. Headaches have many causes, so a new, severe or unusual headache should be checked by a doctor rather than blamed on teeth.

Get same-day care if wisdom tooth pain comes with facial or neck swelling, fever, difficulty swallowing or breathing, inability to open your mouth, or swelling that spreads toward the eye. These can signal a spreading infection.

What helps with wisdom teeth pain?

Wisdom teeth pain can be eased at home with warm saltwater rinses, cold compresses, over-the-counter pain relievers and careful cleaning around the back teeth. These steps help while you wait for a dental appointment, but they do not treat an impacted tooth or an infection.

  1. Rinse with warm salt water (about half a teaspoon of salt in a cup of warm water) several times a day to clean the area and calm the gum.
  2. Use a cold pack on the outside of the cheek for 15 to 20 minutes at a time to reduce swelling.
  3. Take an over-the-counter pain reliever such as ibuprofen or acetaminophen as directed on the label, if they are safe for you. Check with a pharmacist or doctor if you have stomach, kidney, liver or heart conditions, are pregnant, or take other medicines.
  4. Keep the area clean with gentle brushing and, if your dentist suggests it, an irrigating syringe to flush food from under the gum flap.
  5. Chew on the other side and choose soft foods until the flare-up settles.

Wisdom tooth pain relief from numbing gels is short-lived, and aspirin placed directly on the gum can burn the tissue, so avoid that old remedy. If you have signs of infection, a dentist may clean under the gum flap and may prescribe antibiotics; antibiotics calm an infection but do not stop it from returning while the tooth remains.

Is wisdom teeth removal painful?

Wisdom teeth removal itself should not be painful, because the area is numbed and many patients also receive sedation or general anesthesia. You may feel pressure during the procedure, but sharp pain is a signal to tell your surgeon so more anesthetic can be given.

Wisdom teeth removal pain afterwards is normal and usually peaks in the first two to three days, then improves steadily. Swelling and jaw stiffness are common, and most people return to normal routines within a few days to a week. Cleveland Clinic describes the procedure and typical recovery. Our guide to wisdom teeth recovery walks through each day, and our list of what to eat after wisdom teeth removal helps you plan meals.

Wisdom teeth pain that gets worse around three to five days after surgery, rather than better, can mean dry socket, where the blood clot is lost from the extraction site. Cleveland Clinic's dry socket guide explains the signs. Call your surgeon if this happens, or if you have fever, pus, or bleeding that does not slow with firm pressure from gauze.

Follow your own surgeon's aftercare instructions; they take priority over general advice.

How can you prevent wisdom teeth pain?

Wisdom teeth pain is best prevented with regular dental checkups and X-rays that track how the third molars are developing, plus careful cleaning of the back teeth. Some pain cannot be avoided if a tooth is impacted, but early planning prevents many emergencies.

  • Brush the back molars with a small-headed brush, angling it behind the last tooth.
  • Floss or use interdental brushes around the back teeth daily.
  • Rinse after meals if food tends to collect behind the molars.
  • Keep routine dental visits so problems such as decay or a developing infection are caught early.
  • Ask your dentist whether your wisdom teeth are likely to come in fully or whether removal before symptoms start makes sense for you.

Wisdom teeth that are fully erupted, healthy, cleanable and in a good bite may never need removal. Teeth that keep causing infections or damaging neighbors usually do.

What does it cost if wisdom teeth pain leads to removal?

Wisdom teeth removal costs range from $720 for all four fully erupted teeth removed non-surgically to $3,120 for all four removed surgically with sedation, based on out-of-network averages from Delta Dental. Delta Dental lists one surgical wisdom tooth at $550 per tooth.

Wisdom teeth that are impacted cost more to remove. CareCredit reports an average of $835 for a single impacted wisdom tooth and $3,340 for four. Delta Dental notes insurance typically covers about 50% to 80% of the total cost. These are national averages, and local prices vary. Frequently asked questions

Can wisdom teeth cause headaches?

Wisdom teeth can cause headaches, usually one-sided and felt at the temple or behind the eye. The pain is often referred from the back of the jaw or caused by clenching in response to tooth discomfort. Because headaches have many causes, a new, severe or unusual headache should be checked by a doctor rather than assumed to be dental.

Can wisdom teeth cause ear pain?

Wisdom teeth can cause ear pain because the back teeth and the ear share nerve pathways. The ache is often felt deep in or in front of the ear on the same side as the tooth. If an ear exam is normal but pain continues, ask a dentist to check your back teeth and take an X-ray.

How long does wisdom tooth pain last?

Wisdom tooth pain from normal eruption often lasts a few days at a time and may return in waves over months. Pain from an infected gum flap or abscess usually keeps getting worse until it is treated. Any wisdom tooth pain lasting more than a few days, or returning repeatedly, deserves a dental exam.

When is wisdom tooth pain an emergency?

Wisdom tooth pain is an emergency when it comes with facial or neck swelling, fever, trouble swallowing or breathing, inability to open your mouth, or swelling spreading toward the eye. These can mean a spreading infection. Seek same-day dental care or go to an emergency department, especially if breathing or swallowing is affected.

Will antibiotics fix wisdom tooth pain?

Antibiotics can calm an active infection around a wisdom tooth, but they do not fix the underlying cause. If the tooth is impacted or keeps trapping food under a gum flap, infection often returns after the course ends. A dentist or oral surgeon can advise whether cleaning, monitoring or removal is the better long-term option.

How much does it cost to remove a painful wisdom tooth?

Removing one wisdom tooth surgically averages $550 per tooth out of network, according to Delta Dental, while CareCredit reports $835 on average for a single impacted wisdom tooth. Insurance typically covers about 50% to 80% of the total, per Delta Dental. These are national averages, and prices vary by region and complexity.
