How Much Does It Cost to Fix a Chipped Tooth?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated November 3, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

Fixing a small chipped tooth usually costs about the same as a tooth-coloured composite filling: $145 to $480 for a front tooth and $165 to $530 for a back tooth without insurance, according to Delta Dental. Larger chips or cracks may need a crown or veneer, which cost more.

Key takeaways
  • Small chips are usually repaired with composite resin (bonding or a filling).
  • Composite repair runs $145 to $480 (front) and $165 to $530 (back) without insurance (Delta Dental).
  • CareCredit reports a $226 average for a composite resin filling.
  • Large breaks, cracks or nerve damage may need a crown, root canal or veneer.
  • Insurance often covers chip repair as basic care at 50%–80%.

How much does it cost to fix a chipped tooth?

Fixing a chipped tooth usually costs $145 to $480 for a front tooth and $165 to $530 for a back tooth without insurance when the repair is done with tooth-coloured composite, according to Delta Dental's filling cost data. CareCredit reports an average of $226 for a composite resin filling.

The exact cost depends on the size of the chip, how many surfaces are involved, whether the chip affects the biting edge, and the dentist's fee. Small cosmetic chips on front teeth are often quick and inexpensive to fix; larger breaks need more work. See how much a filling costs for related pricing.

What are the options for repairing a chipped tooth?

The options for repairing a chipped tooth depend on how much tooth is missing and whether the nerve is affected.

  • Polishing: a tiny chip may simply be smoothed so it does not cut the tongue.
  • Bonding or composite filling: tooth-coloured resin rebuilds the missing part in one visit.
  • Veneer: covers the front of a chipped front tooth for a more durable cosmetic result.
  • Crown: covers the whole tooth when a large part has broken off.
  • Root canal and crown: needed if the break exposes or damages the nerve.

How are small chips fixed?

Small chips are usually fixed with dental bonding. The dentist lightly roughens the tooth, applies a conditioning liquid, then adds tooth-coloured composite resin in layers, shapes it and hardens it with a curing light. The repair is polished to blend in with the tooth.

Bonding usually takes less than an hour per tooth and rarely needs anesthetic. It is affordable and preserves the natural tooth. The trade-off is that resin can stain and may chip again over the years, so it occasionally needs touching up.

What if a large piece of tooth broke off?

If a large piece of tooth broke off, a filling may not be strong enough. A crown, which covers the whole tooth, is often recommended to protect what remains, especially on back teeth that take heavy chewing forces.

If the break reaches the nerve, you may notice sharp pain, sensitivity or bleeding from the tooth, and a root canal may be needed before the crown. These treatments cost considerably more than a simple repair. The Cleveland Clinic explains how damage and decay affect the inside of the tooth.

Is a chipped tooth an emergency?

A chipped tooth is not usually an emergency, but it should be seen by a dentist soon, especially if it is sharp, sensitive or painful. A sharp edge can cut the tongue or cheek, and a crack can let bacteria reach the inner tooth.

Seek urgent dental care if a large part of the tooth has broken, the tooth is very painful, you see pink or red tissue in the break, the tooth is loose, or you have facial swelling. Seek emergency medical care if the injury also involved a blow to the head or jaw.

What should you do until you see the dentist?

Until you see the dentist, protect the chipped tooth and keep it clean. Rinse your mouth with warm water, cover a sharp edge with sugar-free gum or dental wax, and avoid biting on that tooth.

Take over-the-counter pain relievers as directed on the label if needed. If you have the broken piece, keep it in milk or saliva and bring it to the appointment; sometimes it can be bonded back on.

Does insurance cover a chipped tooth repair?

Insurance often covers chipped tooth repair as a basic procedure, typically at 50%–80% after the deductible, according to Delta Dental. Crowns are usually major procedures covered at a lower percentage.

Purely cosmetic improvements, such as veneers on undamaged teeth, are often excluded. A chip caused by an accident may sometimes be considered by medical insurance. See tooth filling cost with insurance and Delta Dental's coverage guide.

How can you pay less without insurance?

Without insurance, you can pay less for a chipped tooth repair by asking for a cash-pay discount, using an in-house membership plan, visiting a dental school clinic or community health center, or using HSA or FSA funds. Composite bonding is usually the most affordable fix for small chips.

How can you prevent chipped teeth?

You can prevent many chipped teeth by avoiding habits that put teeth under sudden stress. Do not chew ice, hard candy, popcorn kernels or pens, and do not use your teeth to open packages or bottles.

Wear a mouthguard for contact sports and a night guard if you grind your teeth. Treat cavities early, because teeth weakened by decay or large old fillings chip more easily.

How long does a chipped tooth repair last?

How long a chipped tooth repair lasts depends on the type of repair and the forces on the tooth. Composite bonding on a front tooth commonly lasts several years before it needs polishing, touching up or replacing. Repairs on biting edges or back teeth take more stress and may chip again sooner.

Porcelain veneers and crowns usually last longer than composite because porcelain is harder and more stain-resistant, but they cost more upfront. When choosing, think about the total cost over many years, not just the first bill.

You can extend the life of any repair by avoiding biting hard objects, wearing a night guard if you grind, and keeping regular check-ups so small chips in the repair can be fixed before they grow. Coffee, tea, red wine and tobacco can stain composite, so rinsing with water afterward helps keep the repair matching your teeth.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to fix a chipped tooth?

Fixing a small chipped tooth with composite usually costs $145 to $480 for a front tooth and $165 to $530 for a back tooth without insurance, according to Delta Dental. Larger breaks needing a crown or root canal cost more.

Can a chipped tooth be fixed in one visit?

A small chipped tooth can usually be fixed in one visit with dental bonding, which takes under an hour. Larger breaks needing a crown usually take two visits, unless the dentist offers same-day crowns.

Does insurance cover chipped tooth repair?

Many dental plans cover chipped tooth repair as a basic procedure at 50%–80% after the deductible, according to Delta Dental. Crowns for larger breaks are usually major procedures covered at a lower rate. Cosmetic-only work may be excluded.

Is it okay to leave a chipped tooth?

Leaving a tiny chip may be fine if it is smooth and not sensitive, but a dentist should check it. Larger chips or cracks can let bacteria in, weaken the tooth and lead to more expensive problems later.

Can a chipped front tooth be made to look natural?

A chipped front tooth can usually be made to look natural with bonding or a veneer. The dentist matches the resin or porcelain shade to your teeth and shapes it to mirror the neighbouring tooth.
