Wisdom Teeth Recovery Timeline: What to Expect Day by Day

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 21, 20262 price sources
Quick answer

Wisdom teeth recovery usually takes about three days to one week before most people feel back to normal, with swelling peaking around day two or three. The gum over each socket takes several weeks to close, and bone fills in over months. Follow your surgeon's instructions, and see the Cleveland Clinic for more detail.

Key takeaways
  • Most people return to normal routines within three days to a week after wisdom teeth removal.
  • Swelling and soreness usually peak around day two or three, then ease steadily.
  • You can brush your other teeth the day after surgery; stay gentle near the sockets.
  • Pain that suddenly worsens a few days after surgery can signal dry socket and needs a call to your surgeon.
  • Fever, pus, uncontrolled bleeding or swelling that keeps growing are reasons to call your surgeon promptly.

How long does it take for wisdom teeth to heal?

Wisdom teeth usually take about three days to one week to heal enough for normal daily life, while full healing of the sockets takes several weeks for the gum and a few months for the bone. Simple extractions of fully erupted teeth tend to heal faster than surgical removal of impacted teeth.

Several things change your personal wisdom teeth recovery timeline. Impacted teeth that needed bone removal, older age, smoking, and removal of all four teeth at once can all add days to recovery. Teens and young adults often heal more quickly than people in their thirties and beyond.

The Cleveland Clinic guide to impacted wisdom teeth explains why impacted teeth are removed and why their recovery can be longer. Your oral surgeon's instructions always come first, because they are based on how your surgery went.

What is the wisdom teeth recovery timeline day by day?

The wisdom teeth recovery timeline follows a fairly predictable pattern: bleeding settles in the first day, swelling peaks around days two and three, and most people feel much better by the end of the first week. Typical stages look like this:

  • First 24 hours: numbness wears off, light oozing is normal, and you rest with gauze in place as directed. A blood clot forms in each socket. Use ice packs on the cheeks in short intervals.
  • Days 2 to 3: swelling and jaw stiffness are usually at their worst. Soreness is steady but should be manageable with the pain relief your surgeon recommended. Start gentle saltwater rinses if your surgeon allows.
  • Days 4 to 7: swelling fades, opening your mouth gets easier, and many people return to school or work. Bruising on the cheeks may turn yellow before it clears.
  • Week 2: dissolvable stitches usually loosen and fall out; non-dissolvable stitches are often removed around this time. Most normal foods are back on the menu.
  • Weeks 3 to 6: the gum over each socket closes, though small dips can remain for a while.
  • Months 3 to 6: bone fills in beneath the gum.

Pain that improves and then suddenly worsens around days two to four, often spreading toward the ear, can be dry socket. The Cleveland Clinic page on dry socket describes the signs. Our guide to wisdom teeth pain explains what normal and abnormal pain feel like.

When can I brush my teeth after wisdom teeth removal?

You can usually brush your teeth gently the day after wisdom teeth removal, avoiding the extraction sites for the first few days. Many surgeons allow careful brushing of the front teeth the night of surgery, as long as you do not spit forcefully.

How to brush teeth after wisdom teeth removal:

  1. Use a soft-bristled brush and a small amount of toothpaste.
  2. Brush the teeth away from the surgery sites normally, and go slowly near the back of the mouth.
  3. Do not brush directly on the sockets until your surgeon says it is safe.
  4. Let water and toothpaste fall out of your mouth instead of spitting hard, which can dislodge the clot.
  5. Start gentle warm saltwater rinses after the first 24 hours if your surgeon recommends them, especially after meals.

You can and should brush your teeth before wisdom teeth removal. A clean mouth on surgery day lowers the amount of bacteria near the sockets. If you are having sedation, follow the fasting instructions and do not swallow the rinse water.

Some surgeons give you a small irrigating syringe to flush food out of lower sockets, usually starting several days after surgery. Use the syringe only as directed.

How should I sleep after wisdom teeth removal, and how long does swelling last?

Sleep after wisdom teeth removal with your head raised on two or three pillows or in a recliner for the first few nights, because elevation helps limit swelling and bleeding. Lying on your back keeps pressure off the cheeks.

Remove gauze from your mouth before you sleep to avoid a choking risk. Place an old towel over your pillow in case of light oozing overnight, which is common in the first day or two.

Wisdom teeth swelling usually peaks around 48 to 72 hours after surgery and then fades over the following days, with most swelling gone within about a week. Ice packs in short on-and-off intervals help during the first day. After the first day or two, many surgeons suggest switching to moist warmth to ease stiffness.

Swelling that keeps increasing after day three, a hard, hot or very painful area, or swelling that makes swallowing or breathing difficult is not normal. Call your surgeon right away, and seek emergency care if you have trouble breathing or swallowing.

How can I speed up wisdom teeth recovery?

The best way to speed up wisdom teeth recovery is to protect the blood clots in the sockets, rest, and follow your surgeon's instructions closely. Healing has a natural pace, but these habits help you avoid setbacks:

  • Skip straws, smoking and vaping for as long as your surgeon advises; suction and smoke can dislodge clots and delay healing.
  • Rest for the first two days and avoid heavy exercise, which can raise blood pressure and restart bleeding.
  • Eat soft, cool foods at first and chew away from the sockets. See our guide on what to eat after wisdom teeth removal.
  • Stay hydrated with water, and avoid alcohol while taking prescription pain medication.
  • Take medication as directed, including any antibiotics or rinses your surgeon prescribed.
  • Keep the mouth clean with gentle brushing and saltwater rinses once allowed.

Call your oral surgeon promptly for fever, pus or a bad taste that does not go away, bleeding that does not slow with firm gauze pressure, numbness that lasts beyond the first day, or pain that suddenly gets worse. Go to emergency care for difficulty breathing or swallowing.

Knowing when wisdom teeth come in also helps with timing. Removing wisdom teeth in the late teens or early twenties, before roots are fully formed, is often linked with easier recovery.

What does wisdom teeth removal cost if recovery needs more care?

Wisdom teeth removal costs $3,120 on average for all four teeth removed surgically with sedation or anesthesia, based on Delta Dental's out-of-network data. Removing four fully erupted teeth without surgery averages $720, and one surgical tooth averages $550 per tooth.

CareCredit's cost study reports $835 for a single impacted wisdom tooth and $3,340 for four impacted wisdom teeth. The two sources differ because Delta Dental reports out-of-network claim averages while CareCredit reports a separate cost survey. All figures are national averages; local prices vary.

Harder recoveries can add cost. Impacted teeth, sedation and follow-up visits for dry socket or infection may mean extra appointments or prescriptions. Delta Dental notes that insurance typically covers about 50% to 80% of the total cost. For the full breakdown, see our guide to wisdom teeth removal cost.

Frequently asked questions

How long does wisdom teeth pain last?

Wisdom teeth pain usually peaks in the first two to three days after surgery and eases steadily over the first week. Mild tenderness can last a little longer after impacted tooth removal. Pain that suddenly worsens a few days after surgery may signal dry socket, so call your surgeon if that happens.

Can I brush my teeth after wisdom teeth removal?

You can brush your teeth gently after wisdom teeth removal, usually starting the day after surgery. Use a soft brush, avoid the extraction sites for the first few days, and let water fall out of your mouth instead of spitting. Follow your surgeon's instructions on when to clean the sockets directly.

How long does it take for wisdom teeth holes to close?

Wisdom teeth holes usually close at the gum level over about three to six weeks, though small dips can remain longer, especially in lower sockets. The bone underneath fills in over several months. Rinsing food out gently as directed by your surgeon helps the sockets stay clean while they close.

How long does wisdom teeth swelling last?

Wisdom teeth swelling usually peaks around 48 to 72 hours after surgery and fades over the following days, with most swelling gone within about a week. Bruising can take a little longer to clear. Swelling that keeps increasing after day three, or comes with fever, needs a call to your surgeon.

When do wisdom teeth stitches dissolve?

Dissolvable wisdom teeth stitches usually loosen and fall out within about one to two weeks. Some surgeons use stitches that need removal at a follow-up visit around the same time. A stitch coming out early is often not a problem, but call your surgeon if bleeding starts again or the area looks open.

Can I brush my teeth before wisdom teeth removal?

You can and should brush your teeth before wisdom teeth removal. Brushing and flossing on surgery day reduces bacteria near the sockets. If you are having sedation or general anesthesia, follow your fasting instructions and spit out all rinse water rather than swallowing it.
