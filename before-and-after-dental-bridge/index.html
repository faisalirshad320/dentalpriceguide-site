Before and After Dental Bridge: What Changes and What It Costs

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated November 3, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

Before a dental bridge there is a gap from one or more missing teeth; after, a fixed false tooth fills the space, anchored to the neighbouring teeth, restoring the smile and chewing in about two visits. A three-unit bridge averages about $3,600 out-of-network, according to Delta Dental, while CareCredit reports $5,197 for a traditional single-tooth bridge and $1,569 for a Maryland bridge.

Key takeaways
  • A bridge fills a gap with a false tooth fixed to neighbouring teeth.
  • Results usually take two visits over a few weeks.
  • The smile, bite and chewing on that side are restored.
  • Three-unit bridge: about $3,600 out-of-network (Delta Dental).
  • Insurance typically covers 50% to 80% of bridge costs (Delta Dental).

What does a missing tooth look like before a bridge?

Before a dental bridge, a missing tooth leaves a visible gap or a space further back that affects chewing. Over time, the teeth next to the gap can tilt or drift into the space, and the opposing tooth in the other jaw can over-erupt because nothing stops it.

These changes can create food traps, make cleaning harder and alter your bite. A missing front tooth is especially noticeable when smiling and talking, and people often feel self-conscious about it. Our guide to dental bridges explains how they address these problems.

What does a dental bridge look like after it is fitted?

After a dental bridge is fitted, the gap is filled by a false tooth, called a pontic, that is shaped and shaded to match the surrounding teeth. The bridge is fixed in place, so it does not come out like a denture, and the smile looks complete again.

With a traditional bridge, the teeth on either side are covered by crowns that hold the pontic. If those teeth were discoloured or heavily filled, the crowns can also improve their appearance. With a Maryland bridge, small wings are bonded to the backs of the neighbouring teeth instead, so they remain largely untouched.

Which types of bridges give the best before and after results?

Several types of bridges can give excellent before and after results; the best depends on where the gap is and the health of nearby teeth.

  • Traditional bridge: crowns on both neighbouring teeth with a pontic between; strong and widely used.
  • Maryland (resin-bonded) bridge: wings bonded to the backs of neighbouring teeth; conservative, mainly for front teeth.
  • Cantilever bridge: supported by a crown on one side only; used where only one neighbour is available.
  • Implant-supported bridge: anchored to implants rather than natural teeth; used for several missing teeth.

What happens between before and after?

Between before and after, a traditional bridge usually takes two visits over a few weeks. At the first visit, the dentist numbs the area, reshapes the neighbouring teeth for crowns, takes a scan or impression and fits a temporary bridge.

A dental lab then makes the permanent bridge. At the second visit, the temporary bridge is removed, and the permanent one is checked for fit, colour and bite before being cemented. If a tooth was recently extracted, the gum and bone may need a few months to heal before the final bridge is made.

How does a bridge change chewing and speech?

A bridge usually restores chewing on the side where the tooth was missing, so you can bite and grind food more evenly. It also helps keep neighbouring and opposing teeth from shifting, which protects your bite.

A missing front tooth can affect speech, especially sounds made with the tongue against the teeth. Filling the gap with a bridge often improves speech clarity. Most people adjust to a new bridge within a few days.

How should you judge bridge before and after photos?

When judging dental bridge before and after photos, look at how well the pontic matches the shape and colour of the natural teeth, whether the gum around the pontic looks natural, and whether the bridge looks too bulky.

Ask your dentist for photos of bridges they have placed in similar positions, and ask how long ago they were done. A bridge that still looks good several years later is a better indicator of quality than one photographed on the day it was fitted.

How long do bridge results last?

Bridge results commonly last 10 to 15 years or more with good care. The bridge itself is durable; failure most often results from decay or gum disease on the supporting teeth, or from a cracked supporting tooth.

Daily cleaning under the pontic with a floss threader, super floss or water flosser is essential. Regular check-ups catch problems at the edges of the crowns early. A night guard protects the bridge if you grind your teeth.

How much does a dental bridge cost?

A dental bridge costs about $3,600 for a three-unit bridge out-of-network, according to Delta Dental. CareCredit reports averages of $5,197 for a traditional single-tooth bridge and $1,569 for a single-tooth Maryland bridge. Prices vary with materials, the number of teeth and location.

Delta Dental notes insurance typically covers 50% to 80% of bridge costs depending on the plan. See dental bridge cost and dental bridge cost without insurance.

Is a bridge or an implant better for the after result?

A bridge and an implant can both give excellent after results. A bridge is usually faster and cheaper upfront and avoids surgery. An implant does not rely on neighbouring teeth and helps preserve bone, which can keep the gum contour under a replacement tooth more natural long term.

Healthline's implant versus bridge guide and the Cleveland Clinic's prosthodontics overview explain the trade-offs.

Who gets the best before and after results with a bridge?

The best before and after results with a bridge usually come when the teeth on either side of the gap are healthy and strong enough to support it, the gums are free of disease, and the bite does not put excessive force on the bridge. Good oral hygiene before and after treatment matters a great deal.

If the neighbouring teeth already have large fillings or crowns, a traditional bridge can be an especially good choice, because crowning those teeth also protects them. If they are untouched and healthy, an implant or a Maryland bridge may preserve more natural tooth. Gum disease should be treated first, because inflamed gums around a new bridge can recede and spoil the result.

When a tooth has been missing for a long time, neighbouring teeth may have tilted into the gap. Sometimes a short course of orthodontics is recommended first to upright them, which improves both the look and the lifespan of the bridge.

Frequently asked questions

What does a dental bridge look like before and after?

Before a dental bridge, there is a gap from a missing tooth that may affect your smile and chewing. After, a fixed false tooth matched to your natural teeth fills the gap, anchored by crowns or bonded wings on the neighbouring teeth.

How long does it take to get a dental bridge?

A traditional dental bridge usually takes two visits over a few weeks: one to prepare the teeth and take impressions, and one to fit the permanent bridge. If a tooth was recently removed, healing time may be needed first.

How much does a dental bridge cost?

Delta Dental reports an out-of-network average of about $3,600 for a three-unit bridge. CareCredit reports $5,197 for a traditional single-tooth bridge and $1,569 for a Maryland bridge. Insurance typically covers 50% to 80%.

How long does a dental bridge last?

A dental bridge commonly lasts 10 to 15 years or more with good care. Decay or gum disease on the supporting teeth is the most common reason bridges fail, so daily cleaning under the bridge is important.

Does a bridge look natural?

A well-made bridge looks natural, with the false tooth shaped and shaded to match neighbouring teeth. Modern ceramic materials help it blend in. The dentist's and lab's skill matter as much as the material.
