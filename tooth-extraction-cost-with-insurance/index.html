How Much Does a Tooth Extraction Cost With Insurance?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 7, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

With insurance, a tooth extraction is often covered at 80% as a basic procedure on a typical 100-80-50 plan, according to Delta Dental, so you pay about a fifth of the allowed fee after your deductible. Full out-of-network fees run $150 to $310 for a simple extraction and $260 to $460 for a surgical one (Delta Dental).

How much does insurance pay for a tooth extraction?

Insurance often pays about 80% of a tooth extraction after your deductible, because extractions are typically classified as basic procedures on a 100-80-50 plan, according to Delta Dental. You pay the remaining share, called coinsurance.

Some plans classify surgical extractions or oral surgery differently, sometimes at a lower percentage. Check your plan's benefits summary for extractions and oral surgery to see which category applies.

How do you estimate your extraction cost with insurance?

You can estimate your extraction cost with insurance in a few steps.

  1. Find the fee: in-network dentists charge the plan's negotiated fee.
  2. Subtract any unmet deductible.
  3. Apply the coverage percentage, often 80% for extractions.
  4. Check your remaining annual maximum.
  5. Add extras such as X-rays, sedation or a bone graft.

Full out-of-network fees run $150 to $310 for a simple extraction and $260 to $460 for a surgical extraction per tooth, according to Delta Dental. CareCredit reports averages of $177 and $363. Your dental office can give you an exact estimate.

Are X-rays, sedation and grafts covered?

X-rays are usually covered as diagnostic care, often at a high percentage. Sedation coverage varies: local anesthetic is included in the extraction fee, but IV sedation or general anesthesia may be covered only for certain surgical procedures, or not at all.

Bone grafts placed to preserve the socket for a future implant may be covered under oral surgery or major benefits, or excluded if the plan does not cover implants. CareCredit averages an allograft at $811. Ask for each item on your pre-treatment estimate. The Cleveland Clinic explains when grafts are recommended.

Does insurance treat simple and surgical extractions differently?

Insurance may treat simple and surgical extractions differently. Both are often basic services, but some plans place surgical extractions under oral surgery with a different percentage or waiting period.

The fee itself also differs, so even at the same percentage, a surgical extraction costs you more. Your dentist decides which type is needed based on X-rays and the tooth's condition, as described in the Cleveland Clinic's tooth extraction guide.

Do new plans cover extractions right away?

Many new dental plans do not cover extractions right away. Waiting periods for basic services of several months are common, during which only preventive care may be covered.

If you need an urgent extraction because of pain or infection, waiting for coverage may not be safe. Ask your dentist whether treatment can wait, and compare the self-pay price with what insurance would pay later. See tooth extraction cost for self-pay pricing.

Does an in-network dentist lower the cost?

An in-network dentist usually lowers your extraction cost because they accept the plan's negotiated fee and cannot bill you above it for covered services. Out-of-network dentists may charge more than the plan allows, leaving you to pay the difference.

If you are referred to an oral surgeon, check whether they are in your network before booking.

What should you expect after the extraction?

After an extraction, expect some bleeding for a few hours and soreness and swelling for a few days. Bite on gauze as instructed, use cold packs, rest and eat soft foods.

Protect the blood clot by avoiding smoking, straws and vigorous rinsing early on to lower the risk of dry socket, which the Cleveland Clinic explains. See what to eat after tooth extraction, smoking after tooth extraction and what a normal socket looks like after tooth extraction.

Will insurance cover replacing the tooth?

Insurance may cover replacing an extracted tooth with a bridge, partial denture or implant, usually as a major service at a lower percentage. Implant coverage varies; some plans exclude implants or pay only what a bridge would cost.

Ask your dentist to include the replacement plan in your estimate so you understand the full cost of the extraction and what comes after.

Why might your bill be higher than expected?

Your bill might be higher than expected if your deductible was not met, you have reached your annual maximum, the dentist is out of network, the extraction turned out to be surgical, or extras such as sedation, X-rays or a graft were added.

Ask the dental office to explain each line on the bill and compare it with your insurer's explanation of benefits. If something was denied, the office can often resubmit with X-rays or notes.

Should you ask for a pre-treatment estimate?

A pre-treatment estimate is worth asking for whenever an extraction is not an emergency, especially for surgical extractions, multiple teeth, sedation or bone grafts. The dental office sends the planned procedures to your insurer, which replies with what it expects to pay and what your share will be.

Estimates are not guarantees, because your remaining maximum and eligibility can change, but they reveal problems such as waiting periods, exclusions or a sedation code that will not be covered. That gives you time to discuss alternatives before the appointment. For painful emergencies, treatment usually goes ahead first, and the office can still give you an approximate figure.

What if you need several teeth extracted?

If you need several teeth extracted, each tooth is usually billed separately, and the total can use a large part of your annual maximum, especially when combined with sedation or grafts. Ask the office to calculate the full cost with your plan's percentage and remaining maximum.

When extractions are part of a larger plan, such as dentures, ask how the extractions and the replacement teeth will be billed and covered, because extractions are often basic services and dentures major services with a lower percentage.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a tooth extraction with insurance?

With insurance, you often pay about 20% of the allowed fee for an extraction after your deductible, because extractions are commonly covered at 80% as basic care. Full fees run $150 to $310 simple and $260 to $460 surgical out-of-network, according to Delta Dental.

Does insurance cover surgical tooth extraction?

Many plans cover surgical extractions, sometimes as basic care and sometimes under oral surgery at a different percentage. Sedation and bone grafts may be covered differently. Check your plan or request a pre-treatment estimate.

Does dental insurance cover sedation for extractions?

Local anesthetic is included in the extraction fee. IV sedation or general anesthesia may be covered only for certain surgical extractions, or not at all, depending on the plan. Ask your insurer before treatment.

Is there a waiting period for tooth extractions?

Many new dental plans have waiting periods of several months before covering basic services such as extractions. Preventive care is often covered immediately. Check your plan details if you need an extraction soon.

Will insurance pay to replace a pulled tooth?

Insurance may help replace a pulled tooth with a bridge, partial denture or implant, usually as a major service at a lower percentage. Implant coverage varies widely between plans.

This page is general information, not dental advice. Ask your dentist for a written treatment plan and cost estimate, and ask your insurer for a pre-treatment estimate before major work.
