Tooth Abscess Treatment: How Dentists Treat an Abscess and What It Costs

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 29, 20262 price sources
Quick answer

A tooth abscess is treated by a dentist who drains the infection and removes its source, usually with a root canal to save the tooth or an extraction. Antibiotics are added when infection has spread, but they do not cure the tooth on their own. Do not try to drain an abscess at home. A root canal runs about $1,200 for a front tooth out-of-network, per Delta Dental.

Key takeaways
  • Treatment removes the source of infection: root canal, extraction, or gum treatment.
  • Drainage relieves pressure and pain but is only part of the treatment.
  • Antibiotics help when infection spreads but cannot replace dental treatment.
  • Draining an abscess at home is unsafe and can spread infection.
  • Facial swelling with fever or trouble swallowing or breathing is an emergency.

How is a tooth abscess treated?

A tooth abscess is treated by getting rid of the infection and the source that keeps feeding it. A pocket of pus will not clear up permanently until the infected pulp inside the tooth, or the infected gum pocket beside it, is cleaned out or removed.

The dentist first confirms the type of abscess with an exam and X-rays. A periapical abscess forms at the root tip from an infected pulp. A periodontal abscess forms in the gum beside the root, usually from gum disease; the Cleveland Clinic explains this type. The treatment plan depends on which one you have and whether the tooth can be saved.

How does a dentist drain a tooth abscess?

A dentist drains a tooth abscess in a controlled, sterile way after numbing the area. Draining relieves pressure, which often brings fast pain relief, and removes pus so the body can clear the remaining infection.

Drainage can happen through the tooth itself when a root canal is started, through the socket when the tooth is extracted, or through a small cut in the swollen gum. The area is then rinsed with saline. A small drain may occasionally be left in place for a short time. Drainage alone is not the full treatment; the source still needs to be dealt with.

When is a root canal used to treat an abscess?

A root canal is used to treat a tooth abscess when the tooth has enough healthy structure to be saved. The dentist removes the infected pulp, cleans and disinfects the canals, and seals them. Pus can drain through the tooth during the procedure.

After a root canal, the bone around the root usually heals over the following months. Back teeth generally need a crown afterward. Delta Dental's out-of-network data puts a root canal to save an abscessed tooth at about $1,200 for a front tooth and about $1,500 for a molar, before the crown.

When is extraction the better treatment?

Extraction is the better treatment for an abscessed tooth when the tooth is too damaged to restore, cracked below the gum, or surrounded by too little bone. Removing the tooth removes the source of infection, and the socket allows drainage.

Delta Dental lists $150 to $310 for a simple extraction and $260 to $460 for a surgical extraction out-of-network, according to its extraction cost guide. Extraction costs less on the day, but replacing the missing tooth with an implant, bridge or partial denture adds cost later.

Do you need antibiotics for a tooth abscess?

Antibiotics are needed for a tooth abscess when the infection has spread beyond the tooth, for example with facial swelling, fever or swollen lymph nodes, or for people with weakened immune systems. They are not always needed for an abscess that has been fully drained and treated.

Antibiotics cannot reach the dead pulp inside a tooth, which has no blood supply, so they cannot cure the source. Taking antibiotics without treating the tooth usually leads to the infection coming back. Only take antibiotics prescribed for you, exactly as directed, and finish the course unless your prescriber advises otherwise.

Can you drain a tooth abscess at home?

You should not try to drain a tooth abscess at home. Poking, squeezing or cutting the swelling with a needle, pin or blade can push bacteria deeper into the tissues, cause bleeding, introduce new germs, and delay proper treatment.

Sometimes an abscess drains by itself through a small opening in the gum, and the pain eases. That still needs a dental visit, because the source remains. Until you can be seen, you can rinse gently with warm salt water, take over-the-counter pain relievers as directed on the label, and avoid very hot, cold or sugary foods on that side.

Go to an emergency department for facial or neck swelling, swelling under the tongue, fever with swelling, difficulty swallowing or breathing, or trouble opening your mouth. These signs mean the infection may be spreading.

What can you do for a tooth abscess while waiting for the dentist?

While waiting for the dentist, you can ease tooth abscess discomfort with a few safe measures. These do not treat the infection, but they can make the hours or day until your appointment more bearable.

  • Rinse with warm salt water several times a day to keep the area clean.
  • Take over-the-counter pain relievers exactly as directed on the label.
  • Hold a cold pack against the outside of the cheek for short periods.
  • Chew on the other side and avoid very hot, cold or sugary foods.
  • Keep your head raised when lying down, which can ease throbbing.
  • Stay hydrated and eat soft foods.

Avoid putting aspirin or other tablets directly on the gum, which can burn the tissue, and do not apply heat to the face, which can encourage swelling to spread. If swelling increases, you develop a fever, or swallowing becomes difficult while you wait, do not keep waiting for the dental appointment; seek emergency care.

How is a gum (periodontal) abscess treated?

A gum abscess is usually treated by draining it and deep cleaning the infected pocket beside the tooth. The dentist removes tartar and bacteria from the root surface so the gum can heal.

Underlying gum disease then needs ongoing management, such as deep cleanings and more frequent periodontal maintenance visits, to stop new abscesses forming. If the tooth has lost a lot of bone support, extraction may be advised.

What happens after tooth abscess treatment?

After tooth abscess treatment, pain and swelling usually improve within a few days. Mild tenderness is normal. Follow your dentist's instructions on rinsing, eating and medicines, and keep the follow-up appointment.

Your dentist may take X-rays months later to confirm the bone around the root has healed. Preventing future abscesses means treating cavities early, as the Cleveland Clinic's cavity guide describes, keeping regular check-ups and managing gum disease.

Frequently asked questions

How is a tooth abscess treated?

A tooth abscess is treated by draining the infection and removing its source, usually with a root canal to save the tooth or an extraction. Gum abscesses are drained and deep cleaned. Antibiotics are added when infection has spread but do not replace dental treatment.

Can I drain a tooth abscess at home?

You should not drain a tooth abscess at home. Using needles, pins or blades can spread infection deeper, cause bleeding and delay proper care. Rinse with warm salt water and take pain relievers as directed until a dentist can treat the source.

Will antibiotics cure a tooth abscess?

Antibiotics alone will not cure a tooth abscess because they cannot reach the dead pulp inside the tooth. They help control infection that has spread into surrounding tissue. The tooth still needs a root canal or extraction to stop the infection returning.

How much does tooth abscess treatment cost?

Tooth abscess treatment cost depends on the option. Delta Dental's out-of-network figures are about $1,200 for a front tooth root canal and about $1,500 for a molar, or $150 to $310 for a simple extraction and $260 to $460 for a surgical extraction.

When is a tooth abscess an emergency?

A tooth abscess is an emergency when it causes facial or neck swelling, fever, swelling under the tongue, trouble swallowing or breathing, or trouble opening the mouth. Go to an emergency department or call emergency services right away.
