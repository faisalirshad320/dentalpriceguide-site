Braces Before and After: What Changes, How Long It Takes and What It Costs

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 31, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

Braces before and after results usually show crowded or gapped teeth becoming evenly aligned and bites that meet properly, typically over about one to three years. The final result depends on the starting problem, wearing elastics as told, and wearing retainers afterward. Metal braces average $6,343 according to CareCredit.

Key takeaways
  • Braces correct crowding, gaps, rotations and bite problems such as overbites and underbites.
  • Most treatment lasts about one to three years.
  • Bite changes are often as important as visible straightening.
  • Retainers are essential to keep the after result.
  • Metal braces average $6,343; ceramic $5,834; aligners $5,108 (CareCredit).

What changes in a braces before and after?

A braces before and after shows changes in two areas: how the teeth line up within each jaw and how the upper and lower teeth fit together. The first is obvious in photos; the second is often just as important for long-term dental health.

Before treatment, teeth may be crowded, rotated, spaced apart, tilted or sticking out. After treatment, each tooth sits evenly in a smooth arch, front teeth line up with the centre of the face, and the back teeth interlock properly when you bite. A well-finished result also leaves the gums healthy and the teeth easy to clean.

What do crowded teeth look like after braces?

Crowded teeth after braces look evenly spaced and straight, with no overlapping. Crowding happens when there is not enough room in the jaw for all the teeth, so some twist or overlap.

Orthodontists create space by expanding the arch, gently moving teeth outward, slimming the sides of some teeth slightly, or occasionally removing teeth in severe cases. The before and after difference for crowded front teeth is often dramatic, and straight teeth are easier to brush and floss, which lowers the risk of decay and gum disease.

What do gaps look like after braces?

Gaps after braces are closed, with teeth touching neatly side by side. Spaces between teeth can come from small teeth, missing teeth, a large jaw or habits such as tongue thrusting.

Braces close gaps with wires and elastic chains that pull teeth together. Because closed gaps have a strong tendency to reopen, orthodontists often recommend a bonded wire retainer behind the front teeth to hold them. If a tooth is missing, braces may be used to position teeth correctly before an implant or bridge.

How do overbites and underbites change with braces?

Overbites and underbites change with braces as the upper and lower teeth are brought into a healthier relationship. An overbite, where upper front teeth overlap the lower teeth too much, is corrected with wires and rubber bands that adjust how the arches meet. An underbite, where lower teeth sit in front of upper teeth, is often treated with elastics and, in growing children, appliances that guide the jaw.

In adults with a large jaw size difference, braces may be combined with jaw surgery for the best before and after result. Bite corrections can make a noticeable difference to the profile, chewing and wear on the teeth.

How long does it take to see results with braces?

Most people start seeing results with braces within a few months, as early wires level and align the teeth. Gaps and crowding often improve visibly in the first six months. Bite corrections and fine detailing take longer.

Full treatment usually lasts about one to three years. Mild cases finish sooner; complex bite problems or adult treatment can take longer. Keeping every appointment, avoiding broken brackets and wearing elastics as instructed help reach the "after" stage on schedule.

Why do before and after photos vary so much?

Before and after photos vary because every starting point is different. A person with mild crowding will show a subtle change, while someone with a severe overbite and crowding may show a transformation in both smile and facial profile.

When looking at an orthodontist's before and after gallery, look for cases similar to yours, check that the bite is shown as well as the front smile, and ask how long each case took. Photos taken from the same angle and lighting are the most honest comparisons.

How do you keep your braces results?

You keep your braces results by wearing retainers as instructed. Teeth naturally tend to drift back toward their old positions, especially in the first year after braces come off, and continue to shift gradually throughout life.

Orthodontists may provide removable retainers, bonded wire retainers behind the front teeth, or both. Many now recommend wearing removable retainers at night indefinitely. Skipping retainers is one of the most common reasons people need braces a second time. Replace lost or broken retainers promptly.

Do different braces give different results, and what do they cost?

Different types of braces can achieve similar results for many cases, but some complex bite problems are handled more predictably with fixed braces than with aligners. Your orthodontist will advise which type suits your case.

CareCredit reports average costs of $6,343 for metal braces, $5,834 for ceramic, $9,221 for lingual and $5,108 for clear aligners. Delta Dental cites $3,000 to $7,000 for traditional metal braces, and Pearl lists comprehensive treatment at $3,680 to $9,650 nationally. When should you start braces for the best results?

Starting braces at the right time can make results easier to achieve. The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first orthodontic check no later than age 7, as noted in Delta Dental's parent guide, because some jaw problems are easier to correct while a child is growing.

Adults can also achieve excellent results, though treatment may take a little longer. Orthodontic insurance benefits, when available, often have a lifetime maximum; Delta Dental's coverage guide explains how they work, and Healthline offers more background on braces costs.

What health benefits come with braces results?

Braces results bring health benefits beyond a straighter smile. Teeth that line up evenly are easier to brush and floss, so plaque is less likely to build up in tight overlapping spots. That lowers the risk of cavities and gum disease over the long term.

A corrected bite spreads chewing forces more evenly across the teeth. Teeth that meet poorly can wear down unevenly, chip, or put extra strain on the jaw joints. Protruding front teeth are also more likely to be injured in falls and sports, and bringing them back into line lowers that risk.

Some people notice improvements in chewing comfort and speech after an open bite or crossbite is corrected. These functional changes rarely show up in a photo, but they are often the most important part of the after result.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to see results from braces?

Most people see visible results from braces within a few months, especially for crowding and gaps. Full treatment, including bite correction and fine adjustments, usually takes about one to three years depending on the case.

Do braces change your face?

Braces can change the face subtly, especially when correcting overbites, underbites or protruding teeth, which can affect lip position and profile. Most changes are modest. Major jaw differences in adults may need surgery combined with braces for significant facial changes.

Will my teeth move back after braces?

Teeth tend to move back after braces if retainers are not worn. Wearing retainers as instructed, often at night long term, keeps teeth in their new positions. A bonded retainer behind the front teeth adds extra stability.

Can adults get good results from braces?

Adults can get excellent results from braces as long as their teeth and gums are healthy. Treatment may take a little longer than for teens because adult bone is denser, and some adults choose less visible options such as ceramic braces or aligners.

How much do braces cost?

Braces cost about $6,343 for metal braces, $5,834 for ceramic, $9,221 for lingual and $5,108 for clear aligners on average, according to CareCredit. Delta Dental cites $3,000 to $7,000 for traditional metal braces. Your quote depends on your case and location.
