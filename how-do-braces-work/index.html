How Do Braces Work? From Their Invention to Fixing an Overbite

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 27, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

Braces work by applying light, constant pressure through brackets and a wire, which makes the bone around each tooth root slowly reshape so the tooth moves. Modern braces developed in the early 20th century, and bonded brackets became common in the 1970s. Braces can fix many overbites, sometimes with elastics. Metal braces average $6,343, per CareCredit.

Key takeaways
  • Braces move teeth by gently reshaping the bone around the roots.
  • Brackets hold the wire; the wire delivers steady force; elastics correct the bite.
  • Modern fixed braces developed in the early 1900s and became much less bulky later in the century.
  • Braces can correct many overbites, often combined with rubber bands.
  • A first orthodontic check is recommended no later than age 7.

How do braces move teeth?

Braces move teeth by applying gentle, constant pressure in a planned direction. Teeth are not fixed rigidly in the jaw. Each root sits in a socket lined by a thin ligament, and the bone around it is living tissue that constantly rebuilds itself.

When a brace pushes on a tooth, the ligament is compressed on one side and stretched on the other. Cells on the compressed side break down bone, making room, while cells on the stretched side lay down new bone behind the moving root. Over weeks and months, this remodelling lets the tooth travel through the jaw and settle in its new position.

The force has to be light. Heavy force can damage roots and gums and actually slow movement, which is why braces treatment takes months rather than days.

What do the different parts of braces do?

Each part of a set of braces has a specific job in moving teeth.

  • Brackets: small handles bonded to each tooth that hold the wire and transfer force to the tooth.
  • Archwire: the springy wire that wants to return to its ideal arch shape, pulling teeth along with it.
  • Ligatures: tiny elastic or wire ties that hold the archwire in each bracket.
  • Bands: metal rings around back teeth that anchor the system.
  • Elastics: rubber bands between the upper and lower teeth that correct the bite.
  • Springs and chains: extra parts that open or close spaces between teeth.

Modern archwires made of nickel-titanium respond to body heat and deliver gentle, consistent force over long periods, which makes treatment more comfortable than older steel-only systems. Our guide to how much braces cost covers what each type of appliance adds to the fee.

When were braces invented?

People have tried to straighten teeth for thousands of years, but braces as we know them developed in the late 1800s and early 1900s. Early orthodontists designed metal appliances, and in the early 20th century the field became an organised dental specialty with standard ways of classifying bite problems.

For much of the 20th century, braces meant metal bands wrapped around every tooth, which were bulky and visible. In the 1970s, dental adhesives made it possible to glue brackets directly onto the front of each tooth, which made braces smaller and more comfortable. Stainless steel replaced earlier metals, and later decades brought ceramic brackets, lingual braces behind the teeth, heat-activated wires and clear aligners.

Can braces fix an overbite?

Braces can fix many overbites, especially when the problem comes from the position of the teeth rather than the size of the jaws. An overbite, where the upper front teeth overlap the lower teeth too much vertically or stick out too far, is one of the most common reasons people get braces.

Orthodontists correct overbites with a combination of wires that level the teeth and rubber bands that pull the lower teeth forward relative to the upper teeth. In growing children, appliances may also guide jaw growth. In adults with a significant jaw size difference, braces are sometimes combined with jaw surgery for the best result. Your orthodontist determines which applies after X-rays and an exam.

What other problems can braces fix?

Braces can fix a wide range of tooth and bite problems beyond overbites. Common reasons for treatment include crowding, gaps between teeth, underbites, crossbites, open bites, teeth that are rotated or tipped, and impacted teeth that need to be guided into place.

Correcting these problems is not just cosmetic. Crowded teeth are harder to clean and more prone to decay and gum disease, and a poor bite can wear teeth unevenly or strain the jaw. The Delta Dental guide for parents describes common signs a child may need an orthodontic evaluation.

How do you get braces?

Getting braces starts with a consultation with an orthodontist, a dentist who has completed extra specialist training. Your general dentist can refer you, or you can contact an orthodontist directly in most cases.

  1. Consultation: an exam to discuss your concerns and whether treatment is needed.
  2. Records: X-rays, photos and digital scans or impressions of your teeth.
  3. Treatment plan: options, estimated time and a written fee.
  4. Insurance and payment: checking orthodontic benefits and setting up a payment plan.
  5. Fitting: brackets bonded and wire placed, typically in one to two hours.

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first orthodontic evaluation no later than age 7, as noted by Delta Dental. Adults can get braces at any age if their teeth and gums are healthy.

Do all types of braces work the same way?

All types of braces work on the same biological principle of gentle force remodelling bone, but they deliver that force differently. Metal and ceramic braces sit on the front of the teeth; lingual braces sit behind them; clear aligners use a series of removable plastic trays.

Fixed braces work around the clock, while aligners only work when worn. Lingual braces are hidden but cost the most. Ceramic brackets blend in but are more fragile than metal. See clear braces for a comparison and braces colors if you choose metal brackets with coloured ties.

How much do braces cost?

Braces cost about $6,343 on average for metal braces, $5,834 for ceramic braces, $9,221 for lingual braces and $5,108 for clear aligners, according to CareCredit. Delta Dental cites $3,000 to $7,000 for traditional metal braces, and Pearl lists comprehensive treatment at $3,680 to $9,650 nationally.

Frequently asked questions

How do braces work to straighten teeth?

Braces straighten teeth by applying light, constant pressure through brackets and an archwire. The pressure makes the bone around each root break down on one side and rebuild on the other, so the tooth slowly moves into its new position over months.

When were braces invented?

Braces as we know them developed in the late 1800s and early 1900s, when orthodontics became a dental specialty. Bonding brackets directly to teeth became common in the 1970s, making braces smaller. Ceramic brackets, lingual braces and clear aligners came later.

Can braces fix an overbite?

Braces can fix many overbites, usually by combining wires that align the teeth with rubber bands that adjust how the upper and lower teeth meet. Severe overbites caused by jaw size differences in adults may also need jaw surgery. An orthodontist decides after an exam and X-rays.

How do I get braces?

To get braces, book a consultation with an orthodontist, either directly or through a referral from your dentist. The orthodontist examines your teeth, takes X-rays and scans, and gives a treatment plan with a time estimate and fee before brackets are fitted.

Do braces hurt when they are working?

Braces usually cause mild soreness for a few days after they are fitted and after each adjustment as teeth begin to move. Between adjustments most people feel little discomfort. Soft foods and over-the-counter pain relief used as directed on the label help.
