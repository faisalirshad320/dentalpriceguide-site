Cheap Options for Missing Teeth: Alternatives to Dental Implants and What They Cost

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 29, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

The cheapest options for missing teeth are usually removable partial dentures, temporary flippers and full dentures, followed by dental bridges. Each generally costs less upfront than an implant, which runs $2,800 to $5,600 per tooth without dental benefits according to Delta Dental. The trade-offs are comfort, stability and lifespan.

Key takeaways
  • Removable partial dentures and flippers are usually the cheapest ways to replace teeth.
  • Bridges are fixed in place and typically cost less than implants for one tooth.
  • Full dentures replace a whole arch at lower upfront cost than implant-supported options.
  • Implants cost more upfront but do not rely on neighbouring teeth and help preserve bone.
  • Insurance often covers bridges and dentures even when implants are excluded.

What are the alternatives to dental implants?

The main alternatives to dental implants are dental bridges, removable partial dentures, temporary flippers and full dentures. Each replaces missing teeth without placing a post in the jawbone.

  • Dental bridge: a false tooth fixed to crowns on the teeth either side of the gap.
  • Resin-bonded (Maryland) bridge: a false tooth held by small wings bonded to the backs of neighbouring teeth.
  • Removable partial denture: false teeth on a metal or acrylic base that clips onto remaining teeth.
  • Flipper: a lightweight acrylic partial, often used as a temporary tooth.
  • Full denture: replaces all teeth in an arch.
  • Leaving the gap: sometimes acceptable for back teeth, with monitoring.

Is a bridge cheaper than an implant?

A traditional bridge is usually cheaper than an implant for replacing a single tooth, at least upfront. A three-unit bridge covers the missing tooth and the two neighbouring teeth, which are reshaped and crowned to hold it.

The downside is that healthy neighbouring teeth must be filed down, and if one of those supporting teeth develops decay or fails, the whole bridge may need replacing. Bridges also do not stimulate the jawbone where the tooth is missing, so the bone there can shrink over time. Healthline's implant versus bridge guide compares the two in detail.

Are partial dentures the cheapest option?

Removable partial dentures are often among the cheapest options for replacing several missing teeth. A cast metal framework partial is sturdier and more comfortable; an all-acrylic or flexible partial may cost less but tends to be bulkier or less durable.

Partials can feel strange at first, may affect speech briefly, and need to be removed for cleaning and usually at night. The clasps that hold them can put stress on the teeth they grip. Still, they restore appearance and chewing at a fraction of the cost of multiple implants.

What is a flipper tooth and how much is a fake tooth?

A flipper is a lightweight removable acrylic plate with one or a few false teeth, often used as a temporary replacement while healing before an implant or bridge. It is usually one of the least expensive ways to fill a visible gap quickly.

Flippers are not designed for long-term chewing and can break, so they are best as a short-term solution. People asking "how much is a fake tooth" are often comparing a flipper, a partial denture, a bridge and an implant; the cost rises roughly in that order, along with stability and lifespan.

Are dentures cheaper than full-mouth implants?

Full dentures are much cheaper upfront than full-mouth implants. CareCredit reports an average of $15,176 for All-on-4 implants on one arch, and replacing both arches with implants costs considerably more.

Conventional dentures sit on the gums and can move, especially lower dentures, and the jawbone gradually shrinks without tooth roots. Implant-supported or snap-in dentures are a middle ground that uses a few implants to stabilise a denture. Healthline's dentures versus implants guide outlines the trade-offs, and our cost of full mouth dental implants page has the full numbers.

Can you get veneers with missing teeth?

You cannot place a veneer where a tooth is missing, because a veneer needs a natural tooth to bond to. Veneers only cover the front surface of existing teeth.

For a smile makeover with gaps, dentists combine options: a bridge, implant or partial to replace missing teeth, plus veneers or crowns on remaining teeth for a matching appearance. Ask a dentist or prosthodontist for a full plan; the Cleveland Clinic explains what prosthodontists do.

How do you choose the right tooth replacement for your budget?

Choosing the right tooth replacement for your budget starts with a clear picture of your mouth and your priorities. Ask your dentist to lay out every realistic option, not only the most expensive one.

  1. How many teeth are missing, and where? A single front tooth, a few back teeth and a full arch call for different solutions.
  2. How healthy are the neighbouring teeth? Heavily filled neighbours may benefit from crowns, making a bridge more sensible; untouched healthy neighbours favour an implant or partial.
  3. How much bone is left? Implants need enough bone or a graft.
  4. What are your health factors? Smoking, diabetes and some medications affect implant success.
  5. What can you afford now, and later? Consider maintenance and replacement costs over many years.

Many people start with an affordable option such as a partial denture and move to implants later when budget allows. Your dentist can plan the first option so that it does not rule out the second.

Are cheaper options cheaper in the long run?

Cheaper tooth replacement options are not always cheaper over the long run. Bridges, partials and dentures generally need adjustment, relining or replacement over the years, while a well-maintained implant can last decades.

Bone loss is another factor. Without a root or implant, the jawbone where teeth are missing gradually shrinks, which can change facial appearance and make dentures fit less well. If you later choose an implant, you may need a bone graft first; CareCredit reports averages of $811 for an allograft and $2,779 for an autograft. The Cleveland Clinic explains bone grafts.

Does insurance cover implant alternatives?

Insurance often covers bridges and dentures as major services even on plans that exclude implants. Delta Dental describes a typical 100-80-50 structure with major services at 50% up to the annual maximum, using $1,500 as an example, in its coverage guide.

Some plans apply an alternate benefit: they pay for implants only up to what a bridge or denture would cost. See what insurance covers for dental implants, plus dental implant cost and types of dental implants if you are still weighing implants. CareCredit also reports a $466 average for the cone-beam CT scan usually needed for implant planning.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way to replace missing teeth?

The cheapest ways to replace missing teeth are usually a flipper or a removable partial denture for a few teeth, and conventional full dentures for a whole arch. They cost less upfront than bridges or implants but are less stable and need more maintenance over time.

How much does it cost to replace a tooth?

Replacing a tooth costs least with a flipper or partial denture, more with a bridge, and most with an implant. Delta Dental puts a single implant at $2,800 to $5,600 without dental benefits. Your dentist can quote each option for your situation.

Is a bridge better than an implant?

A bridge is usually cheaper and faster than an implant, but it requires filing down neighbouring teeth and does not prevent bone loss. An implant costs more upfront, stands alone and can last decades. The better choice depends on your teeth, bone, health and budget.

Can you get veneers if you have missing teeth?

Veneers cannot replace missing teeth because they need a natural tooth to bond to. Gaps are filled with a bridge, implant or partial denture, and veneers or crowns can then be used on remaining teeth for a matching smile.

Does insurance pay for alternatives to implants?

Many dental plans cover bridges and dentures as major services, often at about 50% up to the annual maximum, even when implants are excluded. Some plans cover implants only up to the cost of a bridge or denture. Check your plan or request a pre-treatment estimate.
