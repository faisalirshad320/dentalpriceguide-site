How Long Do Braces Take? Treatment Length, Getting Them On and Off

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 26, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

Most people wear braces for about one to three years, with many cases finishing in roughly 18 to 24 months. Putting braces on usually takes one to two hours, and taking them off takes about an hour. Your orthodontist estimates your own timeline after planning; cost runs with complexity, with metal braces averaging $6,343 according to CareCredit.

Key takeaways
  • Typical braces treatment lasts about one to three years.
  • Fitting braces takes about one to two hours; removal takes about an hour.
  • Severe crowding, bite problems and adult bone usually mean longer treatment.
  • Wearing elastics and avoiding broken brackets keeps treatment on schedule.
  • A retainer after braces is what keeps teeth from shifting back.

How long do you have to wear braces?

Most people wear braces for about one to three years. Simple cases, such as mild crowding or a small gap, can finish toward the shorter end, while complex bite corrections often take two years or more.

The length of braces treatment depends on how far the teeth must move, how the upper and lower jaws relate, and whether any teeth are impacted or need to be removed. Teeth move slowly by design. Gentle, steady force lets the bone around each root remodel safely, and pushing faster risks damaging roots and gums.

Your orthodontist gives an estimate at the start, but that number is a forecast, not a promise. Teeth respond at different speeds in different people, and progress checks may shorten or extend the plan. Ask at each visit whether you are on track.

How long does it take to put braces on?

Putting braces on usually takes about one to two hours. The appointment is painless because no needles or drilling are involved, though keeping your mouth open for a long time can be tiring.

  1. Cleaning and drying: the teeth are polished and kept dry with cheek retractors.
  2. Etching: a mild gel prepares the tooth surface so the glue bonds well.
  3. Bonding brackets: each bracket is positioned and the glue is hardened with a curing light.
  4. Bands on back teeth: metal rings may be fitted around molars.
  5. Placing the wire: the archwire is threaded through the brackets and held with ties.

Teeth often feel sore for a few days afterward as they start to move. Soft foods help, and many people choose their braces colors at this first visit.

How long does it take to get braces off?

Getting braces off takes about an hour. The orthodontist uses pliers to squeeze each bracket so it pops off the tooth, then removes the remaining glue and polishes the enamel smooth.

Removal day often includes scans or impressions for your retainer, or the retainer may be fitted the same day. Teeth can feel slightly loose and strange for a few days, and that is normal. Your orthodontist decides when treatment is finished based on how your teeth fit together, not on the calendar alone.

What makes braces take longer?

Braces take longer when teeth need to move further or when treatment is interrupted. Some factors are outside your control and others depend on daily habits.

  • Severity: large overbites, underbites, crossbites and severe crowding need more movement.
  • Age: adult bone is denser, so teeth can move a little more slowly than in teens.
  • Impacted teeth: canines stuck in the bone take extra time to guide into place.
  • Broken brackets: each repair can set treatment back while the tooth is not being moved properly.
  • Not wearing elastics: rubber bands correct the bite; skipping them stalls progress.
  • Missed appointments: long gaps between adjustments slow movement.
  • Gum problems: inflamed gums may require pauses for treatment.

Do different types of braces take different amounts of time?

Different types of braces take broadly similar amounts of time for the same case, because the speed of tooth movement is set by biology rather than by the bracket material. Metal, ceramic and lingual braces all move teeth with wires and gentle force.

Clear aligners are the main exception. Aligner treatment depends heavily on how many hours a day the trays are worn. Patients who wear aligners as instructed can finish on a timeline similar to braces, while those who leave trays out often fall behind and need extra aligners.

Lingual braces sit behind the teeth and may need slightly longer adjustment appointments, but overall treatment length is usually comparable. Ceramic brackets are a little more fragile than metal, so breakages can add delays if you are not careful with hard foods.

Age also changes the picture. Children and young teens are often treated while the jaw is still growing, which can make some bite corrections easier. Adults can get excellent results too, but moving teeth through mature bone may take a little longer, and gum health has to be good before treatment begins.

Can you make braces work faster?

The most reliable way to make braces work faster is to avoid delays: follow instructions exactly, keep every appointment and protect your brackets. Simple habits save weeks across the whole treatment.

Wear elastics for the hours your orthodontist prescribes, avoid hard and sticky foods, brush and clean around brackets so your gums stay healthy, and call promptly about loose brackets instead of waiting for the next visit. Some practices offer devices or techniques marketed to speed tooth movement; ask your orthodontist whether there is good evidence for them in your case before paying extra.

Clear aligners can sometimes match or beat braces timelines for mild cases, but only if the trays are worn most of the day. See clear braces for how ceramic brackets and aligners compare.

What happens after braces come off?

After braces come off, a retainer holds the teeth in their new positions while the surrounding bone and gums stabilise. Teeth tend to drift back toward where they started, and that tendency continues for years.

Many orthodontists ask you to wear a removable retainer full time at first and then at night, or they bond a thin fixed retainer wire behind the front teeth. Skipping the retainer is one of the most common reasons people need braces a second time.

Does longer treatment cost more?

Longer braces treatment often costs more because complex cases need more appointments and more orthodontic work. Most orthodontists charge one total fee based on the expected complexity rather than billing per month.

CareCredit reports averages of $6,343 for metal braces, $5,834 for ceramic braces, $9,221 for lingual braces and $5,108 for clear aligners. Pearl lists comprehensive treatment at $3,680 to $9,650 nationally. The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first orthodontic check no later than age 7, as Delta Dental notes. Frequently asked questions

How long do braces usually take?

Braces usually take about one to three years, with many cases finishing in roughly 18 to 24 months. Mild crowding can be quicker, while large bite problems, impacted teeth and adult treatment often take longer. Your orthodontist gives a personal estimate after reviewing X-rays and scans.

How long does it take to put braces on?

Putting braces on usually takes about one to two hours. The orthodontist cleans and prepares the teeth, bonds each bracket with a curing light, fits bands on back teeth if needed and places the archwire. The process is not painful, though teeth often ache for a few days afterward.

How long does it take to get braces off?

Getting braces off takes about an hour. The brackets are popped off with special pliers, the leftover glue is removed and the teeth are polished. Retainer scans or fitting often happen at the same visit.

Can braces be done in 6 months?

Braces can sometimes be done in about six months for very mild cases, usually focused only on straightening front teeth. These short-term approaches do not correct the bite fully and are not suitable for everyone. Ask your orthodontist what a short treatment would and would not fix.

Why are my braces taking so long?

Braces may take longer because of broken brackets, not wearing elastics, missed appointments, gum inflammation or a more complex bite than first expected. Teeth also move at different speeds in different people. Ask your orthodontist at your next visit what is slowing progress.
