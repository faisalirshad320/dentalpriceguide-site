Do You Have to Get Wisdom Teeth Removed? Benefits of Keeping Them and When Removal Is Needed

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 27, 20262 price sources
Quick answer

No, you do not always have to get wisdom teeth removed. Wisdom teeth that are fully erupted, healthy, properly positioned, in a good bite and easy to clean can often stay. Removal is usually advised when they are impacted, causing pain, infection, cysts, decay or damage to neighbouring teeth (Cleveland Clinic).

Key takeaways
  • Healthy, fully erupted wisdom teeth that you can clean well may not need removal.
  • Impacted or partly erupted wisdom teeth are the most common reason for removal.
  • Problems include infection of the gum flap, decay, cysts and damage to second molars.
  • Keeping wisdom teeth means monitoring them with regular check-ups and X-rays.
  • Removal cost ranges from $720 (four erupted teeth) to $3,120 (four surgical, sedated) at Delta Dental's out-of-network averages.

Do wisdom teeth have to be removed?

Wisdom teeth do not have to be removed if they are healthy and cause no problems. Dental guidance has moved away from automatically removing every wisdom tooth, and many dentists now recommend watching healthy wisdom teeth rather than removing them by default.

Wisdom teeth are the third molars, the last teeth to come in, usually in the late teens or early twenties. In many people the jaw does not have enough room for them, so they come in crooked, partly or not at all. In others they erupt fully, line up with the other teeth and work like any other molar.

Whether you need removal depends on the position of each tooth, the health of the surrounding gum and bone, and whether you can keep the area clean. A dentist or oral surgeon assesses this with an exam and a panoramic X-ray or 3D scan.

What are the benefits of keeping wisdom teeth?

Keeping healthy wisdom teeth avoids surgery and its risks, recovery time and cost. Any operation carries small risks, including dry socket, infection and, for lower teeth, rare nerve injury that can cause numbness of the lip or tongue.

Healthy wisdom teeth can also be useful. They add chewing surface, and if a neighbouring molar is lost later, a well-positioned wisdom tooth can sometimes support a bridge or be moved into the gap with orthodontics. There is no health benefit to removing a tooth that is healthy, functional and cleanable.

The trade-off is ongoing monitoring. Wisdom teeth sit far back and are harder to brush and floss, so they need careful cleaning and regular X-rays to catch problems early.

Why do people get their wisdom teeth removed?

People get their wisdom teeth removed mainly because the teeth are impacted or causing problems. The Cleveland Clinic's guide to impacted wisdom teeth describes the most common issues.

  • Impaction: the tooth is stuck under the gum or bone, often at an angle.
  • Pericoronitis: infection of the gum flap over a partly erupted tooth, causing pain and swelling.
  • Decay: wisdom teeth and the back of the neighbouring molar are hard to clean and decay easily.
  • Gum disease and bone loss around the back molars.
  • Damage to the second molar: an angled wisdom tooth can push on or decay the tooth in front.
  • Cysts: fluid-filled sacs can form around impacted teeth and damage bone.
  • Orthodontic or surgical planning: occasionally removed before jaw surgery or other treatment.

What happens if you don't get your wisdom teeth removed?

If you don't get problem wisdom teeth removed, the issues usually continue or worsen over time. Repeated gum infections, decay in hard-to-reach places and damage to the second molar are the most common consequences.

If your wisdom teeth are healthy and well positioned, nothing bad may happen at all. Many people keep their wisdom teeth for life. The key is regular dental check-ups so that any change, such as a new cavity, gum pocket or bone change on an X-ray, is caught while it is easy to treat.

Seek prompt care for swelling around a wisdom tooth, difficulty opening your mouth, fever, pus or a bad taste. Seek emergency care for swelling that spreads to the face or neck or for trouble swallowing or breathing.

Is it better to remove wisdom teeth young?

Removing wisdom teeth in the late teens or early twenties is often easier than later in life, because the roots are not fully formed and the bone is less dense. Recovery tends to be quicker and complications less frequent in younger patients.

That does not mean every teenager should have wisdom teeth removed. It means that if a dentist predicts that a wisdom tooth will cause problems, earlier removal is usually smoother. For adults whose wisdom teeth have caused no trouble for years, the balance often favours leaving them alone and monitoring.

Can wisdom teeth cause pain without needing removal?

Wisdom teeth can cause temporary pain while they erupt, and that pain does not always mean they must come out. Mild tenderness and gum irritation as the tooth breaks through often settle on their own.

Pain that keeps returning, swelling of the gum flap, or pain with a bad taste usually points to infection and is a common reason dentists recommend removal. Our guide to wisdom teeth pain explains the causes and when to be concerned.

How do you decide whether to keep or remove wisdom teeth?

Deciding whether to keep or remove wisdom teeth is a conversation with your dentist or oral surgeon based on X-rays and an exam. Useful questions to ask include:

  1. Are my wisdom teeth impacted, partly erupted or fully erupted?
  2. Is there any decay, gum infection or bone change around them?
  3. Are they close to the nerve in the lower jaw?
  4. What is the risk of problems if I keep them?
  5. What would removal involve, and what type of anesthesia?
  6. What will it cost, and how much will my insurance pay?

A second opinion is reasonable if you are told to remove healthy, symptom-free teeth.

How much does wisdom teeth removal cost?

Wisdom teeth removal cost depends on how many teeth come out and how difficult they are. Delta Dental's out-of-network averages are $720 for all four erupted teeth removed non-surgically, $550 per tooth for one surgical extraction and $3,120 for all four removed surgically with sedation, according to its wisdom teeth cost guide.

CareCredit reports $835 for one impacted wisdom tooth and $3,340 for four. Insurance typically covers about 50% to 80% of the total cost, according to Delta Dental. Frequently asked questions

Do wisdom teeth have to be removed?

Wisdom teeth do not have to be removed if they are fully erupted, healthy, well positioned and easy to clean. Removal is usually recommended when they are impacted or cause pain, infection, decay, cysts or damage to neighbouring teeth. A dentist decides with an exam and X-rays.

What happens if you don't get your wisdom teeth removed?

If healthy wisdom teeth are left in place, often nothing happens and they work like other molars. Problem wisdom teeth left untreated can cause repeated gum infections, decay, cysts and damage to the second molar. Regular check-ups help catch these changes early.

Why do people get their wisdom teeth removed?

People get wisdom teeth removed mostly because they are impacted, partly erupted and repeatedly infected, decayed, hard to clean, or damaging the tooth in front. Some are removed preventively when X-rays suggest future problems.

Are there benefits to keeping wisdom teeth?

Keeping healthy wisdom teeth avoids surgery, recovery time, cost and the small risks of complications such as dry socket and nerve injury. A well-positioned wisdom tooth can also add chewing surface or, occasionally, support a future bridge.

At what age should wisdom teeth be removed?

When wisdom teeth need removal, the late teens or early twenties are often the easiest time because roots are not fully formed and healing tends to be faster. Adults can still have them removed safely, but recovery may take longer.
