What Can You Not Eat With Braces? Foods to Avoid, Foods to Enjoy and Why It Matters

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 21, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

With braces, you should not eat hard, sticky or chewy foods, such as popcorn, nuts, ice, hard candy, caramel, taffy, gum, beef jerky and bagels, or bite straight into whole apples and corn on the cob. These foods bend wires and pop off brackets, which slows treatment. Delta Dental covers braces basics.

Key takeaways
  • Avoid hard, sticky and chewy foods, and cut firm foods into small pieces instead of biting into them.
  • Popcorn, nuts, ice, hard candy, caramel and gum are the most common causes of broken brackets.
  • Pizza, soft chips and soft chocolate are usually fine in moderation if you eat them carefully.
  • Broken brackets and bent wires mean extra visits and can lengthen treatment.
  • Your orthodontist's food list for your specific appliance takes priority over general advice.

What can't you eat with braces?

With braces you cannot safely eat foods that are hard, sticky or chewy, or foods you have to bite into with your front teeth. Those foods put sudden force on the brackets and wires, which can break the bond holding a bracket to the tooth or bend the wire out of shape.

  • Hard foods: nuts, ice, hard candy, hard pretzels, hard taco shells, corn nuts, crusty bread, and popcorn kernels.
  • Sticky foods: caramel, taffy, toffee, gummy candy, licorice, fruit snacks and chewing gum.
  • Chewy foods: bagels, beef jerky, tough steak, thick pizza crust and chewy rolls.
  • Bite-into foods: whole apples, raw carrots, corn on the cob and ribs. These are fine once cut into small pieces or taken off the bone or cob.

Sugary and acidic drinks are not banned, but they are risky. Soda, sports drinks and juice bathe the teeth in sugar and acid, and plaque collects easily around brackets. That can leave white marks on the enamel when the braces come off. Drink them rarely, with a meal, and rinse with water afterward.

Habits count too. Chewing pens, biting fingernails and opening packages with your teeth can damage braces just as easily as food.

What can you eat with braces?

You can eat most soft and tender foods with braces, including pasta, rice, eggs, fish, soft meats, cooked vegetables, soft fruits, yogurt, soup and soft bread. The rule of thumb is that if a food is easy to cut with a fork, it is usually safe.

  • Breakfast: pancakes, scrambled eggs, oatmeal, yogurt, smoothies and soft muffins.
  • Lunch: soft sandwiches, wraps, mac and cheese, soup, tuna or chicken salad.
  • Dinner: pasta, meatballs, tender chicken cut small, fish, rice dishes, mashed potatoes and steamed vegetables.
  • Snacks: bananas, berries, sliced apples, cheese, pudding and soft crackers.

The first few days after braces go on, and after each tightening appointment, teeth often feel sore. Soft foods like soup, mashed potatoes and smoothies are easiest then. Over-the-counter pain relievers taken as the label directs can help, and the soreness usually fades within several days.

If you choose clear aligners instead of brackets, you remove the aligners to eat, so there are few food restrictions. You do need to brush before putting the aligners back in. Our guide to clear braces compares ceramic braces and aligners.

Can you eat popcorn, chips and pizza with braces?

You should not eat popcorn with braces, but you can usually eat pizza and some soft chips if you are careful. The difference lies in how hard the food is and whether small pieces can lodge under the wire.

Popcorn is one of the foods orthodontists warn about most. Unpopped kernels are hard enough to break brackets, and the thin hulls wedge between the brackets and gums, where they are hard to remove and can irritate the gums. Most orthodontists say to skip popcorn until treatment ends.

Chips depend on the type. Hard tortilla chips, kettle-cooked chips and corn chips are risky. Thin, light potato chips or puffed snacks, eaten one small piece at a time with the back teeth, are usually tolerated, though they still leave crumbs around brackets.

Pizza is generally fine. Cut it into bite-sized pieces rather than biting off the tip with your front teeth, and leave thick or crunchy crust edges. Avoid toppings that are very chewy or hard.

If a bracket comes loose or a wire starts poking your cheek, call your orthodontist. Orthodontic wax can cover a sharp end until your appointment. Seek prompt care if a wire injury causes bleeding that will not stop or a sore that swells or becomes infected.

How to eat with braces without breaking brackets

The safest way to eat with braces is to cut food into small pieces and chew slowly with your back teeth. Front teeth are built for biting, and that tearing motion is what most often pops brackets loose.

  • Slice apples, pears and raw vegetables thinly, or steam firmer vegetables until tender.
  • Cut meat off the bone and corn off the cob before eating.
  • Tear bread, bagels and pizza crust into small pieces instead of biting through them.
  • Let frozen treats soften a little rather than biting into them hard.
  • Carry a travel toothbrush, floss threaders or a water flosser so you can clean after eating away from home.

Brush after every meal if you can. Food trapped around brackets feeds the bacteria that cause cavities and swollen gums, and both can delay treatment or leave marks once the braces come off.

Can you chew gum or eat candy with braces?

Most orthodontists advise against chewing gum with braces, because gum sticks to the brackets and wires and can pull on them. Some orthodontists allow sugar-free gum in small amounts, so ask yours before chewing any.

Candy with braces comes down to texture. Hard candy, lollipops, jawbreakers, caramels, taffy, gummies and anything with nuts are on almost every do-not-eat list. Soft chocolate without nuts, caramel or wafers, such as a plain chocolate bar or peanut-butter cups, is usually the safest choice.

Even safe candy raises the risk of decay around the brackets. Eat sweets with a meal rather than as a separate snack, then brush or at least rinse with water. Ask your orthodontist about a fluoride rinse if you are prone to cavities.

What does it cost if you break your braces?

Breaking braces with the wrong food can lead to extra repair visits, and some offices charge separately for repeated repairs. Each broken bracket also means the tooth stops moving correctly until it is fixed, which can stretch out total treatment time.

Treatment itself is a large expense, which is why protecting it makes sense. Frequently asked questions

Can you chew gum with braces?

Chewing gum with braces is usually discouraged because gum sticks to brackets and wires and can loosen them or bend the wire. Some orthodontists permit small amounts of sugar-free gum, which is less sticky and does not feed decay. Ask your own orthodontist before chewing any gum during treatment.

Can you eat popcorn with braces?

Popcorn is not recommended with braces. Hard unpopped kernels can break brackets, and the thin hulls lodge between brackets and gums, where they are hard to clean out and can cause irritation or infection. Most orthodontists advise avoiding popcorn entirely until the braces come off.

Can you eat pizza with braces?

Pizza is usually fine with braces if you eat it carefully. Cut it into small pieces instead of biting with your front teeth, skip thick or crunchy crust edges, and avoid hard or very chewy toppings. Brush afterward, since cheese and sauce collect around brackets.

What candy can you eat with braces?

The safest candy with braces is soft chocolate without nuts, caramel or crunchy wafers, such as a plain chocolate bar. Avoid hard candy, lollipops, gummies, taffy and caramels, which can break brackets or stick to wires. Eat sweets with meals and brush soon after to protect enamel around the brackets.

Can you eat chips with braces?

Some chips are okay with braces and some are not. Hard tortilla chips, corn chips and kettle-cooked chips can break brackets, so avoid them. Thin, light potato chips or puffed snacks eaten one at a time with the back teeth are usually tolerated, but follow your orthodontist's advice.

How long do you have to avoid these foods with braces?

You need to avoid hard, sticky and chewy foods for the whole time your braces are on, not just the first few weeks. Brackets can break at any stage of treatment. After the braces come off, your orthodontist will tell you which foods to avoid with your retainer, if any.
