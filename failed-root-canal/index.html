Root Canal Treatment Side Effects and Signs of a Failed Root Canal

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated November 1, 20264 price sources
Quick answer

Normal root canal side effects are tenderness, mild swelling and sensitivity to biting for a few days. A failed root canal is different: pain, swelling, a gum bump or tenderness that returns or persists weeks, months or years later, often from leftover or new infection. Failed root canals can usually be retreated by a dentist or endodontist (Delta Dental).

Key takeaways
  • Mild tenderness for a few days after a root canal is a normal side effect.
  • Failure signs include returning pain, swelling, a gum bump or a dark area on X-ray.
  • Common causes are missed canals, leaking crowns, new decay and cracks.
  • Options include retreatment, endodontic surgery or extraction.
  • Root canal fees: about $1,200 (front) to about $1,500 (molar) out-of-network (Delta Dental).

What are the normal side effects of root canal treatment?

Normal side effects of root canal treatment are mild and short-lived. Most people notice some tenderness when biting, a bruised feeling in the gum and mild swelling for a few days. The jaw may feel tired from keeping the mouth open, and the injection site may be sore.

These side effects usually respond to over-the-counter pain relievers taken as directed on the label and fade within a week. Avoid chewing hard foods on the treated tooth until the permanent filling or crown is in place. Read more about root canal pain and what is normal.

What less common side effects can occur?

Less common side effects after a root canal include a flare-up of pain and swelling in the first days, a cracked tooth before the crown is placed, a temporary filling that falls out, and, rarely, an allergic reaction to materials or medicines.

A flare-up can happen when inflammation around the root tip becomes more active after treatment. It usually settles with pain relief and, in some cases, a short course of antibiotics prescribed by your dentist. Rarely, a small amount of irrigating solution or filling material can extend beyond the root tip and cause discomfort that needs follow-up.

Contact your dentist for swelling that increases after the first two or three days, fever, severe pain, or a bite that feels too high. Seek emergency care for facial swelling that spreads or trouble swallowing or breathing.

What are the signs of a failed root canal?

The signs of a failed root canal usually appear after a period of feeling fine, sometimes months or years after treatment. Watch for these:

  • Pain that returns in the treated tooth, especially when biting.
  • Swelling in the gum near the tooth or in the face.
  • A pimple-like bump on the gum that may drain fluid.
  • Tenderness when touching or tapping the tooth.
  • A bad taste from drainage.
  • A dark area at the root tip on an X-ray that does not shrink or gets larger.

Some failing root canals cause no symptoms and are only found on routine X-rays, which is one reason check-ups matter.

Why do root canals fail?

Root canals fail when bacteria remain in or re-enter the tooth's canal system. Several situations make that more likely:

  • Missed or untreated canals: some teeth have extra or very narrow canals that are hard to find.
  • Incomplete cleaning: curved or calcified canals can be difficult to clean fully.
  • Delayed crown: waiting too long lets bacteria leak past a temporary filling.
  • Leaking or broken crown: gaps let bacteria back into the tooth.
  • New decay under or around the restoration.
  • A crack in the root, which can be hard to detect and often cannot be repaired.

What is root canal retreatment?

Root canal retreatment is a second root canal on a previously treated tooth. The dentist or endodontist removes the crown or filling, takes out the old filling material from the canals, looks for missed canals, cleans and disinfects again, and reseals the tooth.

Retreatment is more complex than the original root canal, so it often costs more and is commonly done by an endodontist using a microscope. A new crown or filling is usually needed afterward. When successful, retreatment lets you keep your natural tooth.

What is an apicoectomy?

An apicoectomy is a minor surgical procedure used when retreatment through the crown is not possible or has not worked. The endodontist makes a small opening in the gum, removes the infected tissue and the tip of the root, and seals the root end.

This approach is useful when a tooth has a post, a well-made crown that the dentist does not want to remove, or anatomy that blocks access from above. Recovery is usually similar to other minor oral surgery, with a few days of swelling.

When is extraction the best option after a failed root canal?

Extraction is the best option after a failed root canal when the tooth has a vertical root fracture, too little healthy structure remains to restore it, or repeated treatment has not resolved the infection. Removing the tooth eliminates the source of infection.

After extraction, you can replace the tooth with an implant, a bridge or a partial denture, each with its own cost. Your dentist will compare the chances of success and the total costs of saving versus replacing the tooth.

How much does fixing a failed root canal cost?

Fixing a failed root canal usually costs more than the original treatment because retreatment is more complex. For reference, Delta Dental lists first-time root canals at about $1,200 for a front tooth, $1,300 for a premolar and $1,500 for a molar out-of-network, according to its root canal cost guide. CareCredit reports averages of $984 and $1,337, and Pearl lists $685 to $1,800.

Delta Dental notes these fees do not include the crown or filling, which often needs replacing after retreatment. Insurance commonly covers 50%–80% of root canals after the deductible. See root canal cost for full pricing.

How can you lower the risk of root canal failure?

You can lower the risk of root canal failure by getting the final crown or filling placed promptly, keeping up good oral hygiene and attending regular check-ups with X-rays. A well-sealed crown is one of the most important protections, as the Cleveland Clinic's crown guide explains.

Wear a night guard if you grind your teeth, avoid chewing ice and hard objects, and report any returning pain or swelling early. Learn more about what a root canal procedure involves and how long a root canal takes.

Frequently asked questions

What are the side effects of root canal treatment?

Common side effects of root canal treatment are tenderness when biting, mild swelling and a bruised feeling for a few days. Less commonly, a flare-up of pain and swelling occurs. Contact your dentist if symptoms worsen after the first few days.

How do I know if my root canal failed?

A root canal may have failed if pain, swelling or tenderness returns, a pimple-like bump appears on the gum, or an X-ray shows a dark area at the root tip that is not healing. Some failures cause no symptoms and are found on routine X-rays.

Can a failed root canal be fixed?

A failed root canal can often be fixed with retreatment, in which the canals are cleaned and sealed again, or with a minor surgery called an apicoectomy. If the root is cracked or the tooth cannot be restored, extraction may be recommended.

How long after a root canal can it fail?

A root canal can fail at any time, from weeks to many years after treatment. Early failure often relates to missed canals or incomplete cleaning; later failure is more often caused by a leaking crown, new decay or a crack.

Is retreatment more expensive than the first root canal?

Root canal retreatment is usually more expensive than the first root canal because removing the old filling material and finding missed canals is more complex. It is often done by an endodontist, and a new crown may be needed.
