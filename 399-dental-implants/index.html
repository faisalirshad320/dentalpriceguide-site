What do 399 dental implant ads usually include?

Advertisements for 399 dental implants usually include only one piece of the treatment. Most often the price covers the implant post, the small titanium screw placed in the jawbone, or it is a "starting from" price that applies to very simple cases.

A complete implant tooth has three main parts: the implant post in the bone, an abutment that connects to it, and a crown that looks like a tooth. Treatment also often needs a 3D scan, removal of the old tooth and sometimes a bone graft. When all of these are added, the total is typically many times the advertised figure.

Low headline prices are not necessarily dishonest, but they can be misleading if you assume they mean a finished tooth. The fine print usually explains what is and is not included.

How much does a complete dental implant really cost?

A complete dental implant costs $2,800 to $5,600 for one tooth without dental benefits, according to Delta Dental's 2022 internal data. That range covers the implant, abutment and crown together.

CareCredit's procedure cost list reports a national average of $2,143 for a single full-size implant and $4,344 for a standard endosteal implant per tooth. The figures differ because the sources measure different things, but neither comes close to a few hundred dollars for a finished tooth. Our dental implant cost guide explains the full breakdown.

What extra costs are added to a cheap implant?

Extra costs added to a cheap implant offer usually include every step outside the advertised component. Ask specifically about each one.

  • Consultation and exam: sometimes free, sometimes charged.
  • 3D cone-beam CT scan: CareCredit reports an average of $466.
  • Tooth extraction: if the failing tooth is still present.
  • Bone graft: CareCredit averages are $811 for an allograft and $2,779 for an autograft. The Cleveland Clinic explains when grafts are needed.
  • Abutment: the connector between implant and crown.
  • Crown: the visible tooth, often the most expensive single part.
  • Sedation: if you want to be sedated during surgery.
  • Temporary tooth: worn while the implant heals.

What should you ask before booking a low-price implant?

Before booking a low-price implant, ask for a written, itemised quote that covers the whole treatment from start to finished tooth. These questions help you compare offers fairly.

  1. Does the price include the implant, abutment and crown?
  2. What brand and type of implant is used, and is it widely supported by other dentists?
  3. Are the scan, extraction, graft and sedation included?
  4. What is the crown made of?
  5. Who places the implant, and what training and experience do they have?
  6. What warranty applies, and what happens if the implant fails?
  7. How many follow-up visits are included?

An implant from an obscure brand can be hard for another dentist to repair or replace parts on later, so the implant system matters as well as the price.

Are cheap dental implants safe?

Cheap dental implants can be safe when the clinic uses reputable implant systems, plans properly with imaging and is staffed by trained, experienced clinicians. The risk is not the price itself but corners being cut to reach it.

Warning signs include pressure to commit on the day, no 3D imaging before surgery, unclear information about who will do the surgery, and vague answers about materials or warranties. Implant failure, infection or nerve injury are rare when treatment is planned well, but fixing a failed implant can cost more than doing it right the first time.

How can you get dental implants for less?

You can often get dental implants for less through legitimate routes that reduce the total price rather than just the headline number.

  • Dental schools: supervised residents may offer implants at reduced fees.
  • Insurance: many plans pay about 50% for major work up to the annual maximum; see what insurance covers for dental implants.
  • Financing and HSA or FSA funds to spread or reduce the cost.
  • Comparing itemised quotes from several practices.
  • Considering alternatives such as a bridge or partial denture if they suit you.

Healthline's implant versus bridge and dentures versus implants guides outline the trade-offs.

What about cheap full-mouth implant offers?

Cheap full-mouth implant offers deserve the same scrutiny as single-tooth deals. CareCredit reports an average of $15,176 for All-on-4 implants on one arch, and both arches cost more.

Ask whether a quoted full-mouth price covers one arch or both, whether the teeth are a temporary set or the final prosthesis, and how many implants are placed per arch. See the cost of full mouth dental implants and types of dental implants for a fuller picture.

Does insurance help with implant costs?

Insurance can help with implant costs when your plan includes implants. Delta Dental describes a typical 100-80-50 plan that pays 50% for major work such as implants, up to the annual maximum, with $1,500 as an example maximum, in its coverage guide.

Because a single implant can exceed that maximum, insurance usually reduces rather than covers the cost. Ask the clinic to send a pre-treatment estimate to your insurer, and confirm whether any promotional price can be combined with insurance.

Is a 399 implant offer worth considering?

A 399 implant offer can be a starting point for a consultation, but it is rarely the price you will pay for a finished tooth. Treat the advertised number as an invitation to get a full quote, not as the total.

The Cleveland Clinic's prosthodontics guide explains the role of specialists who plan implant teeth. Choose a provider you trust, with transparent pricing and a clear plan, even if the final quote is higher than an ad suggests.

Frequently asked questions

Are 399 dental implants real?

Ads for 399 dental implants are usually real offers for one component, such as the implant post, or a promotional starting price. They rarely include the abutment, crown, scans, extractions or bone grafts needed for a finished tooth. Ask for a complete itemised quote.

How much does a full dental implant cost?

A full single dental implant, including the post, abutment and crown, costs $2,800 to $5,600 without dental benefits according to Delta Dental. CareCredit reports a $2,143 national average for a single full-size implant and $4,344 for a standard endosteal implant.

What is not included in cheap dental implant offers?

Cheap dental implant offers often exclude the 3D scan, tooth extraction, bone graft, abutment, crown, sedation and temporary tooth. These items can add up to far more than the advertised price. Read the fine print and get a written quote.

Are cheap dental implants safe?

Cheap dental implants can be safe if the clinic uses reputable implant systems, plans with 3D imaging and has experienced clinicians. Be cautious of high-pressure sales, missing imaging and vague answers about materials, surgeons or warranties.

How can I get affordable dental implants?

Affordable dental implants may be available through dental school clinics, insurance plans that cover implants, financing, HSA or FSA funds and comparing itemised quotes. A bridge or partial denture may also cost less depending on your situation.

This page is general information, not dental advice. Ask your dentist for a written treatment plan and cost estimate, and ask your insurer for a pre-treatment estimate before major work.
