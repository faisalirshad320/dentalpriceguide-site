Headgear Braces: What Orthodontic Headgear Does and What to Expect

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated November 3, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

Headgear braces are orthodontic appliances worn outside the mouth, usually at home and at night, that apply gentle force to guide jaw growth or move back teeth in growing children. They are most often used to correct overbites, underbites and crowding when braces alone are not enough. Headgear is usually part of a braces fee, which averages $6,343 for metal braces (CareCredit).

Key takeaways
  • Headgear guides jaw growth and tooth position in children who are still growing.
  • Common types are cervical, high-pull and reverse-pull (face mask) headgear.
  • It is usually worn for about 12 to 14 hours a day, mostly evenings and nights.
  • Consistent wear is essential; results depend on hours worn.
  • Headgear is less common today because other appliances can sometimes do the same job.

What is headgear for braces?

Headgear for braces is an appliance that connects to bands on the back teeth and attaches to a strap that goes around the neck or over the head. It uses the head or neck as an anchor to apply steady force to the teeth and jaws from outside the mouth.

Because it relies on growth, headgear is used mainly in children and young teens whose jaws are still developing. It can slow the forward growth of the upper jaw, encourage the lower jaw to catch up, or move upper molars back to create space. Braces then finish aligning the teeth.

What are the types of orthodontic headgear?

The main types of orthodontic headgear differ in where the strap anchors and which direction they pull.

  • Cervical headgear: a strap around the back of the neck; often used to correct overbites by guiding upper molars and jaw growth.
  • High-pull headgear: a strap over the top and back of the head; used when a child has an open bite or long face pattern and the orthodontist wants to avoid extruding back teeth.
  • Reverse-pull headgear (face mask): rests on the forehead and chin and pulls the upper jaw forward; used for underbites.

Your orthodontist chooses the type based on X-rays, facial measurements and the specific bite problem.

What problems does headgear fix?

Headgear fixes bite problems caused by the relationship between the upper and lower jaws, rather than just the position of individual teeth. The most common are an overbite with the upper jaw positioned too far forward, an underbite with the upper jaw too far back, and crowding where back teeth need to move to create space.

By influencing growth, headgear can sometimes reduce the need for tooth extractions or jaw surgery later. Results depend heavily on the child's growth pattern and how consistently the headgear is worn.

How long do you wear headgear each day?

Most orthodontists ask children to wear headgear about 12 to 14 hours a day, usually after school, during the evening and overnight while sleeping. It is not typically worn at school or during sports.

The total treatment period often ranges from several months to a year or more, depending on the problem and the child's growth. Consistency matters more than anything else: headgear only works during the hours it is worn, and skipping nights slows progress considerably.

Is headgear uncomfortable?

Headgear can feel strange and uncomfortable for the first few days, and teeth may ache as they respond to the force. Most children adapt within a week or two, especially when they wear it mainly at night.

Sleeping can take adjustment; lying on the back is often easiest. Soft foods help during sore days, and over-the-counter pain relief can be used as directed on the label. Headgear should never be worn during rough play or sports, and the orthodontist will show how to put it on and take it off safely.

Remove the headgear and call your orthodontist if it breaks, a part pokes into the face or eyes, or you notice sores, bleeding or severe pain. Follow your orthodontist's safety instructions carefully.

Is headgear still used today?

Headgear is used less often today than in the past, because other appliances can sometimes achieve similar results with less visibility. Functional appliances worn inside the mouth, fixed devices that reposition the jaw, and temporary anchorage devices (small screws placed in the bone) can provide anchorage without external straps.

Headgear remains a useful tool in specific cases, especially reverse-pull face masks for young children with underbites. Ask your orthodontist why headgear is recommended and whether alternatives would work for your child.

What age is headgear used?

Headgear is most effective in children who are still growing, often between about 7 and 12 years old, sometimes into the early teens. Reverse-pull headgear for underbites is usually started early, because the upper jaw responds best when the child is young.

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first orthodontic evaluation no later than age 7, as noted by Delta Dental, partly so that growth-based treatments like headgear can be timed well.

How do you look after headgear?

Looking after headgear keeps it working and comfortable. Wash the neck pad or straps regularly with mild soap and water, keep the inner face bow clean, and store the headgear in a safe place when not worn.

Check for loose or bent parts and bring the headgear to every orthodontic appointment so the fit and force can be adjusted. Never bend the face bow yourself.

How much does headgear add to the cost of braces?

Headgear is usually included in the overall orthodontic treatment fee rather than charged separately, although practices differ. Ask whether appliances such as headgear, expanders and retainers are included in your quote.

CareCredit reports average costs of $6,343 for metal braces, $5,834 for ceramic and $5,108 for clear aligners. Delta Dental cites $3,000 to $7,000 for traditional metal braces, and Pearl lists comprehensive treatment at $3,680 to $9,650. How can parents help a child wear headgear?

Parents can help a child wear headgear by building it into a fixed evening routine, praising consistent wear and keeping a simple log of hours on the fridge. Explaining what the headgear does and how it will help their smile and bite gives children a sense of purpose.

Some orthodontists provide wear charts or rewards. If your child struggles, tell the orthodontist; they can adjust comfort or discuss alternatives. Healthline's braces cost guide offers more background for families planning orthodontic care.

Frequently asked questions

What is headgear for braces used for?

Headgear for braces is used to guide jaw growth and move back teeth in growing children, correcting problems such as overbites, underbites and crowding. It works alongside braces when the jaws themselves need to be guided, not just the teeth.

How long do you have to wear headgear?

Headgear is usually worn about 12 to 14 hours a day, mostly in the evening and overnight, for several months to a year or more. The exact period depends on the bite problem and the child's growth. Consistent wear is essential for results.

Is headgear painful?

Headgear is not usually painful, but teeth may ache for a few days when it is first worn or adjusted, and it can feel awkward at first. Most children adapt within a week or two. Soft foods and over-the-counter pain relief can help.

Do kids still wear headgear for braces?

Some kids still wear headgear, though it is less common than in the past because other appliances can sometimes do the same job. Reverse-pull face masks for underbites in young children remain a common use.

Does headgear cost extra?

Headgear is usually included in the overall orthodontic fee, but some practices charge separately for appliances. Ask what your quote includes. Metal braces average $6,343 according to CareCredit.
