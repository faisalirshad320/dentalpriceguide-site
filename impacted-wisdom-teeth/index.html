Impacted Wisdom Teeth: Symptoms, When Removal Is Needed and What It Costs

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 21, 20262 price sources
Quick answer

Impacted wisdom teeth are third molars that cannot fully come through the gum because they are blocked by bone, gum or another tooth. Many cause no trouble, but some lead to pain, infection or damage to nearby teeth. Removing one impacted tooth averages $835, according to CareCredit; local prices vary.

Key takeaways
  • Impacted wisdom teeth are trapped partly or fully in the gum or jawbone, often because the jaw has too little room.
  • Some impacted teeth cause no symptoms and can be monitored with regular X-rays; others need removal because of infection, decay or damage to neighboring teeth.
  • CareCredit lists removal of one impacted wisdom tooth at an average of $835 and four impacted teeth at $3,340.
  • Most people recover from removal within days to a couple of weeks; dry socket is the best-known complication.
  • Get urgent dental care for spreading swelling, fever, trouble swallowing or breathing, or bleeding that will not stop.

What are impacted wisdom teeth?

Impacted wisdom teeth are third molars that cannot erupt into a normal position because something blocks them, usually the jawbone, overlying gum or the second molar in front. Wisdom teeth are the last teeth to arrive, typically in the late teens or early twenties, and many jaws simply do not have enough room for them.

Dentists describe impacted wisdom teeth in two ways. The first is depth: a soft tissue impaction is covered only by gum, a partial bony impaction is partly in bone, and a full bony impaction sits entirely within the jaw. The second is angle:

  • Mesioangular. Tilted forward toward the front of the mouth, the most common pattern.
  • Vertical. Upright but stuck below the gumline.
  • Horizontal. Lying on its side and often pushing against the roots of the second molar.
  • Distoangular. Tilted backward toward the back of the mouth.

A partially erupted wisdom tooth, where part of the crown breaks through the gum, is a common source of trouble because food and bacteria get trapped under the gum flap. The Cleveland Clinic explains the different types and how dentists find them on X-rays.

What are the symptoms of impacted wisdom teeth?

Impacted wisdom teeth often cause no symptoms at all, but when they do, the most common signs are pain or tenderness at the back of the jaw, swollen or bleeding gums, bad breath or a bad taste, and difficulty opening the mouth fully.

Symptoms tend to appear when an impacted tooth becomes infected or starts affecting nearby teeth. Typical problems include:

  • Pericoronitis. Infection of the gum flap over a partially erupted tooth, causing swelling, pain and sometimes a bad taste or pus.
  • Decay. Wisdom teeth and the back of the second molar are hard to clean, so cavities develop easily.
  • Gum disease. Deep pockets can form behind the second molar.
  • Cysts. Rarely, a fluid-filled sac forms around an impacted tooth and can damage bone.
  • Crowding concerns. Some people notice pressure, although whether wisdom teeth shift other teeth is debated.

Jaw pain has many possible causes, including grinding and jaw joint problems, so an exam and X-ray are needed to confirm the source. Our guide to wisdom teeth pain covers home relief while you wait to be seen and how to tell when pain needs prompt attention.

Do impacted wisdom teeth always need to be removed?

Impacted wisdom teeth do not always need to be removed; a dentist or oral surgeon usually recommends removal when the tooth is causing problems or is likely to, and monitoring when it is healthy and symptom-free. Professional guidance has moved toward individual decisions rather than routine removal for everyone.

Removal is commonly advised when an impacted tooth causes:

  • Repeated infections such as pericoronitis.
  • Decay in the wisdom tooth or damage to the second molar.
  • Gum disease or bone loss around neighboring teeth.
  • Cysts or other changes seen on X-rays.
  • Ongoing pain linked to the tooth.

When an impacted tooth is fully covered by bone and causes no problems, many dentists suggest watching it with periodic X-rays. Age matters too: roots are less developed and bone is softer in younger patients, so surgery and healing are often easier earlier in adulthood. The Cleveland Clinic describes how surgeons weigh these factors. Your own dentist's or surgeon's advice should guide the decision, because it is based on your X-rays and health history.

What happens during impacted wisdom teeth removal and recovery?

Impacted wisdom teeth removal is a surgical extraction in which the surgeon opens the gum, may remove some bone or divide the tooth into pieces, takes the tooth out and closes the area, usually with stitches. The procedure is done under local anesthesia, often with IV sedation or general anesthesia for impacted teeth.

Recovery typically follows a predictable pattern. Swelling and soreness usually peak in the first two or three days and then ease, and most people return to normal routines within about a week, though deeper sockets take longer to fill in fully. Our wisdom teeth recovery timeline walks through each stage.

Common aftercare advice includes:

  • Bite on gauze to control early bleeding and rest with your head raised.
  • Use cold packs on the cheek during the first day or so.
  • Avoid straws, smoking and spitting forcefully, which can dislodge the clot.
  • Eat soft, cool foods at first. See what to eat after wisdom teeth removal for ideas.
  • Begin gentle salt-water rinses when your surgeon says it is safe.

Dry socket happens when the blood clot is lost too early, exposing bone and causing throbbing pain a few days after surgery. The Cleveland Clinic describes symptoms and treatment. Always follow your surgeon's own aftercare instructions, which take priority over general advice.

How much does impacted wisdom teeth removal cost?

Impacted wisdom teeth removal costs an average of $835 for one impacted tooth and $3,340 for four impacted teeth, according to the Synchrony Average Procedural Cost Study published by CareCredit. These are national averages, and local prices vary.

Delta Dental 2021 data gives out-of-network averages of $550 per tooth for a surgical extraction, $3,120 for all four wisdom teeth removed surgically with sedation or anesthesia, and $720 for all four when they are fully erupted and need no surgery. The two sources differ because Delta Dental reports out-of-network claims while CareCredit reports survey averages, and the procedures they group together are not identical.

The gap between $720 and $3,120 for four teeth shows how much impaction changes the bill: surgery, bone removal and sedation all add cost. Delta Dental notes that insurance typically covers about 50% to 80% of the total cost. Our full guide to wisdom teeth removal cost covers sedation fees, medical insurance and ways to pay less.

When should you see a dentist urgently for an impacted wisdom tooth?

You should see a dentist or oral surgeon urgently for an impacted wisdom tooth if you have swelling that is spreading, fever, pus, severe pain not controlled by over-the-counter medicine, or difficulty opening your mouth. These signs can mean an infection that needs prompt treatment.

Go to an emergency room right away if swelling affects your eye, neck or floor of the mouth, or if you have trouble swallowing or breathing. After surgery, call your surgeon for bleeding that does not slow with firm pressure on gauze, fever, worsening pain after the third day, numbness that does not wear off, or a bad taste with pus.

For milder, on-and-off soreness, book a regular dental visit so the tooth can be checked and X-rayed. Early assessment gives more options than waiting for an infection to flare.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my wisdom teeth are impacted?

You can only know for sure if your wisdom teeth are impacted with a dental exam and X-ray. Signs that suggest impaction include a wisdom tooth that never fully appears, gum swelling or tenderness at the back of the jaw, a bad taste, and pain when chewing. Many impacted teeth cause no symptoms.

Can impacted wisdom teeth be left alone?

Impacted wisdom teeth can sometimes be left alone if they are healthy, cause no symptoms and show no damage on X-rays. Dentists usually recommend regular monitoring in that case. Removal is more likely to be advised if the tooth causes infections, decay, gum disease or harm to the neighboring molar.

How much does it cost to remove one impacted wisdom tooth?

Removing one impacted wisdom tooth costs an average of $835, according to CareCredit's survey. Delta Dental lists a surgical extraction of one wisdom tooth at an average of $550 per tooth out-of-network. Prices vary by region, impaction depth and sedation type, and insurance often covers part of the cost.

Is impacted wisdom teeth removal painful?

Impacted wisdom teeth removal itself should not be painful because the area is numbed and sedation is often used. Soreness and swelling afterward are normal and usually peak in the first few days. Follow your surgeon's pain plan, and call them if pain worsens after several days, which can signal dry socket.

How long does recovery take after impacted wisdom teeth removal?

Recovery after impacted wisdom teeth removal usually takes several days to two weeks for most daily activities, with swelling easing after the first few days. The bone underneath takes longer to heal fully. Recovery varies by how deep the teeth were, so follow your surgeon's instructions on diet, rinsing and activity.
