Rubber Bands for Braces: What They Do, How to Wear Them and Common Problems

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 26, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

Rubber bands for braces (orthodontic elastics) hook between brackets on the upper and lower teeth to pull the jaws and bite into alignment, correcting problems such as overbites, underbites and crossbites that wires alone cannot fix. They usually need to be worn almost full time, changed several times a day, for as long as your orthodontist prescribes (Healthline).

Key takeaways
  • Rubber bands correct how the upper and lower teeth meet, not just how teeth line up.
  • Most patients wear them about 20 or more hours a day, removing them only to eat and brush.
  • Bands lose strength quickly and are usually replaced several times a day.
  • Soreness for a few days is normal when starting or changing bands.
  • Skipping elastics is one of the most common reasons braces take longer.

What do rubber bands do for braces?

Rubber bands for braces apply force between the upper and lower jaws. Brackets and wires straighten teeth within each arch, but they cannot by themselves change how the two arches fit together. Elastics supply that missing connection.

By pulling in a specific direction, rubber bands move groups of teeth forward or backward relative to the opposite jaw. That is how orthodontists correct overbites, underbites, crossbites, open bites and midlines that do not match. In growing children, elastics can also influence how the jaws develop.

Not everyone needs rubber bands. Your orthodontist adds them when the bite needs correcting, usually after the teeth have been levelled and aligned with the first few wires.

Are rubber bands the same as the coloured bands on braces?

Rubber bands for the bite are different from the tiny coloured bands around each bracket. The small bands, called ligatures or O-rings, hold the archwire in the bracket slot. They are changed by the orthodontist at each adjustment and are the ones people pick colours for.

Bite elastics are larger, removable bands that you put on and take off yourself. They stretch between hooks on different brackets, usually from the upper teeth to the lower teeth. If you enjoy choosing ligature colours, see our guide to braces colors.

What are the different rubber band patterns?

Rubber band patterns depend on the bite problem being corrected. Your orthodontist chooses the hooks, the band size and the strength, and shows you how to place them.

  • Class II elastics run from the upper front area to the lower back teeth to reduce an overbite.
  • Class III elastics run the opposite way to correct an underbite.
  • Vertical and box elastics pull teeth together to close an open bite or settle the final bite.
  • Crossbite elastics run across the bite to move a tooth that sits on the wrong side.
  • Triangle elastics connect three hooks to close small vertical gaps.

Patterns often change during treatment as the bite improves. Ask for a diagram or photo of your current pattern so you place them correctly at home.

How long do you have to wear rubber bands each day?

Most orthodontists ask patients to wear rubber bands about 20 or more hours a day, taking them out only to eat and to brush. Consistency matters more than strength: bands worn all day, every day move teeth steadily, while bands worn on and off allow teeth to bounce back.

Elastics lose much of their pull within hours, so they are usually replaced several times a day, often after each meal and before bed. Carry a pack of spares in your bag. Wash your hands before putting them on, and use the small hook tool your orthodontist gives you if your fingers find it awkward.

The total time you wear elastics varies from a few months to much of the treatment, depending on how much correction is needed.

Do rubber bands for braces hurt?

Rubber bands for braces often cause soreness for the first few days after you start wearing them or move to a new pattern. The teeth may ache when chewing, and the jaw can feel tired.

Soft foods, cold drinks and over-the-counter pain relievers taken as directed on the label usually help. The soreness fades as the teeth adjust. Taking the bands out because they hurt tends to make it worse, because putting them back in restarts the discomfort. Wearing them consistently gets you through the sore phase fastest.

Contact your orthodontist if a band snaps repeatedly, a hook breaks, your jaw clicks or locks painfully, or you notice a tooth becoming very loose. Follow your own orthodontist's instructions over any general advice.

What happens if you do not wear your rubber bands?

If you do not wear your rubber bands as prescribed, your braces treatment usually takes longer. The bite stays uncorrected, and the orthodontist may need to extend treatment or switch to other appliances to finish the job.

Skipping elastics can also undo progress, because teeth move back between the times you wear them. Orthodontists can often tell from how the teeth fit whether the bands are being worn. If you struggle to remember, set phone reminders after meals and keep spares in every bag.

What should you do if a rubber band breaks or you run out?

If a rubber band breaks, replace it with a fresh one from your pack straight away. Bands snap from time to time, especially when you yawn widely or eat with them in, and a single break is nothing to worry about.

If you run out of elastics, call your orthodontist's office; most will post or hand out more packs at no charge. Do not substitute household rubber bands or bands from a different pack size, because the wrong size and strength can push teeth in the wrong direction. If a hook on a bracket breaks so you cannot attach a band, let the office know before your next scheduled visit.

What are the best tips for wearing braces rubber bands?

The best tips for wearing braces rubber bands focus on routine. Small habits make it easier to wear them the full number of hours.

  1. Change bands after every meal and before bed.
  2. Keep spare packs at home, at school or work, and in your bag.
  3. Use a mirror until placement becomes automatic.
  4. Never double up bands unless told to; extra force can harm teeth.
  5. Throw away old bands; do not reuse stretched ones.
  6. Tell your orthodontist if you are allergic to latex; latex-free elastics exist.

Do rubber bands add to the cost of braces?

Rubber bands usually do not add to the cost of braces, because elastics are normally included in the orthodontic fee. Not wearing them, however, can lengthen treatment, and some offices charge extra if treatment runs well beyond the planned time.

CareCredit reports averages of $6,343 for metal braces, $5,834 for ceramic braces and $5,108 for clear aligners on its procedure cost list. Clear aligners can also use elastics with small attachments. See how much braces cost, clear braces and how much braces cost without insurance.

Frequently asked questions

What do rubber bands do for braces?

Rubber bands for braces connect the upper and lower teeth to correct the bite. They pull the jaws or groups of teeth in a set direction to fix overbites, underbites, crossbites and open bites, which brackets and wires cannot correct on their own.

How long do you have to wear rubber bands with braces?

Most patients wear rubber bands about 20 or more hours a day, removing them only to eat and brush, for as many months as the orthodontist prescribes. Some people need them for a short phase; others wear them for much of treatment.

Can I eat with rubber bands on my braces?

Most orthodontists ask you to remove rubber bands to eat and then put fresh ones back on right after brushing. Eating with bands in can stretch or snap them. Follow your orthodontist's instructions, as some patterns may be worn during meals.

How often should I change braces rubber bands?

You should usually change braces rubber bands several times a day, such as after each meal and before bed. Elastics lose their pulling force within hours, so fresh bands keep the force steady. Never reuse stretched or old bands.

Do rubber bands make braces work faster?

Rubber bands help braces finish on time by correcting the bite, which wires alone cannot do. Wearing them consistently prevents delays. Skipping them is one of the most common reasons braces treatment takes longer than planned.
