Cavity Filling: What It Is, How Long It Takes and What to Expect

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 21, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

A cavity filling repairs a tooth damaged by decay: the dentist numbs the area, removes the decayed part and seals the space with a material such as tooth-colored composite or silver amalgam. A single cavity filling usually takes about 20 minutes to an hour, depending on its size and location. Cleveland Clinic explains how cavities form.

Key takeaways
  • A cavity filling removes decay and seals the tooth to stop the cavity from growing.
  • Most single fillings take about 20 minutes to an hour; several fillings or deep back-tooth cavities take longer.
  • Composite fillings match tooth color; amalgam fillings are silver and very durable on back teeth.
  • Composite fillings cost $145 to $480 on a front tooth and $165 to $530 on a back tooth without insurance (Delta Dental).
  • Insurance typically covers 50%–80% of basic procedures such as fillings, per Delta Dental.

What is a cavity filling?

A cavity filling is a dental restoration that replaces the part of a tooth destroyed by decay and seals the space so bacteria cannot keep spreading. Fillings are the most common treatment for cavities that have gone through the outer enamel but have not reached the nerve.

A cavity forms when bacteria in plaque feed on sugars and produce acid that dissolves enamel. Once that damage breaks through the surface, the tooth cannot repair itself. Cleveland Clinic notes that very early enamel damage can sometimes be reversed with fluoride, but a true hole needs a filling.

A cavity filling is also used for things other than decay. Dentists fill small chips, worn areas near the gumline and old fillings that have cracked or leaked. For broken front teeth, see our guide to chipped tooth repair cost, which covers bonding and other options.

Cavity fillings come in several materials:

  • Composite resin: Tooth-colored, bonds to the tooth and is the most common choice today, especially on visible teeth.
  • Amalgam (silver): A metal alloy that is strong and long-lasting, often used on back teeth that take heavy chewing forces.
  • Glass ionomer: Releases a little fluoride; often used near the gumline or in baby teeth.
  • Gold or ceramic inlays: Made in a lab or milled in the office for larger cavities; they cost more and may need two visits.

How long does a cavity filling take?

A cavity filling usually takes about 20 minutes to an hour for one tooth, including time for numbing. A small, single-surface filling on a front tooth sits at the short end, while a large filling covering several surfaces of a back molar takes longer.

Cavity filling time depends on several factors:

  • Size and depth: Deeper decay takes longer to remove carefully, especially close to the nerve.
  • Number of surfaces: A filling that wraps around the side of a tooth needs a matrix band to shape it, which adds time.
  • Material: Composite is placed in thin layers, each hardened with a curing light, so it often takes a little longer than amalgam.
  • Location: A back tooth cavity filling is harder to reach and keep dry than one on a front tooth.
  • Number of fillings: Dentists often fill two or more teeth on the same side in one visit, which makes the appointment longer but saves a second round of numbing.

Cavity filling appointments for inlays or onlays made in a lab usually need two visits, about one to two weeks apart, with a temporary filling in between.

What happens during a cavity filling?

A cavity filling follows the same basic steps in most dental offices: numbing, decay removal, cleaning, placing the filling material and checking your bite.

  1. Numbing: The dentist usually applies a numbing gel, then injects local anesthetic near the tooth. Very small, shallow fillings are sometimes done without an injection.
  2. Isolation: Cotton rolls or a thin rubber sheet (rubber dam) keep the tooth dry, which matters for composite bonding.
  3. Decay removal: A dental drill or hand instruments remove the soft, decayed tooth structure and shape the space.
  4. Cleaning and preparation: The space is cleaned. For composite, an etching gel and bonding agent are applied so the filling sticks to the tooth.
  5. Placing the filling: Composite goes in layers, each hardened with a blue curing light. Amalgam is packed in and shaped.
  6. Bite check and polish: You bite on thin colored paper so the dentist can find and adjust high spots, then the filling is smoothed and polished.

A cavity filling should not hurt once you are numb. Tell your dentist if you feel sharp pain during the procedure, because more anesthetic can be given.

What should you expect after a cavity filling?

After a cavity filling, numbness usually wears off within a few hours, and mild sensitivity to cold, pressure or sweet foods for a few days to a couple of weeks is common. Composite fillings are hard as soon as the light cures them, so you can eat once the numbness fades. Amalgam reaches full strength more slowly, so your dentist may suggest avoiding hard chewing on that side for the rest of the day.

  • Avoid chewing on the numb side until feeling returns, to prevent biting your cheek, lip or tongue.
  • Use a toothpaste for sensitive teeth if cold sensitivity lingers.
  • Brush and floss as usual around the filled tooth.
  • Call your dentist if your bite feels high or uneven; a quick adjustment usually fixes it.

Cavity filling sensitivity that gets worse instead of better, or a lingering throbbing pain, can mean the decay was close to the nerve. A dentist should check it, because some teeth need a root canal later.

Contact your dentist promptly for severe or worsening tooth pain, swelling of the gum or face, fever, or a filling that falls out. Seek urgent care for facial swelling that spreads or makes it hard to swallow or breathe.

Follow your dentist's aftercare instructions, which may differ depending on the material used and how deep the cavity was.

How long do cavity fillings last?

Cavity fillings typically last many years, with amalgam often outlasting composite on heavily used back teeth, though the exact lifespan depends on filling size, your bite and your oral hygiene. Large fillings and teeth that take heavy grinding forces wear out sooner.

Cavity fillings fail most often from new decay at the edge, cracks, or wear. Signs a filling needs attention include a rough edge your tongue catches on, food getting stuck, new sensitivity or a visible dark line around the filling. Regular checkups let your dentist spot these problems early, while a repair is still small.

Cavity prevention is the cheapest way to avoid future fillings: brushing twice a day with fluoride toothpaste, cleaning between teeth daily, cutting back on frequent sugary snacks and drinks, and keeping routine dental visits.

How much is a tooth filling?

A tooth filling costs $145 to $480 for a composite filling on a front tooth and $165 to $530 on a back tooth without insurance, according to 2024 data from Delta Dental. Amalgam (silver) fillings run $110 to $455 depending on the number of surfaces.

Cavity filling averages from CareCredit come in at $226 for a composite resin filling and $139 for a silver amalgam filling. The two sources measure different things: Delta Dental gives ranges by tooth and number of surfaces, while CareCredit reports single national averages from its cost study. Both are national figures, and local prices vary.

Cavity fillings count as basic procedures, which insurance typically covers at 50%–80%, per Delta Dental. Delta Dental's guide to what dental insurance covers explains how deductibles and annual maximums apply. Frequently asked questions

How long does a cavity filling take?

A cavity filling usually takes about 20 minutes to an hour for one tooth, including numbing. Small fillings on front teeth are quicker, while large fillings on back molars or several fillings in one visit take longer. Lab-made inlays and onlays usually need two appointments about one to two weeks apart.

Do cavity fillings hurt?

Cavity fillings should not hurt once the tooth is numb. Most people feel the injection briefly, then only pressure and vibration during the procedure. Mild sensitivity for a few days afterwards is common. Tell your dentist right away if you feel sharp pain during the filling, because more anesthetic can be given.

Can I eat after a cavity filling?

You can usually eat after a composite cavity filling once the numbness wears off, because the material hardens immediately under the curing light. With amalgam, your dentist may suggest avoiding hard chewing on that side for the rest of the day. Waiting until feeling returns prevents biting your cheek or tongue.

Is composite or amalgam better for a back tooth cavity filling?

Both composite and amalgam work for a back tooth cavity filling. Amalgam is very durable under heavy chewing and usually costs less, averaging $139 per CareCredit. Composite matches tooth color and bonds to the tooth, averaging $226. Your dentist will weigh cavity size, moisture control and your preferences.

What happens if you do not fill a cavity?

An unfilled cavity usually keeps growing because decay cannot heal once it breaks through enamel. As it reaches deeper layers, it can cause sensitivity, pain and infection, and the tooth may later need a root canal, a crown or extraction, all of which cost more than a filling. Early treatment is simpler and cheaper.

How much is a tooth filling with insurance?

A tooth filling with insurance typically costs much less than full price, since Delta Dental notes that plans usually cover 50%–80% of basic procedures such as fillings. Your share depends on your deductible, coverage level, annual maximum and whether the dentist is in network. Without insurance, a composite filling on a back tooth runs $165 to $530.
