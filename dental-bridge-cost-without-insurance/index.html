How Much Does a Dental Bridge Cost Without Insurance?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated November 4, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

Without insurance, a three-unit dental bridge (two crowns and a false tooth) averages about $3,600 out-of-network, according to Delta Dental. CareCredit reports averages of $5,197 for a traditional single-tooth bridge and $1,569 for a Maryland bridge. Materials, location and the number of teeth change the price.

How much is a dental bridge without insurance?

A dental bridge without insurance averages about $3,600 for a three-unit bridge out-of-network, according to Delta Dental. A three-unit bridge replaces one missing tooth with a false tooth (pontic) held by crowns on the two neighbouring teeth.

CareCredit's procedure cost list reports an average of $5,197 for a traditional single-tooth bridge and $1,569 for a single-tooth Maryland bridge. The figures differ because the sources measure different things, and both are national averages. A written quote from your dentist is the most reliable number. What affects the price of a dental bridge?

Several factors affect the price of a dental bridge without insurance.

  • Number of units: replacing two or three adjacent teeth means more crowns and pontics.
  • Bridge type: traditional, Maryland, cantilever or implant-supported.
  • Materials: all-ceramic, zirconia, porcelain-fused-to-metal or metal.
  • Location of the tooth: front teeth need more cosmetic detail; back teeth need more strength.
  • Preparatory work: extractions, fillings, root canals or gum treatment beforehand.
  • Dentist and lab fees, which vary by region.

Which bridge type costs least?

A Maryland bridge usually costs least, averaging $1,569 for a single tooth according to CareCredit. It uses small wings bonded to the backs of neighbouring teeth instead of full crowns, so it requires less lab work and tooth preparation.

Maryland bridges are best for front teeth with healthy neighbours and a gentle bite; they are less suited to back teeth. Traditional bridges cost more but are stronger. See Maryland bridge dental for details and dental bridge for all types.

What other costs come with a bridge?

Other costs that come with a bridge can include an exam and X-rays, temporary bridge, extractions if a failing tooth is still present, and any fillings, root canals or gum treatment needed on the supporting teeth first.

Ask for an itemised quote that lists each step. Some practices quote the bridge as a package; others bill each crown and pontic separately. Comparing like with like helps you avoid surprises.

How can you pay less for a bridge without insurance?

You can pay less for a bridge without insurance in several ways.

  • Cash-pay discounts for paying in full.
  • In-house membership plans that discount major work.
  • Dental schools where supervised students and residents charge lower fees.
  • Discount dental plans with reduced fees at participating offices.
  • Payment plans or medical financing; read interest terms carefully.
  • HSA or FSA funds to pay with pre-tax money.
  • Comparing quotes from several practices.

How would insurance change the cost?

With insurance, Delta Dental notes coverage for bridges is typically 50% to 80% of the cost, depending on the plan, subject to the deductible and annual maximum. Bridges are often classified as major services.

If you are considering buying insurance before a bridge, check waiting periods for major services and any missing tooth clause that excludes teeth lost before coverage began. These can mean a new plan does not help with an immediate bridge.

Is a bridge cheaper than an implant without insurance?

A bridge is usually cheaper than an implant upfront without insurance, especially a Maryland bridge. Implants generally cost more but do not rely on neighbouring teeth and help preserve bone.

Over many years, bridges may need replacing and supporting teeth can develop problems, which narrows the long-term cost gap. Healthline's implant versus bridge guide and the Cleveland Clinic's prosthodontics overview explain the trade-offs.

How long does a bridge last, and why does that matter for cost?

A bridge commonly lasts 10 to 15 years or more with good care, which matters because a replacement bridge costs about the same as the original. Daily cleaning under the pontic and regular check-ups help it last.

Decay or gum disease on the supporting teeth is the most common reason bridges fail. A night guard protects the bridge if you grind. See before and after dental bridge for what results look like over time.

What happens when you get a bridge?

Getting a traditional bridge usually takes two visits. At the first, the dentist numbs the area, reshapes the supporting teeth, takes a scan or impression and fits a temporary bridge. At the second, the permanent bridge is checked for fit, colour and bite, then cemented.

Most people adjust to a new bridge within a few days. Mild sensitivity in the supporting teeth is common at first and usually fades.

How much more does a bridge for several missing teeth cost?

A bridge for several missing teeth costs more than a three-unit bridge because each extra false tooth and supporting crown adds to the fee. A bridge replacing two adjacent missing teeth, for example, usually has four units: two pontics and two supporting crowns.

Long-span bridges also put more stress on the supporting teeth, so dentists may recommend extra support, such as an additional crowned tooth, or suggest implants instead. When many teeth are missing, an implant-supported bridge or a partial denture can sometimes be more practical. Ask your dentist to price each option for your specific gap.

What does a temporary bridge cost?

A temporary bridge is usually included in the fee for the permanent bridge. It protects the prepared teeth and fills the gap for the few weeks while the lab makes the final bridge.

If you need a temporary bridge for a longer time, for example while gums heal after extractions, it may be billed separately. Ask your dentist how long you will wear it and whether replacement or adjustment visits cost extra. Treat temporaries gently, because they are not as strong as the final bridge.

What should you ask before paying for a bridge?

Before paying for a bridge without insurance, ask the dentist what material the crowns and false tooth will be made of, whether the quote includes the temporary bridge, X-rays and follow-up adjustments, and what happens if the bridge comes loose in the first year. Ask whether any supporting teeth need fillings or root canals first, because those add to the total. Getting these answers in writing makes it easier to compare practices fairly.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a dental bridge without insurance?

A dental bridge without insurance averages about $3,600 for a three-unit bridge out-of-network, according to Delta Dental. CareCredit reports $5,197 for a traditional single-tooth bridge and $1,569 for a Maryland bridge. Your price depends on type, materials and location.

What is the cheapest type of dental bridge?

A Maryland bridge is usually the cheapest type, averaging $1,569 for a single tooth according to CareCredit. It uses bonded wings instead of crowns but is mainly suited to front teeth with healthy neighbours and a gentle bite.

Why are dental bridges so expensive?

Dental bridges are expensive because they involve custom lab-made crowns and false teeth, careful preparation of the supporting teeth, and multiple visits. Materials such as zirconia and porcelain, and regional dentist fees, also affect the price.

Can I get a dental bridge on a payment plan?

Many dental offices offer payment plans or accept medical financing for bridges, and some have in-house membership plans with discounts. HSA and FSA funds can also be used. Read interest terms carefully before signing.

Is a bridge cheaper than an implant?

A bridge is usually cheaper than an implant upfront, especially a Maryland bridge. Over many years, bridge replacement and problems with supporting teeth can narrow the difference. Your dentist can compare both for your situation.
