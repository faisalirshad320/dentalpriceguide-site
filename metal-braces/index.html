Metal Braces: Cost, How Traditional Braces Work and What to Expect

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 26, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

Metal braces are stainless-steel brackets bonded to the front of the teeth and joined by an archwire that gradually moves teeth into place. They are the most common and usually the most affordable fixed braces: CareCredit reports an average of $6,343, and Delta Dental cites $3,000 to $7,000.

Key takeaways
  • Metal braces use stainless-steel brackets, an archwire and small elastic ties.
  • CareCredit's average for metal braces is $6,343; Delta Dental cites $3,000 to $7,000.
  • Metal braces can treat almost every crowding, spacing and bite problem.
  • Coloured elastic ties let kids and teens personalise the look.
  • A retainer after treatment is what keeps the result in place.

What are metal braces?

Metal braces are the traditional fixed braces most people picture: small stainless-steel brackets glued to the front of each tooth, a thin archwire running through them, and tiny elastic ties (ligatures) holding the wire in the bracket slots. The wire applies light, steady pressure that moves teeth through the bone over months.

Metal braces have been refined for decades. Modern brackets are smaller and smoother than older designs, and heat-activated nickel-titanium wires deliver gentler, more consistent force. Some practices also offer self-ligating metal brackets, which use a built-in clip instead of elastic ties.

Metal braces are the workhorse of orthodontics because they handle the widest range of problems, from simple crowding to complex bite correction, and they cannot be removed or forgotten the way aligner trays can.

How much do traditional metal braces cost?

Traditional metal braces cost about $6,343 on average, according to the CareCredit procedure cost list. Delta Dental's guide for parents gives a range of $3,000 to $7,000 for traditional metal braces. These are national figures, and the fee you are quoted depends on where you live and how long your case will take.

Metal braces sit toward the lower end of the braces price range. CareCredit reports ceramic braces at $5,834 and clear aligners at $5,108 on average, while lingual braces average $9,221. Survey averages for different appliance types can overlap, so get a written quote for your own case rather than relying on the type alone.

Pearl's dental cost data lists comprehensive orthodontic treatment (CDT D8080) at $3,680 to $9,650 nationally, with an average of $5,645. For a full breakdown, see our main guide to how much braces cost.

An orthodontic quote usually bundles the consultation records, brackets, adjustment visits and removal. Retainers and lost-bracket repairs are sometimes extra, so ask what is included.

How do metal braces move teeth?

Metal braces move teeth by applying gentle, continuous force through the archwire. That pressure causes the bone on one side of each tooth root to remodel while new bone forms on the other side, so the tooth shifts slowly in the direction the wire guides it.

Orthodontists change to stiffer or differently shaped wires as treatment progresses. Early wires are flexible and level the teeth; later wires are firmer and fine-tune the bite. Elastics (rubber bands) hooked between the upper and lower braces are often added to correct how the jaws meet.

Adjustment visits for metal braces typically happen every several weeks. Each visit may involve a new wire, fresh ties or new elastics, and the first few days afterward can feel sore.

Are metal braces better than ceramic braces or aligners?

Metal braces are not better for everyone, but they are the most versatile and durable option. Stainless-steel brackets rarely chip or stain, and they handle the heaviest tooth movements well.

  • Ceramic braces work the same way but use tooth-coloured brackets. They are less noticeable, slightly more fragile and can stain if ties discolour.
  • Clear aligners are removable trays. They suit many mild to moderate cases but only work if worn most of the day, every day.
  • Lingual braces sit behind the teeth and are hidden, but they cost the most and can affect speech at first.

If appearance matters but budget is tight, read our guide to clear braces for how ceramic brackets and aligners compare. Kids and teens who choose metal braces often enjoy picking braces colors for the elastic ties at each visit.

How long do you wear metal braces?

Most people wear metal braces for roughly one to three years. Mild crowding or spacing can finish toward the short end, while bite corrections, impacted teeth or extractions usually take longer.

Treatment time with metal braces depends heavily on cooperation. Wearing rubber bands as instructed, avoiding foods that break brackets and keeping every adjustment appointment all keep treatment on schedule. Each broken bracket or missed visit can add weeks.

After the brackets come off, the orthodontist fits a retainer. Teeth naturally tend to drift back toward their old positions, especially in the first year, so retainer wear is the step that protects everything you paid for. Many orthodontists now recommend wearing a retainer at night long term.

What is it like to wear metal braces?

Wearing metal braces means a few weeks of adjustment and some changes to eating and cleaning habits. Most people get used to the feel of the brackets within one to two weeks.

  • Soreness: teeth often ache for a few days after placement and after each adjustment. Soft foods and over-the-counter pain relief, used as directed on the label, help.
  • Food rules: avoid hard, sticky and chewy foods such as hard candy, popcorn kernels, caramel and whole apples bitten directly.
  • Cleaning: brush after meals, and use floss threaders, interdental brushes or a water flosser around the wire.
  • Irritation: orthodontic wax over a bracket that rubs the cheek gives quick relief.

Contact your orthodontist if a wire pokes your cheek and wax does not help, a bracket comes off, or you have swelling or pain that keeps getting worse. Follow your own orthodontist's instructions over any general advice.

Does insurance cover metal braces?

Dental insurance covers metal braces only when the plan includes an orthodontic benefit. Child orthodontic coverage is more common than adult coverage, and orthodontic benefits usually come with a separate lifetime maximum.

Delta Dental's coverage guide explains deductibles, coinsurance and annual maximums. Ask the orthodontist to submit a pre-treatment estimate so you know your share before treatment begins.

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first orthodontic evaluation no later than age 7, as noted in Delta Dental's guide. An early check does not always mean early braces, but it helps time treatment well. If you are paying out of pocket, see how much braces cost without insurance for payment plans and savings options.

Frequently asked questions

How much do metal braces cost?

Metal braces cost about $6,343 on average according to CareCredit, and Delta Dental cites a range of $3,000 to $7,000 for traditional metal braces. Your actual fee depends on your location, the complexity of your bite and how long treatment lasts, so ask for a written quote.

Are metal braces cheaper than clear aligners?

Metal braces are not always cheaper than clear aligners. CareCredit's averages are $6,343 for metal braces and $5,108 for clear aligners, but the price for any one patient depends on the case and the provider. Compare written quotes for the same treatment goals.

How long do you wear metal braces?

Metal braces are typically worn for one to three years, depending on how far the teeth must move and how well you follow instructions, such as wearing elastics. Your orthodontist gives an estimate after planning, and a retainer is needed afterward to hold the result.

Do metal braces hurt?

Metal braces do not hurt to put on, but teeth usually feel sore for a few days afterward and after each adjustment. The brackets can also rub the cheeks at first. Orthodontic wax, soft foods and over-the-counter pain relief used as directed on the label usually help.

Can adults get metal braces?

Adults can get metal braces at any age as long as the teeth and gums are healthy. Adult treatment may take a little longer because the bone is denser, and gum disease must be controlled first. Many adults choose metal braces because they are durable and cost less than lingual braces.

What can you not eat with metal braces?

With metal braces, avoid hard foods like nuts and hard candy, sticky foods like caramel and taffy, and chewy foods like bagels and jerky. Cut apples and corn off the cob. These foods can bend wires or break brackets, which can lengthen treatment.
