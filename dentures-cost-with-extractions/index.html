How Much Do Full Dentures Cost With Extractions?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated November 4, 20264 price sources
Quick answer

Full dentures with extractions are priced as separate items: the extractions (per tooth) plus the dentures (per arch). Delta Dental lists full dentures at $1,115 to $2,425 for a lower and $1,220 to $2,540 for an upper out-of-network, and GoodRx lists an immediate full denture at about $1,900. The extraction fees depend on how many teeth are removed and whether they need surgical extraction (Delta Dental).

Key takeaways
  • Dentures and extractions are billed separately; ask for both on your estimate.
  • Full dentures: $1,115 to $2,425 (lower) and $1,220 to $2,540 (upper) out-of-network.
  • Immediate dentures (about $1,900, GoodRx) let you leave with teeth on extraction day.
  • Relines (about $350 or more, GoodRx) are common as gums heal.
  • Insurance often covers extractions as basic care and dentures as major care.

How do you work out the total cost of dentures with extractions?

You work out the total cost of dentures with extractions by adding several separate parts: the exam and X-rays, each extraction, any bone smoothing, the dentures themselves, sedation if chosen, and follow-up relines. Most practices quote each part separately.

The denture fee depends on whether you need one or both arches and the denture quality. Delta Dental lists a full lower at $1,115 to $2,425 and a full upper at $1,220 to $2,540 out-of-network. Extraction fees are charged per tooth, and teeth that are broken down to the gumline or have curved roots may be billed as surgical extractions. Ask for an itemised written estimate before starting.

What are immediate dentures and what do they cost?

Immediate dentures are made before your teeth are removed and placed on the same day as the extractions, so you never go without teeth. GoodRx lists an immediate full denture at about $1,900 without insurance in its denture cost guide.

Because the gums and bone shrink significantly as extraction sites heal, immediate dentures loosen over the following months and need relines; GoodRx notes a chairside reline costs about $350 or more. Many people later replace immediate dentures with a new conventional denture once healing is complete, which adds to the overall cost.

What about waiting and getting conventional dentures?

Some people choose to have teeth removed, let the gums heal for several weeks to months, and then have conventional dentures made. This approach may result in a better-fitting first denture and avoids paying for both immediate and final dentures.

The downside is spending the healing period without teeth, which many people find difficult socially and for eating. Your dentist can help you weigh the cost savings against the practical challenges.

How does denture quality affect cost?

Denture quality strongly affects cost. CareCredit reports averages for upper and lower sets of $452 for basic dentures, $1,968 for traditional dentures and $6,514 for premium dentures.

Basic dentures use stock teeth and simpler fabrication; premium dentures use higher-quality, more natural-looking teeth, more detailed shaping and often a better fit. Mid-range options balance cost and appearance. Ask your dentist to show examples of each and explain the expected lifespan.

Should you consider implant-supported dentures instead?

Implant-supported dentures are worth considering, especially for the lower jaw, where conventional dentures are often least stable. GoodRx lists implant-supported full dentures at $10,500 to $21,500 without insurance.

Implants can sometimes be placed at the time of extraction or after healing. They cost far more than conventional dentures but greatly improve stability and help preserve jawbone. Healthline's dentures versus implants guide compares the options. See snap in dentures.

How much do dentures with extractions cost with insurance?

With insurance, dentures with extractions are often covered in two categories. Extractions are commonly basic procedures, and dentures are usually major procedures covered at a lower percentage. Both count toward your annual maximum, which a full-mouth treatment can quickly exceed.

Check for waiting periods on major services, and whether the plan has a missing tooth clause. A pre-treatment estimate is especially valuable for full-mouth treatment. Medicare generally does not cover dentures, though some Medicare Advantage plans include dental benefits.

What if some teeth can be saved?

If some teeth can be saved, a partial denture may be an option instead of full dentures. Delta Dental lists partial dentures at $1,425 to $2,490 for a lower and $1,435 to $2,785 for an upper out-of-network. Keeping healthy teeth helps preserve bone and can make a denture more stable.

What is recovery like after extractions and dentures?

Recovery after multiple extractions with immediate dentures usually involves swelling and soreness for several days. Your dentist will likely ask you to keep the dentures in for the first 24 hours to control swelling, then remove them for cleaning as instructed.

Expect several follow-up visits to adjust sore spots as the gums heal. Eat soft foods and follow aftercare instructions carefully. The Cleveland Clinic's prosthodontics guide describes how dentures are fitted and adjusted.

Contact your dentist for bleeding that will not stop with pressure, worsening pain after a few days, fever or pus. Seek emergency care for spreading facial swelling or trouble swallowing or breathing.

How can you lower the cost of dentures with extractions?

You can lower the cost of dentures with extractions by comparing itemised quotes, asking about package pricing for extractions and dentures together, considering dental school clinics, and using payment plans or HSA or FSA funds.

Choosing a mid-range denture rather than premium, or waiting to heal and skipping immediate dentures, can also reduce cost. See our main dentures cost guide for more pricing detail.

What does treatment planning for extractions and dentures involve?

Treatment planning for extractions and dentures starts with a full exam, X-rays and a discussion of which teeth can be saved and which must be removed. The dentist checks the health of your gums and bone, your bite, and any medical conditions or medications that affect surgery and healing.

Impressions or scans are taken before extractions if you are getting immediate dentures, and you will choose the shape and shade of the new teeth. If many teeth are being removed, the dentist may smooth sharp bone edges so the denture fits comfortably, which can be billed separately.

Ask how many appointments you will need, whether sedation is available, how many follow-up adjustments are included, and when you can expect to move from immediate to final dentures. A clear plan helps you budget for every stage, not just the first day.

Frequently asked questions

How much do full dentures cost with extractions?

Full dentures with extractions are priced separately. Delta Dental lists full dentures at $1,115 to $2,425 for a lower and $1,220 to $2,540 for an upper out-of-network, and extractions are charged per tooth on top. Immediate dentures cost about $1,900 according to GoodRx.

Are extractions included in the price of dentures?

Extractions are usually not included in the price of dentures and are billed per tooth. Some practices offer package pricing. Ask for an itemised estimate listing each extraction, the dentures, sedation and follow-up relines.

How much do dentures cost with extractions with insurance?

With insurance, extractions are often covered as basic procedures and dentures as major procedures at a lower percentage, both up to the annual maximum. Full-mouth treatment can exceed the maximum, so get a pre-treatment estimate.

Can you get dentures the same day as extractions?

You can get immediate dentures the same day as extractions. They are made in advance and placed right after the teeth are removed. They usually need relines as the gums heal, and some people later get a new final denture.

How long after extractions can you get dentures?

With immediate dentures, you get them the same day. For conventional dentures, the gums usually heal for several weeks to months before impressions are taken. Your dentist will recommend the timing for your situation.
