Do Dental Implants Hurt? Pain, Safety and Signs of a Bad Dental Implant

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 28, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

Dental implant surgery usually does not hurt during the procedure because the area is numbed, and many patients also choose sedation. Afterward, most people have soreness and swelling for a few days, often less than they expected. Implants are generally safe, but signs of a bad implant include pain that worsens, a loose implant, swelling, pus or receding gums around it (Cleveland Clinic).

Key takeaways
  • Implant surgery is done with local anesthetic, often with sedation, so it should not hurt.
  • Expect soreness, swelling and mild bruising for a few days afterward.
  • Most implants succeed long term when planned and cared for well.
  • Warning signs of a failing implant include looseness, worsening pain, swelling and pus.
  • A single implant costs $2,800 to $5,600 without dental benefits (Delta Dental).

Do dental implants hurt during surgery?

Dental implants should not hurt during surgery because the area is fully numbed with local anesthetic before the dentist begins. You may feel pressure and vibration as the implant site is prepared, but not sharp pain.

Many patients also choose sedation, ranging from nitrous oxide to oral sedatives or IV sedation, especially for multiple implants or if they feel anxious. With IV sedation, many people remember little of the procedure. Placing a single implant in healthy bone often takes about an hour or less.

Tell your surgeon if you feel pain at any point; more anesthetic can be given.

How much do dental implants hurt afterward?

Dental implants usually cause mild to moderate soreness for a few days afterward. Many patients say the recovery from a single implant is easier than having a tooth pulled. Swelling and bruising often peak around the second or third day, then fade.

Over-the-counter pain relievers taken as directed on the label usually manage the discomfort, and your surgeon may prescribe something stronger for more extensive surgery. Cold packs on the cheek, soft foods, rest and gentle salt-water rinses help. Procedures involving bone grafts, sinus lifts or several implants generally bring more swelling and a longer recovery.

What does dental implant recovery feel like day by day?

Dental implant recovery usually follows a predictable pattern, though everyone heals at a slightly different pace. Knowing what to expect makes it easier to tell normal healing from a problem.

  • Day of surgery: numbness wears off within a few hours, followed by aching. Light bleeding or oozing is normal. Rest and use cold packs.
  • Days 2 to 3: swelling and bruising often peak. Stick to soft, cool foods and take pain relievers as directed.
  • Days 4 to 7: soreness and swelling steadily improve. Many people are back to normal routines.
  • Weeks 1 to 2: stitches dissolve or are removed and the gum heals over or around the implant.
  • Months 2 to 6: the implant bonds to the bone. You usually feel nothing during this stage.

The crown is attached once the implant has integrated. Fitting the abutment and crown is generally much less uncomfortable than the original surgery, and often needs only local anesthetic or none at all.

Are dental implants safe?

Dental implants are generally safe and have a long track record. Titanium and zirconia implants are biocompatible, meaning the bone grows tightly around them, a process called osseointegration.

Like any surgery, implants carry some risks. These include infection, bleeding, damage to neighbouring teeth, nerve injury in the lower jaw causing numbness, and sinus problems with upper back implants. Careful planning with 3D imaging reduces these risks; CareCredit reports an average of $466 for a cone-beam CT scan. Choosing an experienced surgeon matters more than any single product choice.

Who has a higher risk of implant problems?

Some people have a higher risk of dental implant problems and need extra planning or care. Risk factors include:

  • Smoking, which reduces blood flow and bone healing.
  • Uncontrolled diabetes, which slows healing and raises infection risk.
  • Gum disease, which can spread to tissue around implants.
  • Insufficient bone, which may need a graft first; the Cleveland Clinic explains bone grafts.
  • Heavy grinding or clenching.
  • Certain medications, such as some bone-strengthening drugs or medicines that suppress immunity.
  • Previous radiation to the head and neck.

Tell your surgeon about your full medical history and all medicines before treatment.

What are the signs of a bad dental implant?

The signs of a bad or failing dental implant usually involve pain, movement or inflammation around the implant. Early detection improves the chance of saving it.

  • Pain that gets worse after the first week instead of better.
  • A loose implant or crown; a healthy implant does not move.
  • Swelling, redness or pus around the implant.
  • Gum recession exposing metal near the gum line.
  • Bleeding when brushing around the implant.
  • Difficulty chewing or a bite that feels wrong.
  • Numbness or tingling that persists after the anesthetic wears off.

Contact your implant dentist promptly for any of these signs. Seek urgent care for spreading facial swelling, fever, or trouble swallowing or breathing.

Why do dental implants fail?

Dental implants fail either early, when the bone does not bond to the implant, or late, after years of use. Early failure can result from infection, poor bone quality, overloading the implant before it heals, or smoking.

Late failure is most often caused by peri-implantitis, a gum-disease-like infection that destroys bone around the implant, usually linked to plaque build-up. Excessive biting forces and loose components can also cause problems. A failed implant can often be removed, the site allowed to heal, and a new implant placed, sometimes with a bone graft.

How do you keep a dental implant healthy?

Keeping a dental implant healthy requires the same care as natural teeth, with extra attention around the gum line. Implants cannot get cavities, but the gum and bone around them can become infected.

  1. Brush twice a day with a soft brush.
  2. Clean around the implant daily with floss, interdental brushes or a water flosser.
  3. Keep regular dental cleanings and check-ups with X-rays.
  4. Wear a night guard if you grind.
  5. Avoid smoking.

Frequently asked questions

Do dental implants hurt?

Dental implant surgery should not hurt because the area is numbed and sedation is often available. Afterward, most people have soreness and swelling for a few days that over-the-counter pain relievers usually manage. Grafts or multiple implants can mean a longer, more uncomfortable recovery.

Is getting a dental implant more painful than an extraction?

Many patients find a single dental implant less uncomfortable than a tooth extraction, because placing an implant in healed bone causes less tissue trauma. Recovery varies with bone grafts, the number of implants and individual healing.

Are dental implants safe?

Dental implants are generally safe and widely used. Risks include infection, nerve injury, sinus problems and implant failure, which careful planning with 3D imaging and an experienced surgeon help reduce. Smoking, uncontrolled diabetes and gum disease raise the risk.

What are the signs of a bad dental implant?

Signs of a bad dental implant include pain that worsens, a loose implant or crown, swelling, redness, pus, bleeding gums, gum recession exposing metal, and persistent numbness. Contact your implant dentist promptly if you notice any of these.

How long do dental implants last?

Dental implants can last many years and often decades with good oral hygiene and regular check-ups. The crown on top may need replacing after some years from wear. Peri-implantitis, smoking and grinding are common reasons implants fail.
