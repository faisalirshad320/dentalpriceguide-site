Floss for Braces: How to Floss and Brush Your Teeth With Braces

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 29, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

To floss with braces, thread floss under the archwire with a floss threader or stiff-ended orthodontic floss, then slide it between each pair of teeth and gently up under the gum. A water flosser and interdental brushes help clean around brackets. Brush after meals at an angle above and below each bracket. Clean teeth during braces protect your investment, which averages $6,343 for metal braces (CareCredit).

Key takeaways
  • Brackets trap food and plaque, so cleaning takes more time and the right tools.
  • Floss threaders, orthodontic floss and water flossers make flossing with braces possible.
  • Brush above, below and on top of each bracket, ideally after every meal.
  • Interdental brushes clean between brackets and under the wire.
  • Poor cleaning can leave white spots and cavities when braces come off.

Why is cleaning teeth with braces so important?

Cleaning teeth with braces is important because brackets, wires and bands create many new places for food and plaque to hide. Plaque left around brackets produces acid that weakens enamel and inflames the gums.

The most common results of poor cleaning during braces are white chalky spots around where the brackets sat, cavities, swollen bleeding gums and bad breath. White spots are early enamel damage and can remain visible after braces come off, spoiling the straight smile you paid for. Swollen gums can also slow tooth movement and make treatment uncomfortable.

How do you brush your teeth with braces?

Brushing your teeth with braces takes a little longer than before, but the technique is easy to learn. Aim to brush after every meal and before bed, or at least twice a day for two to three minutes.

  1. Rinse first to loosen food particles.
  2. Remove elastics and any removable parts.
  3. Brush the gum line at a 45-degree angle with gentle circles.
  4. Angle the brush down onto the top of each bracket, then up onto the bottom of each bracket.
  5. Clean the chewing surfaces and inner surfaces of all teeth.
  6. Check in a mirror that brackets look clean and shiny, without trapped food.

A soft-bristled manual brush, an orthodontic brush with a V-shaped bristle pattern, or an electric toothbrush all work well. Use fluoride toothpaste and replace brush heads often, because brackets wear bristles quickly.

How do you floss with braces?

Flossing with braces means getting the floss under the archwire before you can clean between the teeth. Here is the method with a floss threader:

  1. Pull about 18 inches of floss and thread one end through the loop of the floss threader.
  2. Push the stiff threader under the archwire between two teeth, from the front to the back.
  3. Pull the floss through so it sits under the wire.
  4. Wrap the ends around your fingers and slide the floss gently between the two teeth.
  5. Curve the floss around each tooth and move it up and down, just under the gum.
  6. Pull the floss out and repeat for the next space.

Orthodontic floss with a built-in stiff end works the same way without a separate threader. It feels slow at first, but most people get faster within a couple of weeks.

Does a water flosser work with braces?

A water flosser works well with braces for flushing out food and plaque around brackets and under wires. It is especially helpful for people who find threading floss difficult or time-consuming.

Water flossers are not always a full replacement for string floss, which scrapes plaque from tooth surfaces between teeth. Many orthodontists recommend using both if possible, or at least using a water flosser daily if regular flossing is not happening. Start on a low pressure setting and aim the tip at the gum line and around each bracket.

What are interdental brushes for with braces?

Interdental brushes are tiny cone or cylinder-shaped brushes that clean between brackets and under the archwire. They reach spots a regular toothbrush misses, such as the area right behind the wire and the sides of brackets.

Choose a size that fits snugly without forcing. Slide the brush gently under the wire and between brackets, moving it back and forth a few times. Rinse it after use and replace it when the bristles flatten.

What is a good daily cleaning routine with braces?

A good daily cleaning routine with braces combines brushing after meals with one thorough flossing session, usually at night. Doing it at the same times each day makes it a habit rather than a chore.

  1. Morning: brush thoroughly around every bracket and along the gum line.
  2. After lunch and snacks: brush, or at least rinse with water and use an interdental brush if you are out.
  3. Evening: brush, floss every space with a threader or orthodontic floss, then use a water flosser if you have one.
  4. Before bed: use a fluoride mouthwash if your dentist recommends it, and do not eat or drink anything but water afterward.

Most people need about 10 to 15 minutes for the full evening routine at first, dropping as they get practice. A small travel kit with a folding toothbrush, a tiny toothpaste, interdental brushes and orthodontic wax makes midday cleaning realistic at school or work.

How do food choices affect cleaning with braces?

Food choices make cleaning with braces easier or harder. Sticky, chewy and sugary foods cling to brackets and feed decay-causing bacteria, while hard foods can break brackets and wires.

Limit sweets, sticky snacks and sugary drinks, and rinse with water after eating if you cannot brush right away. Carry a travel toothbrush, interdental brushes and orthodontic wax in your bag. What are signs you are not cleaning well enough?

Signs you are not cleaning well enough with braces include gums that bleed when brushing, swollen or puffy gums around brackets, bad breath, food that stays visible after brushing, and white or yellow patches around brackets.

Tell your orthodontist or dentist if your gums stay swollen or bleed for more than a few days, or if you notice white spots or sensitivity. They can show you techniques and may recommend a fluoride rinse or extra cleanings.

Keep regular dental check-ups and cleanings with your general dentist throughout orthodontic treatment. Braces are not a reason to skip routine dental visits.

Is cleaning easier with clear aligners?

Cleaning is generally easier with clear aligners because you remove the trays to eat, brush and floss normally. The trade-off is that aligners must be cleaned too, and you need to brush before putting them back in to avoid trapping food against your teeth.

Frequently asked questions

How do you floss with braces?

To floss with braces, use a floss threader or orthodontic floss with a stiff end to pass the floss under the archwire. Then slide the floss between the two teeth, curve it around each tooth and clean just under the gum. Repeat for every space.

Is a water flosser enough with braces?

A water flosser is very helpful with braces for flushing debris around brackets, but many orthodontists recommend also using string floss, which scrapes plaque between teeth more effectively. If you cannot floss daily, using a water flosser daily is much better than nothing.

How often should you brush with braces?

You should ideally brush after every meal and before bed when you have braces, or at least twice a day for two to three minutes. Rinse with water after snacks if you cannot brush right away.

What toothbrush is best for braces?

A soft-bristled toothbrush, an orthodontic brush with V-shaped bristles, or an electric toothbrush all work well for braces. Replace the brush or head often, because brackets wear bristles quickly. Interdental brushes help clean between brackets.

What happens if you don't clean your teeth with braces?

If you don't clean your teeth well with braces, plaque builds up around brackets and can cause white spots, cavities, swollen bleeding gums and bad breath. White spots can remain visible after braces come off.
