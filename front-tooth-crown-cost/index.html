How Much Does a Front Tooth Crown Cost?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 6, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

A front tooth crown costs about $1,100 to $2,000 out-of-network for a permanent crown, according to Delta Dental. Front teeth usually get tooth-coloured crowns; CareCredit reports a $1,399 average for a porcelain crown. Insurance typically covers crowns at 50% as a major procedure.

Key takeaways
  • Permanent crowns cost $1,100 to $2,000 out-of-network (Delta Dental).
  • Porcelain crowns average $1,399 (CareCredit), the usual choice for front teeth.
  • Front-tooth crowns focus on appearance as well as strength.
  • Insurance often covers crowns at 50% when the tooth is damaged.
  • A veneer or bonding may be an alternative for less damaged front teeth.

How much does a crown on a front tooth cost?

A crown on a front tooth costs about $1,100 to $2,000 out-of-network for a permanent crown, according to Delta Dental. CareCredit's procedure cost list reports an average of $1,399 for a porcelain crown, which is the most common type for front teeth.

The price depends on the material, the lab's craftsmanship, any build-up needed under the crown, and your dentist's fees and location. Highly aesthetic front-tooth crowns made by skilled ceramists may cost toward the top of the range. See types of dental crowns and cost for a comparison of materials.

What is the best crown material for a front tooth?

The best crown material for a front tooth is usually an all-ceramic material, such as porcelain, lithium disilicate or layered zirconia, because these look most like natural teeth. They let light pass through in a way similar to enamel.

Porcelain-fused-to-metal crowns are strong but can show a dark line at the gum over time if the gum recedes. Metal and gold crowns are very durable but are rarely chosen for front teeth because they are visible. Your dentist chooses based on appearance, strength needed and your bite.

When does a front tooth need a crown?

A front tooth needs a crown when it is too damaged or weakened for a filling or veneer to hold up. Common reasons include a large break, a large old filling that is failing, a crack, or a tooth that has darkened and weakened after a root canal.

The Cleveland Clinic's dental crowns guide explains the main reasons crowns are recommended. Our guide to what a dental crown is covers how crowns work.

Should you get a crown or a veneer on a front tooth?

Whether you get a crown or a veneer on a front tooth depends on how much healthy tooth remains. A veneer covers only the front surface and removes less tooth, making it a good choice for healthy teeth that need a cosmetic change.

A crown covers the whole tooth and is needed when the tooth is broken, heavily filled or root canal treated. Veneers are usually considered cosmetic and often not covered by insurance, while crowns that restore damage often are. Ask your dentist why they recommend one over the other.

How do dentists make a front crown look natural?

Dentists make a front crown look natural by matching its shade, translucency, shape and surface texture to the neighbouring teeth. This often involves photos, a shade guide and sometimes a visit to the lab or a custom shade appointment.

If you plan to whiten your teeth, do it before the crown is made, because crowns do not change colour with whitening. A temporary crown worn for a short time can also help you and your dentist check shape and length before the final crown is made.

Does insurance cover a front tooth crown?

Insurance usually covers a front tooth crown when it is needed to restore a damaged tooth, typically at about 50% as a major procedure on a 100-80-50 plan, according to Delta Dental's coverage guide.

Your share depends on your deductible and annual maximum. Crowns placed purely for cosmetic reasons may be denied. See tooth crown cost with insurance for how to estimate what you will pay.

What is a temporary crown and what does it cost?

A temporary crown protects a prepared front tooth while the permanent crown is made, usually for a couple of weeks. It is typically included in the permanent crown fee. CareCredit reports a $697 average for resin-based or temporary crowns used as longer-term restorations.

Temporary crowns are less strong than permanent ones, so avoid biting into hard foods with them and call your dentist if one comes off.

What happens when you get a front tooth crown?

Getting a front tooth crown usually takes two visits. At the first, the dentist numbs the tooth, removes any decay, shapes it, takes a scan or impression, matches the shade and fits a temporary crown. At the second, the permanent crown is checked for fit, colour and bite and then cemented.

Some offices make same-day ceramic crowns with in-office milling. For front teeth, many dentists prefer lab-made crowns for the finest aesthetic detail.

How long does a front tooth crown last?

A front tooth crown often lasts 10 to 15 years or more with good care. Grinding, biting hard objects and decay at the crown edge shorten its life. Wear a night guard if you grind, and keep regular check-ups. See how much a gold tooth is if you are curious about metal crown options.

Do front teeth need a crown after a root canal?

Front teeth do not always need a crown after a root canal. Because front teeth take less biting force than molars, a front tooth with plenty of healthy structure remaining can often be restored with a tooth-coloured filling to seal the access opening.

A crown is more likely to be recommended if the front tooth was already heavily filled, broken or has darkened noticeably after the nerve died. In some cases internal whitening or a veneer can improve the colour instead. Ask your dentist which option will protect the tooth while keeping as much natural structure as possible, and what each option costs.

What are the cheaper alternatives to a front tooth crown?

Cheaper alternatives to a front tooth crown include bonding and veneers, when the tooth is strong enough. Dental bonding repairs small chips and shape problems with resin in one visit and is typically far less expensive than a crown.

Porcelain or composite veneers can change the colour and shape of a front tooth while removing less tooth than a crown. However, for a tooth that is badly broken, cracked or heavily filled, a crown is usually the only option that will hold up. Choosing a cheaper repair that fails quickly can end up costing more, so ask your dentist how long each option is likely to last.

A veneer and a crown can cost similar amounts per tooth, but crowns are more often covered by insurance when restoring damage. Veneers remove less tooth and suit healthy teeth needing cosmetic changes.
