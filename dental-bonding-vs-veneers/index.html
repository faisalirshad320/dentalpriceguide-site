Teeth Bonding vs Veneers: Which Is Better for Your Smile?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 7, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

Bonding adds tooth-coloured resin directly to teeth in one visit and averages $431 per tooth (CareCredit). Porcelain veneers are custom shells bonded to the front of teeth, costing $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth (Delta Dental). Bonding suits small fixes on a budget; veneers suit bigger, longer-lasting changes.

Key takeaways
  • Bonding averages $431 per tooth; porcelain veneers cost $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth.
  • Bonding is done in one visit with little or no tooth removal.
  • Veneers last longer and resist staining better than bonding.
  • Bonding is best for small chips, gaps and stains; veneers for larger changes.
  • Both are usually considered cosmetic and often not covered by insurance.

What is the difference between bonding and veneers?

The main difference between bonding and veneers is how they are made and applied. Bonding uses composite resin that the dentist applies directly to the tooth, sculpts by hand and hardens with a light, usually in one appointment.

Porcelain veneers are thin custom shells made in a dental lab from impressions or scans, then bonded to the front of the teeth at a second visit. Veneers require removing a thin layer of enamel; bonding often requires little or none. Healthline's veneers guide describes the veneer process in detail.

Is bonding cheaper than veneers?

Bonding is much cheaper than porcelain veneers per tooth. CareCredit reports an average of $431 per tooth for dental bonding, while Delta Dental lists porcelain laminate veneers at $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth, and CareCredit reports a $1,765 average for a single veneer.

For a full smile makeover, CareCredit reports an average of $15,486 for six to eight porcelain veneers. Bonding the same teeth costs far less upfront, though it may need replacing sooner. See our veneers cost guide.

Which lasts longer, bonding or veneers?

Porcelain veneers usually last longer than bonding. Veneers commonly last 10 to 15 years or more with good care, while bonding typically needs touching up or replacing sooner because composite resin is softer and wears faster.

Bonding can usually be repaired by adding resin, and polishing can restore its shine. Veneers rarely need maintenance but must be replaced if they chip badly or come off. Read whether veneers are permanent for more on veneer lifespan.

Which looks more natural?

Both bonding and veneers can look natural when done well, but porcelain veneers generally keep a more lifelike, glossy appearance over time. Porcelain reflects light much like natural enamel and resists staining.

Bonding can look excellent at first, especially for small repairs, but resin can absorb stains from coffee, tea, wine and tobacco and gradually lose its lustre. The artistry of the dentist matters greatly for bonding, because it is sculpted by hand in the mouth. Browse veneers before and after to see typical results.

When is bonding the better choice?

Bonding is usually the better choice for small cosmetic fixes on one or a few teeth, when budget is a priority, or when you want to keep your natural enamel. It works well for:

  • Small chips and cracks.
  • Small gaps between front teeth.
  • Minor shape corrections.
  • Covering small stains or white spots.
  • Younger patients who want reversible options.

When are veneers the better choice?

Veneers are usually the better choice for larger cosmetic changes across several teeth, stains that bonding cannot hide well, or when you want a long-lasting, stain-resistant result. They suit:

  • Significant discoloration that whitening cannot fix.
  • Changing the shape and length of several front teeth.
  • Worn or uneven teeth across the smile.
  • People who want minimal ongoing maintenance.

Veneers require healthy teeth and gums and are not ideal for people who grind heavily without a night guard. Learn what veneers are and the types available.

Is bonding reversible while veneers are not?

Bonding is often reversible because little or no enamel is removed, so the resin can usually be polished away. Traditional porcelain veneers are not reversible, because removing enamel is permanent and the teeth will always need a covering.

That makes bonding a good way to try a cosmetic change before committing to veneers. Some minimal-prep veneers remove very little enamel, but they are not suitable for every case.

Does insurance cover bonding or veneers?

Insurance usually does not cover veneers because they are considered cosmetic, according to Delta Dental. Bonding may be covered when it repairs a chipped or broken tooth or fills a cavity, but not when it is purely cosmetic.

Ask your dentist how the treatment will be coded and request a pre-treatment estimate if you are unsure. The Cleveland Clinic's cosmetic dentistry overview compares cosmetic options.

How do you choose between bonding and veneers?

Choosing between bonding and veneers depends on your goals, budget and how long you want results to last. Start by asking your dentist what problem each would solve, how much tooth would be removed, what each would cost in total, and how long each is likely to last.

Many people start with bonding for a small fix and move to veneers later. Others choose veneers from the start for a complete, long-lasting transformation.

How do the procedures compare?

The bonding and veneer procedures differ mainly in time and tooth preparation. Bonding usually takes about 30 to 60 minutes per tooth in a single appointment. The dentist lightly roughens the tooth, applies resin, sculpts it, hardens it with a curing light and polishes it. Anesthetic is rarely needed.

Porcelain veneers usually take two or three visits over a few weeks. At the preparation visit, the dentist removes a thin layer of enamel, takes impressions or scans and may place temporary veneers. A lab then makes the veneers, which are bonded at the final visit. Many dentists offer a digital preview or trial smile before committing, which is especially helpful when changing several teeth at once.

Which needs more maintenance?

Bonding generally needs more maintenance than porcelain veneers. Composite resin can pick up stains from coffee, tea, red wine and tobacco, lose its shine and chip at the edges, so it often needs periodic polishing and occasional repairs.

Porcelain veneers need less day-to-day upkeep because porcelain resists staining and keeps its glaze. Both require good brushing, cleaning between teeth and regular dental visits, and both benefit from a night guard if you grind. When a veneer does fail, it usually needs replacing rather than a simple repair, which costs more than touching up bonding.

Frequently asked questions

Is bonding cheaper than veneers?

Bonding is much cheaper than porcelain veneers per tooth. CareCredit reports a $431 average for bonding, while Delta Dental lists porcelain veneers at $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth. Bonding may need replacing sooner, which narrows the long-term gap.

Which lasts longer, bonding or veneers?

Porcelain veneers usually last longer than bonding, commonly 10 to 15 years or more. Bonding is softer and more prone to staining and chipping, so it typically needs touching up or replacing sooner, though it is easier to repair.

Can bonding look as good as veneers?

Bonding can look very natural, especially for small repairs, but porcelain veneers usually keep a more lifelike shine and resist staining better over time. The dentist's skill strongly affects bonding results.

Is bonding reversible?

Bonding is often reversible because little or no enamel is removed, so the resin can usually be polished off. Traditional porcelain veneers require removing enamel and are not reversible.

Does insurance cover bonding or veneers?

Veneers are usually considered cosmetic and often not covered, according to Delta Dental. Bonding may be covered when it repairs damage or fills a cavity, but not when purely cosmetic.
