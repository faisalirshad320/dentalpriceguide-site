Are Veneers Permanent? How Long Porcelain and Composite Veneers Last

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 26, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

Veneers are a permanent commitment but not a lifetime restoration. Traditional porcelain veneers require removing a thin layer of enamel, so the teeth will always need a covering, yet the veneers themselves often last around 10 to 20 years before replacement. Composite veneers usually last fewer years. Each porcelain veneer costs $1,180 to $2,185 without insurance, per Delta Dental.

Key takeaways
  • Porcelain veneers often last around 10 to 20 years with good care.
  • Composite veneers are cheaper but tend to chip and stain sooner.
  • Enamel removed for traditional veneers does not grow back, so the decision is permanent.
  • Grinding, biting hard objects and gum recession shorten veneer life.
  • Replacement means paying again: a porcelain veneer is $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth (Delta Dental).

Are veneers permanent?

Veneers are permanent in the sense that the decision cannot be undone. For traditional porcelain veneers, the dentist removes a thin layer of enamel from the front of each tooth so the veneer sits flush. Enamel does not grow back, so those teeth will always need a veneer or another covering to look and function well.

The veneers themselves are not permanent. Like any dental restoration they wear, chip, stain at the edges or loosen over time and eventually need replacing. Thinking of veneers as a long-term commitment with ongoing maintenance costs gives a more realistic picture.

Some minimal-prep or no-prep veneers remove little or no enamel and can, in some cases, be removed later. They are not suitable for every smile, so ask your dentist whether they would work for you. Our guide to what veneers are explains the different types.

How long do veneers last?

Porcelain veneers often last around 10 to 20 years when they are well made and well cared for, and some last longer. Composite resin veneers usually last fewer years because the material is softer and more prone to chipping and staining.

These are typical ranges rather than guarantees. A veneer that is cracked by a fall in year three has a short life regardless of material, while a carefully maintained set in a patient who wears a night guard can outlast the usual estimate. Healthline's veneers guide describes the common lifespans and trade-offs.

Do porcelain veneers last longer than composite?

Porcelain veneers generally last longer than composite veneers. Porcelain is harder, more stain-resistant and keeps its glaze for years, while composite resin can absorb stains from coffee, tea and wine and can chip at the edges.

Composite veneers have their own advantages. They cost less, can often be done in one visit and are easier to repair, because a dentist can add material to a chip without replacing the whole veneer. Dental bonding, a similar resin technique, averages $431 per tooth according to CareCredit, compared with a $1,765 average for a single porcelain veneer.

The right choice depends on your budget, your bite and how long you want the result to last before maintenance.

What makes veneers fail sooner?

Veneers fail sooner when they are exposed to heavy forces, poor hygiene or changes in the teeth and gums around them. Many of these risks can be reduced.

  • Teeth grinding or clenching: nighttime grinding can crack or loosen veneers. A night guard helps.
  • Biting hard objects: ice, pens, fingernails and opening packages with your teeth.
  • Decay at the edges: the natural tooth under and around a veneer can still get cavities.
  • Gum recession: receding gums can expose the edge of the veneer and the tooth root.
  • Accidents and sports injuries: a mouthguard protects veneers during contact sports.
  • Poor bonding or fit: the quality of the original work matters a great deal.

How do you know a veneer needs replacing?

A veneer usually needs replacing when it is damaged, loose or no longer looks right against your natural teeth. Your dentist checks veneers at routine visits, but some signs are visible at home.

  • Chips or cracks in the veneer surface or edge.
  • A dark line at the gum, often from gum recession exposing the tooth or the veneer margin.
  • Staining at the edges, which can signal that the bond is breaking down.
  • A loose or moving veneer, or one that has come off completely.
  • Sensitivity or pain in the tooth underneath, which may mean decay.
  • A colour mismatch after whitening neighbouring natural teeth.

If a veneer comes off, keep it in a clean container and call your dentist. It can sometimes be re-bonded if it is intact and the tooth underneath is healthy. Do not try to glue it back yourself with household adhesives.

How do you make veneers last longer?

Veneers last longer with the same habits that protect natural teeth, plus a few extra precautions. Daily care and regular check-ups catch small problems before they become replacements.

  1. Brush twice a day with a soft brush and non-abrasive toothpaste.
  2. Clean between teeth daily to protect the gum line around each veneer.
  3. Wear a night guard if you grind or clench.
  4. Use a mouthguard for contact sports.
  5. Avoid biting ice, hard candy and non-food objects.
  6. Limit heavy staining drinks, especially with composite veneers.
  7. Keep regular dental cleanings, and ask the hygienist to use veneer-safe polishing.

What happens when veneers need replacing?

Replacing veneers means removing the old veneer, checking the tooth underneath and bonding a new one. Because some tooth structure may be lost each time, the replacement can be more involved than the original, and occasionally a crown is needed instead.

Replacement also means paying again. Delta Dental lists a porcelain laminate veneer at $1,180 to $2,185 per tooth without insurance, and CareCredit reports a $15,486 average for a full set of six to eight porcelain veneers. Delta Dental notes veneers are usually treated as cosmetic and often not covered by insurance. Budget for future replacements when you decide. See veneers cost and veneers vs crowns.

Are veneers worth it if they are not permanent?

Veneers can be worth it for people who want a significant, lasting change to the shape, colour or alignment of front teeth and who accept the long-term commitment. Many patients are very happy with results that last well over a decade.

Veneers are less suitable for people who grind heavily without wearing a guard, have untreated gum disease, or want a fully reversible option. Whitening, bonding or orthodontics may achieve your goal with less commitment. Frequently asked questions

Are veneers permanent?

Veneers are permanent in that traditional porcelain veneers require removing some enamel, which does not grow back, so the teeth will always need a covering. The veneers themselves are not permanent and usually need replacing after many years.

How long do porcelain veneers last?

Porcelain veneers often last around 10 to 20 years with good care, and sometimes longer. Grinding, biting hard objects, decay at the edges and gum recession can shorten their life. Regular check-ups and a night guard help them last.

How long do composite veneers last?

Composite veneers usually last fewer years than porcelain because the resin is softer and stains more easily. They are cheaper and easier to repair, so small chips can often be fixed without replacing the whole veneer.

Can veneers be removed?

Traditional porcelain veneers can be removed and replaced, but the tooth underneath has lost enamel and will still need a covering. Some minimal-prep or no-prep veneers remove little or no enamel and may be more reversible. Ask your dentist which type you would have.

What happens to your teeth under veneers?

The teeth under veneers stay alive and healthy if you care for them. They can still decay at the edges or along the gum line, so brushing, cleaning between teeth and regular dental visits remain important.
