Braces Colors: How to Choose the Best Color for Your Braces

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 21, 20265 price sources
Quick answer

Braces colors come from the elastic ties on each bracket and can usually change at every adjustment. Darker shades such as navy, dark purple, dark green and metallic gold or silver make teeth look whiter, while white, clear and yellow can make teeth look duller. The color does not change the price; metal braces average $6,343 according to CareCredit.

Key takeaways
  • Braces colors come from small elastic ties (ligatures) that hold the wire in each bracket.
  • Dark and metallic shades make teeth look whiter; white, clear and yellow ties can make teeth look more yellow.
  • Most patients can change colors at each adjustment visit, usually every several weeks.
  • Choosing a color does not normally add to the cost of braces, which average $6,343 for metal braces per CareCredit.
  • Self-ligating and ceramic braces may limit color choices, so ask your orthodontist what your system allows.

How do braces colors work?

Braces colors come from the tiny elastic rings, called ligatures or O-ties, that hold the archwire in each bracket. The brackets and wire stay silver (or tooth-colored with ceramic braces), and the elastics are what add color.

Orthodontists replace the elastic ties at most adjustment appointments, because the elastics lose their stretch and pick up stains over time. Each new set of ties is a chance to pick a new color, so a color you dislike only lasts until your next visit.

Many offices let you mix colors too. You can alternate two shades, pick team or school colors, or match a holiday. Some people choose a single color on the top teeth and a different color on the bottom.

Not every braces system uses colored elastics. Self-ligating braces have a built-in clip that holds the wire, so they may not need ties at all. Some orthodontists also use a continuous elastic chain instead of separate rings during certain stages; our guide to power chain braces explains when that happens and how the chain comes in colors as well.

What is the best color for braces?

The best color for braces is usually a dark or metallic shade, such as navy, dark blue, dark purple, dark green, teal, or silver and gold, because those shades contrast with teeth and make them look whiter. Beyond that, the best color is one you like seeing in the mirror for the next several weeks.

Popular braces colors and how they tend to look:

  • Dark blue and navy: make teeth look brighter and suit most skin tones.
  • Light blue and teal: fresh, cheerful colors that still contrast well with teeth.
  • Purple: deeper purples flatter most smiles; very pale lilac can look washed out.
  • Green: dark green and emerald look sharp; lime and neon green draw more attention.
  • Silver or gray: blends with metal brackets for a subtle, low-key look.
  • Gold: warm and bold, and pairs well with darker skin tones.
  • Pink and red: fun choices, though bright red can make gums look redder.

When people search for braces colors for girls or boys, the honest answer is that any shade works for anyone. Picking by favorite color, outfit or season keeps braces more fun, especially for kids and teens who wear them for a long time.

What color braces make your teeth look white?

Braces colors that make your teeth look whiter are darker, cooler shades such as navy, dark blue, dark purple and dark green, plus metallic silver. Those colors create strong contrast, so the enamel next to them looks brighter by comparison.

Some colors work against you. Avoid these if whiter-looking teeth are your goal:

  • White and clear: these ties pick up stains from coffee, tea, curry and soda and can turn yellowish before your next visit.
  • Yellow and gold-toned beige: close to the natural tint of enamel, so teeth can look more yellow.
  • Brown: can look like food or plaque caught on the brackets.
  • Very bright green: can look like food stuck in the teeth from a distance.

Black is a divisive choice. Black elastics give strong contrast, but some people feel black can look like decay or debris on the brackets. If you are unsure, try navy instead, which gives a similar effect with a softer look.

Good cleaning matters more than color. Plaque around brackets makes any shade look worse, so brush around each bracket, use floss threaders or a water flosser, and follow your orthodontist's instructions on cleaning.

What color braces should I get?

The color braces you should get depends on the look you want: pick dark or metallic shades for a subtle, whiter-looking smile, or bright colors if you want your braces to stand out. A few practical tips make the choice easier.

  1. Look at the color wheel at the office. Orthodontists keep a chart of available colors; hold it near your teeth and lips in the mirror.
  2. Think about your skin tone. Deep blues, purples and gold often suit darker skin tones; softer blues, teal and pink often suit fair skin.
  3. Plan for upcoming events. Before photos, a wedding or a job interview, many people choose silver, gray or navy so braces fade into the background.
  4. Match your style. School colors, sports teams and holiday combinations are common choices for teens.
  5. Remember it is temporary. You can change colors at most adjustment visits, so there is little risk in trying something new.

If you want braces to be as discreet as possible, ceramic braces with clear or tooth-colored ties are an option, though the clear ties can stain between visits. Our guide to clear braces covers how ceramic braces compare with metal ones.

Changing colors does not affect how the braces move your teeth. Your orthodontist's treatment plan, the wire and the adjustments do that work, whatever color you choose.

Does choosing braces colors cost extra, and what do braces cost?

Choosing braces colors does not normally cost extra, because the elastic ties are replaced at routine adjustment visits that are already part of your treatment fee. The bigger cost question is the braces themselves.

According to CareCredit's cost study, metal braces average $6,343, ceramic braces average $5,834, lingual braces average $9,221 and clear aligners average $5,108. Pearl dental cost data, based on ADA fee surveys, puts comprehensive braces at $3,680 to $9,650, with an average of $5,645. Delta Dental gives a range of $3,000 to $7,000 for traditional metal braces and $8,000 to $10,000 for lingual braces.

These figures differ because they measure different things: CareCredit reports a lender-commissioned cost survey, Pearl draws on dentist fee surveys, and Delta Dental gives an insurer's typical range. All are national averages, and local prices vary by city and orthodontist. For a full breakdown, see how much are braces.

Dental insurance with orthodontic benefits can lower the bill, often for children more than adults. Check what your plan includes using Delta Dental's coverage guide, and read how much are braces without insurance if you are paying yourself.

How do I keep braces colors from staining?

Keeping braces colors from staining comes down to limiting strongly pigmented foods and drinks and cleaning around the brackets after you eat. Light-colored and clear ties stain most easily.

  • Rinse with water after coffee, tea, cola, red sauces, curry or berries.
  • Brush around every bracket after meals, angling the brush above and below the wire.
  • Use an interdental brush or water flosser to reach under the wire.
  • Avoid tobacco, which stains ties and teeth quickly.

If an elastic tie breaks or falls off, the bracket may hold the wire less firmly. Call your orthodontist for advice; a single missing tie is often not urgent, but a loose bracket or a wire poking your cheek should be checked. Orthodontic wax can cover a sharp wire until your appointment.

Contact your orthodontist or dentist promptly if you notice swelling in the face or gums, fever, a sore that will not heal, or severe pain. Those symptoms are not normal braces discomfort and need professional attention. The Healthline guide to braces also covers what to expect during treatment, and the American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first orthodontic evaluation no later than age 7.

Frequently asked questions

What color braces should I get?

Get a dark or metallic color such as navy, dark purple, dark green, silver or gold if you want teeth to look whiter and braces to look polished. Choose bright colors if you want them to stand out. You can usually change colors at each adjustment visit, so trying a new shade carries little risk.

What color braces make your teeth look white?

Navy, dark blue, dark purple, dark green and metallic silver make teeth look whiter because they contrast strongly with enamel. White, clear and yellow ties tend to make teeth look more yellow, and clear ties can stain between visits. Good brushing around brackets also keeps teeth looking brighter.

What braces colors should I avoid?

Many people avoid white, clear, yellow, brown and very bright green braces colors. White and clear ties stain from food and drinks, yellow makes enamel look duller, and brown or lime green can look like food stuck on the brackets. Black is a matter of taste, since some feel it resembles debris.

How often can you change braces colors?

You can usually change braces colors at every adjustment appointment, when the orthodontist replaces the elastic ties. Adjustment visits are typically spaced several weeks apart, depending on your treatment plan. Ask your orthodontist about their schedule and which colors they stock.

Do braces colors cost extra?

Braces colors do not normally cost extra, because new elastic ties are placed during regular adjustment visits included in your treatment fee. The cost that matters is the braces themselves; CareCredit reports an average of $6,343 for metal braces and $5,834 for ceramic braces. Local prices vary.

Can you get colors on ceramic or clear braces?

Ceramic braces can use colored elastic ties, but most people choose clear or tooth-colored ties to keep the braces discreet. Clear ties can stain before the next visit. Some ceramic and self-ligating systems do not use elastics at all, so ask your orthodontist what your braces allow.
