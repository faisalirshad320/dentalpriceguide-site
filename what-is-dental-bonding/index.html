What Is Dental Bonding? How It Works, What It Fixes and What It Costs

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 4, 20262 price sources
Quick answer

Dental bonding is a treatment in which a dentist applies tooth-coloured composite resin to a tooth, shapes it and hardens it with a light to repair chips, close small gaps, reshape teeth or cover stains, usually in one visit. It costs about $431 per tooth on average according to CareCredit and typically lasts about 5 to 10 years (Healthline).

Key takeaways
  • Bonding uses composite resin to repair or reshape teeth.
  • It is usually done in one visit with little or no drilling.
  • CareCredit averages $431 per tooth; Healthline cites $300 to $600.
  • Bonding usually lasts about 5 to 10 years before replacement.
  • It stains and chips more easily than porcelain veneers.

What is dental bonding, in simple terms?

Dental bonding is a way of adding tooth-coloured material to a tooth to change its shape, size or colour, or to repair damage. The material is composite resin, a putty-like mix of plastic and fine glass particles that hardens when exposed to a special blue light.

Because the resin bonds directly to the tooth, bonding often requires little or no removal of natural tooth structure. The Cleveland Clinic's dental bonding guide describes it as one of the simplest and least expensive cosmetic dental procedures.

What can dental bonding fix?

Dental bonding can fix a range of small cosmetic and minor structural problems.

  • Chipped or cracked teeth.
  • Small gaps between teeth.
  • Discoloured teeth or spots that whitening cannot remove.
  • Short or uneven teeth that need lengthening or reshaping.
  • Exposed roots from gum recession, to reduce sensitivity.
  • Small cavities, as a tooth-coloured filling.

Bonding works best for small changes on front teeth. For larger transformations or back teeth under heavy biting forces, veneers or crowns may be better.

How is dental bonding done?

Dental bonding is usually done in a single visit of about 30 to 60 minutes per tooth. Anesthetic is often not needed unless bonding is filling a cavity.

  1. Shade selection: the dentist picks a resin shade that matches your teeth.
  2. Preparation: the tooth surface is lightly roughened and treated with a conditioning liquid.
  3. Application: resin is applied and sculpted into shape.
  4. Curing: a blue light hardens each layer.
  5. Finishing: the dentist trims, shapes and polishes the bond to a natural shine.

How much does dental bonding cost?

Dental bonding costs about $431 per tooth on average, according to CareCredit. Healthline cites $300 to $600 per tooth. The price depends on how much resin is needed, how complex the shaping is, and the dentist's skill and location.

Bonding is generally much cheaper than porcelain veneers. Insurance may cover bonding when it repairs a chipped or broken tooth or fills a cavity, but often not when it is purely cosmetic. See how much teeth bonding costs for more pricing detail.

What are the pros and cons of dental bonding?

The main advantages of dental bonding are low cost, speed and minimal tooth removal. Most bonding is finished in one appointment, and the natural tooth is largely preserved, so the treatment is often reversible.

The main disadvantages are durability and stain resistance. Composite resin is softer than porcelain and can chip, especially on biting edges. It can absorb stains from coffee, tea, wine and tobacco over time and does not respond to whitening. Bonding also usually needs replacing sooner than veneers or crowns.

How long does dental bonding last?

Dental bonding typically lasts about 5 to 10 years before it needs replacing, according to Healthline. Lifespan depends on where the bonding is, how much force it takes and your habits.

Avoid biting nails, ice, pens and hard foods directly with bonded teeth, wear a night guard if you grind, and keep regular cleanings with polishing. Small chips can usually be repaired by adding new resin.

Is bonding better than veneers?

Bonding is not better than veneers overall, but it is a better fit for some situations. Bonding costs less, is done in one visit and preserves more tooth, making it ideal for small fixes on one or a few teeth.

Porcelain veneers are better for larger cosmetic changes, very dark stains and results that resist staining and last longer. The Cleveland Clinic's cosmetic dentistry overview compares the options.

Who is a good candidate for bonding?

A good candidate for bonding has healthy teeth and gums and wants a modest improvement, such as fixing a chip or closing a small gap. People with untreated decay or gum disease need those problems treated first.

If you plan to whiten your teeth, do it before bonding, because resin is matched to your tooth shade at the time and will not lighten later. People who grind heavily may find bonding chips more often and may be better served by other options.

How do you care for bonded teeth?

Caring for bonded teeth is the same as caring for natural teeth, with a few extra precautions. Brush twice a day with a soft brush and non-abrasive toothpaste, clean between teeth daily, and keep regular dental cleanings.

Limit staining drinks or rinse after them, avoid tobacco, and avoid biting hard objects. Browse dental bonding before and after examples to see typical results.

Does insurance cover dental bonding?

Insurance often covers dental bonding when it is used to repair damage or decay, such as fixing a chipped tooth or filling a cavity with tooth-coloured resin. In those cases, bonding is usually treated as a basic restorative procedure.

Bonding done purely for appearance, such as closing a small gap or reshaping healthy teeth, is often considered cosmetic and excluded. Ask your dentist how the procedure will be coded and request a pre-treatment estimate if you are unsure. If insurance will not pay, bonding is still one of the most affordable cosmetic options available.

What are the alternatives to dental bonding?

Alternatives to dental bonding include teeth whitening for discoloration, orthodontics for gaps and crooked teeth, porcelain or composite veneers for larger cosmetic changes, and crowns for teeth with significant damage.

The best choice depends on the problem, your budget and how long you want the result to last. Whitening is the least invasive for colour. Orthodontics fixes alignment at the root of the problem. Veneers give a more durable cosmetic result than bonding. Your dentist can explain which option fits your goals.

Frequently asked questions

What is dental bonding?

Dental bonding is a procedure in which a dentist applies tooth-coloured composite resin to a tooth, shapes it and hardens it with a special light. It is used to repair chips, close small gaps, reshape teeth and cover stains, usually in one visit.

How much does dental bonding cost?

Dental bonding costs about $431 per tooth on average according to CareCredit, and Healthline cites $300 to $600 per tooth. The price depends on the amount of resin, complexity and the dentist's fees.

How long does tooth bonding last?

Tooth bonding usually lasts about 5 to 10 years before needing replacement, according to Healthline. Biting hard objects, grinding and staining drinks shorten its life; good care and regular polishing extend it.

Does dental bonding hurt?

Dental bonding usually does not hurt and often does not need anesthetic, because little or no tooth is removed. Numbing may be used if bonding is filling a cavity or the tooth is sensitive.

Can dental bonding be removed?

Dental bonding can usually be removed or replaced by a dentist, because it requires little or no removal of natural tooth. The dentist polishes away the resin and can restore the tooth or replace the bonding with veneers.
