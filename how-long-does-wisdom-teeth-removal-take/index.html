How Long Does Wisdom Teeth Removal Take? Surgery Time, Prep and Recovery

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 26, 20262 price sources
Quick answer

Wisdom teeth removal usually takes about 20 minutes to an hour in the chair, depending on how many teeth come out and whether they are impacted. Plan on two to three hours at the office including check-in, sedation and recovery, and about a week for initial healing, as described by the Cleveland Clinic.

Key takeaways
  • The extraction itself typically takes about 20 minutes to an hour.
  • Impacted teeth that need bone removal or sectioning take longer than erupted ones.
  • Allow two to three hours at the office if you are having IV sedation or general anesthesia.
  • Most people feel much better within a few days and heal the gums over a couple of weeks.
  • Delta Dental's out-of-network average for all four teeth surgically with sedation is $3,120.

How long is wisdom teeth surgery itself?

Wisdom teeth surgery itself usually lasts about 20 minutes to an hour. A single fully erupted wisdom tooth can come out in a few minutes, while four impacted teeth that are partly covered by bone take the longest.

Several factors change how long wisdom teeth removal takes. The number of teeth matters, but the position of each tooth matters more. A tooth sitting sideways under the gum, roots that curve, dense jawbone or roots close to the nerve that runs through the lower jaw all lengthen the procedure because the surgeon works slowly and carefully.

Your surgeon reviews a panoramic X-ray or 3D scan before the appointment and can usually tell you whether your case is simple or complex. Asking for an estimated chair time helps you plan your day and arrange a driver. Our guide to wisdom teeth removal cost explains how complexity also changes the price.

How are wisdom teeth removed?

Wisdom teeth are removed in a series of steps that depend on whether the tooth has broken through the gum. An erupted wisdom tooth is loosened and lifted out much like any other extraction.

An impacted wisdom tooth needs a surgical approach, which the Cleveland Clinic describes in its guide to impacted wisdom teeth:

  1. Anesthesia: local numbing, often combined with IV sedation or general anesthesia.
  2. Incision: the surgeon opens the gum over the tooth.
  3. Bone removal: a small amount of bone covering the tooth may be removed.
  4. Sectioning: the tooth may be divided into pieces so it comes out through a smaller opening.
  5. Removal and cleaning: the pieces are removed and the socket is rinsed.
  6. Stitches and gauze: dissolvable stitches may close the gum, and gauze controls bleeding.

Sectioning a tooth sounds alarming, but it usually makes removal gentler because less bone needs to be removed.

How long will you be at the office?

Most people spend two to three hours at the office for wisdom teeth removal under sedation. The extra time covers check-in, health checks, setting up the IV, the procedure itself and time in recovery until you are alert enough to leave.

If you have only local anesthetic, the visit is shorter because there is no sedation to wear off. With IV sedation or general anesthesia, you must have an adult drive you home and ideally stay with you for the rest of the day. You will not be allowed to drive, work or make important decisions until the next day.

Wear comfortable clothes with short sleeves for the IV, skip jewellery and contact lenses, and bring your list of medicines. The office will give you a written sheet of aftercare instructions to take home.

How should you prepare for wisdom teeth removal?

Preparing for wisdom teeth removal mostly means following your surgeon's pre-op instructions and getting your home ready for a few quiet days. Good preparation makes the recovery smoother.

  • Fasting: if you are having IV sedation or general anesthesia, you will usually be told not to eat or drink for several hours beforehand. Follow the exact instructions you are given.
  • Medicines: tell the surgeon about every medicine and supplement, especially blood thinners. Do not stop prescribed medicines unless your prescriber tells you to.
  • Ride home: arrange an adult driver and someone to stay with you if you are sedated.
  • Soft foods: stock yogurt, smoothies, broth, mashed potatoes and scrambled eggs.
  • Ice packs and gauze: have them ready for the first day.
  • No smoking: stopping before and after surgery lowers the risk of dry socket.

How long does recovery take after wisdom teeth removal?

Recovery after wisdom teeth removal usually takes about three to seven days for the worst of the swelling and soreness to pass. Swelling often peaks around the second or third day before easing.

The gum over each socket typically closes over a few weeks, and the bone underneath fills in gradually over several months. Most people return to school or desk work within a few days, while strenuous exercise is best avoided for about a week. Impacted teeth and surgery on the lower jaw tend to mean a slower recovery.

Follow our day-by-day wisdom teeth recovery timeline for what is normal at each stage. Pain that suddenly worsens a few days after surgery can be a sign of dry socket, which the Cleveland Clinic explains.

Call your surgeon for bleeding that will not stop with firm gauze pressure, fever, pus, swelling that keeps growing after day three, numbness that does not wear off, or severe pain. Seek emergency care for trouble breathing or swallowing.

How much does wisdom teeth removal cost?

Wisdom teeth removal cost depends on how many teeth come out, how impacted they are and the type of anesthesia. Delta Dental's out-of-network averages are $720 for all four fully erupted wisdom teeth removed non-surgically, $550 per tooth for one surgical extraction, and $3,120 for all four removed surgically with sedation or anesthesia, according to its wisdom teeth cost guide.

CareCredit reports averages of $835 for a single impacted wisdom tooth and $3,340 for four impacted wisdom teeth on its procedure cost list. Delta Dental notes insurance typically covers about 50% to 80% of the total cost. Longer, more complex surgeries usually cost more, so time and price tend to rise together.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to remove all four wisdom teeth?

Removing all four wisdom teeth usually takes about 45 minutes to an hour of surgery time, and longer if they are deeply impacted. Including check-in, sedation and recovery, most people spend two to three hours at the office. Your surgeon can estimate your chair time from your X-rays.

How long does it take to remove one wisdom tooth?

Removing one erupted wisdom tooth can take just a few minutes once you are numb. A single impacted wisdom tooth that needs an incision, bone removal or sectioning usually takes longer, often around 20 to 30 minutes. Anesthesia and recovery add to the total visit.

Will I be asleep for wisdom teeth removal?

Whether you are asleep for wisdom teeth removal depends on the anesthesia you and your surgeon choose. Options include local numbing only, nitrous oxide, IV sedation where you are drowsy and usually remember little, or general anesthesia. Impacted teeth are often removed under IV sedation.

How long after wisdom teeth removal can I eat?

You can usually have cool liquids and very soft foods a few hours after wisdom teeth removal, once the numbness wears off and bleeding has slowed. Avoid straws, hot drinks and crunchy food for the first several days. Most people return to a normal diet within about a week.

How long until I can go back to work after wisdom teeth removal?

Many people return to desk work or school two to three days after wisdom teeth removal. Physically demanding jobs may need about a week off. Recovery is slower after impacted lower teeth, so take your surgeon's advice and plan a few quiet days.
