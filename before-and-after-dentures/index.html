Before and After Dentures: What Changes and What to Expect

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated November 2, 20264 price sources
Quick answer

Before and after dentures, most people see a fuller smile, restored lip and cheek support, and the ability to chew and speak more normally again, after an adjustment period of a few weeks. A full lower denture costs $1,115 to $2,425 and a full upper $1,220 to $2,540 out-of-network, according to Delta Dental.

Key takeaways
  • Dentures restore the smile and support the lips and cheeks.
  • Expect a few weeks of adjustment for speaking and eating.
  • Full dentures: $1,115 to $2,425 (lower) and $1,220 to $2,540 (upper) out-of-network.
  • Implant-supported dentures cost $10,500 to $21,500 without insurance (GoodRx) but are far more stable.
  • Relines and replacements are needed over the years as the gums change.

What changes before and after dentures?

Before dentures, people with missing or failing teeth often have gaps in their smile, difficulty chewing, and sunken lips or cheeks where teeth no longer support the face. Some feel self-conscious about smiling or speaking.

After dentures, the smile looks complete, the lips and cheeks are supported again, and chewing and speaking improve once you adjust. A well-made denture is shaped and shaded to suit your face, and many people say they look younger. The degree of change depends on how many teeth were missing and how much bone loss has occurred.

How do dentures change your face?

Dentures can change your face by restoring the support that teeth used to give the lips and cheeks. Without teeth, the lower face can look shorter and the lips can fold inward. Dentures restore the height of the bite and fill out the profile.

Premium dentures with carefully designed teeth and gums can improve facial appearance further. Over the years, as the jawbone shrinks, a denture may need relining or replacement to keep that support. The Cleveland Clinic's prosthodontics guide explains how dentures are designed.

How long does it take to adjust to new dentures?

Most people take a few weeks to adjust to new dentures, and some take a couple of months. At first, dentures can feel bulky, and you may produce more saliva, have sore spots, or notice changes in speech.

  • Speech: practise reading aloud; most speech changes settle within weeks.
  • Eating: start with soft food cut into small pieces, chewing on both sides at once.
  • Sore spots: your dentist can adjust the denture to relieve pressure points.
  • Lower dentures: usually take longest to get used to because they are less stable.

What are immediate dentures?

Immediate dentures are placed on the same day your remaining teeth are extracted, so you are never without teeth. GoodRx lists an immediate full denture at about $1,900 without insurance in its denture cost guide.

Because the gums and bone shrink a lot in the months after extractions, immediate dentures usually need relines as healing progresses, and many people later move to a new conventional denture. GoodRx notes a chairside reline costs about $350 or more. Ask your dentist how immediate dentures fit into your full treatment plan and budget.

What types of dentures are available?

The types of dentures available range from basic to premium and from removable to implant-supported. Delta Dental lists full and partial dentures out-of-network, and CareCredit reports averages for upper and lower sets: $452 for basic dentures, $1,968 for traditional dentures and $6,514 for premium dentures.

  • Full dentures: replace all teeth in an arch.
  • Partial dentures: replace some teeth and clip onto remaining ones; see before and after partial dentures.
  • Flexible dentures: GoodRx lists a flexible nylon partial at about $1,700; see flexible dentures.
  • Implant-supported dentures: GoodRx lists $10,500 to $21,500 without insurance; see snap in dentures.

What can you eat before and after getting dentures?

After getting dentures, start with soft foods such as eggs, yogurt, soup, mashed vegetables and fish. As you gain confidence, add more textures. Cut food into small pieces and chew slowly on both sides to keep dentures stable.

Very sticky, hard or chewy foods remain difficult for many conventional denture wearers. Implant-supported dentures restore much more chewing power. Healthline's dentures versus implants guide compares chewing ability.

How do you care for dentures?

Caring for dentures keeps them clean, comfortable and longer-lasting. Remove and rinse them after eating, brush them daily with a soft denture brush and denture cleaner rather than regular toothpaste, and soak them overnight unless your dentist advises otherwise.

Clean your gums, tongue and any remaining teeth daily. Handle dentures over a folded towel or basin of water so they do not break if dropped. Keep regular dental visits so the fit and the health of your mouth can be checked.

What happens to dentures over time?

Over time, dentures gradually lose their fit because the jawbone and gums continue to shrink after teeth are lost. Loose dentures can cause sore spots, difficulty eating and a change in appearance.

Dentures usually need relining periodically and replacing every several years. Signs you need an adjustment include slipping, clicking, sore spots, or food getting trapped underneath. Implants can help reduce bone loss and stabilise dentures long term.

How much do dentures cost?

Dentures cost $1,115 to $2,425 for a full lower and $1,220 to $2,540 for a full upper out-of-network, according to Delta Dental. Partial dentures run $1,425 to $2,490 (lower) and $1,435 to $2,785 (upper). Prices depend on materials, the dentist and your location.

Many dental plans cover dentures as major services. See our main dentures cost guide for pricing with and without insurance.

What is the process of getting dentures?

Getting dentures usually involves several appointments over a few weeks, unless immediate dentures are placed on the day of extractions. The process starts with an exam and X-rays, and any teeth that cannot be saved are planned for removal.

  1. Impressions: detailed moulds or scans of the gums and any remaining teeth.
  2. Bite records: measurements of how the jaws relate and how much space the teeth need.
  3. Try-in: a wax model with the teeth set in place lets you and the dentist check appearance, speech and bite before the final denture is made.
  4. Delivery: the finished denture is fitted and adjusted.
  5. Follow-ups: several short visits to relieve sore spots and fine-tune the fit.

The try-in stage is your chance to request changes to tooth shape, shade or position, so bring a photo of your old smile if you have one.

Frequently asked questions

How do dentures change your appearance?

Dentures restore a complete smile and support the lips and cheeks, which can reduce a sunken look and make the lower face appear fuller. Well-designed dentures are matched to your face and skin tone. Results depend on bone loss and denture quality.

How long does it take to get used to dentures?

Most people take a few weeks to get used to dentures, and some take a couple of months. Speech and eating improve with practice. Your dentist can adjust sore spots. Lower dentures usually take longest because they are less stable.

How much do dentures cost?

Delta Dental lists full dentures at $1,115 to $2,425 for a lower and $1,220 to $2,540 for an upper out-of-network. CareCredit reports averages of $452 for basic sets, $1,968 for traditional and $6,514 for premium upper and lower dentures.

Do dentures look natural?

Modern dentures can look very natural, especially premium dentures with carefully shaped and shaded teeth and realistic gum colour. The dentist adjusts tooth size, shape and position to suit your face. Cheaper dentures may look less lifelike.

How long do dentures last?

Dentures usually last several years before needing replacement, with periodic relines in between as the gums and bone change shape. Signs you need a reline or new dentures include slipping, sore spots and difficulty chewing.
