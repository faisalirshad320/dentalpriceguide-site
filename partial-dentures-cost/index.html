How much do partial dentures cost?

Partial dentures cost about $1,425 to $2,490 for a lower partial and $1,435 to $2,785 for an upper partial at out-of-network dentists, according to Delta Dental's 2024 data. A flexible nylon partial costs about $1,700 without insurance, according to GoodRx. These are national figures, and prices in your area can differ.

Partial denture prices cover a wide range because partials differ so much. A partial replacing one front tooth is a very different appliance from one replacing several back teeth on both sides of the jaw with a cast metal framework.

The CareCredit cost study reports averages for full upper and lower sets rather than partials: $452 for basic dentures, $1,968 for traditional dentures and $6,514 for premium dentures. Those survey averages measure something different from Delta Dental's out-of-network fees, and they show how much materials and quality tiers move the price. Our dentures cost guide covers full dentures in detail.

Ask whether a partial denture quote includes extractions, adjustment visits after delivery, and any relines in the first year. Those extras can change the real total.

What changes before and after partial dentures?

Before and after partial dentures, the main changes are a filled gap in your smile, easier chewing where teeth were missing, and support that helps keep the remaining teeth from drifting. A well-made partial blends with your natural teeth, though metal clasps may show on some designs.

  • Appearance: replacement teeth fill gaps and can support the lips and cheeks where teeth were missing.
  • Chewing: you regain biting surfaces, though a partial does not match the force of natural teeth or implants.
  • Speech: missing front teeth can affect some sounds, and a partial often helps once you adapt.
  • Tooth position: filling the space helps stop neighboring teeth from tipping into the gap.

Daily care keeps the after result looking good. Take the partial out to clean it with a soft brush and a non-abrasive denture cleaner, brush the clasped teeth carefully because plaque collects around clasps, and store the partial in water or a soaking solution overnight unless your dentist says otherwise. Healthy anchor teeth are what keep a partial stable for years.

The first few weeks take adjustment. Extra saliva, sore spots, a bulky feeling and small changes in speech are common early on and usually ease. Your dentist will relieve pressure points at follow-up visits; follow their instructions on wear time, sleeping without the partial and cleaning. Seek prompt care for a sore that does not heal within a couple of weeks, facial swelling, fever or bleeding that will not stop.

What types of partial dentures are there, and how do costs compare?

The main types of partial dentures are cast metal frameworks, acrylic partials (including temporary "flippers") and flexible nylon partials, and each balances cost, comfort and durability differently.

  • Cast metal partial: a thin chrome-cobalt framework with clasps, topped with acrylic gums and teeth. Strong, slim and long-lasting, it is the usual choice for a long-term partial, including partial dentures for back teeth where chewing forces are heaviest.
  • Acrylic partial or flipper: an all-plastic base, often used as a temporary tooth while gums heal or before an implant. Dental flipper cost is generally at the low end of partial pricing, but flippers are bulkier and more fragile.
  • Flexible partial: a nylon-based resin with gum-colored clasps, about $1,700 without insurance per GoodRx. Comfortable and less visible, but harder to adjust or reline. See our guide to flexible dentures.
  • Implant-supported options: implants anchor the denture for more stability. GoodRx puts implant-supported full dentures at $10,500 to $21,500 without insurance; see snap in dentures for the removable implant version.

What drives the cost of a partial denture?

The cost of a partial denture depends mainly on the material, the number of teeth replaced, whether extractions or other preparation are needed, the dental lab and where you live.

  • Material: cast metal and flexible partials generally cost more than basic acrylic.
  • Number of teeth: more replacement teeth and a larger base raise lab costs.
  • Preparation: extractions, fillings or crowns on the anchor teeth are billed separately.
  • Upper vs lower: Delta Dental's upper partial range tops out higher than its lower range.
  • Provider and lab: prosthodontists and premium labs usually charge more.
  • Location: fees are typically higher in large cities.

Ongoing costs matter too. As gums and bone change shape, partials loosen and need relining; GoodRx lists a chairside reline at about $350 or more. Repairs to a broken clasp, or adding a tooth after another extraction, are extra.

How much do partial dentures cost with and without insurance?

Without insurance, partial dentures cost about $1,425 to $2,490 for a lower partial and $1,435 to $2,785 for an upper partial, based on Delta Dental's out-of-network figures, while with insurance you usually pay only part of that after your deductible. Dentures are often classed as major services, so the plan pays a smaller share than it does for basic care, and your annual maximum limits the total.

  • Check whether the plan has a waiting period for major services.
  • Ask about replacement limits; many plans pay for a new partial only once every several years.
  • Confirm whether the dentist is in network, which lowers the base fee.
  • Request a pre-treatment estimate before impressions are taken.

Original Medicare generally does not cover dentures, though some Medicare Advantage plans include dental benefits. Medicaid coverage of dentures for adults varies by state.

How can you pay less for partial dentures?

The best ways to pay less for partial dentures are choosing an in-network dentist, using a dental school or community clinic, comparing material options, and paying with HSA or FSA funds or a payment plan.

  1. Dental schools make partials under faculty supervision, usually for lower fees.
  2. Community health centers may offer sliding-scale prices based on income.
  3. HSA and FSA funds cover dentures with pre-tax money.
  4. Payment plans spread the cost; review the terms on any financing.
  5. Written quotes that list materials, lab fees and follow-up visits make comparison fair.
  6. Good care avoids repair costs: clean the partial daily, keep it moist when out, and never use boiling water.

If many teeth are missing or the remaining ones are weak, a full denture or implant option may be more cost-effective over time. Healthline compares dentures with implants, and Cleveland Clinic explains how prosthodontists plan tooth replacement.

Frequently asked questions

How much do partial dentures cost without insurance?

Partial dentures cost about $1,425 to $2,490 for a lower partial and $1,435 to $2,785 for an upper partial at out-of-network dentists, according to Delta Dental's 2024 data. GoodRx lists a flexible nylon partial at about $1,700 without insurance. Extractions, follow-up adjustments and relines can add to the total.

How much is a partial denture with insurance?

With insurance, you pay only part of a partial denture's cost after your deductible. Dentures are usually a major service, so the plan's share is smaller than for basic care, and your annual maximum caps the payout. Ask your dentist to send a pre-treatment estimate so you know your share before treatment starts.

How long does it take to get used to a partial denture?

Most people adjust to a partial denture within a few weeks. Early on, extra saliva, sore spots, a bulky feeling and small speech changes are common. Your dentist can ease pressure points at follow-up visits. See your dentist if sores do not heal, and promptly if you notice swelling, fever or bleeding.

Are flexible partial dentures better?

Flexible partial dentures are more comfortable and less visible for many people because they use gum-colored nylon clasps instead of metal. They cost about $1,700 without insurance, according to GoodRx. They are harder to adjust, reline or add teeth to than cast metal partials, so they are not the best choice for every mouth.

Can you eat normally with partial dentures?

You can eat most foods with partial dentures once you adjust, but chewing is less forceful than with natural teeth. Start with soft foods cut into small pieces, chew on both sides, and add harder foods gradually. Very sticky or hard foods can dislodge or damage a partial, so follow your dentist's advice.

What is a dental flipper?

A dental flipper is a lightweight acrylic partial denture, usually replacing one or two teeth, often worn temporarily while an extraction site heals or an implant is planned. Flippers are generally among the least expensive partials, but they are bulkier and more fragile than metal or flexible partials and are not meant for long-term use.
