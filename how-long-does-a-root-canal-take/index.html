How Long Does a Root Canal Take? Appointment Time, Recovery and Aftercare

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBSUpdated October 21, 20264 price sources
Quick answer

How long does a root canal take? A root canal usually takes one or two appointments, each lasting roughly 30 to 90 minutes, with front teeth at the shorter end and molars at the longer end. Most people return to normal activities the next day. See Delta Dental for an overview of the procedure.

Key takeaways
  • A root canal typically takes one or two visits of about 30 to 90 minutes each.
  • Molars take longest because they have more canals; front teeth are usually quickest.
  • Mild soreness for a few days is common; worsening pain or swelling needs a call to your dentist.
  • Back teeth usually need a crown after a root canal, and that crown is billed separately.
  • Your dentist's or endodontist's instructions take priority over general guidance.

How long is a root canal appointment?

A root canal appointment usually lasts about 30 to 90 minutes, and many root canals are finished in one visit, though some need a second. A simple front tooth with one canal is often at the shorter end, while a molar with three or four canals usually takes the full appointment or longer.

The time in the chair includes more than the cleaning itself. The dentist or endodontist numbs the tooth, places a small rubber sheet called a dental dam to keep it dry, opens the top of the tooth, cleans and shapes each canal, and then fills and seals the canals. A temporary filling usually closes the tooth at the end. Our guide to what a root canal procedure is explains each step in plain English.

Plan for a little extra time on the day. Check-in, X-rays and waiting for the anesthetic to work can add time before treatment starts, and you may want a short rest afterward before heading back to work.

Why do some root canals take longer?

Some root canals take longer because of the type of tooth, the number and shape of its canals, the amount of infection, and whether the tooth has been treated before. Your dentist can usually tell you at the consultation whether to expect one visit or two.

  • Tooth type: Front teeth usually have one canal, premolars one or two, and molars three or more, so molars take the most time.
  • Curved or narrow canals: Calcified or sharply curved canals take longer to locate and clean, and may be referred to an endodontist.
  • Active infection: When there is a lot of infection, the dentist may place medicine inside the tooth and finish at a second visit.
  • Retreatment: Redoing an old root canal means removing the previous filling material first, which adds time.
  • The final restoration: A crown is usually placed at a separate visit after the root canal, often a few weeks later.

A two-visit root canal is not a sign that something went wrong. Splitting treatment can give the tooth time to settle and lets the dentist confirm the infection is clearing before sealing it.

What is the recovery time after a root canal?

Root canal recovery time is usually short: most people go back to work or school the next day, and any tenderness typically eases over a few days. The numbness from the anesthetic usually wears off within a few hours.

Mild soreness when biting, and some tenderness in the gum, are common in the first several days. Over-the-counter pain relievers usually control this, as long as they are safe for you and you follow the label or your dentist's advice. If you are worried about pain, our page on root canal pain explains what most people feel during and after treatment.

Call your dentist promptly if pain gets worse after the first few days, your bite feels high, or the temporary filling falls out. Seek urgent care for facial or neck swelling, fever, trouble swallowing or breathing, bleeding that does not stop with pressure, or severe pain that pain relievers do not control.

Follow your dentist's or endodontist's aftercare instructions, including any antibiotics if they were prescribed. Finish the full course rather than stopping when you feel better.

Can you eat, drive or smoke after a root canal?

You can usually eat after a root canal once the numbness wears off, and most people can drive themselves home if they had only local anesthetic. Avoid smoking for as long as your dentist advises, because smoking slows healing.

  • Eating: Wait until feeling returns so you do not bite your cheek, lip or tongue. Good choices for the first day or two include yogurt, eggs, soup that is not too hot, mashed potatoes, pasta and smoothies.
  • What to avoid: Skip hard, crunchy or sticky foods, and chew on the other side until the tooth has its permanent filling or crown. A treated tooth without a crown can crack under heavy force.
  • Driving: Local anesthetic alone does not usually affect driving. If you had sedation, such as oral sedatives or IV sedation, arrange for someone else to drive you.
  • Smoking and vaping: Tobacco slows healing and raises the risk of problems. Ask your dentist how long to wait, and use the procedure as a reason to cut back.
  • Cleaning: Keep brushing and flossing gently, including around the treated tooth.

These are general guidelines. Your own dentist knows the details of your tooth and may give different advice, which takes priority.

How long does a root canal last, with or without a crown?

A root canal can last for many years, often for life, when the tooth is properly restored with a crown or filling and kept clean. The final restoration matters a lot for how long the treated tooth survives.

Back teeth such as molars and premolars usually need a crown after a root canal, because they take heavy chewing forces and a treated tooth can become more brittle. Cleveland Clinic lists protecting a tooth after root canal treatment as a common reason for a crown. Front teeth may do well with a filling alone if enough tooth structure remains.

How long a root canal lasts without a crown varies widely and depends on the tooth and how much of it is left. Leaving a back tooth with only a temporary filling for a long time raises the risk of a crack or new leakage into the canals. Book the crown when your dentist recommends it.

Root canal treatment is usually a response to deep decay, cracks or trauma. Our guide to signs you may need a root canal and how to avoid one covers early warning signs and prevention.

What does a root canal cost, and does the time affect it?

A root canal costs about $1,200 for a front tooth, about $1,300 for a premolar and about $1,500 for a molar out-of-network, according to Delta Dental. Molars cost more largely because they have more canals and take longer to treat.

Other sources measure cost differently. A cost study published by CareCredit reports averages of $984 for a front tooth and $1,337 for a molar, and Pearl dental cost data, based on ADA fee survey data, gives a national range of $685 to $1,800 (average $1,050). Delta Dental's figures reflect its out-of-network claims, while the others reflect surveys of typical fees. All are national figures, and local prices vary.

Delta Dental notes that these fees do not include the final crown or filling, and that insurance commonly pays 50%–80% after the deductible. For a full breakdown, see our root canal cost guide.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a root canal take on a molar?

A root canal on a molar usually takes the longest, often around 90 minutes or more, and sometimes two visits. Molars have three or more canals that each need cleaning, shaping and sealing. Front teeth with a single canal are often finished in a shorter appointment.

Can you go to work after a root canal?

Most people can go back to work the same day or the next day after a root canal. Your mouth may stay numb for a few hours, and mild soreness is common. If you had sedation, plan to rest for the remainder of the day and follow your dentist's instructions.

Can you drive after a root canal?

You can usually drive after a root canal if you had only local anesthetic, because numbing the tooth does not affect alertness. If you had oral or IV sedation, you should not drive, and you will need someone to take you home. Ask your dental office in advance which type you will receive.

What should I eat after a root canal?

After a root canal, eat soft foods such as yogurt, eggs, soup, pasta, mashed potatoes and smoothies once the numbness wears off. Avoid hard, crunchy and sticky foods, and chew on the other side until the tooth has its permanent filling or crown, unless your dentist advises otherwise.

How long does a root canal last without a crown?

How long a root canal lasts without a crown varies a lot and depends on the tooth and how much structure remains. Back teeth are at higher risk of cracking without a crown, so dentists usually recommend one. Front teeth sometimes do well with a filling alone. Follow your dentist's recommendation.

Is it normal to have pain days after a root canal?

Mild tenderness for several days after a root canal is common and usually improves steadily. Pain that gets worse, a bite that feels high, or swelling is not typical and needs a call to your dentist. Facial swelling, fever or severe pain should be treated as urgent.
