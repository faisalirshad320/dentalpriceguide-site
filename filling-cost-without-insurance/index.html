How Much Is a Filling Without Insurance?

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 1, 20263 price sources
Quick answer

Without insurance, a tooth-coloured (composite) filling costs $145 to $480 on a front tooth and $165 to $530 on a back tooth, and a silver amalgam filling costs $110 to $455 depending on the number of surfaces, according to Delta Dental. CareCredit reports averages of $226 for composite and $139 for amalgam.

Key takeaways
  • Composite fillings: $145 to $480 (front) and $165 to $530 (back) without insurance.
  • Amalgam (silver) fillings: $110 to $455, depending on surfaces.
  • CareCredit averages: $226 composite, $139 amalgam.
  • Bigger fillings covering more surfaces cost more.
  • Membership plans, dental schools and community clinics can lower the bill.

How much does a filling cost without insurance?

A filling without insurance costs $145 to $480 for a composite filling on a front tooth and $165 to $530 on a back tooth, according to Delta Dental. Silver amalgam fillings cost $110 to $455 depending on how many surfaces of the tooth are filled.

CareCredit's procedure cost list reports national averages of $226 for a composite resin filling and $139 for a silver amalgam filling. The two sources measure different things, Delta Dental giving ranges and CareCredit giving survey averages, so the most useful number is a written quote from your own dentist.

Why does the number of surfaces change the price?

The number of surfaces changes the price of a filling because larger fillings take more time and material. A tooth has up to five surfaces that can be filled: the chewing surface, the cheek side, the tongue side and the two sides that touch neighbouring teeth.

A small cavity on one surface is quick to fill. A cavity that wraps around the side of a back tooth into the chewing surface may involve two or three surfaces and costs more. Ask your dentist how many surfaces your filling involves when you compare quotes.

Is composite or amalgam cheaper without insurance?

Amalgam fillings are usually cheaper than composite fillings without insurance. CareCredit's averages are $139 for amalgam and $226 for composite resin.

Composite fillings match tooth colour, bond to the tooth and often need less drilling, which is why they are the standard choice for front teeth and very popular for back teeth. Amalgam is durable and long-lasting on back teeth, but it is visible and many practices no longer offer it. The cost difference is usually modest compared with the overall bill.

What else is on the bill besides the filling?

Besides the filling itself, a self-pay bill often includes an exam and X-rays to diagnose the cavity, especially at a first visit. These are separate line items at most offices.

Local anesthetic is normally included in the filling fee. If the cavity turns out deeper than expected, the dentist may add a protective liner or, if the nerve is affected, recommend a root canal and crown instead. Ask before treatment whether that is likely, so the cost does not surprise you.

How can you save on a filling without insurance?

You can save on a filling without insurance in several ways. A few are worth checking before you book.

  • Ask for a cash-pay discount: many offices reduce fees for payment at the time of service.
  • In-house membership plans: a yearly fee often includes cleanings and a discount on fillings.
  • Dental schools: supervised students provide care at lower fees.
  • Community health centers: sliding-scale fees based on income.
  • Discount dental plans: reduced fees at participating dentists.
  • HSA or FSA funds: pay with pre-tax money.

Should you delay a filling to save money?

Delaying a filling to save money usually costs more in the end. Cavities grow over time, and a small one-surface filling can become a large multi-surface filling, a crown or even a root canal if decay reaches the nerve.

If cost is a real barrier, ask your dentist which cavities are most urgent and whether treatment can be staged. Keep up with fluoride toothpaste and cleaning between teeth to slow new decay in the meantime. The Cleveland Clinic's cavity guide explains how decay progresses.

How much would a filling cost with insurance instead?

With insurance, fillings are typically covered at 50%–80% as basic procedures, according to Delta Dental. You pay the remaining share after your deductible, up to the plan's annual maximum.

Whether insurance is worth buying for a filling depends on waiting periods, premiums and how much other care you need. Delta Dental's coverage guide explains how plans work, and our page on tooth filling cost with insurance shows how to estimate your share.

What happens during a filling appointment?

A filling appointment usually takes 20 minutes to an hour, depending on the size and number of fillings. The dentist numbs the area, removes the decay, cleans the cavity and fills it with composite or amalgam.

Composite is placed in layers and hardened with a curing light, then shaped and polished. Amalgam is packed in and carved. Your bite is checked at the end. Mild sensitivity for a few days is common. Learn more about cavity fillings and what to expect.

Is a chipped tooth repair priced like a filling?

A chipped tooth repair is often priced similarly to a composite filling when the chip is small, because the dentist uses the same tooth-coloured material to rebuild the missing part. Larger breaks may need a crown or veneer, which cost more.

How long do fillings last, and does that affect the real cost?

How long a filling lasts affects its real cost, because a cheaper filling that needs replacing sooner can end up costing more over time. Amalgam fillings often last many years on back teeth, while composite fillings typically last a little less on heavily loaded chewing surfaces, though modern composites have improved considerably.

Lifespan depends on the size of the filling, where it sits, how hard you bite, whether you grind your teeth, and how well you keep the edges free of new decay. Large fillings on back teeth are under the most stress and are the most likely to crack or leak over time.

When comparing self-pay options, ask your dentist which material is most likely to last in that particular tooth. A slightly higher price for a filling that lasts longer may be the better value. Regular check-ups catch worn or leaking fillings early, when a small repair or replacement is still possible rather than a crown.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a filling without insurance?

A filling without insurance costs $145 to $480 for a composite on a front tooth and $165 to $530 on a back tooth, according to Delta Dental. Amalgam fillings cost $110 to $455 depending on surfaces. CareCredit reports averages of $226 for composite and $139 for amalgam.

Why are fillings on back teeth more expensive?

Fillings on back teeth usually cost more because molars are larger, often need fillings on more surfaces, are harder to reach and must withstand heavier chewing forces. Delta Dental lists $165 to $530 for a back-tooth composite filling.

Is a silver filling cheaper than a white filling?

A silver amalgam filling is usually cheaper than a white composite filling. CareCredit reports averages of $139 for amalgam and $226 for composite. Many dentists now place mostly composite fillings because they match tooth colour and need less drilling.

Can I get a cheap filling without insurance?

Cheaper fillings are often available at dental schools, community health centers with sliding-scale fees, and through in-house membership plans or discount dental plans. Many offices also offer discounts for paying in full at the visit.

What happens if I don't get a cavity filled?

If a cavity is not filled, decay usually grows deeper and can reach the nerve, causing pain or infection. A small filling may then become a larger filling, crown, root canal or extraction, all of which cost more.
