Dental Bonding Before and After: What It Can Fix and What to Expect

Medically reviewed by Dr. Faisal Irshad, MBBS, M.Phil Chemical PathologyUpdated November 2, 20262 price sources
Quick answer

Dental bonding before and after results typically show chips repaired, small gaps closed, uneven edges evened out and stains covered in a single visit, using tooth-coloured resin sculpted onto the teeth. Bonding costs about $431 per tooth on average, according to CareCredit, and usually needs replacing about every 5 to 10 years (Healthline).

What is dental bonding?

Dental bonding is a cosmetic and restorative treatment in which a dentist applies tooth-coloured composite resin to a tooth, shapes it and hardens it with a curing light. The resin bonds to the tooth surface and is polished to match the surrounding teeth.

Bonding is used for small repairs and improvements, usually on front teeth. Because it requires little or no removal of enamel and is usually finished in one visit, it is one of the most conservative and affordable cosmetic options. The Cleveland Clinic's dental bonding guide explains the procedure, and our page on what dental bonding is covers it in more detail.

What do chipped teeth look like after bonding?

Chipped teeth after bonding look whole again, with the missing corner or edge rebuilt in resin matched to the tooth's shade. Small chips on front teeth are among the most common reasons for bonding and often give the most striking before and after results.

The dentist builds the missing part in thin layers, shapes it to mirror the neighbouring teeth, and polishes it to blend in. A well-done bond is very hard to spot at normal speaking distance. Larger breaks, especially on back teeth, may need a crown instead.

Can bonding close gaps between teeth?

Bonding can close small gaps between teeth by adding resin to the sides of the teeth on either side of the space. This is often used for a small gap between the two upper front teeth.

Results depend on the size of the gap. Closing a large gap with bonding alone can make teeth look too wide, so orthodontics may be suggested first, with bonding used afterward to fine-tune shape. Ask your dentist to show you a preview or mock-up before committing.

Can bonding fix stains and uneven teeth?

Bonding can cover stains that whitening cannot remove, such as some intrinsic stains or white spots, by placing a thin layer of resin over the front of the tooth. It can also reshape teeth that are short, pointed, worn or slightly uneven, giving a more balanced smile.

If you plan to whiten your teeth, do it before bonding. Resin does not respond to whitening, so the bonding is matched to your final whitened shade. Bonding is less suited to covering very dark stains, where veneers may give a better result.

What should you realistically expect from bonding?

You should expect bonding to give a noticeable but natural improvement for small cosmetic issues. It is not designed to transform an entire smile in the way a full set of porcelain veneers can, and results depend heavily on the dentist's artistic skill.

Resin is less stain-resistant and less glossy over time than porcelain. Expect bonding to need occasional polishing and eventual replacement. Many people find bonding an excellent value option for one or a few teeth.

How long does dental bonding last?

Dental bonding usually lasts about 5 to 10 years before it needs replacing, according to Healthline. How long it lasts depends on where it is placed, how much force it takes and your habits.

Biting nails, chewing ice or pens, and grinding can chip bonding. Coffee, tea, red wine and smoking stain resin over time. Regular dental cleanings and polishing help it look its best, and small chips can usually be repaired by adding more resin.

How much does dental bonding cost?

Dental bonding costs about $431 per tooth on average, according to CareCredit's procedure cost list. Healthline cites a range of $300 to $600 per tooth. Price depends on how much resin is needed, the complexity of the shaping and the dentist's experience and location.

Bonding is usually much cheaper than veneers. Insurance may cover bonding when it repairs a chipped or broken tooth, but often not when it is purely cosmetic. See how much teeth bonding costs for more detail.

What happens during a bonding appointment?

A bonding appointment usually takes about 30 to 60 minutes per tooth. The dentist chooses a resin shade that matches your teeth, lightly roughens the tooth surface and applies a conditioning liquid so the resin adheres.

The resin is applied, sculpted into shape and hardened with a curing light, often in several layers. Finally, the dentist trims, shapes and polishes it. Anesthetic is rarely needed unless bonding is used to fill a cavity. You can eat and drink normally afterward, though staining drinks are best avoided for a day or two.

How do you care for bonded teeth?

You care for bonded teeth by brushing twice a day with a soft brush, cleaning between teeth daily and keeping regular dental cleanings. Ask your hygienist to use polishing methods safe for composite resin.

Avoid biting hard objects, use a night guard if you grind, and limit or rinse after staining drinks. The Cleveland Clinic's cosmetic dentistry overview discusses how bonding compares with veneers and other options.

Is bonding or veneers better for before and after results?

Bonding and veneers can both produce strong before and after results, but they suit different goals. Bonding is ideal for small, targeted fixes on one or a few teeth: a chip, a small gap, a worn edge or a single stain. It costs less, usually needs no enamel removal and is done in one visit.

Porcelain veneers are better for larger transformations, such as changing the colour and shape of several front teeth at once, or covering very dark stains. They resist staining, keep their shine for many years and usually last longer than bonding, but they cost more and require removing a thin layer of enamel. Some people start with bonding and move to veneers later. Ask your dentist to show before and after photos of their own bonding and veneer cases to judge which fits your expectations.

Frequently asked questions

What does dental bonding look like before and after?

Before bonding, teeth may have chips, small gaps, uneven edges or stains. After bonding, the resin rebuilds and reshapes the teeth to look whole and even, matched to the natural shade. Good bonding blends in at normal speaking distance.

How long does dental bonding last?

Dental bonding usually lasts about 5 to 10 years before needing replacement, according to Healthline. Biting hard objects, grinding and staining drinks shorten its life, while regular polishing and good habits extend it.

How much does dental bonding cost?

Dental bonding costs about $431 per tooth on average according to CareCredit, and Healthline cites $300 to $600 per tooth. It is usually much cheaper than veneers. Insurance may help when bonding repairs damage.

Does bonding damage your teeth?

Bonding does not usually damage teeth because little or no enamel is removed. The tooth surface is lightly roughened so the resin adheres. Bonding can typically be removed or replaced later without major harm to the natural tooth.

Can bonded teeth be whitened?

Bonded teeth cannot be whitened because composite resin does not respond to bleaching. If you want whiter teeth, whiten before bonding so the resin can be matched to your new shade. Stained bonding can be polished or replaced.
